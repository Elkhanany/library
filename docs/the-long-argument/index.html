Eight arguments that never finished

Philosophy does not really proceed by subject. It proceeds by a small number of questions that get handed down, reformulated, and handed on again. Each row below follows one of them through time. The same thinker appears in several rows, which is the point. Aristotle is not doing metaphysics on Tuesday and ethics on Thursday; the answers are load-bearing for one another.

Read them in pairs

Almost every major philosopher is answering someone. Read alone, a thinker is a list of positions to memorise. Read second, as a reply, the same positions become moves in an argument you can follow. These seven pairs are the fastest way in.

      Where this page is uncertain

      Dates before about 400 BCE are reconstructions, and bars with faded ends mark them. Even the order of Heraclitus and Parmenides is disputed. The Buddha's traditional dates (563–483 BCE) and the revised ones (c. 480–400) differ by most of a century, and Laozi may not have existed. Two popular formulas are wrong as usually stated: the familiar wording of Ockham's razor is not in Ockham, and Hegel never wrote “thesis, antithesis, synthesis.” Lines of influence are simplifications, and the dashed ones are explicitly parallels rather than transmissions. Check anything load-bearing against the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy.
