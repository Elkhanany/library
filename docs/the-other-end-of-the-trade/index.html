How market makers move markets

The Other End
of the Trade

Every option has a dealer on the other side of it, and the dealer hedges in the underlying to stay neutral. That hedging is forced, structural and measurable. This book builds it from the ground up, from return distributions and the Greeks to gamma exposure across the whole market, and then applies it to trading SPX.

Every option ever traded created an obligation for someone to transact in the underlying. The market is, in part, the integral of those obligations.

How it is arranged

Twelve parts, meant to be read in order. The mechanism sits in the middle of the book, with its foundations before it and its uses after.

In order

Foundations, mechanism, practice

Parts I to V lay the ground: distributions, quantitative trading, the products, and options. Parts VI to IX are the mechanism, from one dealer's hedge to the whole market. Parts X to XII turn it into trading, a strategy atlas and discipline.

Figures

Illustrations, not replacements

Every interactive widget of the Dealer Mechanics Lab is redrawn here as a figure, with its controls under it. A figure illustrates the passage beside it. The prose stands on its own without one.

Not advice

Education and analysis

Nothing here is a recommendation to trade. Where a number is a heuristic rather than a fitted estimate, the text says so, and no figure prints a probability of profit.

The parts

In reading order. The chapter count beside each part is computed when the book is built.

Part I
Foundation

Four ideas the rest of the book stands on: what a return distribution looks like, why returns are taken in logs, what edge means in expected value, and why the Sharpe ratio cannot see skew.

4 of 4 chapters
Part II
Quant Trading

Create a statistical edge, then execute it. The model, the simplest model worth studying, how a model is fitted, how its predictions meet an order book, and how a position is sized.

5 of 5 chapters
Part III
Markets

What is actually being traded. “The S&P 500” is at least six products with their own settlement, tax treatment and trading hours, and beneath them sits the routing that decides who fills an order and at what cost.

5 of 5 chapters
Part IV
Derivatives

Options as one member of a family. Futures, swaps and options share one logic and differ mainly in their payoff, and the part closes on why derivative markets exist at all.

3 of 3 chapters
Part V
Options

The contract and its four primary positions, the option chain, the Greeks of every order, volatility from log returns to the implied surface, and put-call parity. Every later part assumes it.

7 of 7 chapters
Part VI
Dealer Mechanics

Every option has a specific business on the other side of it. What that dealer inherits in the trade, how it neutralises the direction, and why gamma keeps undoing the hedge.

6 of 6 chapters
Part VII
Theory

Why delta hedging is the correct strategy, derived in five steps to the Black-Scholes-Merton equation. The result is that a delta-hedged option is a bet on realised volatility against implied.

2 of 2 chapters
Part VIII
Aggregation

From one dealer's book to the market. Gamma summed across strikes and expirations into GEX, the two regimes it describes, and the episodes in which the framework broke.

4 of 4 chapters
Part IX
Vol & Practice

The mechanism applied. VIX read as the same machinery seen from above, 0DTE and what it has done to intraday volatility, and a classifier for reading the tape.

3 of 3 chapters
Part X
Tactical Swing

How to swing-trade SPX and SPY once the mechanics are known. The central claim is narrow: short-horizon mean reversion that holds only while specific, measurable conditions do.

14 of 14 chapters
Part XI
Strategy Atlas

About fifty-eight named strategies, each reduced to the four primitives and sorted by tier, with margin and assignment in two appendices. Written to be looked up rather than read through.

8 of 8 chapters
Part XII
Discipline

The gap between paper and live performance, and the discipline that closes it: the retail haircut, a conviction score, kill rules, worked examples, setup cards and portfolio vega.

How it is built

The chapters are the Education curriculum of the Dealer Mechanics Lab, imported from its twelve source files. Every build checks that each word of that source reaches the book, in order, and that nothing is added to the prose. The widgets were rewritten as figures in the library's own style, so they draw in the book's colours, follow its light and dark themes, and need no network.

Part and chapter numbers are the curriculum's own. A reference in the text to Section 8A is chapter 8A here, and it links there.

The whole book installs to a home screen and reads with the network switched off. Nothing on any page is fetched from anywhere.

Part of A Library. For education only, and not investment advice. Black–Scholes assumes idealised markets; reality is messier.
