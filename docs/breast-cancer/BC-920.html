Part XII · Metastatic systemic therapy by subtype and line · Chapter 75

Systemic therapy in metastatic triple-negative disease from first line onwards

Three biomarkers decide first line, and after them the sequence is chemotherapy with better delivery.

1 · Scope, and the biomarker triage at diagnosis

Triple-negative is a description by absence, and treating it as one disease is the error this chapter exists to prevent. The transcriptional and genomic diversity within it is set out in Triple-negative and other biologically diverse phenotypes, and the clinical decision architecture is in Metastatic triple-negative disease.

Three tests decide first-line therapy and all three should be sent at metastatic diagnosis rather than at progression. PD-L1, with the assay and threshold named, because they differ between trials. Germline BRCA1 and BRCA2. And HER2 immunohistochemistry, because a tumour called triple-negative may be HER2-low or HER2-ultralow and therefore eligible for a conjugate approved on expression the older classification called negative.

That third point is the one most often missed. HER2-low is not a new biology, it is the same tumours under a threshold that moved, which is the measurement argument in Receptor assessment and the measurement problem.

The story below is the argument the rest of this chapter is evidence for, and it carries no figures at all. It states what each group of trials was built to find out, what came back, and what the design cannot settle, so the tables are read as answers to a question rather than as a list. It is one node of the [Chapter Stories](stories.html) map.

Finding something to aim at in a disease defined by absence

Triple-negative is the output of three negative assays, so the name says what the tumour lacks and nothing about what it needs. Every question in this cell is therefore a search for something to aim at. The things that have changed practice here were not lineage targets at all, but a repair defect, an immune infiltrate and a surface antigen, each carrying a measurement problem of its own.

Does naming a molecular dependency inside triple-negative disease produce a subset that a drug can be aimed at?

Rationale
The definition names no driver, so the search has been for whatever else the tumour depends on. Expression profiling supplied the candidates: epidermal growth factor receptor signalling, death receptor signalling, the androgen receptor, and the PI3K-AKT pathway. Each looked like a receptor-defined subtype waiting for its own drug.
Experiment
Each candidate was tested by adding its agent to chemotherapy, or by giving it alone in a population selected for the marker. EGFR antibody blockade went with carboplatin, and a death receptor agonist with a taxane. One androgen receptor antagonist was given alone to tumours staining positive for the receptor. A second antagonist was then randomised with and without a PI3K inhibitor. The AKT axis was carried furthest. Two randomised phase II trials added an AKT inhibitor to paclitaxel in unselected untreated disease. Two phase III trials followed, one restricting entry to tumours carrying a PIK3CA, AKT1 or PTEN alteration, the other enrolling regardless and reading the altered tumours alongside the whole population from testing done after the fact.
Finding
Every candidate produced responses in somebody. None produced a group that a controlled comparison could separate from the rest. The AKT axis is the clearest case. Both phase II trials favoured the AKT inhibitor on progression-free survival, and both phase III trials were negative, the one that admitted only pathway-altered tumours and the one that read the altered tumours inside an unselected population. Selection had already failed once inside the phase II work, where the trial that prespecified a PTEN-low population separated nothing in it.
Limitation
Most of these designs cannot say what the added agent contributed. The EGFR trial crossed over by protocol, so the same patients supply more than one response group. The death receptor trial set its primary endpoint as the response rate within each arm rather than the difference between arms. The two androgen receptor trials fail in different ways. The bicalutamide trial asked for oestrogen and progesterone receptor negativity and said nothing about HER2, so its enrolled population is wider than the cell it is filed under. The enzalutamide trial reports a clinical benefit rate for the combination and none for the antagonist given alone, and its dose-finding part admitted oestrogen receptor-positive patients. The AKT programme changed the question between phases, and it did so differently for each drug. One phase II trial read progression-free survival against a one-sided threshold, and the phase III trial of that same drug read overall survival as a primary endpoint, so the measurement that justified going forward is not the measurement that refuted it. The phase III trial of the other drug kept progression-free survival but moved to a biomarker-restricted population, so it never tested the population the phase II signal came from.

Does any measure of defective DNA repair select who benefits from platinum, beyond a germline BRCA mutation?

Rationale
Triple-negative tumours carry genomic scars that resemble those of BRCA1-mutant cancers. Platinum kills cells that cannot repair a crosslink. If the resemblance is functional rather than cosmetic, an assay of repair ought to find platinum responders among the large majority who carry no germline mutation.
Experiment
Single-agent platinum was given in a single-arm trial with a gene expression predictor written in as a coprimary endpoint. Platinum was then randomised against a taxane in unselected advanced disease, with germline BRCA and several other repair-defect markers prespecified as interaction tests. A platinum doublet was randomised against a taxane doublet in untreated disease, gemcitabine sitting in both arms, and tested for non-inferiority before superiority. Two further trials asked the same question of PARP inhibition. One added a PARP inhibitor to platinum and taxane in germline carriers. The other gave a PARP inhibitor alone to tumours wild-type for BRCA1 and BRCA2 that carried a mutation in another repair gene.
Finding
Platinum holds its own against a taxane in unselected disease. The single-agent comparison found no difference in response rate between carboplatin and docetaxel, and the doublet comparison favoured the platinum arm on progression-free survival. Germline BRCA status is the one marker that tracks response wherever it was measured, raising the response to carboplatin in the randomised trial and to single-agent platinum in the single-arm one. Nothing else reproduced it. The prespecified expression predictor failed outright, the commercial instability score carried as an interaction test in the randomised trial showed no effect, and the panel of other repair genes gave regressions only in a single gene and only on very few patients.
Limitation
The predictive claims rest on subgroups and on single arms rather than on randomised selection. In the platinum monotherapy trial the choice between cisplatin and carboplatin was left to the treating physician, so the two agents are not being compared. Its germline carriers were a subgroup rather than an entry criterion, and there were very few of them. Its prespecified expression predictor was the negative half of a coprimary endpoint, while the genomic instability analyses that did separate responders there were exploratory and did not survive the randomised trial's prespecified test of an instability score. The randomised single-agent comparison read objective response rate as its primary endpoint, so a difference in how long benefit lasted would not have shown, and its overall survival is not yet reported. The doublet comparison enrolled untreated patients at Chinese centres only, so its superiority is not a general statement about platinum against a taxane at any line. The trial that added a PARP inhibitor to platinum and taxane required a germline mutation but allowed any HER2-negative disease, so hormone receptor-positive patients sit inside a result filed here.

Does the PD-L1 assay identify who gains from a checkpoint inhibitor, whether it is added to first-line chemotherapy or given alone?

Rationale
Triple-negative tumours carry more lymphocytic infiltrate and more PD-L1 than the other subtypes do. Only a minority of them carry either. A checkpoint inhibitor can release a response only where one already exists, so each trial carried a companion assay meant to find the tumours where it does.
Experiment
Four randomised first-line trials added a checkpoint inhibitor to a chemotherapy backbone and compared it against the same backbone alone. They differ in the checkpoint antibody, in the chemotherapy partner, and in how PD-L1 was stained, scored and thresholded. Two used nab-paclitaxel, one a plain taxane, and one allowed a choice of backbones. A fifth trial gave the checkpoint inhibitor alone against the physician's choice of chemotherapy after prior treatment, with the score as the stratifying question.
Finding
Survival benefit is confined to the assay-positive population. The trial that read overall survival by score found it only above the higher cut-off, and it did not reach significance in everyone enrolled. Progression-free survival is less tidy than the selection story suggests, because in the first atezolizumab trial it improved in the whole enrolled population as well as in the assay-positive subgroup. Nothing appeared at all in the trial that paired the same antibody with a plain taxane, at any level of the score. Given alone after prior treatment, a checkpoint inhibitor did not beat chemotherapy at either score threshold tested.
Limitation
There is no single PD-L1 test in this set. The trials used different antibodies, scored different compartments, tumour cells against immune cells against a combined count, and set different cut-offs. A patient positive in one trial is not necessarily positive in another. The discordance between the two atezolizumab trials is a comparison across trials rather than a randomised one. Those two differ in the taxane, in the corticosteroid premedication a plain taxane requires, and in the population enrolled, so the failure cannot be assigned to the backbone alone. In the first atezolizumab trial the hierarchy stopped when overall survival in the whole enrolled population missed, so the survival figure clinicians quote for the assay-positive group was never formally tested.

Where the assay calls the tumour uninflamed, can another drug make it respond to checkpoint blockade?

Rationale
The infiltrate an assay measures on one biopsy at one moment is not a fixed property of the tumour. Cytotoxic drugs and radiation release antigen and change what sits in the microenvironment. PARP inhibition leaves cytosolic DNA that drives an interferon response. Each of those is a way of manufacturing the condition the assay was looking for.
Experiment
Induction was tested first. Patients were allocated between short courses of radiation, cyclophosphamide, cisplatin or doxorubicin, or no induction at all, before a common PD-1 backbone, with no control group. A separate single-arm study gave radiation with a checkpoint inhibitor and looked for response in the lesions that were not irradiated. Concurrent combination was tested by randomising carboplatin against carboplatin with PD-L1 blockade. The PARP route was tested three times. One trial gave a PARP inhibitor with PD-1 blockade in disease unselected for BRCA status. One gave a PARP inhibitor run-in followed by added PD-L1 blockade in germline carriers. One gave chemotherapy-free maintenance with a PARP inhibitor, with or without PD-L1 blockade, after platinum.
Finding
Each of these combinations produces disease control in a minority. In the trial that enrolled irrespective of BRCA status the responses concentrated in tumours carrying a BRCA mutation, while the maintenance trial singles out durable control in tumours without one. The randomised platinum comparison favoured adding PD-L1 blockade on both progression-free and overall survival. The benefit there did not follow PD-L1 status, which is the reverse of the selection the first-line trials were built on. Response after the common checkpoint backbone differed by which induction preceded it, highest in the anthracycline cohort and next in the platinum cohort.
Limitation
None of these designs isolates what the immune agent adds. The induction trial allocated patients between priming cohorts but never compared those cohorts statistically. Its cohorts are small and only its first stage is reported, so the ranking of priming strategies is a hypothesis rather than a result. The radiation study had no control arm either, so a shrinking unirradiated lesion cannot be credited to the abscopal effect it was built to look for. The randomised platinum trial is an exploratory phase II and its result is not the confirmatory evidence the approved chemotherapy-immunotherapy regimens rest on. Two of the three PARP combinations are single arms, so the contribution of either drug cannot be separated out. The third randomised its two arms but judged each against a historical control of continued platinum rather than against the other, so the longer progression-free survival in the combination arm is not evidence that the immune agent did anything, and its clinical benefit rate ran the other way. Eligibility drifts as well. One PARP cohort required only HER2-negative disease. The maintenance trial defined triple-negative at a receptor staining threshold rather than at no expression. Hormone receptor-positive tumours sit inside both.

Does an antibody-drug conjugate do better than the chemotherapy it replaces, and does it belong in the first line?

Rationale
TROP2 sits on the surface of most triple-negative tumours. A conjugate puts a topoisomerase inhibitor inside the cells that carry it, at a dose higher than free drug would allow. If chemotherapy earns its place in these regimens by killing cells, a conjugate should earn more of it.
Experiment
The conjugate was first randomised against the physician's choice of single-agent chemotherapy in patients who had already been through several lines. It was then moved to the first line in two randomised trials that split the population by whether checkpoint blockade was an option. Where PD-L1 was positive the conjugate was given with pembrolizumab against chemotherapy with pembrolizumab. Where it was not, the conjugate was given alone against chemotherapy. A second TROP2 conjugate was randomised against the investigator's choice of chemotherapy in the first line. A platform study gave that second conjugate with PD-L1 blockade in two single-arm first-line cohorts, one taking all comers and one restricted to PD-L1-high tumours.
Finding
The conjugate beat chemotherapy in every randomised comparison, in later lines and in the first line, with a checkpoint partner and without one. The second conjugate beat chemotherapy on both progression-free and overall survival in the first line. The single-arm cohorts of conjugate with PD-L1 blockade report the highest confirmed response rate of any first-line regimen recorded at this position.
Limitation
Those cohorts have no control arm. They cannot show what the immune agent adds to the conjugate, and they do not validate other pairings of a conjugate with immunotherapy. The two cohorts differ in selection rather than in treatment, so their response rates are not a comparison with each other, and the PD-L1-high cohort is early with immature duration of response. In the randomised first-line combination the control arm was chemotherapy with pembrolizumab rather than the conjugate alone, so the trial does not say whether the checkpoint inhibitor is still needed once the conjugate is in the regimen. Neither first-line trial of the conjugate that carries the later-line evidence has reported overall survival yet, so the move forward rests on progression-free survival for that agent while the competing conjugate already has a survival result to show.

When a tumour is picked out by its histology or by an uncommon alteration rather than by its receptors, is there enough evidence to act on?

Rationale
A small number of tumours filed as triple-negative carry something else that is actionable: a metaplastic histology, a high tumour mutational burden, an activating HER2 mutation without amplification, or a mutation in a repair gene other than BRCA1 and BRCA2. None is common enough to support a randomised trial in breast cancer alone. Each has therefore been studied as a cohort of a basket or as a single arm.
Experiment
Dual checkpoint blockade was given to a metaplastic cohort of a rare-tumour basket, and separately to patients selected on tumour mutational burden within HER2-negative disease. A HER2 kinase inhibitor with trastuzumab was given to a cohort with an activating HER2 mutation in disease that was HER2-negative by conventional testing. A PARP inhibitor was given alone to tumours wild-type for BRCA1 and BRCA2 carrying a mutation in another repair gene. All four are single arms reporting response rate.
Finding
Every cohort produced responses. The HER2-mutant cohort responded most often of the four and the repair-gene cohort next. In the metaplastic cohort the responses were few and still running at the time of the report, and every responder there developed adrenal insufficiency. In the repair-gene cohort the regressions were confined to a single gene. None of these is a rate a clinic can plan around, because each rests on a cohort small enough that one more responder would move it.
Limitation
None of these is a trial of triple-negative disease. A clinic has to match its patient to the entry criterion and not to the label on the cell. The metaplastic cohort was entered on histology alone, and its publication does not report hormone receptor or HER2 status at all. The mutational burden cohort was entered on burden within HER2-negative disease, so hormone receptor-positive patients are inside it with no split reported. The subgroup in it that carries the message about where to set the burden threshold is a handful of patients in a prespecified correlative analysis rather than a randomised comparison, so it establishes no cut-off. The HER2 mutation cohort likewise took both receptor groups, and its intervals are reported at a looser confidence level than the rest of the registry uses. Single arms of this size in selected populations establish that a response can happen, not how often it will.

2 · First-line chemoimmunotherapy and the PD-L1 threshold

Two trials established chemoimmunotherapy in this setting and they used different antibodies, different scoring systems and different thresholds, which is why their results are not interchangeable.

IMpassion130 used the SP142 assay scoring PD-L1 on immune cells. KEYNOTE-355 used 22C3 scoring a combined positive score across tumour and immune cells. A tumour can be positive by one and negative by the other. The claim "PD-L1-positive" without naming the assay is not a claim about the patient, and Immunotherapy makes the general version of this argument.

The formal statistical structure of IMpassion130 matters and is instructive. Overall survival was to be tested in the intention-to-treat population first, and only if significant there would it be tested in the PD-L1-positive subgroup. It was not significant in the intention-to-treat population, so the hierarchy stopped, and the PD-L1-positive figures are descriptive rather than confirmatory. That is a textbook illustration of the hierarchical testing point in Clinical trials and evidence interpretation.

KEYNOTE-119 is the negative that completes the picture. Single-agent pembrolizumab in pretreated disease did not significantly improve survival at any PD-L1 threshold. Checkpoint blockade in this disease needs a chemotherapy partner and needs to be given early.

Caution

The PD-L1 threshold selects in metastatic disease and does not select in the early setting, where KEYNOTE-522 showed benefit regardless. Do not carry the metastatic selection rule backwards into the neoadjuvant decision. Neoadjuvant therapy in triple-negative disease including checkpoint blockade explains why the same marker behaves differently.

3 · PARP inhibition for germline carriers

OlympiAD and EMBRACA established single-agent PARP inhibition against physician's-choice chemotherapy in germline BRCA carriers. The mechanism, synthetic lethality with homologous recombination deficiency, is in DNA repair, replication stress, and genomic instability.

Two points govern use. The benefit is in progression-free survival and quality of life more than in overall survival, which is the interpretive problem Clinical trials and evidence interpretation describes for the metastatic setting generally. And the eligible population is defined by germline testing, so a patient never tested is a patient never eligible. That makes the access argument in Disparities across the breast cancer continuum a treatment argument here, not only an equity one.

TBCRC 048 extended activity beyond germline BRCA to PALB2 and somatic BRCA, which is a biologically coherent extension because the same repair pathway is disabled.

4 · Conjugates in second line and beyond

Conjugates changed this setting more than any other class. ASCENT established sacituzumab govitecan against physician's choice in pretreated disease, and TROPION-Breast02 and ASCENT-03 and ASCENT-04 moved the class earlier.

For patients whose tumours express HER2 at low or ultralow levels, DESTINY-Breast04 brought trastuzumab deruxtecan into a population previously classified as HER2-negative. The practical consequence is that HER2 immunohistochemistry must be read and reported at the low end, where concordance is poorest. The measurement problem is in Receptor assessment and the measurement problem and the computational attempt to fix it in Data science and precision oncology.

The unresolved question is sequencing. A patient may now be eligible for two or three conjugates, and whether activity persists after failure of one with the same payload class is not established. The mechanism is in Antibody-drug conjugates and targeted delivery and the general form of the problem in Unresolved questions and future directions.

5 · Chemotherapy sequencing when no target is present

A substantial fraction of patients have no PD-L1 positivity, no germline alteration and no HER2 expression, and for them the sequence is single-agent chemotherapy.

The principles are settled and worth stating plainly because they are frequently violated. Sequential single agents are preferred to combinations outside visceral crisis, because combinations improve response rate and not survival while adding toxicity. Treat to progression or intolerance rather than to a fixed number of cycles. And the choice among agents is driven by prior exposure, residual neuropathy, and what the patient is willing to tolerate.

The evidence base here is older and thinner than for the targeted options, and that asymmetry is itself informative. The patients with no target are the patients with the least recent evidence, and they are not a small group.

6 · Antigen-defined and alteration-defined options

A small number of patients have alterations that cross subtype boundaries, and finding them requires testing that is not routine in this disease.

Somatic alterations in the homologous recombination pathway, HER2 mutations treated in HER2-directed therapy in metastatic disease from first line onwards, and high tumour mutational burden or mismatch repair deficiency all define eligibility for agents not otherwise indicated in triple-negative disease. The alteration-defined logic and its trial designs are in Expression and alteration-defined treatment across subtypes.

The argument for broad sequencing in this subtype is stronger than the yield suggests, because the alternative is a chemotherapy sequence with modest expectations. A low probability of finding an actionable alteration is weighed against what is available if nothing is found.

7 · Unresolved questions and what reads out next

What to do after a conjugate is the dominant unanswered question, and it is now the commonest clinical situation in later-line triple-negative disease.

Whether checkpoint blockade has any role after first line. KEYNOTE-119 says single-agent does not. Whether combinations do, and in whom, is unsettled.

Whether PD-L1 is the right marker at all. It selects imperfectly, the assays disagree, and the biology in Host and tumor interactions suggests tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes carry information PD-L1 does not. A better marker would change first-line practice more than a better drug.
