Metastatic therapy from first line onwards

What the three first-line assays actually select for, and what the conjugate record has and has not settled.

1 · Scope, and the biomarker triage at diagnosis

This chapter is the trial record for metastatic triple-negative disease, arranged by line. It says what was tested, what came back, and what each design cannot settle. How to approach the patient who carries this disease is in The biomarker triage, and what is left when it finds nothing, which covers risk, burden, symptoms and goals. Where the two chapters touch, The biomarker triage, and what is left when it finds nothing points forward to the tables here.

Triple-negative is a description by absence, and treating it as one disease is the error this chapter exists to prevent. The transcriptional and genomic diversity inside the label is set out in Triple-negative and other biologically diverse phenotypes1.

Three tests decide first-line therapy. All three should be sent at metastatic diagnosis rather than at progression, because the first-line decision cannot wait for a result ordered later.

PD-L1, with the assay and the threshold named in the report. The antibody clone and the scored compartment differ between the trials that produced the indications, so a bare positive is not a statement about the patient.

Germline BRCA1 and BRCA2, extended to PALB2 and to somatic BRCA alterations. A patient never tested is a patient never eligible, which makes the access argument in Disparities across the breast cancer continuum a treatment argument here.

And HER2 immunohistochemistry, read specifically at the 0 against 1+ boundary. A tumour called triple-negative may be HER2-low, and therefore eligible for a conjugate approved on expression that the older classification called negative. The ultralow category below that boundary carries randomised evidence only in hormone receptor-positive disease, so it does not open the same route here HER2-low and HER2-ultralow disease.

That third point is the one most often missed. HER2-low is not a new biology. It is the same tumours under a threshold that moved, which is the measurement argument in Receptor assessment and the measurement problem and HER2-low and ultralow reframed as a heterogeneity problem. The order in which to send the three tests, and what to do while they are outstanding, is set out in Biomarker-driven treatment selection rather than here.

Each section below opens with the question its trials were built to answer, taken from the [Chapter Stories](stories.html) map, and closes with a table filtered on that same question. The story says what was being looked for and what the design cannot settle. The table says what else is in the cell. The prose argues the decision.

2 · First-line chemoimmunotherapy and the PD-L1 threshold

Advanced disease · TNBC · any

Finding something to aim at in a disease defined by absence

Does the PD-L1 assay identify who gains from a checkpoint inhibitor, whether it is added to first-line chemotherapy or given alone?

Rationale
Triple-negative tumours carry more lymphocytic infiltrate and more PD-L1 than the other subtypes do. Only a minority of them carry either. A checkpoint inhibitor can release a response only where one already exists, so each trial carried a companion assay meant to find the tumours where it does.
Experiment
Four randomised first-line trials added a checkpoint inhibitor to a chemotherapy backbone and compared it against the same backbone alone. They differ in the checkpoint antibody, in the chemotherapy partner, and in how PD-L1 was stained, scored and thresholded. Two used nab-paclitaxel, one a plain taxane, and one allowed a choice of backbones. A fifth trial gave the checkpoint inhibitor alone against the physician's choice of chemotherapy after prior treatment, with the score as the stratifying question.
Finding
Survival benefit is confined to the assay-positive population. The trial that read overall survival by score found it only above the higher cut-off, and it did not reach significance in everyone enrolled. Progression-free survival is less tidy than the selection story suggests, because in the first atezolizumab trial it improved in the whole enrolled population as well as in the assay-positive subgroup. Nothing appeared at all in the trial that paired the same antibody with a plain taxane, at any level of the score. Given alone after prior treatment, a checkpoint inhibitor did not beat chemotherapy at either score threshold tested.
Limitation
There is no single PD-L1 test in this set. The trials used different antibodies, scored different compartments, tumour cells against immune cells against a combined count, and set different cut-offs. A patient positive in one trial is not necessarily positive in another. The discordance between the two atezolizumab trials is a comparison across trials rather than a randomised one. Those two differ in the taxane, in the corticosteroid premedication a plain taxane requires, and in the population enrolled, so the failure cannot be assigned to the backbone alone. In the first atezolizumab trial the hierarchy stopped when overall survival in the whole enrolled population missed, so the survival figure clinicians quote for the assay-positive group was never formally tested.

Randomised first-line trials adding a checkpoint inhibitor to a chemotherapy backbone. They differ in the antibody, the backbone and the PD-L1 assay.

Four of those rows test one idea. Add a checkpoint inhibitor to a chemotherapy backbone in untreated disease, and compare it against the same backbone alone. On the direction of selection they agree. Where overall survival was read in the whole enrolled population, it did not separate. Every survival signal that survives scrutiny sits inside an assay-positive population. That is the class finding, and it is narrower than the way the class is usually described.

There is no single PD-L1 test behind it. IMpassion130 used SP142 and scored staining on immune cells as a percentage of tumour area. KEYNOTE-355 used 22C3 and scored a combined positive score across tumour cells, lymphocytes and macrophages. A tumour positive by one is not necessarily positive by the other. The practical consequence is that the request has to name the assay the intended regimen was registered with. A report giving a score without a clone cannot be mapped onto either indication. The general form of this problem is in Immunotherapy and Predictive biomarkers and patient selection.

The trials diverge in three ways at once, and the antibody is the least important of them. The backbone differs: nab-paclitaxel in IMpassion130 and TORCHLIGHT, plain paclitaxel in IMpassion131, and a choice of three regimens in KEYNOTE-355. The assay differs, as above. The testing order differs, and that matters most. IMpassion130 required overall survival to reach significance in the intention-to-treat population before the PD-L1-positive subgroup could be tested formally. IMpassion131 tested progression-free survival in the PD-L1-positive population first. KEYNOTE-355 read overall survival at a score of 10 or higher, at 1 or higher, and in everyone.

Read that way, the two atezolizumab trials are not a disagreement about atezolizumab. They differ in the taxane, in the corticosteroid premedication a plain taxane requires, and in the population enrolled5. The comparison between them is across trials and was never randomised. The failure cannot be assigned to the backbone alone. TORCHLIGHT then reproduced the selection pattern with a third antibody on a nab-paclitaxel backbone3, which is the best available argument that the pattern belongs to the class rather than to one drug.

The first-line decision rests on KEYNOTE-355. It is the only trial here that carried overall survival as a primary endpoint to a prespecified score threshold and met it, against the chemotherapy the clinic would otherwise have given4. Three backbones were permitted, so the result does not belong to one taxane. Progression-free survival had already been reported separately7. The trial also shows where selection stops working, because the survival curves at a score of 1 or higher are not the curves at 10 or higher. That decay is itself the argument. A marker whose effect weakens as its threshold is lowered is behaving predictively, which is what makes this cut-point load-bearing rather than administrative.

IMpassion130 is the cautionary case rather than the load-bearing one, and it is a worked illustration of the hierarchical testing point in Clinical trials and evidence interpretation. Overall survival missed in the intention-to-treat population, so the hierarchy stopped there6. Every survival figure quoted for the assay-positive group descends from a test that was never formally performed8. The drug is used on that basis anyway, which is a defensible clinical judgement and not a statistical result. Progression-free survival there improved in the whole enrolled population as well as in the assay-positive subgroup, which is the one place the selection story is untidy.

ASCENT-04 renders in this table because it carries a checkpoint inhibitor in both arms. It is not a test of whether to add one. Both arms received pembrolizumab, and what was randomised was the cytotoxic partner against a conjugate, on a progression-free survival endpoint that favoured the conjugate2. It belongs to the argument in Conjugates in second line and beyond and appears here only because the filter is on modality. Its practical consequence belongs here, though. A positive PD-L1 result no longer determines the whole regimen. It determines only that a checkpoint inhibitor belongs in it.

What the table cannot show is the variable that decides eligibility. There is no column for the antibody clone, the scored compartment or the cut-off, so assays that select different patients print as one word. Nobody ran two assays on the same tumours inside a randomised trial and compared who they selected. Nobody randomised nab-paclitaxel against plain paclitaxel within a single checkpoint trial, which is the only design that could settle the backbone question. No first-line trial was powered to exclude a small benefit in the assay-negative population, which is not the same as showing there is none. Nobody randomised the band between the two thresholds as a stratum of its own, so the weak result at a score of 1 or higher is a diluted superset rather than a test of the patients between 1 and 9. And nobody randomised how long a checkpoint inhibitor should continue, or whether it should be carried past progression.

KEYNOTE-119 completes the picture from outside this table, because its line of therapy puts it elsewhere. Single-agent pembrolizumab after one or two prior treatments did not improve survival at either score threshold tested9. Checkpoint blockade in this disease needs a cytotoxic partner and needs to be given early. Nothing in the later-line record contradicts that.

Caution

The PD-L1 threshold selects in metastatic disease and does not select in the early setting, where KEYNOTE-522 showed benefit without selection at all10,11. Do not carry the metastatic selection rule backwards into the neoadjuvant decision, and do not carry a neoadjuvant PD-L1 result forwards into this one. Neoadjuvant therapy and checkpoint blockade sets out why the same marker behaves differently on either side of surgery.

Reported PARP inhibitor trials reaching this population. Both registration trials enrolled hormone receptor-positive patients as well.

What those five rows agree on is narrow and firm. Where a germline BRCA1 or BRCA2 mutation is present, a PARP inhibitor delays progression better than the single agent a clinic would otherwise reach for. It also produces a markedly higher response rate. Neither registration trial converted that into longer survival on final analysis17,18.

The rows differ in what the inhibitor was compared against rather than in the inhibitor. OlympiAD and EMBRACA gave it alone against the physician's choice of chemotherapy in carriers. BROCADE3 added it to carboplatin and paclitaxel, so its control arm already contained the platinum that the same repair defect selects for. That trial therefore asks the smaller question of what a PARP inhibitor adds to a regimen already aimed at the defect. DORA tested maintenance without chemotherapy after platinum, and judged each of its two arms against a historical control rather than against the other12. It enrolled by response to platinum rather than by germline status, and sustained benefit tracked neither germline BRCA nor PD-L1. TBCRC 048 is a single arm, and its question is which gene rather than which drug.

The decision rests on OlympiAD and EMBRACA together, because they are the only randomised comparisons against the treatment a carrier would otherwise receive at that point. They used different PARP inhibitors, different chemotherapy baskets and different randomisation ratios, and they returned the same shape of answer. Two trials reproducing one another with different drugs is the strongest evidence this section has.

The gap between the two endpoints is the interpretive problem, and it has a candidate explanation rather than a settled one. Subsequent PARP inhibitor and platinum use was commoner in the chemotherapy arm of EMBRACA than in the talazoparib arm. Adjusting for it moved the hazard ratio for death in the right direction, with a bootstrap interval still crossing one18. Crossover is a reason the survival signal may be diluted. It is not evidence that the signal exists.

TBCRC 048 widened the eligible set and, more usefully, narrowed it. Confirmed responses occurred only with germline PALB2 and with somatic BRCA1 or BRCA2 alterations. Nothing responded with ATM or CHEK2 alterations alone14,19. That is a result about which gene rather than about how much repair deficiency, and it does not license a panel-wide indication. Its population was mostly oestrogen receptor-positive, so the row sits in this table on mechanism rather than on receptor status.

Two things the table cannot show follow from that. Nobody has randomised a PARP inhibitor against platinum, which is the other drug the same defect selects for, so the choice between them at first progression rests on no head-to-head evidence at all. And there is no column for what patients received afterwards, which is where the survival reading of both registration trials lives.

One result from outside this table belongs in the argument. In TNT, germline BRCA status raised the response rate to carboplatin and not to docetaxel20. The same defect that PARP inhibition exploits also selects for platinum, which is why the two decisions are made together rather than in sequence. It is also why they fail together. Secondary intragenic mutations that restore the BRCA2 reading frame confer resistance both to platinum and to PARP inhibition21. The mechanism is in DNA repair, replication stress, and genomic instability and the shared resistance in PARP inhibitor and platinum resistance, including restoration of repair.

Caution

A homologous recombination deficiency score records past genomic instability. Scars persist after repair function returns, so a tumour can score homologous recombination deficiency high while repairing normally at the moment of testing. The score describes history. The drug needs the present.

4 · Conjugates in second line and beyond

Advanced disease · TNBC · any

Finding something to aim at in a disease defined by absence

Does an antibody-drug conjugate do better than the chemotherapy it replaces, and does it belong in the first line?

Rationale
TROP2 sits on the surface of most triple-negative tumours. A conjugate puts a topoisomerase inhibitor inside the cells that carry it, at a dose higher than free drug would allow. If chemotherapy earns its place in these regimens by killing cells, a conjugate should earn more of it.
Experiment
The conjugate was first randomised against the physician's choice of single-agent chemotherapy in patients who had already been through several lines. It was then moved to the first line in two randomised trials that split the population by whether checkpoint blockade was an option. Where PD-L1 was positive the conjugate was given with pembrolizumab against chemotherapy with pembrolizumab. Where it was not, the conjugate was given alone against chemotherapy. A second TROP2 conjugate was randomised against the investigator's choice of chemotherapy in the first line. A platform study gave that second conjugate with PD-L1 blockade in two single-arm first-line cohorts, one taking all comers and one restricted to PD-L1-high tumours.
Finding
The conjugate beat chemotherapy in every randomised comparison, in later lines and in the first line, with a checkpoint partner and without one. The second conjugate beat chemotherapy on both progression-free and overall survival in the first line. The single-arm cohorts of conjugate with PD-L1 blockade report the highest confirmed response rate of any first-line regimen recorded at this position.
Limitation
Those cohorts have no control arm. They cannot show what the immune agent adds to the conjugate, and they do not validate other pairings of a conjugate with immunotherapy. The two cohorts differ in selection rather than in treatment, so their response rates are not a comparison with each other, and the PD-L1-high cohort is early with immature duration of response. In the randomised first-line combination the control arm was chemotherapy with pembrolizumab rather than the conjugate alone, so the trial does not say whether the checkpoint inhibitor is still needed once the conjugate is in the regimen. Neither first-line trial of the conjugate that carries the later-line evidence has reported overall survival yet, so the move forward rests on progression-free survival for that agent while the competing conjugate already has a survival result to show.

Four randomised comparisons, two conjugates, one direction. In every one of them the conjugate beat the chemotherapy it was built to replace, in refractory disease and in the first line, with a checkpoint partner and without one. No other class in this chapter has that record.

ASCENT established it in patients who had already been through several lines of chemotherapy24,25. The comparator arm is the part that changed practice, because it records the honest baseline this disease had before conjugates, and that baseline is short.

The class then moved forward in three trials that split the first line by whether checkpoint blockade was an option. ASCENT-03 took the patients for whom it was not23. ASCENT-04 took the PD-L1-positive patients and held pembrolizumab constant in both arms2. TROPION-Breast02 took a second conjugate into the immunotherapy-ineligible slot22.

Read the control arm column before the result column, because it explains the spread across those four rows. ASCENT was set against single-agent chemotherapy in heavily pretreated disease. ASCENT-03 and TROPION-Breast02 were set against first-line chemotherapy, which in ASCENT-03 could be a gemcitabine and carboplatin doublet. ASCENT-04 was set against chemotherapy with pembrolizumab, the strongest comparator in the table. The conjugate's margin narrows as the comparator strengthens, and none of that is a statement about the conjugates.

The divergence in that group is maturity and endpoint, not efficacy. TROPION-Breast02 carried overall survival as a dual primary endpoint and met it. ASCENT-03 reports progression-free survival with survival not yet mature, and a published methodological critique argues that crossover-adjusted analysis is needed before the first-line position is treated as settled26. ASCENT-04 reports its survival data as immature at the analysis published. ASCENT-03 and TROPION-Breast02 were built for the same slot, immunotherapy-ineligible first-line disease. They are not equivalent in what has been demonstrated.

So the first line in immunotherapy-ineligible disease rests on TROPION-Breast02, which is currently the only first-line conjugate trial with a survival result against the chemotherapy a clinic would otherwise give. ASCENT-03 will become load-bearing when its survival reads out, and ranking the two before then is a statement about follow-up rather than about drugs. One detail of ASCENT-03 is worth carrying into the consent conversation. Confirmed response rates were close to identical between the arms while duration of response was not. What the conjugate buys there is the length of a response rather than the chance of one.

ASCENT-04 leaves its own question open. Its control arm was chemotherapy with pembrolizumab, not the conjugate alone, so the trial cannot say whether the checkpoint inhibitor is still needed once the conjugate is in the regimen. The single-arm cohorts of BEGONIA arms 7 and 8 pair the other conjugate with PD-L1 blockade and report the highest confirmed response rate recorded at this position27. Neither cohort has a control arm, and they are exploratory rather than part of the record above. One enrolled all comers and the other only PD-L1-high tumours, which makes the two rates a difference in selection rather than a comparison.

Three things the table cannot show. No trial has randomised one TROP2 conjugate against the other, and none is planned, so the choice between them rests on indication and toxicity. No trial has randomised the order of conjugate and chemotherapy, so the case for giving the class first rather than keeping it in reserve is inferred from separate trials at separate lines. No trial selected patients on TROP2 expression, and the biomarker analysis of ASCENT found progression-free survival tracking an expression gradient without producing a threshold anyone could act on28. And the population that established the class in ASCENT, heavily pretreated and conjugate-naive, has largely been abolished by moving the class forward.

DESTINY-Breast04 brought trastuzumab deruxtecan into a population the older classification called HER2-negative, and it improved both progression-free and overall survival against the physician's choice of chemotherapy30,31. Read it with its population in view. The great majority of those enrolled were hormone receptor-positive, and the primary endpoint was read in that cohort. The triple-negative patients are a minority inside an all-patient secondary analysis. The row that opens the HER2-low route for this chapter is therefore mostly evidence from another chapter's population.

DAISY argues that the boundary is arbitrary in the other direction, because confirmed responses occurred in immunohistochemistry 0 disease at a rate well above zero29. It is a single-arm phase II trial in cohorts defined by staining, so it establishes that such tumours can respond and not how often they will. The apparent disagreement between the two rows is about measurement rather than biology. Pathologist concordance is poorest exactly at the 0 against 1+ boundary Where heterogeneity sets a ceiling on precision medicine, which means a proportion of the patients in each cohort would have been assigned to the other by a second reader.

TRADE-DXd sits in this table without a result, because it is still enrolling. Its contribution is discussed in Unresolved questions and what reads out next, where the sequencing question it was built for belongs.

The comparison this table most needs does not exist. No trial has randomised a HER2-directed conjugate against a TROP2 conjugate in a tumour eligible for both, which is now the commonest choice a clinic faces in HER2-low triple-negative disease. Nor is there randomised evidence restricted to HER2-low tumours without hormone receptor expression.

Toxicity rather than efficacy separates the three conjugates in practice, and the dominant toxicity differs by linker and payload handling rather than by target Toxicity mechanisms, interstitial lung disease, ocular, and mucosal. Sacituzumab govitecan is limited by neutropenia and diarrhoea, and UGT1A1 genotype is worth knowing before the first dose in a patient with a suggestive history. Trastuzumab deruxtecan carries the interstitial lung disease risk that should shape ordering in anyone with poor baseline reserve or prior thoracic radiotherapy. Datopotamab deruxtecan is dominated by mucosal and ocular events instead, which are more tolerable in a frail patient and more disruptive in a working one.

Interplay

Three conjugates now reach this population and all three carry a topoisomerase I inhibitor payload. A patient who receives sacituzumab govitecan, datopotamab deruxtecan and trastuzumab deruxtecan has had three exposures to one payload class delivered by three antibodies. The line count says three lines. The biology says one. Does any measure of defective DNA repair select who benefits from platinum, beyond a germline BRCA mutation?

Rationale
Triple-negative tumours carry genomic scars that resemble those of BRCA1-mutant cancers. Platinum kills cells that cannot repair a crosslink. If the resemblance is functional rather than cosmetic, an assay of repair ought to find platinum responders among the large majority who carry no germline mutation.
Experiment
Single-agent platinum was given in a single-arm trial with a gene expression predictor written in as a coprimary endpoint. Platinum was then randomised against a taxane in unselected advanced disease, with germline BRCA and several other repair-defect markers prespecified as interaction tests. A platinum doublet was randomised against a taxane doublet in untreated disease, gemcitabine sitting in both arms, and tested for non-inferiority before superiority. Two further trials asked the same question of PARP inhibition. One added a PARP inhibitor to platinum and taxane in germline carriers. The other gave a PARP inhibitor alone to tumours wild-type for BRCA1 and BRCA2 that carried a mutation in another repair gene.
Finding
Platinum holds its own against a taxane in unselected disease. The single-agent comparison found no difference in response rate between carboplatin and docetaxel, and the doublet comparison favoured the platinum arm on progression-free survival. Germline BRCA status is the one marker that tracks response wherever it was measured, raising the response to carboplatin in the randomised trial and to single-agent platinum in the single-arm one. Nothing else reproduced it. The prespecified expression predictor failed outright, the commercial instability score carried as an interaction test in the randomised trial showed no effect, and the panel of other repair genes gave regressions only in a single gene and only on very few patients.
Limitation
The predictive claims rest on subgroups and on single arms rather than on randomised selection. In the platinum monotherapy trial the choice between cisplatin and carboplatin was left to the treating physician, so the two agents are not being compared. Its germline carriers were a subgroup rather than an entry criterion, and there were very few of them. Its prespecified expression predictor was the negative half of a coprimary endpoint, while the genomic instability analyses that did separate responders there were exploratory and did not survive the randomised trial's prespecified test of an instability score. The randomised single-agent comparison read objective response rate as its primary endpoint, so a difference in how long benefit lasted would not have shown, and its overall survival is not yet reported. The doublet comparison enrolled untreated patients at Chinese centres only, so its superiority is not a general statement about platinum against a taxane at any line. The trial that added a PARP inhibitor to platinum and taxane required a germline mutation but allowed any HER2-negative disease, so hormone receptor-positive patients sit inside a result filed here.

Finding something to aim at in a disease defined by absence

Does naming a molecular dependency inside triple-negative disease produce a subset that a drug can be aimed at?

Rationale
The definition names no driver, so the search has been for whatever else the tumour depends on. Expression profiling supplied the candidates: epidermal growth factor receptor signalling, death receptor signalling, the androgen receptor, and the PI3K-AKT pathway. Each looked like a receptor-defined subtype waiting for its own drug.
Experiment
Each candidate was tested by adding its agent to chemotherapy, or by giving it alone in a population selected for the marker. EGFR antibody blockade went with carboplatin, and a death receptor agonist with a taxane. One androgen receptor antagonist was given alone to tumours staining positive for the receptor. A second antagonist was then randomised with and without a PI3K inhibitor. The AKT axis was carried furthest. Two randomised phase II trials added an AKT inhibitor to paclitaxel in unselected untreated disease. Two phase III trials followed, one restricting entry to tumours carrying a PIK3CA, AKT1 or PTEN alteration, the other enrolling regardless and reading the altered tumours alongside the whole population from testing done after the fact.
Finding
Every candidate produced responses in somebody. None produced a group that a controlled comparison could separate from the rest. The AKT axis is the clearest case. Both phase II trials favoured the AKT inhibitor on progression-free survival, and both phase III trials were negative, the one that admitted only pathway-altered tumours and the one that read the altered tumours inside an unselected population. Selection had already failed once inside the phase II work, where the trial that prespecified a PTEN-low population separated nothing in it.
Limitation
Most of these designs cannot say what the added agent contributed. The EGFR trial crossed over by protocol, so the same patients supply more than one response group. The death receptor trial set its primary endpoint as the response rate within each arm rather than the difference between arms. The two androgen receptor trials fail in different ways. The bicalutamide trial asked for oestrogen and progesterone receptor negativity and said nothing about HER2, so its enrolled population is wider than the cell it is filed under. The enzalutamide trial reports a clinical benefit rate for the combination and none for the antagonist given alone, and its dose-finding part admitted oestrogen receptor-positive patients. The AKT programme changed the question between phases, and it did so differently for each drug. One phase II trial read progression-free survival against a one-sided threshold, and the phase III trial of that same drug read overall survival as a primary endpoint, so the measurement that justified going forward is not the measurement that refuted it. The phase III trial of the other drug kept progression-free survival but moved to a biomarker-restricted population, so it never tested the population the phase II signal came from.

7 · Unresolved questions and what reads out next

Advanced disease · TNBC · any

Finding something to aim at in a disease defined by absence

Where the assay calls the tumour uninflamed, can another drug make it respond to checkpoint blockade?

Rationale
The infiltrate an assay measures on one biopsy at one moment is not a fixed property of the tumour. Cytotoxic drugs and radiation release antigen and change what sits in the microenvironment. PARP inhibition leaves cytosolic DNA that drives an interferon response. Each of those is a way of manufacturing the condition the assay was looking for.
Experiment
Induction was tested first. Patients were allocated between short courses of radiation, cyclophosphamide, cisplatin or doxorubicin, or no induction at all, before a common PD-1 backbone, with no control group. A separate single-arm study gave radiation with a checkpoint inhibitor and looked for response in the lesions that were not irradiated. Concurrent combination was tested by randomising carboplatin against carboplatin with PD-L1 blockade. The PARP route was tested three times. One trial gave a PARP inhibitor with PD-1 blockade in disease unselected for BRCA status. One gave a PARP inhibitor run-in followed by added PD-L1 blockade in germline carriers. One gave chemotherapy-free maintenance with a PARP inhibitor, with or without PD-L1 blockade, after platinum.
Finding
Each of these combinations produces disease control in a minority. In the trial that enrolled irrespective of BRCA status the responses concentrated in tumours carrying a BRCA mutation, while the maintenance trial singles out durable control in tumours without one. The randomised platinum comparison favoured adding PD-L1 blockade on both progression-free and overall survival. The benefit there did not follow PD-L1 status, which is the reverse of the selection the first-line trials were built on. Response after the common checkpoint backbone differed by which induction preceded it, highest in the anthracycline cohort and next in the platinum cohort.
Limitation
None of these designs isolates what the immune agent adds. The induction trial allocated patients between priming cohorts but never compared those cohorts statistically. Its cohorts are small and only its first stage is reported, so the ranking of priming strategies is a hypothesis rather than a result. The radiation study had no control arm either, so a shrinking unirradiated lesion cannot be credited to the abscopal effect it was built to look for. The randomised platinum trial is an exploratory phase II and its result is not the confirmatory evidence the approved chemotherapy-immunotherapy regimens rest on. Two of the three PARP combinations are single arms, so the contribution of either drug cannot be separated out. The third randomised its two arms but judged each against a historical control of continued platinum rather than against the other, so the longer progression-free survival in the combination arm is not evidence that the immune agent did anything, and its clinical benefit rate ran the other way. Eligibility drifts as well. One PARP cohort required only HER2-negative disease. The maintenance trial defined triple-negative at a receptor staining threshold rather than at no expression. Hormone receptor-positive tumours sit inside both.
