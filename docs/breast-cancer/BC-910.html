Part XII · Metastatic systemic therapy by subtype and line · Chapter 73

Metastatic triple-positive disease

The subgroup that is half of HER2-positive disease and has almost no trial designed for it.

1 · Why this group is under-served by both the hormone receptor-positive and HER2-positive trials

Hormone receptor-positive HER2-positive metastatic disease is a large fraction of HER2-positive breast cancer and it is studied almost entirely as a subgroup. The hormone receptor-positive trials excluded it because HER2 positivity was an exclusion criterion. The HER2-positive trials enrolled it and stratified for it, but were not powered within it.

The result is an evidence base with a specific shape. Every headline figure a reader knows for this group comes from a subgroup of a trial designed to answer a different question. The standard for what a subgroup can support is set in Clinical trials and evidence interpretation. Applying it here is uncomfortable. Most of what is practised in this group rests on analyses that were not powered and often not prespecified as primary.

The biology says this should matter. Reciprocal signalling between the oestrogen receptor and the HER2 axis, described in Estrogen receptor signaling and endocrine resistance and HER family signaling, means each pathway is an escape route from blockade of the other. A tumour with both receptors active has a route out that a tumour with one does not.

2 · Endocrine maintenance after induction

The standard pattern in practice is chemotherapy with dual HER2 blockade until best response, then chemotherapy stops and endocrine therapy is added to continuing antibody therapy. That pattern is widely used and its randomised support is thinner than its adoption suggests.

The rationale is straightforward. If the oestrogen receptor is an escape route from HER2 blockade, leaving it unblocked during maintenance leaves the escape route open. The mechanism argues for concurrent blockade rather than sequential, and the practice follows the mechanism.

What the practice lacks is a trial comparing endocrine maintenance against no endocrine maintenance in this specific population with modern first-line therapy. Reasoning from mechanism to practice is legitimate when the evidence is absent, and it should be labelled as what it is.

3 · CDK4/6 inhibition with HER2 blockade

PATINA is the trial this group actually has. It tested palbociclib added to endocrine therapy and HER2 blockade as maintenance after induction, in hormone receptor-positive HER2-positive metastatic disease specifically.

That specificity is what makes it valuable, and it is rare enough to be worth naming as such. This is a trial designed for this population rather than a subgroup of a trial designed for a neighbouring one.

The mechanistic rationale connects three chapters. Cyclin D1 and CDK4/6 sit downstream of both the oestrogen receptor and the HER2 axis, which is the convergence described in Cell cycle regulation. Blocking the convergence point rather than the two upstream inputs is a different strategy from blocking both inputs, and it predicts activity where single-pathway blockade fails.

4 · Chemotherapy omission and the evidence that supports it

The live de-escalation question in this group is whether chemotherapy is needed at all in a patient with low-burden, indolent, hormone receptor-positive HER2-positive disease.

The argument for omission is that such a patient has two targetable pathways and a disease trajectory measured in years, and that chemotherapy contributes toxicity to a regimen whose activity comes from targeted agents. The argument against is that the induction trials all included chemotherapy, so omitting it departs from every regimen with randomised support.

This is a de-escalation question, and the margin discipline in Clinical trials and evidence interpretation applies. A trial showing no significant difference between a chemotherapy-containing and a chemotherapy-free regimen does not establish equivalence unless it was powered against a prespecified margin. Retrospective and real-world comparisons of chemotherapy omission are confounded by indication, since the patients in whom clinicians omit chemotherapy are the patients with indolent disease.

In practice

Before omitting chemotherapy outside a trial, state what you would accept as the cost. A patient with a decade of expected survival and low disease burden is a different proposition from one with visceral crisis, and the second is not a de-escalation candidate whatever the receptor profile.

5 · Conjugates in triple-positive disease

Conjugates do not care about the oestrogen receptor. A HER2-directed conjugate delivers payload to a HER2-expressing cell whether or not that cell also expresses the oestrogen receptor, so there is no mechanistic reason to expect reduced activity in this group.

What does change is the sequencing context. A patient with both receptors has endocrine options that a hormone receptor-negative patient does not, which means the conjugate competes for position against endocrine therapy as well as against other HER2-directed agents. The choice is not only which agent but which axis to pressure first.

The receptor heterogeneity argument in Part VI has a specific consequence here. A tumour scored positive for both receptors on one core may contain regions expressing one and not the other, which is the discordance documented in Receptor discordance and conversion and the HER2 spatial variation in HER2 heterogeneity. Dual-axis treatment is partly a hedge against that sampling problem rather than only a hedge against resistance.

6 · Subgroup analyses from the major trials and what they can support

Most of what is known about this group comes from hormone receptor-positive subgroups of the HER2-positive registration trials. Those subgroups are informative and they have three limits that should be stated whenever they are used.

They were not powered. A subgroup containing roughly half the trial has roughly half the events and a correspondingly wider confidence interval, so an apparently smaller effect in one subgroup is frequently compatible with the overall effect.

They are prone to the interaction fallacy. A treatment effect that is significant in one subgroup and not the other is not evidence of a differential effect. That requires a test of interaction, which is less often reported and less often significant.

They are consistent across trials in one respect worth noting. Hormone receptor-positive HER2-positive disease shows a more indolent trajectory and a later relapse pattern than hormone receptor-negative HER2-positive disease. That is a prognostic observation rather than a predictive one. It should not be used to infer that a treatment works less well.

7 · Unresolved questions and what reads out next

The questions this group needs answered are questions no current trial is powered for.

Whether endocrine therapy should be given concurrently with induction rather than started at maintenance. Mechanism argues for concurrent. No trial has compared the two.

Whether chemotherapy can be omitted in selected patients. This needs a de-escalation trial with a prespecified margin, in this population, and it does not exist.

Whether this group should be enrolled separately rather than stratified. That is a trial design question and it is the one that would fix the others. A population that is half of a common subtype and is studied only as a subgroup will keep generating practice that rests on subgroups.
