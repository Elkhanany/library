Part XII · Metastatic systemic therapy by subtype and line · Chapter 67

Metastatic hormone receptor-positive, HER2-negative disease

Four biomarker branches, and what each one actually buys.

1 · Defining endocrine sensitivity and resistance in clinical language

Endocrine resistance is a clinical label applied to a heterogeneous set of failures. The consensus definitions divide it by timing. Primary resistance means relapse within the first two years of adjuvant endocrine therapy, or progression within six months of starting endocrine therapy for advanced disease. Secondary resistance means failure after a longer period of benefit.

These are useful conventions and they are conventions. Nobody has shown that six months marks a point where biology changes. The cut-points exist so that trial populations can be defined reproducibly, which is the same argument made about assay thresholds in HER2 heterogeneity. Reading a trial eligibility boundary as a biological category is the recurring error in this literature.

What the timing does encode is real, and it is worth naming. A tumour that never responded probably never depended on oestrogen receptor signalling in a way the drug could exploit. A tumour that responded for four years and then failed has produced something. The first situation argues for changing class. The second argues for characterising what changed before choosing. The mechanisms behind each are separated in Endocrine resistance, ESR1 alterations, ER loss, and bypass signaling and the pathway detail is in Estrogen receptor signaling and endocrine resistance.

One practical consequence follows. The single most informative number at progression is how long the last line worked. It is free, it is already in the chart, and it predicts the yield of a molecular test better than any feature of the scan1.

2 · First-line endocrine therapy with CDK4/6 inhibition

The evidence behind first-line CDK4/6 inhibition is not uniform across the three agents, and the differences matter more than the class label suggests.

Ribociclib with letrozole carries the clearest first-line survival result. Median overall survival was 63.9 months with ribociclib and 51.4 months with placebo, both with letrozole, in 668 postmenopausal patients followed for a median of 6.6 years. The hazard ratio for death was 0.76, with a 95% confidence interval of 0.63 to 0.932.

Abemaciclib with a non-steroidal aromatase inhibitor did not reach statistical significance for survival in the first line. In 493 patients, median overall survival was 66.8 months with abemaciclib and 53.7 months with placebo. The hazard ratio was 0.804, with a 95% confidence interval of 0.637 to 1.0153. A 13-month difference in medians that does not clear its boundary is a genuinely awkward result, and it should be presented as what it is.

SONIA asked whether the inhibitor has to be given first. It randomised 1,050 patients to CDK4/6 inhibition added to first-line aromatase inhibitor or to second-line fulvestrant. The primary endpoint was progression-free survival from randomisation to second progression. It was 31.0 months against 26.8 months, with a hazard ratio of 0.87 and a 95% confidence interval of 0.74 to 1.034. Median overall survival was 47.9 months against 48.1 months, with a hazard ratio of 0.91 and an interval of 0.77 to 1.075. Giving the inhibitor first produced a median 24.6 months of inhibitor exposure against 8.1 months, and more grade 3 or worse adverse events4.

Caution

SONIA is often read as showing that first-line CDK4/6 inhibition does not matter. It shows something narrower. The trial permitted investigator choice of agent, and the agent chosen determines how far the result travels, since the three inhibitors do not carry the same survival evidence. Two further findings restrain the simple reading. A post hoc subgroup analysis suggested a survival benefit from first-line use in premenopausal patients, with a hazard ratio of 0.53. Postmenopausal patients showed none, and the test for interaction gave P equal to 0.015. A prespecified circulating tumour DNA analysis of 409 SONIA patients divided them by baseline genome-wide aneuploidy score. Those with a high score did benefit from first-line use, with a hazard ratio of 0.58. Those with a low score did not, at 1.366. A negative trial overall is not a negative trial in everyone.

3 · Choosing among CDK4/6 inhibitors and continuing beyond progression

The three inhibitors differ on three axes that a class label hides.

They differ in evidence position. Ribociclib has a positive first-line survival result2. Abemaciclib has a positive survival result in the second line, where median overall survival with fulvestrant was 46.7 months against 37.3 months, with a hazard ratio of 0.7577. Its first-line survival analysis did not reach significance3.

They differ in toxicity. Neutropenia dominates with palbociclib and ribociclib. Diarrhoea dominates with abemaciclib. Ribociclib carries a QT and hepatic monitoring requirement that the others do not. Agent detail belongs to Targeted therapy.

They differ in what they do to a schedule. Continuous dosing and intermittent dosing produce different lives, and patients notice.

Continuing CDK4/6 inhibition beyond progression is a separate question and it is weakly answered. The reason to doubt it is mechanistic. Most progression on these agents is not explained by any measurable mechanism, and the marker with the best evidence, cyclin E1, describes a cell cycle that does not depend on the kinase being blocked8. Emergent RB1 mutations are real and uncommon, appearing in 6 of 127 progressing patients in one paired analysis1. A population that progressed because it stopped needing CDK4 and CDK6 is not obviously a population to keep blocking them in. The mechanisms are set out in CDK4/6 inhibitor resistance, Rb loss, cyclin E, and bypass pathways.

4 · ESR1-mutant disease and receptor-degrading strategies

ESR1 mutation is the cleanest branch in this chapter because the mechanism and the action line up. The mutation confers ligand independence. It is acquired under aromatase inhibitor exposure and is not found in patients who have not had it9. Oestrogen deprivation therefore fails as a class. A drug that acts on the receptor still has a target.

Three trials fill in what that is worth.

EMERALD randomised 477 patients after a CDK4/6 inhibitor to elacestrant or standard endocrine monotherapy. The hazard ratio for progression was 0.70 in the whole population and 0.55 in the 47.8% with a detectable ESR1 mutation10. The separation between those two figures is the point.

EMBER-3 randomised 874 patients to imlunestrant, standard endocrine monotherapy, or imlunestrant with abemaciclib. Among 256 patients with ESR1 mutations, median progression-free survival was 5.5 months with imlunestrant against 3.8 months with standard therapy. In the overall population it was 5.6 against 5.5 months, with a hazard ratio of 0.87 that did not reach significance. Adding abemaciclib to imlunestrant gave 9.4 months against 5.5 months, with a hazard ratio of 0.57, regardless of ESR1 status. Grade 3 or higher adverse events occurred in 17.1% on imlunestrant and 48.6% on the combination11.

SERENA-6 moved the decision earlier. Patients on first-line aromatase inhibitor with a CDK4/6 inhibitor had plasma tested every two to three months. Those who developed an ESR1 mutation without radiological progression switched to camizestrant while continuing the same inhibitor. Median progression-free survival was 16.8 months against 9.2 months, with a hazard ratio of 0.45. Median second progression-free survival was 25.7 months against 19.1 months, with a hazard ratio of 0.6312.

Caution

SERENA-6 is the strongest available case for acting on a molecular result before the scan changes, and its denominator deserves attention. Of 3,256 patients who had at least one ESR1 test during first-line therapy, 548 tested positive and 315 were randomised12. The trial establishes the strategy in one mutation, with one drug, in patients who stayed on first-line therapy long enough to be tested repeatedly. Whether pre-emptive switching generalises to other resistance alterations is an open question, not an extension of this result.

5 · PI3K, AKT, and mTOR directed therapy and its toxicity burden

This branch has three approved options and the differences between them are about population and tolerability rather than about pathway.

SOLAR-1 established PIK3CA mutation as a predictive marker. In the 341 patients with tumour-tissue PIK3CA mutations, median progression-free survival with alpelisib and fulvestrant was 11.0 months against 5.7 months with fulvestrant alone, with a hazard ratio of 0.65. In the cohort without the mutation the hazard ratio was 0.8513. The toxicity is the reason the drug is hard to give. Grade 3 or 4 hyperglycaemia occurred in 36.6% against 0.7%, and 25.0% of patients discontinued alpelisib for adverse events against 4.2% on placebo13.

CAPItello-291 widened the biomarker and improved the tolerability. Of 708 randomised patients, 289 had PIK3CA, AKT1 or PTEN alterations. In that group median progression-free survival with capivasertib and fulvestrant was 7.3 months against 3.1 months, with a hazard ratio of 0.50. In the overall population it was 7.2 against 3.6 months14. Grade 3 or higher rash occurred in 12.1% against 0.3%, and diarrhoea in 9.3% against 0.3%.

INAVO120 is a different population and the strongest numbers in the chapter. It enrolled patients with PIK3CA-mutated disease who relapsed during or within 12 months of adjuvant endocrine therapy, which is a deliberately endocrine-resistant group. Median progression-free survival with inavolisib, palbociclib and fulvestrant was 15.0 months against 7.3 months with placebo, palbociclib and fulvestrant, with a hazard ratio of 0.4315. Median overall survival was 34.0 months against 27.0 months, with a hazard ratio of 0.67 and a 95% confidence interval of 0.48 to 0.9416.

Everolimus with exemestane remains available and predates biomarker selection. BOLERO-2 showed a progression-free survival benefit in unselected patients after non-steroidal aromatase inhibitor failure17. Stomatitis, pneumonitis and metabolic toxicity limit it. Pathway biology is in PI3K, AKT, mTOR, and related networks and agent detail in Targeted therapy.

Interplay

PIK3CA mutation frequency is not evenly distributed. In The Cancer Genome Atlas, comparing 154 patients of African ancestry with 776 of European ancestry, the African-ancestry group had more TP53 mutations and fewer PIK3CA mutations. Most of those molecular differences disappeared after adjusting for intrinsic subtype, which means the mutation-frequency difference is entangled with the subtype distribution rather than independent of it18. Two consequences follow and they belong together. Biologically, an alteration frequency is a property of a population's subtype mix, not a fixed attribute of ancestry. Operationally, a drug whose eligibility is defined by that alteration will be prescribed at different rates across populations for reasons that are partly biological and partly about who gets sequenced at all. Access and representation are developed in Disparities across the breast cancer continuum.

6 · PARP inhibition in germline-altered disease

Germline BRCA status is the branch most often checked late and it changes both treatment and family counselling.

Two randomised trials established the class in advanced disease, and both enrolled hormone receptor positive and triple-negative patients. In OlympiAD, 302 patients with germline BRCA mutations and HER2-negative disease received olaparib or physician's choice single-agent chemotherapy. Median progression-free survival was 7.0 months against 4.2 months, with a hazard ratio of 0.5819. In EMBRACA, 431 patients received talazoparib or single-agent chemotherapy. Median progression-free survival was 8.6 months against 5.6 months, with a hazard ratio of 0.54. The interim hazard ratio for death was 0.76, with a confidence interval crossing one20.

TBCRC 048 extended the target set. Among 54 patients, 76% of whom had hormone receptor positive HER2-negative disease, confirmed responses occurred only in two groups. Germline PALB2 carriers had an objective response rate of 82% and median progression-free survival of 13.3 months. Somatic BRCA1 or BRCA2 carriers had a response rate of 50% and median progression-free survival of 6.3 months. No responses were seen with ATM or CHEK2 alterations alone21.

That last sentence is the useful one. Homologous recombination related is not a single eligibility category. The genes within it behave differently, and a panel that reports them together invites a decision the data do not support. Repair biology is in DNA repair, replication stress, and genomic instability and resistance in PARP inhibitor and platinum resistance, including restoration of repair.

Where PARP inhibition sits in the sequence for hormone receptor positive disease is not settled by these trials. Their comparator was chemotherapy, not endocrine-based therapy, so they establish activity rather than position.

7 · Transition to antibody-drug conjugates

The transition out of endocrine-based therapy is now a transition into conjugates rather than into single-agent chemotherapy. Three trials define the options and they do not carry the same weight.

DESTINY-Breast06 tested trastuzumab deruxtecan after one or more lines of endocrine therapy and before any chemotherapy for metastatic disease. Of 866 randomised patients, 713 had HER2-low disease and 153 had ultralow disease. Among the HER2-low group, median progression-free survival was 13.2 months against 8.1 months with physician's choice chemotherapy, with a hazard ratio of 0.62. Overall survival data were immature. Adjudicated interstitial lung disease or pneumonitis occurred in 11.3% of patients receiving the conjugate, including three grade 5 events, against 0.2% on chemotherapy22.

TROPiCS-02 tested sacituzumab govitecan in a much later line. All patients had received an endocrine therapy, a taxane and a CDK4/6 inhibitor, with two to four prior chemotherapy regimens. Median progression-free survival was 5.5 months against 4.0 months, with a hazard ratio of 0.6623. Median overall survival was 14.4 months against 11.2 months, with a hazard ratio of 0.7924. The absolute numbers are small and the survival result is real.

TROPION-Breast01 tested datopotamab deruxtecan after one to two prior chemotherapy lines. The hazard ratio for progression was 0.6325. The final overall survival analysis gave a hazard ratio of 1.01, with a 95% confidence interval of 0.83 to 1.22. Subsequent use of other conjugates was imbalanced, at 12.3% in the datopotamab arm against 24.0% in the chemotherapy arm26.

Caution

A progression-free survival benefit that does not become a survival benefit is the most important pattern in this section. TROPION-Breast01 is the clearest instance, and the imbalance in subsequent conjugate use offers one explanation without confirming it26. Read alongside the survival result in TROPiCS-0224, the lesson is that these agents are not interchangeable on the strength of shared payload class. Sequential conjugate use is developed in Cross-resistance between classes and what it means for sequencing and the chemistry in Antibody-drug conjugates and targeted delivery.

8 · When cytotoxic chemotherapy is still the right answer

Conjugates have displaced single-agent chemotherapy from several positions and they have not abolished it.

Chemotherapy remains the answer in four situations. The first is after conjugate exposure, where the remaining options are cytotoxic and the expectations are covered in Long-term disease control and the cost of staying on treatment. The second is where a conjugate is contraindicated, and interstitial lung disease history is the common example given the 11.3% pneumonitis rate in DESTINY-Breast0622. The third is where access to a conjugate is not available, which is a real constraint in most of the world Global breast cancer care. The fourth is rapid progression requiring a response now, covered in Rapid progression and visceral crisis.

One principle is old and still correct. Sequential single agents and combination chemotherapy produce different trade-offs. Combinations raise response rate and toxicity. Single agents preserve function and allow more lines. In disease that is not immediately threatening, the single agent is usually right.

The choice among agents is driven by prior exposure, residual neuropathy, marrow reserve and schedule. Capecitabine, eribulin, vinorelbine, gemcitabine and weekly taxane are the working set. Agent detail is in Cytotoxic chemotherapy.

9 · Rapid progression and visceral crisis

Visceral crisis is a clinical diagnosis and it is overused. It means organ dysfunction from tumour burden that requires a rapid response, judged by symptoms, examination and laboratory values. Extensive liver metastases with normal liver function are not a visceral crisis. They are a large burden.

The distinction matters because the reflex answer, combination chemotherapy, is not supported in the population where it is most often used. RIGHT Choice randomised 222 pre- and perimenopausal women with clinically aggressive hormone receptor positive, HER2-negative disease to ribociclib with endocrine therapy or to investigator's choice of combination chemotherapy. Median progression-free survival was 21.8 months against 12.8 months, with a hazard ratio of 0.61. Response rates were similar at 66.1% against 61.8%, and median time to response was 4.9 months against 3.2 months27.

Caution

RIGHT Choice is a phase II trial and its population is not a population in organ failure. Of 222 patients, 106 had investigator-assessed visceral crisis, which is a clinical judgment rather than a physiological threshold27. The result argues strongly against reaching for combination chemotherapy in aggressive but compensated disease. It does not establish endocrine therapy with a CDK4/6 inhibitor as the answer in a patient with failing liver function. There the binding constraint is time to response, and the 1.7-month difference in median time to response may be the whole decision.

The operational question in true crisis is how fast a response is needed, measured in weeks. If the answer is under four weeks, the agent with the fastest response is right, and that is usually chemotherapy.

10 · Invasive lobular disease in the metastatic setting

Lobular carcinoma behaves differently in the metastatic setting, and most of the difference is a measurement problem rather than a treatment problem.

The pattern of spread is distinctive. Serosal surfaces, peritoneum, retroperitoneum, gastrointestinal tract and gynaecological organs are involved more often than in ductal disease. Bone involvement is common and is frequently diffuse rather than focal.

That pattern defeats the assessment tools. Lobular deposits infiltrate in single files without forming a mass, so they produce little to measure on computed tomography and can be invisible until an organ stops working. Linitis plastica of the stomach and diffuse peritoneal thickening are the classic traps. Response assessment by unidimensional criteria is therefore less reliable in this histology than in ductal disease, and a stable scan carries less reassurance.

The systemic treatment algorithm is the same. Lobular disease is overwhelmingly hormone receptor positive and HER2-negative, so it sits in this chapter by default. Two differences are worth holding. Loss of E-cadherin defines the histology and does not currently select a therapy. And the higher frequency of HER2-activating mutations in lobular disease makes sequencing worthwhile, which links this section to HER2-mutant disease as a separate therapeutic problem.

Histology is developed in Histopathology and the spectrum of breast neoplasia and staging in Diagnostic evaluation and staging. The practical point here is to choose a modality that can see the disease before relying on it to declare response.

11 · Long-term disease control and the cost of staying on treatment

Many patients now spend years on continuous treatment for this disease. That is the achievement of the last decade and it creates its own problem.

Three costs accumulate and none of them appear in a progression-free survival curve.

The first is physiological. Cumulative neuropathy, marrow suppression, ocular toxicity and metabolic effects build over lines. The distinction between cumulative and exposure-dependent toxicity, and what it implies for treatment breaks, is in Treatment-free intervals and cumulative toxicity.

The second is financial. Oral targeted agents given for years produce cost exposure that persists as long as the response does. This is the one cost that improves with a longer remission and worsens with it at the same time, and it is developed in Access, affordability, and financial toxicity.

The third is what a treatment schedule does to a life. Three-weekly infusions, monthly monitoring and the interval before each scan are the structure of the year. Patients rarely raise this unprompted.

In practice

Ask at every line what the treatment is for. Control of symptoms, control of a threatened organ, or extension of time are different aims, and only the first is verifiable within a month. Name the expected magnitude before starting. Medians in the pretreated setting are short, and a patient told that a line offers months rather than years is better placed to decide what to spend them on. Review the whole regimen annually rather than at progression only. Agents accumulate. Supportive medications outlive the episode that prompted them. Ask directly about cost. Patients who are rationing doses report it when asked and rarely otherwise, and a rationed dose will present as resistance Distinguishing resistance from under-dosing, non-adherence, and pseudo-progression.
