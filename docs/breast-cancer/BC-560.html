Metastatic hormone receptor-positive, HER2-negative disease

Four biomarker branches, the order they are spent in, and what each one actually buys.

1 · Defining endocrine sensitivity and resistance in clinical language

Endocrine resistance is a clinical label applied to a heterogeneous set of failures. The consensus definitions divide it by timing. Primary resistance means relapse within the first two years of adjuvant endocrine therapy, or progression within six months of starting endocrine therapy for advanced disease. Secondary resistance means failure after a longer period of benefit.

These are useful conventions and they are conventions. Nobody has shown that six months marks a point where biology changes. The cut-points exist so that trial populations can be defined reproducibly, which is the same argument made about assay thresholds in HER2 heterogeneity. Reading a trial eligibility boundary as a biological category is the recurring error in this literature.

What the timing does encode is real, and it is worth naming. A tumour that never responded probably never depended on oestrogen receptor signalling in a way the drug could exploit. A tumour that responded for four years and then failed has produced something. The first situation argues for changing class. The second argues for characterising what changed before choosing. The mechanisms behind each are separated in Endocrine resistance, ESR1 alterations, ER loss, and bypass signaling and the pathway detail is in Estrogen receptor signaling and endocrine resistance.

One practical consequence follows. The single most informative number at progression is how long the last line worked. It is free, it is already in the chart, and it predicts the yield of a molecular test better than any feature of the scan1.

This chapter is about how to approach the patient. Its subjects are disease burden, visceral crisis, endocrine sensitivity, the goal of each line, and when to stop. It is not the trial record for this disease, and does not try to be.

Three neighbouring chapters hold that record. Endocrine therapy and receptor degraders in metastatic disease owns endocrine therapy and the receptor degraders. CDK4/6 inhibition in metastatic disease, combinations and sequencing owns CDK4/6 inhibition, including agent choice and continuation beyond progression. Conjugates and chemotherapy in later-line hormone receptor-positive disease owns the later-line conjugates and single-agent chemotherapy. Each is read here as the source of a table rather than repeated.

2 · First-line endocrine therapy with CDK4/6 inhibition

The evidence behind first-line CDK4/6 inhibition is not uniform across the agents, and the differences matter more than the class label suggests.

Ribociclib with letrozole carries the clearest first-line survival result2. Abemaciclib with a non-steroidal aromatase inhibitor did not reach significance for survival in the same line. The separation in medians there was about 13 months, on a confidence interval running from 0.637 to 1.0153. A 13-month difference that does not clear its boundary is a genuinely awkward result, which should be presented as what it is rather than rounded in either direction.

That table settles whether the class works in the first line. It does not settle the question this chapter owns, which is whether the inhibitor has to be spent there. Almost every patient will receive one of these drugs at some point, so what is being allocated is a position in a sequence rather than a treatment.

SONIA is the only trial that randomised the strategy instead of the drugs. Its endpoint ran across both lines, which is the design the question requires. Neither that endpoint nor overall survival separated the two strategies6,14.

The price of giving the inhibitor first is the part that does not appear in a hazard ratio. It produced a median 24.6 months of inhibitor exposure against 8.1 months, and more grade 3 or worse adverse events6. That is the cost side of the decision, and it is paid by every patient rather than by the subgroup that benefits.

Caution

SONIA is often read as showing that first-line CDK4/6 inhibition does not matter. It shows something narrower. The trial permitted investigator choice of agent, and the agent chosen determines how far the result travels, since the three inhibitors do not carry the same survival evidence. Two further findings restrain the simple reading. A post hoc subgroup analysis suggested a survival benefit from first-line use in premenopausal patients, with a hazard ratio of 0.53. Postmenopausal patients showed none, and the test for interaction gave P equal to 0.0114. A prespecified circulating tumour DNA analysis of 409 SONIA patients divided them by baseline genome-wide aneuploidy score. Those with a high score did benefit from first-line use, with a hazard ratio of 0.58. Those with a low score did not, at 1.3615. A negative trial overall is not a negative trial in everyone.

3 · Choosing among CDK4/6 inhibitors and continuing beyond progression

The three inhibitors differ on three axes that a class label hides.

They differ in evidence position. Ribociclib has a positive first-line survival result2. Abemaciclib has a positive survival result in the second line, while its first-line survival analysis did not reach significance16,3. The trials behind each of those statements are tabulated in CDK4/6 inhibition in metastatic disease, combinations and sequencing.

They differ in toxicity. Neutropenia dominates with palbociclib and ribociclib. Diarrhoea dominates with abemaciclib. Ribociclib carries a QT and hepatic monitoring requirement that the others do not. Agent detail belongs to Targeted therapy.

They differ in what they do to a schedule. Continuous dosing and intermittent dosing produce different lives, and patients notice.

Continuing CDK4/6 inhibition beyond progression is a separate question and it is weakly answered. The reason to doubt it is mechanistic. Most progression on these agents is not explained by any measurable mechanism, and the marker with the best evidence, cyclin E1, describes a cell cycle that does not depend on the kinase being blocked17. Emergent RB1 mutations are real and uncommon, appearing in 6 of 127 progressing patients in one paired analysis1. A population that progressed because it stopped needing CDK4 and CDK6 is not obviously a population to keep blocking them in. The randomised continuation and switch trials are set out in CDK4/6 inhibition in metastatic disease, combinations and sequencing and the mechanisms in CDK4/6 inhibitor resistance, Rb loss, cyclin E, and bypass pathways.

4 · ESR1-mutant disease and receptor-degrading strategies

ESR1 mutation is the cleanest branch in this chapter because the mechanism and the action line up. The mutation confers ligand independence. It is acquired under aromatase inhibitor exposure and is not found in patients who have not had it18. Oestrogen deprivation therefore fails as a class. A drug that acts on the receptor still has a target.

What each degrader is worth after progression, and how the trials split by mutation status, is the argument of Endocrine therapy and receptor degraders in metastatic disease. The decision this chapter owns is earlier and narrower. It is whether to act on a rising mutation in a patient whose scan is still reassuring.

Advanced disease · HR+/HER2- · 1L

Choosing the first-line regimen and the moment to leave it

Should a rising ESR1 mutation end the first line before the scan does?

Rationale
ESR1 mutations are selected by oestrogen deprivation and leave the receptor active without ligand, which is why an aromatase inhibitor stops working while a degrader still has a target. The mutation is detectable in blood before the scan changes. That interval is an opportunity if acting inside it changes anything, and a source of premature treatment change if it does not.
Experiment
Two trials sampled blood serially during first-line treatment and randomised only those patients whose ESR1 mutation rose while the disease was still controlled radiologically. One swapped the aromatase inhibitor for fulvestrant and kept palbociclib. The other swapped it for an oral degrader and kept whichever inhibitor the patient was already taking. Both left the control arm on the aromatase inhibitor the tumour had already learned to ignore.
Finding
Switching at the molecular signal lengthened progression-free survival measured from that randomisation in both trials. The receptor-directed drugs tested after clinical progression point the same way and show the same dependence on ESR1 status, which is what makes the mechanism credible rather than the timing.
Limitation
Neither trial randomised the comparison a clinic actually weighs, which is making the same switch at clinical progression. The control arm continued a treatment already known at the molecular level to be failing. The contrast is therefore against something close to inert. The endpoint is progression on the new line measured from the molecular randomisation, which is a surrogate for what the strategy is worth across the whole line and a clock that starts at a point chosen by an assay. The population is defined by that assay and its threshold, which makes molecular progression here a laboratory convention as much as a biological event. Patients whose resistance takes another route never enter. An earlier trial that switched treatment on a persistent blood marker rather than on a scan did not improve survival, which is the reason this design needs an outcome and not only a progression result.

That strategy has a cost the progression curves do not show, because it requires plasma testing every two to three months in patients who are well and responding. It then commits the service to changing treatment on a laboratory result while the imaging is unchanged. A clinic that cannot deliver the testing reliably should not adopt the strategy, because an irregularly tested patient gets the control arm with extra appointments.

Caution

SERENA-6 is the strongest available case for acting on a molecular result before the scan changes, and its denominator deserves attention. Of 3,256 patients who had at least one ESR1 test during first-line therapy, 548 tested positive and 315 were randomised4. The trial establishes the strategy in one mutation, with one drug, in patients who stayed on first-line therapy long enough to be tested repeatedly. Whether pre-emptive switching generalises to other resistance alterations is an open question, not an extension of this result.

5 · PI3K, AKT, and mTOR directed therapy and its toxicity burden

This branch has three approved options, and the differences between them are about population and tolerability rather than about pathway. The trials, their biomarker definitions and their efficacy are tabulated in Endocrine therapy and receptor degraders in metastatic disease. What belongs here is the toxicity, because it decides how much of the benefit a patient actually receives.

SOLAR-1 established PIK3CA mutation as a predictive marker and also established the reason the drug is hard to give. Grade 3 or 4 hyperglycaemia occurred in 36.6% of patients against 0.7% on placebo. One quarter of patients discontinued alpelisib for adverse events, against 4.2% in the control arm19. A quarter is the figure to hold when consenting a patient, because it is the rate at which the treatment ends for a reason other than the cancer.

Capivasertib is better tolerated and its toxicity is differently shaped. Grade 3 or higher rash occurred in 12.1% of patients against 0.3%, and diarrhoea in 9.3% against 0.3%20. Rash is early and manageable, whereas hyperglycaemia is not and needs a plan that exists before the first prescription.

Inavolisib, given as a triplet in a deliberately endocrine-resistant population, is the only regimen in this group carrying a survival benefit5,21. Whether that reflects the population, the triplet or the drug cannot be separated from one trial.

Everolimus with exemestane remains available and predates biomarker selection22. Stomatitis, pneumonitis and metabolic toxicity limit it. Pathway biology is in PI3K, AKT, mTOR, and related networks and agent detail in Targeted therapy.

Interplay

PIK3CA mutation frequency is not evenly distributed. In The Cancer Genome Atlas, comparing 154 patients of African ancestry with 776 of European ancestry, the African-ancestry group had more TP53 mutations and fewer PIK3CA mutations. Most of those molecular differences disappeared after adjusting for intrinsic subtype, which means the mutation-frequency difference is entangled with the subtype distribution rather than independent of it23. Two consequences follow and they belong together. Biologically, an alteration frequency is a property of a population's subtype mix, not a fixed attribute of ancestry. Operationally, a drug whose eligibility is defined by that alteration will be prescribed at different rates across populations for reasons that are partly biological and partly about who gets sequenced at all. Access and representation are developed in Disparities across the breast cancer continuum.

Two of these trials established the class against single-agent chemotherapy as the comparator. Their populations were defined by a germline mutation rather than by receptor status, so roughly half of each was hormone receptor positive. A third added a PARP inhibitor to platinum and a taxane instead of replacing chemotherapy with it, which is a different question about the same class.

TBCRC 048 extended the target set, and its negative result is the useful one. Confirmed responses appeared in only two groups, germline PALB2 carriers and somatic BRCA1 or BRCA2 carriers. No response occurred with ATM or CHEK2 alterations alone25.

Homologous recombination related is therefore not a single eligibility category. The genes within it behave differently, and a panel that reports them under one heading invites a decision the data do not support. Repair biology is in DNA repair, replication stress, and genomic instability and resistance in PARP inhibitor and platinum resistance, including restoration of repair.

Where PARP inhibition sits in the sequence for hormone receptor positive disease is not settled by these trials. Their comparator was chemotherapy, not endocrine-based therapy, so they establish activity rather than position.

7 · Transition to antibody-drug conjugates

The transition out of endocrine-based therapy is now a transition into conjugates rather than into single-agent chemotherapy. The decision here is when to make it rather than which conjugate is better, since the second question is argued in Conjugates and chemotherapy in later-line hormone receptor-positive disease.

Advanced disease · HR+/HER2- · later

Choosing the next treatment after CDK4/6 inhibition fails

When should endocrine-directed treatment give way to chemotherapy or an antibody-drug conjugate?

Rationale
The convention is to hold cytotoxic treatment back while any endocrine option remains, because endocrine treatment is easier to live with. The convention assumes the remaining endocrine option still works. After a CDK4/6 inhibitor that is exactly what is in doubt. A conjugate was meant to move the threshold by delivering a cytotoxic payload only to cells carrying a surface antigen.
Experiment
Two trials randomised a CDK4/6 inhibitor with endocrine therapy against capecitabine in endocrine-resistant disease. Two more randomised a TROP2-directed conjugate against chemotherapy of the physician's choice, at different depths of prior chemotherapy. One asked whether adding a checkpoint inhibitor to such a conjugate helped. A single-arm study took a HER3-directed conjugate into disease that had had a CDK4/6 inhibitor and a line of chemotherapy. One ongoing trial moves the TROP2 conjugate ahead of the first chemotherapy line.
Finding
The two capecitabine trials disagree on progression-free survival. The disagreement follows their populations rather than their drugs. Neither of them produced an overall survival difference, which is the one thing they agree on. Both TROP2-directed conjugates lengthened progression-free survival against physician's choice chemotherapy. Only one of the two also lengthened overall survival. Adding a checkpoint inhibitor to a conjugate did not lengthen progression-free survival. The HER3-directed conjugate produced responses in a single arm.
Limitation
Neither capecitabine trial belongs to this position as it stands, because both gave a CDK4/6 inhibitor to patients who had never had one. They differ from each other in menopausal status and in how much of the population had had any treatment for metastatic disease, which is enough to explain the disagreement without invoking the drugs. Physician's choice names a different set of agents in each conjugate trial and in each country. The comparator is therefore not a fixed quantity across them. The two conjugate trials also sit at different depths of prior chemotherapy, which is the other reason their results are not interchangeable. A progression-free survival gain not followed by a gain in overall survival is a difference measured on imaging. The conjugate whose survival curves lay on top of each other is the warning this class has to answer. The checkpoint result that looks positive is a secondary analysis confined to the patients with evaluable tissue scoring PD-L1-positive. It is not significant.

Three points survive reading the table rather than the individual trials.

The first is that the entry requirement, not the drug, places each result in the sequence. One trial enrolled before any chemotherapy for metastatic disease, while another required two to four prior regimens. Two of them select on HER2-low expression rather than on receptor status, which is a scoring convention read at the bottom of an assay built to find amplification Expression and alteration-defined treatment across subtypes. A hazard ratio from one does not describe the decision the other tested.

The second is that a progression-free survival benefit does not reliably become a survival benefit here. TROPION-Breast01 is the clearest instance. Subsequent use of other conjugates was imbalanced in it, at 12.3% in the datopotamab arm against 24.0% in the chemotherapy arm, which offers one explanation without confirming it34.

The third is that the toxicity is not shared. Adjudicated interstitial lung disease or pneumonitis occurred in 11.3% of patients receiving trastuzumab deruxtecan in DESTINY-Breast06, including three grade 5 events, against 0.2% on chemotherapy30. That rate is why the next section still exists.

8 · When cytotoxic chemotherapy is still the right answer

Conjugates have displaced single-agent chemotherapy from several positions and they have not abolished it.

Advanced disease · HR+/HER2- · later

Antibody-drug conjugates after endocrine therapy

Should a conjugate replace the first chemotherapy rather than follow it?

Rationale
Every trial that established these drugs enrolled after chemotherapy had already been given. What they measured is the value of a conjugate at that point in the course. Moving it forward means giving a drug with its own distinctive toxicity to people who might have done well for a long time on one gentle cytotoxic. It also means giving it to people who might not have needed cytotoxic treatment at all yet.
Experiment
One phase III trial moved the HER2-directed conjugate ahead of any chemotherapy for metastatic disease in endocrine-pretreated patients, against physician's choice chemotherapy. Two TROP2 trials are doing the same in the first chemotherapy slot and have not reported. From the opposite direction, a randomised trial in aggressive disease compared endocrine therapy with a CDK4/6 inhibitor against combination chemotherapy, asking whether cytotoxic treatment was needed at that point at all.
Finding
The conjugate given before any chemotherapy lengthened time to progression against physician's choice chemotherapy, with overall survival immature at that analysis. Endocrine therapy with a CDK4/6 inhibitor outlasted combination chemotherapy in aggressive disease that would once have gone straight to cytotoxic treatment. Both results push conventional single-agent chemotherapy out of the slot it used to hold, from opposite directions.
Limitation
The control arm in the earlier-line conjugate trial is the same physician's-choice menu. Its overall survival was immature at the primary analysis. A longer time to first progression is currently the whole of the case for moving the drug forward. The aggressive-disease trial is phase II and premenopausal. It enrolled on a clinical judgement of aggressiveness rather than on a stated threshold. It therefore does not define who can safely be kept off chemotherapy. Most of all, no trial here randomised the order of the same drugs. The design that would answer that randomises the position and then measures progression on the line after next. That is how the first-line CDK4/6 question was settled. It is not how any conjugate in this position has been studied.

Chemotherapy remains the answer in four situations. The first is after conjugate exposure, where the remaining options are cytotoxic and the expectations are covered in Long-term disease control and the cost of staying on treatment. The second is where a conjugate is contraindicated, and interstitial lung disease history is the common example given the 11.3% pneumonitis rate in DESTINY-Breast0630. The third is where access to a conjugate is not available, which is a real constraint in most of the world Global breast cancer care. The fourth is rapid progression requiring a response now, covered in Rapid progression and visceral crisis.

One principle is old and still correct. Sequential single agents and combination chemotherapy produce different trade-offs. Combinations raise response rate and toxicity. Single agents preserve function and allow more lines. In disease that is not immediately threatening, the single agent is usually right.

The choice among agents is driven by prior exposure, residual neuropathy, marrow reserve and schedule. Capecitabine, eribulin, vinorelbine, gemcitabine and weekly taxane are the working set. Agent detail is in Cytotoxic chemotherapy.

9 · Rapid progression and visceral crisis

Visceral crisis is a clinical diagnosis and it is overused. It means organ dysfunction from tumour burden that requires a rapid response, judged by symptoms, examination and laboratory values. Extensive liver metastases with normal liver function are not a visceral crisis. They are a large burden.

The distinction matters because the reflex answer, combination chemotherapy, is not supported in the population where it is most often used.

Advanced disease · HR+/HER2- · 1L

Choosing the first-line regimen and the moment to leave it

Can endocrine therapy with a CDK4/6 inhibitor replace chemotherapy when the disease is moving fast?

Rationale
Aggressive visceral disease is where clinicians reach for chemotherapy first, on the belief that only cytotoxic treatment produces a response quickly enough to matter. That belief was formed against the slow and partial responses of endocrine monotherapy. The combination is not endocrine monotherapy, which is reason enough to test the belief rather than carry it forward.
Experiment
A randomised comparison of ribociclib with endocrine therapy against investigator's choice combination chemotherapy, in first-line disease the investigator judged aggressive enough to warrant chemotherapy. Alongside it, two trials set an inhibitor with endocrine therapy against capecitabine, one in premenopausal patients with ovarian suppression and one in aromatase inhibitor-resistant disease. A further trial randomises immediate abemaciclib with endocrine therapy against a short course of paclitaxel given before the same combination.
Finding
In aggressive first-line disease the endocrine combination held the disease longer than combination chemotherapy did. Against capecitabine the answer depends on the population. In premenopausal patients with ovarian suppression the combination lengthened time to progression against capecitabine. In aromatase inhibitor-resistant disease it did not beat capecitabine.
Limitation
The aggressive-disease trial was a phase II. Its comparator was a combination chemotherapy regimen rather than the sequential single agents many clinics would use, which is a comparator chosen for toxicity as much as for efficacy. Its endpoint was progression-free survival and not the speed of response on which the practice it challenges actually rests. Entry turned on the investigator's judgement that chemotherapy was indicated rather than on a stated visceral crisis threshold. That makes the population a product of a decision rather than of a measurement, which no protocol can reproduce. Of the two capecitabine trials, one enrolled a population only half of whom were untreated for metastatic disease. The other was not first line at all. The induction trial has no verified peer-reviewed result on the record. Nothing about giving chemotherapy before the combination can be read from it yet. An early response to induction would in any case not be an answer for every urgent presentation. Its eligibility and its exact sequence have to travel with it whenever it is cited.

Caution

RIGHT Choice is a phase II trial and its population is not a population in organ failure. Of 222 patients, 106 had investigator-assessed visceral crisis, which is a clinical judgment rather than a physiological threshold35. The result argues strongly against reaching for combination chemotherapy in aggressive but compensated disease. It does not establish endocrine therapy with a CDK4/6 inhibitor as the answer in a patient with failing liver function. There the binding constraint is time to response, and the 1.7-month difference in median time to response may be the whole decision35.

The operational question in true crisis is how fast a response is needed, measured in weeks. If the answer is under four weeks, the agent with the fastest response is right, and that is usually chemotherapy.

10 · Invasive lobular disease in the metastatic setting

Lobular carcinoma behaves differently in the metastatic setting, and most of the difference is a measurement problem rather than a treatment problem.

The pattern of spread is distinctive. Serosal surfaces, peritoneum, retroperitoneum, gastrointestinal tract and gynaecological organs are involved more often than in ductal disease. Bone involvement is common and is frequently diffuse rather than focal.

That pattern defeats the assessment tools. Lobular deposits infiltrate in single files without forming a mass, so they produce little to measure on computed tomography and can be invisible until an organ stops working. Linitis plastica of the stomach and diffuse peritoneal thickening are the classic traps. Response assessment by unidimensional criteria is therefore less reliable in this histology than in ductal disease, and a stable scan carries less reassurance.

The systemic treatment algorithm is the same. Lobular disease is overwhelmingly hormone receptor positive and HER2-negative, so it sits in this chapter by default. Two differences are worth holding. Loss of E-cadherin defines the histology and does not currently select a therapy. And the higher frequency of HER2-activating mutations in lobular disease makes sequencing worthwhile, which links this section to HER2-mutant disease as a separate therapeutic problem.

Histology is developed in Histopathology and the spectrum of breast neoplasia and staging in Diagnostic evaluation and staging. The practical point here is to choose a modality that can see the disease before relying on it to declare response.

11 · Long-term disease control and the cost of staying on treatment

Many patients now spend years on continuous treatment for this disease. That is the achievement of the last decade and it creates its own problem.

Three costs accumulate and none of them appear in a progression-free survival curve.

The first is physiological. Cumulative neuropathy, marrow suppression, ocular toxicity and metabolic effects build over lines. The distinction between cumulative and exposure-dependent toxicity, and what it implies for treatment breaks, is in Treatment-free intervals and cumulative toxicity.

The second is financial. Oral targeted agents given for years produce cost exposure that persists as long as the response does. This is the one cost that improves with a longer remission and worsens with it at the same time, and it is developed in Access, affordability, and financial toxicity.

The third is what a treatment schedule does to a life. Three-weekly infusions, monthly monitoring and the interval before each scan are the structure of the year. Patients rarely raise this unprompted.

In practice

Ask at every line what the treatment is for. Control of symptoms, control of a threatened organ, or extension of time are different aims, and only the first is verifiable within a month. Name the expected magnitude before starting. Medians in the pretreated setting are short, and a patient told that a line offers months rather than years is better placed to decide what to spend them on. Review the whole regimen annually rather than at progression only. Agents accumulate. Supportive medications outlive the episode that prompted them. Ask directly about cost. Patients who are rationing doses report it when asked and rarely otherwise, and a rationed dose will present as resistance Distinguishing resistance from under-dosing, non-adherence, and pseudo-progression.
