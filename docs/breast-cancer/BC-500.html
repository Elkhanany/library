1 · Stage and risk-adapted treatment selection

Early disease · HER2+ · adjuvant

Matching adjuvant HER2 treatment to baseline risk

What decides whether a HER2-positive tumour goes to treatment first or to surgery first?

Rationale
Every other question in this part assumes the setting has already been chosen. The choice itself is the first decision a clinic takes, and it determines what can be learned and what can be offered afterwards. Treating first makes the response measurable and opens post-neoadjuvant escalation. Operating first closes both and allows a lighter regimen.
Experiment
No trial randomises the order. What exists instead are two separate bodies of evidence built in populations defined by the choice. Single-group and randomised de-escalation studies in small node-negative tumours taken to surgery first, and randomised escalation on residual disease in tumours treated first.
Finding
Both strategies work in the populations they were tested in. A small node-negative tumour does well on a markedly reduced adjuvant regimen. A tumour with residual disease after preoperative treatment does better when treatment is escalated on that finding.
Limitation
The comparison a clinic wants has never been randomised, and the two bodies of evidence enrolled different disease. Reading them together makes the choice look settled at the extremes and leaves it unevidenced in the middle, which is where most patients are. The eligibility criteria, not the results, are what separate the two literatures.
Next question
If preoperative treatment is what makes escalation possible, is a tumour small enough for de-escalation also small enough that nothing would have been found to escalate on?

Selection in this subtype turns on one question asked before any drug is given. Would knowing whether the tumour was eradicated change what happens next?

If the answer is yes, treat first and operate second. The post-neoadjuvant evidence in Post-neoadjuvant therapy for residual disease converts residual disease into a different regimen, and that option exists only if response was observed. If the answer is no, because the tumour is small and node-negative and the plan would be the same either way, then the neoadjuvant phase buys information nobody will act on.

That produces three bands in practice.

Small node-negative tumours, broadly up to 2 cm with clinically negative nodes, are treated with surgery first and a de-escalated adjuvant regimen De-escalation in small node-negative tumors.

Tumours of stage II or III, and any clinically node-positive disease, are treated with neoadjuvant systemic therapy. The backbone is in Neoadjuvant backbone and dual blockade and the response-adapted consequences in Pathologic response as a decision point.

Between those bands sits genuine uncertainty, and the commonest error is to resolve it with imaging alone. Clinical nodal staging is imperfect, so a tumour called node-negative before surgery may not be. Where that uncertainty is material, treating first preserves the option that surgery-first destroys. The general form of this reasoning is in Principles of multidisciplinary treatment and the staging limitations in Diagnostic evaluation and staging.

This chapter is the decision architecture. The randomised trial record it stands on is next door and is not restated here. Neoadjuvant regimens are in Neoadjuvant therapy, with adjuvant and post-neoadjuvant regimens in Adjuvant and post-neoadjuvant therapy. Hormone receptor-positive HER2-positive disease takes its own algorithm in Early triple-positive disease. This chapter carries only the part of it that changes a decision. One finding holds wherever that table tests it. Two agents directed at HER2 eradicate more tumour than one. It does not matter much whether the second agent is an antibody or a kinase inhibitor. Nothing in the table contradicts it.

The seven rows are not seven versions of one experiment, and the differences are structural rather than pharmacological. NeoSphere and NeoALTTO added a second HER2-directed agent on top of a taxane. TRYPHAENA held dual blockade constant and varied the chemotherapy around it. IMpassion050 held both constant and added a third mechanism. TBCRC 006, TBCRC 023 and WSG-ADAPT HER2+/HR− took the chemotherapy away altogether. Four questions share one filter. Only the first of them is about dual blockade as such.

The decision to give pertuzumab before surgery rests on NeoSphere. It is the randomised comparison against the regimen the clinic would otherwise use. Its four-arm design has not been repeated7. Those four arms also settle something a two-arm trial cannot. Dropping the docetaxel from the triplet cost more response than dropping either antibody did. That ordering is readable straight off the result column, and it is the most important single constraint on the chemotherapy-free strategies in Chemotherapy-sparing strategies.

NeoALTTO is the row that should be read next to the adjuvant table in Adjuvant escalation and extended HER2-directed therapy. It raised pathological complete response substantially with lapatinib as the partner, and ALTTO then failed to convert the same combination into a disease-free survival benefit6,8. One drug, two settings, opposite conclusions. That pair is the surrogacy problem of Pathologic response as a decision point in its cleanest form.

IMpassion050 is the only row testing something other than HER2 blockade. Checkpoint inhibition added nothing in the intention-to-treat population, and the PD-L1-positive population moved the wrong way. Five grade 5 events occurred in the trial. All of them were in the atezolizumab arm1. Immunotherapy is not a lever here, and the reason is not that it was tested at the wrong dose.

Two things the table cannot show are worth naming. Nobody randomised pertuzumab against lapatinib as the partner agent, so the class finding above rests on separate trials with separate control arms. And the endpoint column hides a scoring difference. Some of these trials scored eradication in the breast alone. Others required the nodes to be clear as well. The taxane is not contested. The anthracycline is. Five rows sit under that heading and only one of them asks the question.

NOAH is the historical row rather than a comparison. It established trastuzumab on an anthracycline and taxane backbone, which is the reason the anthracycline is in the regimen at all12. TRYPHAENA appears here because it varied the chemotherapy under constant dual blockade, but its primary endpoint was cardiac safety and its three response rates were never compared against one another statistically5. GeparSixto is about carboplatin rather than about the anthracycline. It randomised triple-negative and HER2-positive patients in the same trial. The HER2-positive stratum gained nothing, and the interaction with subtype was formally significant11. That is why platinum is argued in Platinum agents, BRCA status, and repair-directed selection and not here. HELEN-006 compares two anthracycline-free backbones against each other, so it presupposes the omission rather than testing it. It also enrolled only Asian women at six Chinese hospitals, with short follow-up and no survival data9.

That leaves TRAIN-2. It is the only randomised test of the anthracycline itself against a time-to-event endpoint10. Its interval is wide enough to exclude neither a benefit nor a harm from omission. No non-inferiority margin was set in advance. So the case for omitting the anthracycline does not rest on proven equivalence. It rests on a toxicity difference measured inside the same randomisation, which is priced in Cardiac surveillance and cumulative toxicity and argued in full in Chemotherapy backbones with trastuzumab.

What none of these trials reports is the patient the decision matters most for. No row here gives a result in patients with established cardiac disease. The omission argument is applied to exactly that patient in clinic.

DESTINY-Breast11 is load-bearing because it did something else as well. It placed the conjugate in front of the same taxane and antibody triplet instead of in place of it. That sequence raised pathological complete response above the anthracycline sequence rather than matching it13. The difference between it and KRISTINE is sequencing against substitution. It is not a difference between trastuzumab deruxtecan and trastuzumab emtansine, and reading it that way is the common error.

TRAIN-3 asks a third question under the same filter. It used magnetic resonance imaging to decide when to stop chemotherapy, reserving a conjugate for residual disease14. It has no comparator arm, so its event-free survival by number of cycles describes one cohort and cannot show that a shorter course was sufficient. The control arm is what this table cannot show. DESTINY-Breast11 compared its conjugate sequence against dose-dense doxorubicin and cyclophosphamide followed by the triplet. The anthracycline-free regimen that the previous table supports was not in the trial. So the new sequence has beaten a backbone this chapter argues against giving, and the comparison a clinic actually faces has never been randomised.

Caution

Two findings in DESTINY-Breast11 point in opposite directions and both are easy to lose. The conjugate given alone returned the lowest pathological complete response of the three arms, and enrolment to that arm closed early, so it was never the trial's formal comparison. It is not a substitute for the taxane and the antibodies. Event-free survival is barely mature, at the fraction the overall survival column records13. A pathological complete response difference at that maturity is a hypothesis about survival, not a measurement of it. Why that distinction is load-bearing is set out in Pathologic response as a decision point and developed in The neoadjuvant setting as a scientific platform.

KRISTINE is the cautionary row in the same table. It removed most of the cytotoxic burden and lost response for doing so, which Conjugates in the neoadjuvant setting reads in full.

3 · Pathologic response as a decision point

Pathological complete response does two different jobs, and confusing them is the most common error in reading neoadjuvant trials.

The first job is prognostic, at the level of the individual patient. One pooled analysis covered 12 trials and 11,955 patients. Eradication of tumour from breast and nodes was associated with better event-free survival, with a hazard ratio of 0.48 and a 95% confidence interval of 0.43 to 0.54. For overall survival the hazard ratio was 0.36, with an interval of 0.31 to 0.4216. The association was strongest in HER2-positive, hormone receptor-negative disease treated with trastuzumab, which Scope, and why this subtype made the neoadjuvant platform work quantifies. Nowhere in breast cancer does response carry more information about an individual than here.

The second job would be surrogacy, at the level of the trial. In the same analysis, the correlation between a treatment's increase in pathological complete response and its effect on outcome was negligible. The coefficient of determination was 0.03 for event-free survival and 0.24 for overall survival, and the authors could not validate pathological complete response as a surrogate endpoint16.

Those two results are not in conflict. Patients who respond do better. Drugs that raise response rates do not reliably extend survival. The first is a statement about biology already present in the tumour. The second would be a statement about what a drug does, and it does not follow.

This subtype supplies the cleanest demonstration of the gap. Lapatinib added to trastuzumab raised preoperative eradication substantially in NeoALTTO. The adjuvant trial of the same combination did not convert that into a disease-free survival result at its amended threshold6,8. The tumour that disappeared was still the tumour that was going to behave well.

The definition matters for the first job. Eradication from breast and nodes, scored either as ypT0 ypN0 or as ypT0/is ypN0, was better associated with outcome than eradication from the breast alone. For eradication from the breast alone the event-free survival hazard ratio was 0.60, with an interval of 0.55 to 0.6616. A response report that does not state the nodal status is not reporting the quantity that carries the prognosis. Scoring conventions, including Residual cancer burden, are in Histopathology and the spectrum of breast neoplasia and the endpoint argument in full is in The neoadjuvant setting as a scientific platform.

The legitimate use of response in this chapter is therefore patient-level and observed. Nothing in Post-neoadjuvant therapy for residual disease depends on predicting who will respond. It depends on knowing who did.

Post-neoadjuvant conjugate therapy for residual invasive disease. The population column is the one that decides which trial a patient belongs to.

Every trial in this table accepts the same premise. Residual disease is treated by changing to a conjugate rather than by extending the antibody, and no row proposes continuing trastuzumab alone. That premise was itself tested only once.

KATHERINE is the trial the decision rests on. It randomised a conjugate against continuing trastuzumab, which was the alternative the clinic actually had. It is the only member of this table with a mature overall survival result20,21. Every later trial here inherits its conclusion and asks a narrower question on top of it.

DESTINY-Breast05 is the only one of those that has reported. It compared a bystander-capable conjugate against the established one and improved on it17. Read as a sequence, the two trials moved this decision from whether to escalate to which conjugate to escalate with.

The eligible populations are not the same, and the divergence is entry criteria rather than drug. DESTINY-Breast05 required residual invasive disease together with node-positive disease at surgery, or inoperable disease at diagnosis17. CompassHER2 RD requires oestrogen receptor-negative or node-positive disease. A patient with small-volume residual disease and negative nodes was studied in KATHERINE. That patient sits outside the entry criteria of DESTINY-Breast05 and of CompassHER2 RD. Each successive design has enriched for events. That raises the event rate against which a hazard ratio is computed. Ranking these trials against one another by effect size therefore compares populations more than it compares drugs.

CompassHER2 RD and ASTEFANIA share a second feature that their rows cannot carry. Both add an agent on top of trastuzumab emtansine, and both were designed before DESTINY-Breast05 reported19,18. Whatever they show, it will be a comparison against the conjugate that has now lost a randomised trial. They will be internally valid and externally awkward. Post-neoadjuvant intensification for residual disease follows what each is built to settle.

The comparison nobody has run is between the two conjugates in the population KATHERINE enrolled and DESTINY-Breast05 excluded. For node-negative residual disease the honest position is that escalation is established and the choice of agent is extrapolated.

Caution

The price is respiratory and it is not small. Adjudicated drug-related interstitial lung disease occurred in 9.6% of patients on trastuzumab deruxtecan against 1.6% on trastuzumab emtansine, and two patients with interstitial lung disease in the deruxtecan group died17. Overall survival in that trial is immature. So the current position is a large recurrence benefit, no survival result yet, and two treatment-related deaths from a toxicity that is detectable early. The surveillance that makes the difference between grade 1 and grade 5 is set out in Toxicity mechanisms, interstitial lung disease, ocular, and mucosal and should be in place before the first dose.

Both rows report excellent outcomes and neither row is a comparison against a full regimen. APT had no comparator at all24. A single-arm study in a population with a very low event rate can show that outcomes are excellent. It cannot show that a shorter regimen is equivalent to a longer one, because there is no longer one in the dataset. The reason that distinction matters, and why de-escalation requires a stated margin rather than an impressive rate, is developed in Clinical trials and evidence interpretation and in Designing de-escalation trials and the non-inferiority margin problem.

ATEMPT did randomise, and against the wrong thing for this purpose. Its comparator was the APT regimen rather than standard chemotherapy, so it tests substitution within de-escalation. Its co-primary toxicity endpoint was a protocol-defined composite of clinically relevant toxicity, and it did not separate the arms23. That composite counted qualifying events without weighting them. Matching totals are not the same as two regimens costing a patient the same things.

The randomised de-escalation trial does not exist and probably cannot be run. At the event rate these populations produce, a non-inferiority trial against the full regimen would need a cohort nobody will fund.

In practice

For a node-negative tumour of 3 cm or less, weekly paclitaxel with trastuzumab is the default. The evidence for it is a single-arm trial with an excellent rate24. Say that out loud rather than implying a randomised comparison exists.

Do not add pertuzumab in node-negative disease. The randomised trial that tested it found no benefit in that group25, and the evidence is in Adjuvant escalation and extended HER2-directed therapy.

Trastuzumab emtansine is an alternative where the toxicities of a weekly taxane are the dominant concern, not where total toxicity is23.

Neither trial reports its result for T1a and T1b tumours separately. Treatment at that size is extrapolation, and the conversation should say so.

Three trials tried to improve on chemotherapy with a year of trastuzumab. Two added a drug and one swapped the taxane for a conjugate. Only one crossed its primary endpoint. Only APHINITY has reported overall survival. That result has not reached significance. That is the class finding, and it is the reason escalation here is a small decision argued at length.

The three rows fail in three different ways, which is why they should not be pooled in the reading. ALTTO added lapatinib, a second inhibitor class rather than a second antibody. It lost on a threshold rather than on an effect. After the lapatinib-alone arm was closed for futility the protocol was amended to require a p value of 0.025 or less for the remaining comparisons, and the combination returned 0.0488. That is a multiplicity convention producing a negative trial, not a biological statement about dual blockade.

KAITLIN substituted a conjugate for the taxane and did not improve on it. Its informative number is not the hazard ratio. Treatment completion was 88.4% in the taxane arm and 65.0% in the conjugate arm26. A regimen that a large minority of patients do not finish cannot demonstrate superiority whatever its biology. Dual blockade in the adjuvant setting sets out both failures in full.

APHINITY is the trial the decision rests on, because it is the only one that added a drug the class had already validated and held everything else constant. It found its benefit in the node-positive stratum and nothing in the node-negative one. Longer follow-up sharpened rather than softened that split27,25. Overall survival has never reached its significance boundary, including at the third interim analysis28.

The nodal split is a subgroup gradient inside one randomisation rather than a randomised comparison of two populations. That is the limit on how hard it can be pushed, and it is also the only thing the table offers for selecting patients. Extended oral blockade is the other escalation option, and the single row is the whole evidence base. ExteNET bought its invasive disease-free survival difference at the cost of grade 3 diarrhoea in 40% of patients against 2% on placebo29. No antidiarrhoeal strategy was part of the randomisation, so that is the tolerability of the drug given without one. Randomisation was stratified by hormone receptor status and the receptor-positive group was reported separately30, which is where the benefit has been claimed to concentrate.

The overall survival column is the one to read here. At final analysis after eight years the two arms are indistinguishable31. A recurrence difference at two years that has not become a survival difference at eight is the shape this section exists to flag. The agents themselves are covered in Targeted therapy and the full reading is in Extended adjuvant therapy after trastuzumab.

7 · Hormone receptor-positive HER2-positive disease as a distinct algorithm

A tumour that is both hormone receptor-positive and HER2-positive carries two dependencies, and the treatment algorithm has to service both on different timescales. The trial record for this group is in Early triple-positive disease. This section carries only what changes a decision here.

The first consequence is that it responds less often to HER2-directed neoadjuvant therapy. In TBCRC 006 the in-breast response rate under chemotherapy-free dual blockade was lower in oestrogen receptor-positive than in receptor-negative disease, inside one trial and one regimen4. A plan that depends on observing eradication is working from a lower base rate in this group. The measurement point is separate from the biology. A response rate quoted for the subtype as a whole averages two groups that do not respond alike.

The second consequence is that adjuvant escalation is not selected by hormone receptor status. About two thirds of APHINITY was triple-positive, so this population supplied most of its result. The variable that decided that result was nodal status rather than the receptor27,25. An exploratory subanalysis of the same trial sorted the benefit by oestrogen receptor status and by HER2 expression level32. That analysis is hypothesis-generating and Subgroup evidence from the HER2 trials, and its limits reads it properly.

The third consequence is temporal. HER2-directed therapy finishes at one year. Endocrine therapy runs for five to ten, and the late recurrence hazard described in Late recurrence risk and surveillance implications applies to this population too. A patient who completes HER2-directed treatment has not completed treatment.

Interplay

Triple-positive disease is the clinical form of the two-receptor heterogeneity problem in HER2 heterogeneity and hormone receptor signaling in triple-positive disease. The compartment that survives HER2 blockade is not necessarily the compartment that survives oestrogen deprivation. A report calling both receptors positive has averaged twice over a tumour that is uniform in neither. The lower pathological complete response rate in oestrogen receptor-positive disease4 is what that looks like from the clinic. It is also why residual disease in this subtype should not be read as HER2-directed failure alone, and why the conjugate literature in Antibody-drug conjugates and targeted delivery treats antigen level and dependence as separate variables.

Preoperative regimens that remove conventional cytotoxic chemotherapy. Compare the eligibility criteria before comparing the response rates.

Two findings hold across the whole table. A regimen without cytotoxic chemotherapy eradicates tumour in a substantial minority of patients, and it costs far less toxicity than the regimen it replaces. Neither finding is in dispute and neither is sufficient.

The rows divide into three designs, and almost all the apparent disagreement between them is the design rather than the regimen.

The first group randomised a chemotherapy-free arm against the taxane the clinic would otherwise give. WSG-ADAPT HER2+/HR− dropped the taxane from dual blockade in hormone receptor-negative disease3. WSG TP-II put endocrine therapy in its place in receptor-positive disease34. KRISTINE did it with a conjugate in place of the chemotherapy15. All three lost on response, by margins the result column makes plain. These are the load-bearing rows, and they say the same thing NeoSphere said in Neoadjuvant backbone and dual blockade. The chemotherapy is the component carrying most of the response.

The second group is single-arm or not powered for comparison. TBCRC 006 gave twelve weeks of chemotherapy-free dual blockade to stage II and III disease of any receptor status, adding letrozole where the oestrogen receptor was positive4. TBCRC 023 extended the same idea to twenty-four weeks under a pick-the-winner design that was explicitly not powered for a direct comparison2. PAMELA was built to test whether the HER2-enriched intrinsic subtype predicts response rather than to test the regimen at all37. Reading these rows as rival response rates is the error the caption warns against. Median tumour size differs several-fold across this table, and a response rate tracks who was enrolled before it tracks what they were given.

The third group routes patients on a measurement taken after treatment has started. PerELISA used the relative fall in Ki67 after two weeks of letrozole36. PHERGain used a positron emission tomography response after two cycles, switching non-responders to a chemotherapy-containing regimen35. PHERGain-2 routes adjuvant treatment by pathological response instead and has not reported its recurrence-free interval endpoint33. A biomarker read on treatment is not a selection tool. It tells you who is already responding, which is a different service from telling you in advance who can be spared.

That distinction runs deeper than the table shows, and it is the single most important thing about this literature. Where these protocols say what follows surgery, a patient with residual disease went on to receive chemotherapy, a conjugate, or both3,34,36,35,33. What was tested was deferral of chemotherapy in patients who turned out not to need it. None of these trials reports a non-responder left untreated. A reader who takes these rows as evidence that chemotherapy can be dropped has read a response-adapted strategy as a subtraction.

Two intrinsic-subtype rows point at a selection tool that does not yet exist. HER2-enriched tumours by PAM50 respond far more often than others in PAMELA and in the responder cohort of PerELISA37,36. That is a real biological signal measured on pre-treatment tissue. It has never been used prospectively to assign treatment, and an assay that enriches a response rate is not the same object as an assay validated to withhold a drug.

Caution

PHERGain is read as showing that a substantial minority of patients can safely omit chemotherapy. What it demonstrated is a three-year rate in a phase 2 trial whose endpoint for that group was a single-arm rate against a fixed threshold35. The trial randomised, but not to power that comparison. Its chemotherapy group held 71 patients. Grade 3 or higher treatment-related adverse events occurred in 33% of the response-adapted group against 62% of the chemotherapy group, which is the part of the trial that is not in doubt. That is a promising strategy, not an established equivalence, and Designing de-escalation trials and the non-inferiority margin problem explains what would be needed to make it one.

In practice

Anthracycline omission is the de-escalation with the best randomised evidence behind it10. It should be the default question rather than an exception.

Chemotherapy omission remains a trial question outside a trial. Where it is used off protocol, say that the supporting evidence is phase 2 and response-adapted35.

In hormone receptor-positive disease, replacing the taxane with endocrine therapy lowers the pathological complete response rate sharply34. If the plan depends on observing a response, that matters.

Do not offer a chemotherapy-free strategy to a patient who would decline chemotherapy for residual disease. These strategies were built on giving it.

9 · Cardiac surveillance and cumulative toxicity

Two cardiac toxicities run through this chapter and they behave differently. Conflating them produces the wrong surveillance schedule.

Anthracycline injury is dose-dependent and largely irreversible. In a prospective cohort of 2,625 patients receiving anthracycline-containing therapy with serial ejection fraction measurement, cardiotoxicity occurred in 9%. The median interval from the end of chemotherapy to the event was 3.5 months, and 98% of events occurred within the first year. With heart failure therapy, 11% of affected patients recovered fully and 71% recovered partially. Two variables predicted the event independently. Ejection fraction at the end of chemotherapy carried a hazard ratio of 1.37 for each one-point decrement. Cumulative doxorubicin dose carried a hazard ratio of 1.09 for each 50 mg per square metre38.

HER2-directed injury is not dose-dependent in the same way and is usually reversible on stopping the drug. In the randomised adjuvant setting the rates are low. Primary cardiac events were uncommon in both arms of APHINITY25.

The strongest cardiac intervention available is not surveillance. It is avoiding the anthracycline where the evidence permits, which is where the toxicity half of Neoadjuvant backbone and dual blockade gets priced. In TRAIN-2, an ejection fraction fall of 10 points or more to below 50% occurred in 7.7% of patients given an anthracycline and 3.2% of those who were not10.

Baseline risk stratification, monitoring schedules and the management of asymptomatic ejection fraction decline are codified in39, and the survivorship-phase management is in Treatment toxicity and supportive care.

In practice

Measure ejection fraction before the first anthracycline dose and again at the end of chemotherapy. The end-of-chemotherapy value was an independent predictor of subsequent cardiotoxicity, alongside cumulative anthracycline dose38.

Concentrate surveillance in the first year after treatment. That is when 98% of anthracycline events occurred38.

Start heart failure therapy when a decline is detected rather than when symptoms appear. Most affected patients treated on that basis recovered fully or partly38.

A fall on HER2-directed therapy usually warrants holding the drug and reassessing rather than abandoning it. A fall after anthracycline is treated, not observed38.

In a patient with cardiac risk factors, the anthracycline decision is the cardiology decision. The evidence that it can be omitted under dual blockade is randomised10.
