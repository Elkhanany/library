Adjuvant endocrine therapy in hormone receptor-positive disease

Why most rows in these tables answer a question one step to the side of the one the clinic is asking.

1 · Scope, and how to read the evidence tables in this chapter

This chapter is the evidence compendium for adjuvant endocrine therapy. It collects the randomised trials that settled which agent, for how long, and with or without ovarian suppression. It ends on adherence, because that is the largest modifiable determinant of how much of the benefit is actually delivered.

Three neighbouring chapters carry what is deliberately absent from this one. The pharmacology of each agent class is in Endocrine therapy. The decision architecture for early hormone receptor-positive disease, including the genomic assays and the chemotherapy question, is in Early hormone receptor-positive, HER2-negative disease. Adjuvant CDK4/6 inhibition is a separate decision and has its own chapter in Adjuvant CDK4/6 inhibition.

The table below is a filter on registry fields rather than on what a trial randomised. That distinction does more work here than anywhere else in the book, because endocrine therapy was a component of nearly every adjuvant trial run in this subtype for forty years. A trial enters the table if endocrine therapy was anywhere in its design. It did not have to be the variable.

Every randomised adjuvant endocrine trial in the registry for this subtype. Outcomes appear in the per-question tables that follow.

Sorted by what was actually randomised, the thirty-two rows fall into six groups. Five of them are the sections that follow: tamoxifen duration, aromatase inhibitor against tamoxifen, ovarian suppression, extension beyond five years, and the oral degraders. The sixth group has no section of its own, and its rows are worth naming so that none of them goes unexplained.

Four rows sit in the table because endocrine therapy was the constant rather than the variable. NSABP B-20 and SWOG-8814 / INT-0100 added chemotherapy to tamoxifen. WSG-PlanB randomised two chemotherapy regimens. Its assay-driven omission of chemotherapy sat outside that randomisation. WSG-ADAPT HR+/HER2− compared two assay-defined groups who both received endocrine therapy alone, which is a selection rule tested for non-inferiority rather than a randomised drug comparison. All four belong to Genomic assays and the decision to omit chemotherapy, and none of them randomised an endocrine decision.

They do carry one thing the endocrine sections cannot. The 21-gene recurrence score was built and validated in cohorts whose endocrine therapy was tamoxifen alone31,32. The assay now used to place a patient on endocrine therapy without chemotherapy was calibrated against a backbone most of those patients no longer receive. That is a measurement problem rather than a biological one, and Clinical and genomic risk assessment is where it is argued.

NSABP B-14 is the oldest row and the one the rest of the chapter stands on. It randomised tamoxifen against placebo in node-negative receptor-positive disease25. No trial since has carried a no-treatment arm in this population. Every comparison below is therefore between two active strategies, and the absolute benefit of endocrine therapy itself is measured nowhere in this chapter.

Two rows randomised nothing that bears on a drug choice. CALGB 9343 randomised radiotherapy in women aged seventy or older, with tamoxifen in both arms, and belongs to Omission in low-risk and older patients27. POSITIVE is a single-group study of a planned interruption to attempt pregnancy, with its comparison drawn from an external cohort rather than from randomisation28. It is discussed in Pregnancy after breast cancer and interrupting endocrine therapy.

The last two rows are the within-class question, and this section rather than the next one carries them because the filter below is built on the comparison against tamoxifen. FACE randomised letrozole against anastrozole in node-positive disease. MA.27 randomised exemestane against anastrozole under a two-sided superiority design. Neither trial separated the drugs on its primary endpoint. FATA-GIM3 randomised across the same three drugs inside a larger design and also separated them on nothing8. Three randomised comparisons agree that the choice among the three aromatase inhibitors is not an efficacy choice. It is a tolerability and interaction choice, and it should be made on that basis12,16.

What none of the three can show is the manoeuvre a clinic makes after an arthralgia. Nobody randomised a switch between aromatase inhibitors against continuing the first one. Moving a symptomatic patient from letrozole to exemestane therefore rests on the absence of an efficacy difference and on nothing else.

The overview table prints no result column at all, which is deliberate. Outcomes belong to the per-question tables below, where the control arm is stated next to them. Read the arms column of any generated table in this book before reading anything else in it.

Several of the sections below open with the question their trials were built to answer, taken from the [Chapter Stories](stories.html) map, where the whole landscape is laid out as questions. The division of labour is that tables enumerate and prose argues. A figure that sits in a table is not repeated in the text unless the argument is about that figure.

The duration question is not about how long the drug works. It is about how long its effect persists after it stops.

The table holds two rows and one result. ATLAS randomised continuation to ten years against stopping at five, and reported. aTTom sits in the registry as awaited, with no primary duration result on its record and its primary endpoint not stated there. The ten-against- five decision therefore rests on a single trial. The pooled reading of the two that circulates in review articles is an arithmetic performed outside either trial's own publication.

The accounting matters more than the size of the result. ATLAS counts recurrence and breast cancer mortality over years 5 to 1415. That window runs five years past the last tablet in the continuation arm. The hazard of recurrence in this subtype does not fall away when treatment stops33. A duration trial that closed its books at year ten would be reading the effect while it was still arriving.

That is the whole case for extending anything. It is the mirror image of the late recurrence hazard curve set out in Annual hazard of recurrence and the long tail of hormone receptor-positive disease. A treatment whose effect arrives late is worth giving only to a woman whose risk also arrives late.

Two features of the population constrain how the figures transfer. ATLAS randomised women already free of disease at five years, so the estimate is conditional on surviving that far. Its breast cancer outcomes are reported only in the women with ER-positive disease, and the remainder were ER-negative or never assayed, which reflects practice between 1996 and 200515.

The price of the second five years is mostly endometrial and thrombotic. Continuing to ten years raised endometrial cancer by a rate ratio of 1.74 and pulmonary embolus by 1.87, each against stopping at five15. Both are ratios of event rates. The absolute excess depends on the baseline incidence in the woman being counselled, which a ratio does not carry.

What the table cannot show is the comparison a postmenopausal woman is actually offered. ATLAS set continuation against stopping. Nobody randomised ten years of tamoxifen against five years of tamoxifen followed by an aromatase inhibitor, which is the sequence the next two sections make the default. The one analysis that does sit on the aTTom record asks instead whether an expression assay identifies the women who gain29, and Unresolved questions and what reads out next returns to it.

The patient-level overview of the first five years remains the anchor, and it established that a carry-over effect exists at all34. Tamoxifen is still the first choice in a premenopausal woman who will not receive ovarian suppression, and in anyone for whom bone loss is the dominant competing risk.

Menopausal status is not a patient characteristic in this decision. It is a statement about where oestrogen is being made, and Selection of endocrine therapy by menopausal status develops the consequences.

The six trials agree on one thing, and it is easy to lose in the detail. Wherever tamoxifen exposure is replaced by aromatase inhibition, recurrence falls. Wherever both arms finish on an aromatase inhibitor and only the order differs, nothing separates them. That is one finding about the class rather than six findings about six protocols.

The six divide by control arm rather than by drug. ATAC and BIG 1-98 compared five years of an aromatase inhibitor against five years of tamoxifen from the start. IES and ABCSG-8 / ARNO 95 compared a switch at two to three years against continuing tamoxifen to the end of five. TEAM and FATA-GIM3 compared starting with an aromatase inhibitor against starting with tamoxifen and switching later. Three different questions, habitually read as one.

TEAM constrains practice more than the others and is cited least. Its null result means that starting with an aromatase inhibitor is not superior to starting with tamoxifen and switching later18. FATA-GIM3 reached the same place from a six-arm design8. The case for starting with an aromatase inhibitor therefore rests on tolerability and on the gynaecological and thromboembolic profile. It does not rest on efficacy, because the two trials built to test the order found none.

The decision rests on BIG 1-98. It was double-blind. It randomised the whole five years rather than a switch part-way. It is also the only one of the six whose intention-to-treat overall survival comparison favoured the aromatase inhibitor. ATAC, with ten years of follow-up, did not reproduce that19. The divergence is not anastrozole against letrozole, because FACE randomised those two head to head and separated them on nothing. It is blinding, crossover and duration of follow-up.

BIG 1-98 also shows how much of its own survival claim is model-dependent. After the 2005 analysis, patients still on tamoxifen were unblinded and offered letrozole. A censoring-weighted analysis that removes those crossovers moves the overall survival estimate further in favour of letrozole than the intention-to-treat estimate in the table17. Both are correct estimates of different quantities. Only the intention-to-treat estimate describes the policy that was actually studied.

The two switch trials differ from each other in a way the table hides. IES randomised inside a single protocol with a fixed five-year total and reported overall survival22. The ABCSG-8 and ARNO 95 row is a combined analysis of two trials, reporting event-free survival with no overall survival in the pooled paper26. Their agreement is real. It is agreement between one trial and one pooling, not between two replications.

The measurement point is the one that causes harm in clinic. Menopausal status after chemotherapy is inferred from amenorrhoea and from gonadotrophin and oestradiol assays. Amenorrhoea is not menopause. An aromatase inhibitor given to a woman with recoverable ovarian function lowers oestrogen, raises gonadotrophins and can restart the ovary.

Three things the table cannot show. Every one of the six enrolled postmenopausal women, so none of it transfers to a premenopausal woman who is not suppressed. No trial randomised the length of the tamoxifen lead-in, so the two to three years the switch trials used is a protocol convention rather than an optimum. And bone is not a column here. The patient-level meta-analysis that prices recurrence, mortality and fracture together in genuinely postmenopausal women is35, and its figures sit in Selection of endocrine therapy by menopausal status.

4 · Ovarian function suppression and who benefits

Early disease · HR+/HER2- · adjuvant

Choosing the intensity and the duration of endocrine treatment

How completely does oestrogen have to be removed?

Rationale
Tamoxifen blocks the receptor; an aromatase inhibitor removes the ligand; ovarian suppression removes the source. Each step is a deeper intervention on the same axis, and each was expected to buy more disease control at more cost.
Experiment
Direct comparisons of an aromatase inhibitor against tamoxifen, and of ovarian suppression added to either, in premenopausal and postmenopausal populations separately.
Finding
Deeper suppression lowers recurrence, and the absolute gain tracks baseline risk rather than being uniform across the cell.
Limitation
None of these trials randomised the decision that a clinic actually makes, which is whether this particular patient's risk justifies the menopausal symptoms and the bone loss. Subgroup gradients are not the same as a randomised selection rule.
Next question
If the benefit tracks risk, can risk be measured well enough to allocate the intensity rather than infer it after the fact?

Two questions live under one heading and they have different answers. The four rows split two and two.

SOFT and ASTRRA randomised whether to add suppression to tamoxifen. SOFT/TEXT and ABCSG-12 randomised what to pair with suppression, with every patient suppressed in both arms. A reader who takes all four as tests of ovarian suppression has read two of them backwards.

The decision rests on SOFT, because it is the only trial here that randomised the addition against the thing a clinic would otherwise give, which is five years of tamoxifen. Its first report did not find the addition significant36. The eight-year report reversed that reading on disease-free and overall survival alike10, and the twelve- year report held it37. No biology changed between the first report and the second. The events needed time to accrue, which is the structural problem with adjuvant endpoints set out in Neoadjuvant, adjuvant, and metastatic design compared.

ASTRRA asks the same addition question in a narrower population. It enrolled women who remained premenopausal or resumed ovarian function after chemotherapy, assessed every six months by follicle-stimulating hormone and bleeding history5. Two years of goserelin was enough to improve disease-free survival, and the separation persisted at eight years38. Its overall survival comparison did reach significance. The interval around that estimate is wide enough that the point estimate should not be quoted to a patient as a survival benefit.

On the partner question the two trials disagree, and the disagreement is not about the drugs. SOFT/TEXT found exemestane better than tamoxifen on disease-free survival and not on overall survival1. ABCSG-12 found anastrozole no better than tamoxifen. Both held suppression constant in every arm, so that is not what divides them. Three things are. ABCSG-12 gave three years of treatment rather than five, anastrozole rather than exemestane, and a factorial randomisation to zoledronic acid. Its endocrine comparison is therefore a marginal one inside a bone-agent trial20,39. The trial cannot separate any of that, and the patient-level meta-analysis that pools the field averages its result into the total40. The practice guidance that follows is in Ovarian function suppression and who benefits.

What nobody randomised is the part of the decision that is hardest in clinic. The duration of suppression has never been compared head to head, so the two, three and five years in this table are protocol choices rather than findings. The method has never been randomised either, which leaves agonist, oophorectomy and ablation interchangeable by assumption. And discontinuation is not a column, although it is what decides how much of any of this is delivered.

Caution

Every trial in this table enrolled premenopausal women. Where a benefit was found, it concentrated in those who were young, node-positive, high grade or chemotherapy-treated. That gradient comes from subgroup analysis and not from randomised allocation. Being premenopausal is the entry criterion, not the indication.

A woman with a small grade 1 node-negative tumour belongs to the enrolled population and not to the group that gained. Saying the absolute number out loud before prescribing is the discipline Ovarian function suppression and who benefits asks for.

5 · Extended adjuvant therapy and the shape of the late benefit

Early disease · HR+/HER2- · adjuvant

Choosing the intensity and the duration of endocrine treatment

How long should endocrine treatment continue?

Rationale
Recurrence in this cell continues at a roughly constant rate for at least twenty years, so treatment that stops at five years stops while the hazard is still running.
Experiment
Randomised extension beyond five years, in each of the sequences a clinic actually uses. Tamoxifen to ten years, an aromatase inhibitor after tamoxifen, an aromatase inhibitor after an aromatase inhibitor. Then, against the same question from the other side, trials of shorter or intermittent extension.
Finding
Extension lowers recurrence, the effect is smaller than the first five years bought, and several extension trials found that more was not better than less.
Limitation
The extension trials enrolled patients who had already tolerated five years, which is a selected population, and they differ in the drug taken first, so the results are not interchangeable across sequences.
Next question
Since the benefit of extension is real but small, can the patients who get it be identified before ten years of treatment are prescribed to everyone?

The case for extension is a statement about hazard. Distant recurrence in this subtype accrues at a roughly constant rate from year 5 to year 2033. The size of that residual risk by tumour size and nodal status is tabulated in Annual hazard of recurrence and the long tail of hormone receptor-positive disease and Late recurrence risk and surveillance implications. Extension is therefore a dormancy intervention rather than consolidation of a falling risk, and Dormancy, residual disease, and late recurrence is where that argument is made properly.

One thing is worth settling before the rows are read. Four of the eight report overall survival, and none of those four shows an advantage for the longer arm. The other four do not report it at all. An extension decision is a recurrence decision, and it should be described to a patient as one.

The eight look inconsistent until they are sorted by control arm, which is the axis that decides what each can answer. MA.17, MA.17R and NSABP B-42 randomised further treatment against placebo, and they are the only three that bear on whether to extend at all. ABCSG-16, DATA, GIM4 and IDEAL randomised a longer extension against a shorter one, so they answer how long rather than whether. SOLE answers neither, because both of its arms were extended.

The three placebo-controlled trials answer whether to extend at all, and they answer it in both sequences. MA.17 extended letrozole after tamoxifen. MA.17R extended it after an aromatase inhibitor, and both separated from placebo on disease-free survival23,14. NSABP B-42 asked the same question in the same place as MA.17R and did not clear its own significance threshold6. Extending after an aromatase inhibitor is therefore not the weaker version of the question, which is how it is usually read.

The four duration-against-duration trials are where the field reads a disagreement that may not be there. DATA and GIM4 asked the same thing, a longer aromatase inhibitor course after two to three years of tamoxifen, and returned disease-free survival hazard ratios of 0.79 and 0.78. Only GIM4 crossed significance, and it read out at a median follow-up of 11.7 years against DATA's five-year adapted endpoint11,4. What separates a positive extension trial from a null one here is events and follow-up rather than drug or sequence. IDEAL admitted women after any five years of endocrine therapy, so each of its arms mixes the two sequences9.

NSABP B-42 is where a convention rather than a biology decided the reported verdict. Its disease-free survival p value fell below 0.05 and above the threshold its own design had prespecified, so the trial is recorded as negative6. The ten-year update reached significance on the same endpoint41. Nothing about letrozole changed between those reports. An interim-analysis alpha changed what the same comparison was permitted to be called, which is worth knowing before quoting either one.

ABCSG-16 is the only row that priced the longer course in fractures as a named secondary endpoint rather than as an adverse event count3. Its two arms differed only in how many further years of anastrozole were given. The fracture excess is therefore attributable to duration and not to starting the drug, which is the one place in this table where the cost of extension is reported as an outcome.

SOLE answers a question the others do not ask. It gave letrozole intermittently against continuously, on the hypothesis that a break restores sensitivity, and found no difference at five years or at seven13,42. How to give an extension is separate from whether to give one. A null schedule trial is not evidence about duration.

The reading that fits is about what the control arm was doing, not about the molecule or the sequence. Against no further treatment, the longer arm lowered recurrence in MA.17 and MA.17R, in neither case with a survival difference. Against a shorter further course, three of the four trials did not separate. MA.17R is the awkward row for any tidy account of diminishing returns. Its patients had already had about five years of an aromatase inhibitor, and most had taken tamoxifen before that.

Caution

MA.17 is quoted as the trial that established extension, and its estimate is more fragile than that reputation suggests. It was stopped at its first interim analysis on short follow-up, and the placebo group was then unblinded and offered letrozole23,43. Its long-term estimate compares five further years against a shorter delayed course, not against none.

Collapsing this table into one message is the commonest error in this literature. The general form of that error, and how to spot it in a report whose comparator changed mid-trial, is in Designing de-escalation trials and the non-inferiority margin problem.

The patient-level meta-analysis of twelve aromatase inhibitor extension trials in 22,031 postmenopausal women now prices both sides44. Its recurrence, mortality and fracture figures belong to Extended endocrine therapy and duration. What a clinic needs from it is the absolute framing rather than the proportional one, and that arithmetic is in Absolute and relative benefit, and why the difference matters.

One component of the extension benefit is not recurrence at all. Contralateral and second primary breast cancers fall with continued aromatase inhibition, and IDEAL isolates that effect because its two arms differed only in duration9. MA.17R reports the same direction on annual contralateral incidence14. Preventing a new primary is a real benefit. It is not the same achievement as preventing relapse, and Treatment duration and extended therapy sets out why the distinction matters when quoting a disease-free survival difference.

Two populations are missing from every row. No extension trial enrolled premenopausal women, so duration in a woman treated with tamoxifen and suppression is unstudied. And every placebo- controlled trial enrolled women who had already tolerated five years, which selects for adherence and against the patients most likely to stop. No trial in the table allocated extension on a molecular signal, which is the gap Unresolved questions and what reads out next returns to.

No adjuvant oral selective estrogen receptor degrader has reported. The table carries three rows and no outcome behind any of them. Everything in this section is a hypothesis with a trial design attached to it.

The rationale was transposed from advanced disease, where the target is an ESR1 ligand-binding domain mutation conferring ligand-independent receptor activity. The biology is in Estrogen receptor signaling and endocrine resistance and the agents are in Oral degraders and next-generation agents. The transposition has a problem that is rarely stated. ESR1 mutations are selected by aromatase inhibitor exposure and are uncommon at diagnosis Endocrine therapy and the emergence of ESR1 alterations. In the adjuvant setting there is no measurable tumour, and usually no mutation to target.

The adjuvant hypothesis therefore cannot be that a degrader treats a mutant receptor. It has to be that more complete receptor blockade stops resistance from forming. That is a different claim and it has never been tested in any setting.

The CAMBRIA pair tests two versions of it, split by when the degrader is given rather than by which degrader it is. CAMBRIA-1 switches patients to camizestrant after two to five years of standard endocrine therapy, which is the late-selection version. CAMBRIA-2 replaces standard endocrine therapy from the start, which is the complete-blockade version. lidERA puts a second molecule into the same question, giredestrant against standard adjuvant endocrine therapy. Its registry record does not yet fix a primary endpoint, so what it will be judged on is not settled here. Two agents behaving the same way would say something about the strategy. One of each would say something about the molecules.

Two design features deserve attention now rather than at readout. Invasive breast cancer-free survival counts second primaries, so part of any positive result will be prevention rather than recurrence control, exactly as in the extension trials above. And CAMBRIA-1 admits patients who received a CDK4/6 inhibitor, while CAMBRIA-2 permits abemaciclib in both arms for its first two years30. The comparator in each is current practice rather than endocrine monotherapy.

What no trial in the class does is select. None of the three randomises on circulating tumour DNA, on ESR1 status or on any measure of residual disease. A positive trial would therefore license a drug for a population in which most patients were never going to relapse. The interception argument is in Liquid biopsy and longitudinal monitoring and Strategies to detect and intercept residual disease.

Caution

The prior from advanced disease is weaker than the enthusiasm suggests. In ESR1 wild-type populations the oral degraders have produced modest effects against standard endocrine therapy. The progression-free survival figures that support that statement are in Oral degraders and next-generation agents.

Reading a positive metastatic result as support for adjuvant use is the error that four adjuvant CDK4/6 trials made visible. Two were positive and two were negative within one class, and Adjuvant CDK4/6 inhibition works through why.

7 · Adherence, toxicity, and the gap between prescribed and taken

Every hazard ratio in this chapter was generated in a population in which a large minority stopped the drug. Adherence and long-term tolerability makes that point about what an effect estimate means. This section makes the arithmetic point that follows from it.

Compare the size of the two levers a clinician actually controls. Choosing an aromatase inhibitor over tamoxifen in a postmenopausal woman changes recurrence by a few percentage points over a decade. Not taking the tablet removes the entire effect of the drug. In a cohort of 8,769 women with early-stage disease, about half did not take adjuvant endocrine therapy for the full intended duration45. Clinic time is spent almost entirely on the smaller lever.

The randomised evidence says most of that loss is not pharmacological. In the placebo-controlled extension trials pooled by the EBCTCG, non-adherence ran at nearly the same rate on further aromatase inhibition as on placebo44. Almost as many women stopped an inert tablet as stopped an active one. Symptom management therefore has a ceiling, and the rest of the problem is structural.

Measurement matters here more than usual, because the three common methods disagree by design. Self-report overestimates, and most of all when the prescriber is the one asking the question. Pharmacy refill measures collection from a dispensary rather than ingestion. Serum tamoxifen or endoxifen, and serum oestradiol during aromatase inhibition, measure exposure directly and are almost never used. A trial reporting adherence by refill and a clinic reporting it by self-report are not describing the same quantity, and neither is wrong.

Dose is the one pharmacological lever with randomised evidence behind it. TAM-01 tested 5 mg of tamoxifen daily against placebo and reported durable benefit at ten years46. That trial enrolled breast intraepithelial neoplasia rather than invasive disease. Using low-dose tamoxifen to rescue an intolerant patient with invasive cancer is an extrapolation, and it should be described to her as one.

In practice

Ask about adherence at every visit, and ask in a form that permits a true answer. Roughly half of patients do not complete the prescribed course45.

Set the expectation at the first visit rather than at the first symptom. Say that most women get vasomotor or musculoskeletal symptoms, that these usually settle, and that stopping is the outcome you are trying to prevent.

Treat the symptom before shortening the course. Switching between aromatase inhibitors, adding a vasomotor agent, or moving the dose to bedtime all leave the patient on treatment. Stopping does not.

Quantify the loss out loud when a patient asks to stop early. A woman stopping at year two has taken about two fifths of the intended course, and she is entitled to know roughly what that costs in the absolute framing of Absolute and relative benefit, and why the difference matters.

Assess bone density before and during aromatase inhibition or ovarian suppression, because fractures rise with both35,40. The management belongs in the same visit and is in Bone health and cardiovascular risk after treatment.

Record which adherence measure you used. A refill ratio and a patient's answer are different numbers. Record which of the two you used, because they will disagree.

The filter above asks for unreported trials in early disease with an endocrine or cell-cycle component. It returns three adjuvant phase 3 trials and one neoadjuvant phase 2 with no control arm. Read the line and phase columns before treating the four as one programme.

Five questions are open, and only two of them have a trial that will answer them.

Whether an oral degrader beats standard endocrine therapy in the adjuvant setting is being tested in two positions at once. CAMBRIA-1 and CAMBRIA-2 could disagree. A split result would be more informative than two concordant ones. A positive switch trial with a negative upfront trial would say the benefit lies in late blockade, after resistance has begun to form.

Whether extension can be targeted rather than offered broadly has no trial at all. Nobody has randomised extended endocrine therapy on a molecular signal, and circulating tumour DNA is the obvious candidate. The assay performance and the interception problem are in Liquid biopsy and longitudinal monitoring and Strategies to detect and intercept residual disease.

Duration in premenopausal women is close to unstudied. Every aromatase inhibitor extension trial in this chapter required a postmenopausal population. ATLAS carried no such requirement, and it tested tamoxifen rather than the suppression-based regimens these women now receive.

The duration of ovarian suppression has never been compared head to head in a randomised trial. ASTRRA used two years and SOFT used five. The question is not academic, because suppression is the least tolerated component of the regimen in young women Young adults, fertility, and pregnancy.

Whether cell cycle inhibition changes the endocrine question is being approached from two directions here. WSG ADAPTcycle asks whether ribociclib with an aromatase inhibitor can replace chemotherapy in intermediate-risk disease, with entry decided by a recurrence score and by Ki-67 measured after short preoperative endocrine therapy47. A positive result would move part of the chemotherapy decision in Genomic assays and the decision to omit chemotherapy into the endocrine column. That is a larger change than any degrader result would produce.

Its entry rule is also the clearest example in this chapter of a measurement standing in for a biology. Endocrine response is defined by a Ki-67 threshold, scored locally in some centres and centrally in others, and the two readings do not agree in every patient47. Allocation therefore depends partly on which laboratory read the slide. The scoring conventions are set out in Ki67, PEPI and response measures for a setting where pCR fails.

NEOLETRIB is the odd row and the one worth keeping. It is a single-group neoadjuvant study of letrozole with ribociclib, with serial biopsy and single-cell sequencing during treatment48. It will not settle a treatment question and is not built to. It is the only study in this chapter that samples a tumour while endocrine pressure is being applied, which is the measurement the adjuvant setting can never make. The window designs it belongs among are in Neoadjuvant CDK4/6 inhibition and window-of-opportunity designs.

Interplay

Adjuvant endocrine therapy applies selective pressure for five to ten years to a population that cannot be sampled. That is the heterogeneity argument of Part VI running in slow motion. What emerges at year eight has been shaped by the agent chosen at year zero. The ESR1 mutations that define endocrine resistance in metastatic disease CDK4/6 inhibition and the evolution of cell cycle escape are selected by exactly the aromatase inhibitor exposure this chapter recommends. The oral degrader trials in Oral selective estrogen receptor degraders in the adjuvant setting are the first attempt to act on that selection before it has an observable output. They will be reported as duration trials. They are better understood as experiments on which escape route the adjuvant years build.
