Early triple-positive disease

Most of HER2-positive breast cancer, treated on subgroups of trials designed for something else.

1 · Why hormone receptor-positive HER2-positive disease behaves as its own entity

This chapter covers early hormone receptor-positive, HER2-positive breast cancer as a disease in its own right. The word triple-positive is clinical shorthand rather than a guideline category. It names the intersection of three separate assays, each with its own threshold and its own reproducibility, and Receptor assessment and the measurement problem sets those out.

The intersection is not a small corner. In the largest adjuvant trial of dual HER2 blockade the hormone receptor-positive group was the clear majority. The population column of the table below records the split. Whatever the exact figure in an unselected population, this is most of HER2-positive breast cancer rather than a fraction of it.

Four things make it behave differently, each of them developed in a section below.

Pathological complete response rates are consistently lower than in hormone receptor-negative HER2-positive disease, across regimens and across two decades Lower pathological complete response rates and what that does to the post-neoadjuvant decision.

Pathological complete response also carries less prognostic information here. In a pooled analysis of seven randomised trials it was associated with disease-free survival in HER2-positive non-luminal disease and was not associated with it in luminal B HER2-positive disease1.

The two dependencies run on different clocks, because HER2-directed therapy finishes at one year while endocrine therapy runs for five to ten. The late recurrence hazard described in Late recurrence risk and surveillance implications applies here as it does to any other hormone receptor-positive group.

Almost everything known about the entity comes from hormone receptor subgroups of trials designed for HER2-positive disease as a whole Subgroup evidence from the HER2 trials, and its limits.

What this chapter leaves to neighbours. The HER2-directed neoadjuvant backbone belongs to Neoadjuvant HER2-directed therapy, and adjuvant and post-neoadjuvant HER2-directed therapy to Adjuvant and post-neoadjuvant HER2-directed therapy. Choice and duration of adjuvant endocrine therapy belongs to Adjuvant endocrine therapy in hormone receptor-positive disease. The metastatic setting belongs to Metastatic triple-positive disease. 2 · Reciprocal cross-talk and what it predicts for treatment

The mechanism is set out in ER and HER2 cross-talk, the bidirectional plane and Estrogen receptor signaling and endocrine resistance and is not restated. The short form is that the two pathways regulate each other in both directions, so blocking one raises traffic on the other. This section is about what that predicts in clinic.

It predicts that single-axis therapy selects for the other axis. Oestrogen receptor signalling is upregulated as an adaptive survival mechanism in HER2-positive tumours under anti-HER2 treatment40. A tumour carrying both receptors therefore has an escape route that a receptor- negative one does not, which is the direct handoff from Resistance to antibodies, tyrosine kinase inhibitors, and conjugates. The clinical form of that prediction is simple. HER2 blockade without endocrine therapy should underperform here, as should endocrine therapy given without HER2 blockade.

It predicts that the two blockades should be given together rather than in sequence. Every gap between them is a window in which the escape route is open. The prediction is clean and the supporting randomised evidence in the adjuvant setting does not exist Endocrine therapy alongside HER2 blockade in the adjuvant setting.

It predicts a lower complete response rate. If the oestrogen receptor supplies a survival route that HER2-directed therapy does not close, then eradication should be less frequent when the receptor is present. That is what the neoadjuvant trials show Lower pathological complete response rates and what that does to the post-neoadjuvant decision.

It predicts that endocrine sensitivity should be measurable early and should matter. In PerELISA, a fall in Ki67 after two weeks of letrozole selected a group treated to surgery without chemotherapy13. A two-week endocrine response was being used to predict the result of a HER2-directed regimen, which only makes sense if the pathways are coupled.

It predicts convergence on the cell cycle. Both axes end at the same restriction point Cell cycle regulation, which is the rationale for PATINA and is developed in CDK4/6 inhibition with HER2 blockade.

Interplay

A report calling both receptors positive has averaged twice, over a tumour that is uniform in neither HER2 heterogeneity and hormone receptor signaling in triple-positive disease. The compartment that survives HER2 blockade need not be the compartment that survives oestrogen deprivation. Prospective assessment in the neoadjuvant setting showed that HER2 heterogeneity separated a group in which no patient reached a complete response from a group in which most did41, and Consequences for pathologic complete response in the neoadjuvant setting gives the numbers. The clinical reading for this chapter is narrow. The lower complete response rate in triple-positive disease is partly a statement about how much of the tumour lay outside the reach of the regimen. It is not a statement about how much HER2 the average cell expressed.

3 · Lower pathological complete response rates and what that does to the post-neoadjuvant decision

The observation is unusually consistent across drug classes and eras. Dual antibody blockade, lapatinib-containing blockade, conjugate-based regimens and conjugate-plus-taxane sequences all produce lower complete response rates when the oestrogen receptor is present. Hormone receptor-positive HER2-positive disease as a distinct algorithm gives the most recent figures from the conjugate era. The effect survives changes of drug class, which is what makes it a property of the disease rather than of any one regimen.

Now put that next to the second finding, because the combination is what matters. Complete response was not associated with disease-free survival in luminal B HER2-positive disease in the pooled German analysis, while it was strongly associated with it in non-luminal HER2-positive disease1. So in this subtype the endpoint is both rarer and less informative.

The consequence falls on the post-neoadjuvant decision, where KATHERINE and DESTINY-Breast05 both assign escalation on the presence of residual invasive disease. Residual disease is more common in this group, for reasons that are partly biological rather than a failure of the treatment given. More triple-positive patients are therefore routed into escalation by a trigger that carries less prognostic information than it does in receptor-negative disease.

That is not an argument against escalating, but an argument about what the trigger means. The distinction is the one made in Response-adapted and de-escalation designs. A response-adapted trial demonstrates that a population defined by residual disease benefits from a specific drug. It requires neither that the defining measure be prognostic nor that a regimen producing more complete responses produce longer survival.

Caution

The mirror error is more dangerous than the first one. A complete response in triple-positive disease is regularly read as permission to de-escalate what follows. The trials that produced these response rates did not test that. PHERGain and WSG TP-II both gave chemotherapy after residual disease, then kept dual blockade running for a year afterwards in every patient. A complete response inside such a design tells you the tumour responded. It does not tell you that the rest of the plan was unnecessary.

4 · Endocrine therapy alongside HER2 blockade in the adjuvant setting

Every patient in this chapter receives both treatments. The question is whether they should overlap, which no randomised trial in the adjuvant setting has answered.

The mechanistic case for overlapping them is the one in Reciprocal cross-talk and what it predicts for treatment. Oestrogen receptor signalling rises under anti-HER2 pressure40, so the months between finishing HER2-directed therapy and starting endocrine therapy are months in which the escape route is unopposed. On that reasoning the two should start as close together as the chemotherapy schedule allows.

The case against is practical rather than biological, because nobody has shown harm from concurrent administration and nobody has shown benefit either. Aromatase inhibitor arthralgia, tamoxifen symptoms and trastuzumab infusions accumulate in the same weeks. Most of the adherence that is ever lost is lost in that first year Adherence, toxicity, and the gap between prescribed and taken.

What the neoadjuvant trials contribute is limited but real. In WSG-ADAPT-TP, adding endocrine therapy to twelve weeks of trastuzumab emtansine changed the complete response rate hardly at all. The conjugate itself accounted for a large difference against trastuzumab with endocrine therapy22. In TBCRC 006, letrozole was given concurrently with dual HER2 blockade throughout, on an explicit hypothesis that blocking both receptors was required27. Neither trial isolates the contribution of timing, because neither randomised it.

Two practical points sit outside the trials. Endocrine therapy in this group runs long after HER2-directed therapy has finished. The late recurrence hazard in Late recurrence risk and surveillance implications is therefore the dominant long-term risk. Extended duration is decided on the same grounds as in HER2-negative disease Extended adjuvant therapy and the shape of the late benefit. And in the metastatic setting, receipt of endocrine therapy at all has been associated with better survival in this subtype in a real-world cohort42. That is an association in advanced disease and not a randomised comparison in early disease, but the direction is consistent with the mechanism.

In practice

Start endocrine therapy as soon as chemotherapy finishes. Give it alongside the remainder of the HER2-directed year rather than after it. There is no evidence of harm from the overlap, and the mechanism argues against leaving a gap.

Do not delay endocrine therapy for radiotherapy, since the two are routinely given together.

Treat the first year as the adherence problem it is. A patient managing infusions, radiotherapy and a new aromatase inhibitor at the same time is the patient most likely to stop the tablet.

Plan the endocrine duration conversation for the point at which HER2-directed therapy ends. That is when treatment appears to the patient to be finishing. It is also the point at which most of the remaining benefit has yet to be delivered.

5 · Chemotherapy omission and the de-escalation questions specific to this group

This is the live de-escalation question. It is more live here than in receptor-negative disease for one reason. A triple-positive tumour has a second systemic therapy that works, which a receptor-negative one does not.

Three strategies have been tested and all three work in some patients.

Antibodies with endocrine therapy and no chemotherapy produced complete responses in a minority of patients in TBCRC 006, PerELISA and WSG TP-II. A conjugate without chemotherapy produced them in a larger minority in WSG-ADAPT-TP. Response-adapted strategies used a two-week Ki67 fall or an early PET response to decide who continued without chemotherapy, in PerELISA and PHERGain.

The selection tools are the weak point, because both are continuous measurements converted into a gate. A two-week Ki67 threshold and a PET metabolic response threshold are allocation conventions. The reproducibility problem set out in Ki67, PEPI and response measures for a setting where pCR fails applies directly to the first of them.

WSG TP-II is the trial to read closely, because its design explains both its result and its limits. Substituting endocrine therapy for the weekly taxane more than halved the complete response rate, from 56.4% to 23.7%. Five-year survival was nonetheless close to complete in both arms. The reason is in the protocol rather than in the biology. Chemotherapy was mandatory after residual disease, so most of the endocrine arm received it anyway. The trial demonstrates that a response-guided strategy is safe. It does not demonstrate that chemotherapy can be omitted, which is a different claim.

That gap is the general shape of the problem. Demonstrating omission requires showing that events did not accumulate, which needs more patients, longer follow-up and a non-inferiority margin somebody has to justify Designing de-escalation trials and the non-inferiority margin problem. No trial confined to early triple-positive disease has been built at that size.

In practice

Outside a trial, chemotherapy remains part of treatment for node-positive or larger triple-positive tumours. Where it is omitted, say plainly that the supporting evidence is phase 2 and response-adapted.

The de-escalation with the strongest evidence is not omission of chemotherapy. It is omission of the anthracycline, which TRAIN-2 supports irrespective of hormone receptor status.

6 · Subgroup evidence from the HER2 trials, and its limits

Almost every statement made in this chapter rests on a hormone receptor subgroup of a trial powered for HER2-positive disease as a whole. That is worth saying explicitly rather than leaving implicit, and Clinical trials and evidence interpretation sets the standard this section is held to.

Start with what the design does well. Most of the trials in the table stratified randomisation by hormone receptor status, which means the subgroups are balanced for the factors randomisation balances. A stratified subgroup is better behaved than an unstratified one. Stratification supplies neither power nor a randomised comparison for the question of whether receptor status modifies the treatment effect.

APHINITY is the instructive case. The hormone receptor-positive and receptor-negative subgroup hazard ratios for adjuvant pertuzumab differ in point estimate and overlap substantially in interval43, and Hormone receptor-positive HER2-positive disease as a distinct algorithm gives both. The defensible reading is that nothing in those data distinguishes the two groups. The common reading, that pertuzumab works better in triple-positive disease, requires an interaction the trial did not demonstrate. A later exploratory subanalysis of the same trial went further and located the largest benefit in a receptor-positive subset defined by HER2 amplification level44. That analysis is hypothesis-generating and its authors say so.

Now the ceiling on the direct evidence. The largest randomised trial confined to early triple-positive disease enrolled 375 patients, and the next two enrolled 207 and 6422,6,13. Those are the trials that studied this entity rather than containing it. Everything else is a subgroup.

The asymmetry that follows is worth naming. Escalation decisions in this group rest on large randomised trials whose populations included it, which is a reasonable basis. De-escalation decisions rest on small trials designed for it, which is a weaker basis for a stronger claim. Practice has been more willing to de-escalate on the second than to question the first.

Caution

A subgroup result does not become more reliable because the subgroup is large. The hormone receptor-positive subgroup of APHINITY is large enough that its estimate is precise. It is still not a randomised comparison between receptor-positive and receptor-negative disease. Precision within a subgroup says nothing about whether the subgroups differ Statistical and clinical significance are different claims.

7 · Unresolved questions and what reads out next

The largest gap is an absence rather than a pending readout.

CompassHER2 RD tests escalation of post-neoadjuvant treatment for residual HER2-positive disease. Its eligibility is restricted to high-risk residual disease, defined as oestrogen receptor-negative or node-positive10. Receptor-positive, node-negative residual disease is therefore outside it. That is a large part of the triple-positive residual population, and precisely the group in which Lower pathological complete response rates and what that does to the post-neoadjuvant decision argues that residual disease is least informative about risk. The trial that would settle what to do with them is not running.

Three readouts will change something. Overall survival from DESTINY-Breast05 will show whether the invasive disease-free survival gain in residual disease converts into longer life. Its population was assembled on residual disease, in which this chapter's group is over-represented for the reasons given in Lower pathological complete response rates and what that does to the post-neoadjuvant decision. Longer follow-up from the response-adapted trials will show whether the strategy holds beyond the horizons currently reported. And the adjuvant question raised by PATINA has no early-disease trial behind it, so whether CDK4/6 inhibition belongs in early triple-positive disease is unasked rather than unanswered CDK4/6 inhibition with HER2 blockade.

Two questions will probably never be answered directly. Concurrent against sequential endocrine therapy alongside HER2 blockade would need a large trial for a difference nobody expects to be large Endocrine therapy alongside HER2 blockade in the adjuvant setting. Chemotherapy omission in an unselected triple-positive population would need a non-inferiority trial whose margin would be hard to justify to a patient Designing de-escalation trials and the non-inferiority margin problem.

What a reader should take from that list is a calibration rather than a plan. This chapter describes the best-treated common subtype in breast cancer. It is also the one whose treatment rests most heavily on inference from populations assembled to answer a different question.
