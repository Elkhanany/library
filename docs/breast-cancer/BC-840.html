Early triple-positive disease

Most of HER2-positive breast cancer, treated on subgroups of trials designed for something else.

1 · Why hormone receptor-positive HER2-positive disease behaves as its own entity

This chapter covers early hormone receptor-positive, HER2-positive breast cancer as a disease in its own right. The word triple-positive is clinical shorthand rather than a guideline category. It names the intersection of three separate assays, each with its own threshold and its own reproducibility, and Receptor assessment and the measurement problem sets those out.

The intersection is not a small corner. In APHINITY, the adjuvant pertuzumab trial, the hormone receptor-positive group was the clear majority. The population column of the table below records that split. Whatever the exact figure in an unselected population, this is most of HER2-positive breast cancer rather than a fraction of it.

Four things make it behave differently, each of them developed in a section below.

Pathological complete response rates are consistently lower than in hormone receptor-negative HER2-positive disease, across regimens and across two decades Lower pathological complete response rates and what that does to the post-neoadjuvant decision.

Pathological complete response also carries less prognostic information here. In a pooled analysis of seven randomised trials it was associated with disease-free survival in HER2-positive non-luminal disease and was not associated with it in luminal B HER2-positive disease1.

The two dependencies run on different clocks, because HER2-directed therapy finishes at one year while endocrine therapy runs for five to ten. The late recurrence hazard described in Late recurrence risk and surveillance implications applies here as it does to any other hormone receptor-positive group.

Almost everything known about the entity comes from hormone receptor subgroups of trials designed for HER2-positive disease as a whole Subgroup evidence from the HER2 trials, and its limits.

What this chapter leaves to neighbours. The HER2-directed neoadjuvant backbone belongs to Neoadjuvant therapy, and adjuvant and post-neoadjuvant HER2-directed therapy to Adjuvant and post-neoadjuvant therapy. Choice and duration of adjuvant endocrine therapy belongs to Adjuvant endocrine therapy. The metastatic setting belongs to Metastatic triple-positive disease. What is left here is the reasoning that makes this group its own entity rather than a subgroup of two others.

That table is the map of the chapter rather than a menu of options. It holds eight distinct questions, and the reason two rows disagree is usually that they were asked different ones.

Four trials built the backbone that everything else in the table sits on. HERA, NSABP B-31/N9831 and BCIRG-006 put adjuvant trastuzumab against chemotherapy alone, and NOAH did the same before surgery. None of the four was designed to say anything about the oestrogen receptor.

Five asked how long that backbone has to run. HERA carried a two-year arm, PERSEPHONE and PHARE tested six months, and Short-HER and SOLD tested nine weeks. Why those four do not agree with one another is argued in Subgroup evidence from the HER2 trials, and its limits.

Six asked what should be added to that backbone, or exchanged for part of it. NeoALTTO and NeoSphere added a second HER2-directed agent before surgery, and DESTINY-Breast11 exchanged the anthracycline for a conjugate ahead of the same triplet. ALTTO, APHINITY and ExteNET added an agent after surgery.

Two asked which chemotherapy belongs underneath the HER2-directed treatment. TRYPHAENA and TRAIN-2 both removed the anthracycline, the first as a cardiac safety study and the second as a randomised efficacy comparison.

Three substituted an antibody-drug conjugate for the cytotoxic chemotherapy. KRISTINE did it before surgery, KAITLIN after it, and WSG-ADAPT-TP did it in a twelve-week window confined to this population.

Two asked how little a small node-negative tumour needs. APT gave twelve weeks of paclitaxel with trastuzumab in a single group, which ATEMPT then used as the comparator for a conjugate.

Nine tried to manage without a full course of cytotoxic chemotherapy. TBCRC 006, TBCRC 023, PAMELA, PerELISA, WSG TP-II and TOUCH relied on endocrine therapy to hold the second pathway instead. PHERGain, PHERGain-2 and CompassHER2 pCR let an early or pathological response decide how much was given, which is the argument Chemotherapy omission and the de-escalation questions specific to this group carries.

Three asked what to do when the specimen still contains invasive disease, of which KATHERINE and DESTINY-Breast05 have reported and CompassHER2 RD has not.

2 · Reciprocal cross-talk and what it predicts for treatment

The mechanism is set out in ER and HER2 cross-talk, the bidirectional plane and Estrogen receptor signaling and endocrine resistance and is not restated. The short form is that the two pathways regulate each other in both directions, so blocking one raises traffic on the other. This section is about what that predicts in clinic.

It predicts that single-axis therapy selects for the other axis. Oestrogen receptor signalling is upregulated as an adaptive survival mechanism in HER2-positive tumours under anti-HER2 treatment42. A tumour carrying both receptors therefore has an escape route that a receptor- negative one does not, which is the direct handoff from Resistance to antibodies, tyrosine kinase inhibitors, and conjugates. The clinical form of that prediction is simple. HER2 blockade without endocrine therapy should underperform here, as should endocrine therapy given without HER2 blockade.

It predicts that the two blockades should be given together rather than in sequence. Every gap between them is a window in which the escape route is open. The prediction is clean and the supporting randomised evidence in the adjuvant setting does not exist Endocrine therapy alongside HER2 blockade in the adjuvant setting.

It predicts a lower complete response rate. If the oestrogen receptor supplies a survival route that HER2-directed therapy does not close, then eradication should be less frequent when the receptor is present. That is what the neoadjuvant trials show Lower pathological complete response rates and what that does to the post-neoadjuvant decision.

It predicts that endocrine sensitivity should be measurable early and should matter. In PerELISA, a fall in Ki67 after two weeks of letrozole selected a group treated to surgery without chemotherapy14. A two-week endocrine response was being used to predict the result of a HER2-directed regimen, which only makes sense if the pathways are coupled.

It predicts convergence on the cell cycle. Both axes end at the same restriction point Cell cycle regulation, which is the rationale for PATINA and is developed in CDK4/6 inhibition with HER2 blockade.

Interplay

A report calling both receptors positive has averaged twice, over a tumour that is uniform in neither HER2 heterogeneity and hormone receptor signaling in triple-positive disease. The compartment that survives HER2 blockade need not be the compartment that survives oestrogen deprivation. Prospective assessment in the neoadjuvant setting showed that HER2 heterogeneity separated a group in which no patient reached a complete response from a group in which most did43, and Consequences for pathologic complete response in the neoadjuvant setting gives the numbers. The clinical reading for this chapter is narrow. The lower complete response rate in triple-positive disease is partly a statement about how much of the tumour lay outside the reach of the regimen. It is not a statement about how much HER2 the average cell expressed.

3 · Lower pathological complete response rates and what that does to the post-neoadjuvant decision

Early disease · HR+/HER2+ · neoadjuvant

Taking the chemotherapy out when the oestrogen receptor is also on

Why does pathological complete response fall when the oestrogen receptor is also expressed?

Rationale
The two receptors talk to each other. Blocking HER2 lifts the suppression it exerts on oestrogen receptor signalling. A tumour carrying both can therefore shift its dependence instead of dying. A tumour with somewhere to go should be harder to eradicate before surgery than one without.
Experiment
The neoadjuvant HER2 trials stratified randomisation by hormone receptor status and reported response within each stratum. Between them they cover a taxane with one antibody, a taxane with an antibody and a tyrosine kinase inhibitor, a taxane with two antibodies, and a conjugate substituted for the taxane. The same split was reported in the large modern single-arm cohort given a taxane with dual blockade. Separately, intrinsic subtype was called on pretreatment biopsies in chemotherapy-free cohorts and set against response, in one cohort that admitted any receptor status and in one restricted to receptor-positive disease.
Finding
Wherever the split is reported, response is lower in the receptor-positive stratum than in the receptor-negative one. Intensifying the HER2 blockade raises response in both strata without closing the distance between them. Substituting a conjugate for the taxane does not close it either. What separates the receptor-positive tumours that clear from the rest is whether the pretreatment biopsy is called HER2-enriched.
Limitation
Hormone receptor status was a stratification factor and a reporting subgroup in these trials. It was never randomised. The two strata therefore differ in more than the receptor. The endpoint is not the same endpoint across them either. Some scored the breast alone and some the breast and the axilla. Receptor-positive tumours are also where low-volume residual disease concentrates, so where the line between a complete response and a near-complete one is drawn changes the size of the gap it produces. Subtype was called by a signature assay run on a single pretreatment core, which classifies that core rather than the tumour.

Rationale
In this subtype the adjuvant decision is made on what is left in the specimen. Residual invasive disease sends a patient to a conjugate. A neoadjuvant regimen that raises the residual disease rate therefore does not take cytotoxic treatment out of the pathway. It moves it later. It also changes which agent is given and on what evidence that agent was chosen.
Experiment
The randomised comparison of two antibodies with endocrine therapy against the same antibodies with weekly paclitaxel reported response and then followed the same patients for invasive disease-free survival. The comparison of a conjugate against docetaxel and carboplatin did the same. Set against those are the post-neoadjuvant trials, which randomised patients with residual invasive disease to a conjugate against trastuzumab, and then to one conjugate against another. A further trial is testing whether escalation can be restricted by receptor and nodal status.
Finding
The chemotherapy-free arms leave more residual disease than their cytotoxic comparators do. They therefore send more patients into post-neoadjuvant escalation. In the endocrine comparison further chemotherapy was obligatory for anyone without a complete response, so the saving before surgery was spent again by protocol after it. Escalation with a conjugate is worth a great deal against trastuzumab alone. The newer conjugate is worth more again than the older one. The survival estimates in the endocrine comparison did not follow its response gap, on intervals too wide to settle the point in either direction.
Limitation
Nobody randomised the pathway as a whole. The neoadjuvant substitution trials were sized for response, so their survival curves are secondary. The post-neoadjuvant trials enrolled patients who arrived at residual disease after a cytotoxic backbone rather than after a chemotherapy-free one, so the benefit of escalation is being read across from a different upstream treatment. Residual disease left by a taxane is not obviously the same disease as residual disease left by endocrine therapy. The trial of the newer conjugate required nodal involvement in the surgical specimen, which is not the low-risk population a chemotherapy-free neoadjuvant course is aimed at. The trial that restricts escalation by receptor and nodal status has not reported.

The observation is unusually consistent, and it is a finding about the class rather than about any member of it. Wherever the receptor split is reported, response is lower in the receptor-positive stratum. It holds for a taxane with one antibody, for a taxane with an antibody and a tyrosine kinase inhibitor, and for a taxane with two antibodies. It holds for a conjugate substituted for the taxane. It holds in CompassHER2 pCR, the largest modern cohort given a taxane with dual blockade. Intensifying the HER2 blockade raises response in both strata and does not close the distance between them. Twenty years of drug development have moved the level and left the gradient alone, which is what makes it a property of the disease.

The trials in that table are not disagreeing about the gap. They are measuring different things. Three sources of variation account for most of the spread, and none of them is the drug.

The first is the endpoint. Some of these trials scored the breast alone and some the breast and the axilla. That convention costs more here than elsewhere, because low-volume residual disease concentrates in receptor-positive tumours. TBCRC 006 found residual ypT1a-b disease in a third of its receptor-positive cases and in a small fraction of its receptor-negative ones. Where the line between a complete response and a near-complete one is drawn therefore changes the size of the gap it produces. That is measurement rather than biology.

The second is the comparator. NOAH, NeoALTTO, NeoSphere, TRYPHAENA, TRAIN-2 and DESTINY-Breast11 added or exchanged agents on top of a cytotoxic backbone. KRISTINE, WSG-ADAPT-TP, WSG TP-II and TOUCH took the cytotoxic away. TBCRC 006, TBCRC 023, PAMELA, PHERGain, PHERGain-2 and CompassHER2 pCR reported response without testing it against a randomised cytotoxic comparator. Two response rates from those three designs are not the same quantity.

The third is what was measured on the pretreatment biopsy. PerELISA found that the receptor-positive tumours which clear are largely the ones called HER2-enriched. PAMELA found the same split in a cohort that admitted any receptor status. That is a signature assay run on a single core, and it classifies the core rather than the tumour. Hormone receptor status was a stratification factor or a reporting subgroup in these trials and was randomised nowhere, so the two strata differ in more than the receptor.

Now put the lower response rate next to the second finding, because the combination is what matters. Complete response was not associated with disease-free survival in luminal B HER2-positive disease in the pooled German analysis, while it was strongly associated with it in non-luminal HER2-positive disease1. In this subtype the endpoint is both rarer and less informative.

The consequence falls on the post-neoadjuvant decision, where KATHERINE and DESTINY-Breast05 both assign escalation on the presence of residual invasive disease. Those two are the load-bearing trials for this section even though neither is in its table. KATHERINE randomised against trastuzumab, which is what the clinic would otherwise have given. Its survival update is the only mature overall survival result in the post-neoadjuvant record. DESTINY-Breast05 then moved the comparator to the older conjugate, in a population that also had to carry nodal disease at surgery or inoperable disease at diagnosis. They are a chain and not a disagreement. More triple-positive patients are routed into that chain by a trigger that carries less prognostic information than it does in receptor-negative disease.

That is not an argument against escalating, but an argument about what the trigger means. The distinction is the one made in Response-adapted and de-escalation designs. A response-adapted trial demonstrates that a population defined by residual disease benefits from a specific drug. It requires neither that the defining measure be prognostic nor that a regimen producing more complete responses produce longer survival.

Three things the table cannot show are worth naming. It has no column for the volume of residual disease, which is the axis on which this population actually differs. It has no column for what was given after surgery, and in most of these protocols that was decided by the specimen. And two of its most relevant rows cannot yet answer the question they were built for. DESTINY-Breast11 reports the most recent neoadjuvant response gain in the table on event-free survival data that are 4.5% mature. CompassHER2 pCR is the largest modern cohort in this population. Its primary endpoint is recurrence-free survival among the patients who reached a complete response. That endpoint has not reported.

The trials that produced these response rates did not test that. PHERGain and WSG TP-II both sent patients with residual disease on to chemotherapy. In WSG TP-II every patient also received dual HER2 blockade after surgery. A complete response inside such a design tells you the tumour responded. It does not tell you that the rest of the plan was unnecessary.

4 · Endocrine therapy alongside HER2 blockade in the adjuvant setting

Every patient in this chapter receives both treatments. The question is whether they should overlap, which no randomised trial in the adjuvant setting has answered.

The mechanistic case for overlapping them is the one in Reciprocal cross-talk and what it predicts for treatment. Oestrogen receptor signalling rises under anti-HER2 pressure42, so the months between finishing HER2-directed therapy and starting endocrine therapy are months in which the escape route is unopposed. On that reasoning the two should start as close together as the chemotherapy schedule allows.

The case against is practical rather than biological, because nobody has shown harm from concurrent administration and nobody has shown benefit either. Aromatase inhibitor arthralgia, tamoxifen symptoms and trastuzumab infusions accumulate in the same weeks. Most of the adherence that is ever lost is lost in that first year Adherence, toxicity, and the gap between prescribed and taken.

What the neoadjuvant trials contribute is limited but real. In WSG-ADAPT-TP, adding endocrine therapy to twelve weeks of trastuzumab emtansine changed the complete response rate hardly at all. The conjugate itself accounted for a large difference against trastuzumab with endocrine therapy24. In TBCRC 006, letrozole was given concurrently with dual HER2 blockade throughout, on an explicit hypothesis that blocking both receptors was required29. Neither trial isolates the contribution of timing, because neither randomised it.

Two practical points sit outside the trials. Endocrine therapy in this group runs long after HER2-directed therapy has finished. The late recurrence hazard in Late recurrence risk and surveillance implications is therefore the dominant long-term risk. Extended duration is decided on the same grounds as in HER2-negative disease Extended adjuvant therapy and the shape of the late benefit. And in the metastatic setting, receipt of endocrine therapy at all has been associated with better survival in this subtype in a real-world cohort44. That is an association in advanced disease and not a randomised comparison in early disease, but the direction is consistent with the mechanism.

In practice

Start endocrine therapy as soon as chemotherapy finishes. Give it alongside the remainder of the HER2-directed year rather than after it. There is no evidence of harm from the overlap, and the mechanism argues against leaving a gap.

Do not delay endocrine therapy for radiotherapy, since the two are routinely given together.

Treat the first year as the adherence problem it is. A patient managing infusions, radiotherapy and a new aromatase inhibitor at the same time is the patient most likely to stop the tablet.

Plan the endocrine duration conversation for the point at which HER2-directed therapy ends. That is when treatment appears to the patient to be finishing. It is also the point at which most of the remaining benefit has yet to be delivered.

5 · Chemotherapy omission and the de-escalation questions specific to this group

Early disease · HR+/HER2+ · neoadjuvant

Taking the chemotherapy out when the oestrogen receptor is also on

Does blocking the oestrogen receptor, HER2 and the cell cycle at once let the taxane be left out?

Rationale
Both receptors converge on cyclin D and CDK4/6. That node is where a tumour switching drivers has to pass. Blocking it as well as both receptors should close the escape route rather than leave it open. It should do so with no cytotoxic anywhere in the regimen.
Experiment
A single-arm study gave dual HER2 antibodies with a CDK4/6 inhibitor and a receptor degrader before surgery. Its coprimary readouts were the change in proliferation at two weeks and again at surgery, and the change in apoptosis by surgery. Response was only a secondary endpoint there. A randomised phase II trial then substituted a CDK4/6 inhibitor with an aromatase inhibitor for weekly paclitaxel, keeping the same two antibodies in both arms. Its primary question was neither of the two response rates. It asked whether a gene signature of E2F pathway activity predicted which arm a tumour would do better on.
Finding
Proliferation collapses within two weeks of starting the chemotherapy-free triplet. It has risen again by surgery without returning to baseline. Apoptosis fell over the same interval rather than rising, which is the opposite direction to the one the design expected. When the substitution was put against a taxane, the trial's own primary question came back negative. The signature did not identify an arm for a tumour to prefer. The two arms reached the same response rate, which is a descriptive comparison and not the thing the trial was built to test.
Limitation
The single-arm study had no control group. Its primary endpoint was the change in proliferation rather than response, so what it shows is that the drugs are reaching their target and not that the strategy suffices. Its first cohort was small, and several of the patients in it turned out on retrospective review not to be HER2-positive. The randomised trial was not designed as a non-inferiority comparison, so two response estimates that land in the same place are not evidence that the chemotherapy-free arm is as good as the taxane. Its population was postmenopausal and at the older end of the range, which is narrower than the group the strategy would be offered to. Its signature was read by sequencing a pretreatment biopsy, so a null interaction is a verdict on that assay as much as on the biology behind it.

Can HER2 blockade with endocrine therapy and no cytotoxic clear enough disease to leave the taxane out?

Rationale
Dual HER2 blockade eradicates a real fraction of tumours by itself. In receptor-positive disease endocrine therapy is on hand to hold the other pathway while it does. If the pair is enough for a substantial minority, then the taxane is being given to everyone in order to reach the rest.
Experiment
The trial that established neoadjuvant dual blockade carried an arm of the two antibodies with no chemotherapy at all. Dedicated studies followed. One was a single-arm window of an antibody with a tyrosine kinase inhibitor and concurrent oestrogen deprivation. A second randomised that same window for duration. A third gave a conjugate with or without endocrine therapy and set both against a bare antibody with endocrine therapy. Two trials then put a chemotherapy-free arm against a cytotoxic one, substituting a conjugate for docetaxel and carboplatin in the first and endocrine therapy for weekly paclitaxel in the second.
Finding
A chemotherapy-free window clears disease in a minority of receptor-positive tumours. It leaves low-volume residual disease in many more of them, and that residue concentrates in the receptor-positive group rather than the receptor-negative one. Longer exposure produced more complete responses than shorter exposure in the one trial that randomised duration. That gain sat in the receptor-positive subgroup. Adding endocrine therapy to a conjugate changed response not at all, while dropping the conjugate for a bare antibody with the same endocrine therapy cut response to a fraction of it. Against a cytotoxic comparator the chemotherapy-free arms lose response, by a little when a conjugate carries the payload and by a lot when nothing cytotoxic is given at all. The conjugate arm also carried the higher risk of an event-free survival event on longer follow-up. Severe adverse events were far less frequent without the cytotoxic in the trial that reported them.
Limitation
The duration trial combined a phase II design in its experimental arm with a pick-the-winner design and was not powered to compare the two durations, so its gap is a numerical difference with no test behind it. Its duration effect also sat in the receptor-positive subgroup of a trial that admitted any receptor status, which is a subgroup of a design that was not asking about receptors. Both windows used an antibody with a tyrosine kinase inhibitor, which is not the dual blockade a clinic gives today. The conjugate comparison and the endocrine comparison were both sized for response, so their survival curves are secondary readouts on samples chosen for a different endpoint. The interval around the endocrine trial's survival estimate is wide enough to hold very different truths.

Can the patients who can safely be spared chemotherapy be identified in advance, or from their early response?

Rationale
A chemotherapy-free strategy is only defensible if it is offered to tumours that will answer it. Two kinds of marker were proposed for that. One is read on the pretreatment biopsy and says what the tumour is. The other is read after a short exposure and says what the tumour does.
Experiment
On the baseline side, intrinsic subtype was called on pretreatment biopsies in two chemotherapy-free cohorts. HER2 was scored prospectively on biopsies taken from two separate sites of each tumour before a conjugate given with a second antibody and no chemotherapy. A signature of E2F pathway activity was tested as a formal treatment interaction in the randomised substitution trial. On the dynamic side, a fall in proliferation after two weeks of an aromatase inhibitor was used to route patients either to a chemotherapy-free regimen or to a taxane. Metabolic response on imaging was used the same way, with chemotherapy added only on failure to respond. Pathological response at surgery was used to assign what came after it, in a single-arm strategy trial and in a large response-adapted cohort.
Finding
Baseline biology separates the population. Tumours called HER2-enriched respond far more often than tumours that are not, both in a cohort of any receptor status and within the receptor-positive route of the trial that allocated on proliferation. Tumours whose HER2 scores as heterogeneous across the same specimen did not respond at all to a conjugate given without chemotherapy. The one marker tested as a treatment interaction rather than as a prognostic split, the E2F signature, showed no interaction with the treatment given. Early response separates the population too, although the route it sent molecular responders down cleared only a minority of them. The imaging-guided pathway that withholds chemotherapy from responders holds up on freedom from invasive disease against the threshold it was set.
Limitation
The subtype and heterogeneity results come from cohorts that were not randomised on the marker, so they say who responds and not who needed the chemotherapy that was withheld. Heterogeneity in particular is a sampling convention rather than a property of the disease. It is two biopsies from one tumour scored against a threshold, and only a small handful of tumours met that definition, so the contrast rests on a very small denominator. In the trial that routed on proliferation the two routes were given different treatments, so the higher response among the non-responders is the paclitaxel they were sent to and not a failure of the marker. The response-adapted trials measure their strategy against a fixed expectation rather than against the same patients treated the other way, and a patient who has declared herself a responder is by construction one who was going to do well.

This is the live de-escalation question. It is more live here than in receptor-negative disease for one reason. A triple-positive tumour has a second systemic therapy that works, which a receptor-negative one does not.

Seven trials in the table agree on one thing. A chemotherapy-free regimen clears disease in a minority of receptor-positive tumours, and that minority is real rather than anecdotal. They also agree, without having been designed to, on what governs the size of it. In WSG-ADAPT-TP the response rate tracked what carried the payload and not what was added alongside. Adding endocrine therapy to the conjugate changed the rate hardly at all, while replacing the conjugate with a bare antibody on the same endocrine therapy cut it to a fraction. TBCRC 023 points the same way from the other direction, since doubling the duration of chemotherapy-free dual blockade raised response in the receptor-positive subgroup rather than the receptor-negative one.

What separates these seven is evidentiary status, not drug. TBCRC 006 and PAMELA are single-arm windows. TBCRC 023 combined a phase II design in its experimental arm with a pick-the-winner design. Its two response rates landed in the same place, which is a description and not a demonstration of equivalence, because no non-inferiority margin was ever set.

NA-PHER2 is the trial a reader will not find in that table, and the reason is a convention worth stating. An evidence table carries the record a decision rests on, and a single-arm exploratory study is not part of that record. It has coprimary endpoints of the change in proliferation at two weeks and at surgery and the change in apoptosis. Proliferation collapsed within two weeks of the chemotherapy-free combination and had risen again by surgery. Apoptosis fell over the same interval rather than rising. That is a statement about target engagement and it belongs beside its question rather than beside a randomised phase III.

WSG TP-II is the trial to read closely, because its design explains both its result and its limits. Substituting endocrine therapy for the weekly taxane more than halved the complete response rate. Five-year overall survival was nonetheless close to complete in both arms. The invasive disease-free survival estimates did not follow the response gap either, on an interval around the taxane arm that runs from 55.6% to 100%. That width is a statement about the size of the trial rather than about the treatments. The reason the arms converge is in the protocol rather than in the biology. Chemotherapy was mandatory after residual disease, so most of the endocrine arm received it anyway. The trial is consistent with a response-guided strategy being safe. It does not demonstrate that chemotherapy can be omitted, which is a different claim.

A two-week Ki67 threshold and a PET metabolic response threshold are continuous measurements converted into a gate, and the reproducibility problem set out in Ki67, PEPI and response measures for a setting where pCR fails applies directly to the first. In practice

Outside a trial, chemotherapy remains part of treatment for node-positive or larger triple-positive tumours. Where it is omitted, say plainly that the supporting evidence is phase 2 and response-adapted.

The de-escalation with the strongest evidence is not omission of chemotherapy. It is omission of the anthracycline, which TRAIN-2 supports irrespective of hormone receptor status.

In a small, node-negative tumour the de-escalated regimens in De-escalated adjuvant therapy for small node-negative tumours apply to this group as they do to receptor-negative disease. The endocrine therapy that follows is the longer half of the treatment.

6 · Subgroup evidence from the HER2 trials, and its limits

Almost every statement made in this chapter rests on a hormone receptor subgroup of a trial powered for HER2-positive disease as a whole. That is worth saying explicitly rather than leaving implicit, and Clinical trials and evidence interpretation sets the standard this section is held to.

Start with what the design does well. Randomisation was never done on the receptor. It balanced everything else inside each receptor stratum, so a receptor subgroup of a randomised trial remains a randomised comparison of the treatments that trial gave. Some of these trials, ExteNET among them, stratified randomisation on hormone receptor status as well, which makes the strata better behaved still. Stratification supplies neither power nor a randomised comparison for the question of whether receptor status modifies the treatment effect. In none of the reported trials in this table did a receptor subgroup reverse the direction of the trial's own answer.

Five of the sixteen rows do not speak to the receptor at all, and saying so is part of reading the table. HERA, NSABP B-31/N9831 and BCIRG-006 asked whether adjuvant trastuzumab works. They answered it in the same direction in every subgroup they examined. ALTTO asked whether lapatinib adds to trastuzumab and found nothing that cleared its own revised threshold. That is a verdict on one tyrosine kinase inhibitor rather than on dual blockade, and the pertuzumab question was asked separately. CompassHER2 RD has not reported, and its eligibility is the subject of Unresolved questions and what reads out next.

The four duration trials look like the sharpest disagreement in the table and are the clearest case of divergence by design. PERSEPHONE declared non-inferiority for six months. PHARE tested the same six months and could not. Short-HER and SOLD tested nine weeks and could not either. The four used four different margins, a hazard ratio of 1.15, of 1.29 and of 1.3, and three percentage points of four-year disease-free survival. PHARE also randomised patients who were already several months into treatment rather than at the start. Four margins and two durations produce four answers about study design and one answer about trastuzumab. No shorter schedule has displaced the year.

ExteNET is the one place in the table where the receptor looks as though it matters. The benefit of extended neratinib concentrated in hormone receptor-positive disease, and a final analysis of that subgroup exists45. The mechanism section above supplies a reason to believe it. The trial's own eight-year overall survival comparison found nothing, in either direction, which is the endpoint this chapter is spending five to ten years of endocrine therapy on. A subgroup with a mechanism behind it is still a subgroup.

APHINITY is the instructive case for the general problem. The hormone receptor-positive and receptor-negative subgroup hazard ratios for adjuvant pertuzumab differ in point estimate and overlap substantially in interval46. The defensible reading is that nothing in those data distinguishes the two groups. The common reading, that pertuzumab works better in triple-positive disease, requires an interaction the trial did not demonstrate. The axis that did separate in that trial was nodal status rather than receptor status. A later exploratory subanalysis went further and located the largest benefit in a receptor-positive subset defined by HER2 expression47. A subanalysis of that kind generates a hypothesis rather than testing one.

Two rows carry a negative result that is more useful than it looks. KAITLIN substituted a conjugate for the taxane after an anthracycline in high-risk disease, and ATEMPT substituted it for the whole regimen in stage I disease. KAITLIN found no invasive disease-free survival difference against the taxane arm. ATEMPT was randomised three to one and was never sized to rank the two on outcome. On its other co-primary endpoint it failed to lower clinically relevant toxicity against the taxane comparator. Neither trial gave a reason to move. The same answer at both ends of the risk range is worth more than either trial alone. It is why APT rather than a conjugate remains the reference regimen for a small tumour.

The trial the decision rests on for a patient with residual disease is KATHERINE, for the same reason as in Lower pathological complete response rates and what that does to the post-neoadjuvant decision. It is the randomised comparison against what the clinic would otherwise do. It is also the only post-neoadjuvant trial in this table with a mature overall survival result. DESTINY-Breast05 raises the comparator and reports survival that is immature at its primary analysis.

Now the ceiling on the direct evidence. Four trials in the whole record enrolled early triple-positive disease and nothing else, and they enrolled 375, 207, 147 and 64 patients24,6,7,14. None of them is in this table, because all four are neoadjuvant. Every adjuvant and post-neoadjuvant decision in this chapter is therefore made on a subgroup.

The asymmetry that follows is worth naming. Escalation decisions in this group rest on large randomised trials whose populations included it, which is a reasonable basis. De-escalation decisions rest on small trials designed for it, which is a weaker basis for a stronger claim. Practice has been more willing to de-escalate on the second than to question the first.

Three columns this table will never have. None of these trials reports overall survival within the hormone receptor-positive stratum as a tested comparison. None randomised when endocrine therapy should start relative to the HER2-directed year, which is the decision Endocrine therapy alongside HER2 blockade in the adjuvant setting is about. And the duration trials read disease-free survival at four and five years, in a group whose recurrence hazard is still running at ten Late recurrence risk and surveillance implications.

Caution

A subgroup result does not become more reliable because the subgroup is large. The hormone receptor-positive subgroup of APHINITY is large enough that its estimate is precise. It is still not a randomised comparison between receptor-positive and receptor-negative disease. 7 · Unresolved questions and what reads out next

The largest gap is an absence rather than a pending readout.

CompassHER2 RD tests escalation of post-neoadjuvant treatment for residual HER2-positive disease. Its eligibility is restricted to high-risk residual disease, defined as oestrogen receptor-negative or node-positive11. Receptor-positive, node-negative residual disease is therefore outside it. That is a large part of the triple-positive residual population, and precisely the group in which Lower pathological complete response rates and what that does to the post-neoadjuvant decision argues that residual disease is least informative about risk. The trial that would settle what to do with them is not running.

The other two trials in the table were built to test the opposite move. Both will read out a recurrence endpoint at three years. CompassHER2 pCR asks whether a complete response after a taxane with dual blockade licenses stopping there, measured as recurrence-free survival among the patients who reached one. PHERGain-2 asks the same of a chemotherapy-free course in node-negative tumours of 5 to 30 mm, with a recurrence-free interval outstanding alongside a quality-of-life endpoint that has reported. Between them they will say what a complete response in this subtype is worth, which is the question Lower pathological complete response rates and what that does to the post-neoadjuvant decision shows nobody can currently answer.

Two further readouts will change something. Overall survival from DESTINY-Breast05 will show whether the invasive disease-free survival gain in residual disease converts into longer life. Its population was assembled on residual disease with nodal involvement, in which this chapter's group is over-represented for the reasons given in Lower pathological complete response rates and what that does to the post-neoadjuvant decision. And the adjuvant question raised by PATINA has no early-disease trial behind it. Whether CDK4/6 inhibition belongs in early triple-positive disease is unasked rather than unanswered CDK4/6 inhibition with HER2 blockade.

Two questions will probably never be answered directly. Concurrent against sequential endocrine therapy alongside HER2 blockade would need a large trial for a difference nobody expects to be large Endocrine therapy alongside HER2 blockade in the adjuvant setting. Chemotherapy omission in an unselected triple-positive population would need a non-inferiority trial whose margin would be hard to justify to a patient Designing de-escalation trials and the non-inferiority margin problem.

What a reader should take from that list is a calibration rather than a plan. This chapter describes the best-treated common subtype in breast cancer. It is also the one whose treatment rests most heavily on inference from populations assembled to answer a different question.
