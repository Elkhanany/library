Part X · Adjuvant and neoadjuvant therapy by subtype · Chapter 61

Early triple-negative disease

Where the survival gain outran the response gain, and what that did to the endpoint.

1 · Risk assessment and treatment selection

Selection here is driven by stage more than by biology, because no biological variable in routine use changes the regimen.

Stage II and stage III disease is treated with neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy, which is the population KEYNOTE-522 enrolled1. The evidence in Neoadjuvant chemotherapy with immunotherapy is confined to that population and does not extend below it.

Below stage II the evidence thins fast. The smallest node-negative tumours were excluded from the trials that define current practice, so treatment there is extrapolation from prognosis rather than from a randomised result. Node-negative triple-negative tumours with dense lymphocytic infiltration have a substantially better prognosis without chemotherapy than those without it2,3. That observation is prognostic. It has not been used prospectively to withhold treatment, and the scoring convention that makes it reproducible is in4.

Two things should happen at diagnosis in every patient regardless of stage.

Germline testing should be arranged before the systemic plan is fixed. A BRCA1 or BRCA2 pathogenic variant changes the post-neoadjuvant option Post-neoadjuvant systemic therapy, the surgical conversation and the family's testing, and the strategy is set out in Inherited susceptibility and familial breast cancer.

And the neoadjuvant decision should be taken deliberately rather than by default. If the tumour is large enough or node-positive enough that residual disease would trigger a different adjuvant regimen, treating first is what makes that regimen available.

2 · Neoadjuvant chemotherapy with immunotherapy

KEYNOTE-522 randomised 1,174 patients with previously untreated stage II or III disease in a 2:1 ratio. All received neoadjuvant paclitaxel and carboplatin followed by an anthracycline-containing regimen, with pembrolizumab or placebo throughout and for up to nine adjuvant cycles after surgery. There was no PD-L1 eligibility criterion.

The three endpoints reported in sequence are the substance of this chapter.

Pathological complete response among the first 602 patients randomised was 64.8% with pembrolizumab and chemotherapy, with a 95% confidence interval of 59.9 to 69.5. It was 51.2% with chemotherapy alone, with an interval of 44.1 to 58.3. The estimated treatment difference was 13.6 percentage points, with an interval of 5.4 to 21.81.

Event-free survival at 36 months was 84.5% with pembrolizumab and chemotherapy, with an interval of 81.7 to 86.9, against 76.8% with chemotherapy alone, with an interval of 72.2 to 80.7. The hazard ratio for event or death was 0.63, with an interval of 0.48 to 0.825.

Overall survival at 60 months, at a median follow-up of 75.1 months, was 86.6% with pembrolizumab and chemotherapy, with an interval of 84.0 to 88.8. It was 81.7% with chemotherapy alone, with an interval of 77.5 to 85.2 and a p value of 0.0026.

That is a mortality benefit of roughly five percentage points at five years in a curable population, and it is the reason this regimen is standard. It is also the single result in this book that does most damage to pathological complete response as a surrogate endpoint, and Pathologic response and residual disease adaptation takes that up.

A second trial supplies the dissociation in cleaner form. GeparNuevo randomised 174 patients to durvalumab or placebo added to nab-paclitaxel followed by dose-dense epirubicin and cyclophosphamide. Durvalumab was not continued after surgery. Pathological complete response was 53.4% with durvalumab against 44.2% with placebo, and the difference was not significant, with a p value of 0.2877. Three-year outcomes diverged anyway. Invasive disease-free survival was 85.6% against 77.2%, with a hazard ratio of 0.48 and an interval of 0.24 to 0.97. Overall survival was 95.2% against 83.5%, with a hazard ratio of 0.24 and an interval of 0.08 to 0.728. At a median follow-up of 86.4 months the hazard ratio for overall survival was 0.33, with an interval of 0.14 to 0.799.

A third neoadjuvant trial raised response without yet establishing survival. Atezolizumab added to nab-paclitaxel and anthracycline-based chemotherapy produced a pathological complete response in 95 of 165 patients, which is 58%, against 69 of 168, which is 41%. The rate difference was 17 percentage points, with a 95% confidence interval of 6 to 2710.

The class effect is not automatic. NSABP B-59/GeparDouze randomised 773 patients to atezolizumab and 777 to placebo, added to sequential taxane, carboplatin and anthracycline-based neoadjuvant chemotherapy in stage II to III disease. The primary endpoint of event-free survival was not met, with a hazard ratio of 0.80, a stratified log-rank p value of 0.083 and a four-year event-free survival difference of 3.3 percentage points. The hazard ratio for overall survival was 0.86, with an interval of 0.62 to 1.19 and a four-year benefit of 0.7 percentage points11.

And timing appears to matter more than the agent. ALEXANDRA/IMpassion030 gave atezolizumab with adjuvant chemotherapy and no neoadjuvant phase, and was stopped for futility12. The argument that an intact primary and its draining nodes are the compartment where priming happens is developed in Why response rates in breast cancer sit where they do.

Caution

Three trials of the same class in the same disease gave a survival benefit, a missed primary endpoint and a futility stop. The differences include the agent, the chemotherapy backbone, whether the tumour was in place, and whether treatment continued after surgery. None of those variables was isolated. Reading any one of these results as a statement about checkpoint blockade in triple-negative breast cancer, rather than about the regimen actually tested, overstates what the evidence supports.

3 · Platinum agents, BRCA status, and repair-directed selection

The platinum question in early disease has been overtaken rather than answered.

The mechanistic case is clear and it comes from the metastatic setting. In a randomised comparison of carboplatin with docetaxel in 376 patients with unselected advanced triple-negative disease, the objective response rate was 31.4% against 34.0%, with a p value of 0.66. Among patients with germline BRCA1 or BRCA2 variants, carboplatin produced double the response rate of docetaxel, at 68% against 33%, with a biomarker-by-treatment interaction p value of 0.0113.

The same study is the more useful one for what does not work as a selector. Benefit from platinum was not seen in patients selected by BRCA1 methylation, by low BRCA1 messenger RNA, or by a high score on a commercial homologous recombination deficiency assay13. A germline mutation predicted platinum sensitivity. The assays intended to capture the same phenotype did not.

In early disease the question is now structural rather than empirical. Carboplatin is part of the KEYNOTE-522 backbone, given to both arms1. The trial therefore cannot estimate the contribution of platinum, and no current trial isolates it in the presence of checkpoint blockade. Platinum in stage II to III disease is not a separate decision. It is part of the regimen that was tested.

The biology of homologous recombination deficiency and the difference between a germline mutation, a functional deficiency and a genomic scar are developed in DNA repair, replication stress, and genomic instability, and the assay landscape in DNA repair and homologous recombination deficiency assays. The practical position for now is that germline testing changes management and somatic repair assays do not.

4 · Pathologic response and residual disease adaptation

Pathological complete response in this subtype is the strongest patient-level prognostic marker in breast oncology, and it is not a valid trial-level surrogate. Both statements come from the same analysis and both are true.

One pooled analysis covered 12 trials and 11,955 patients. Among those with triple-negative disease, eradication of tumour from breast and nodes carried an event-free survival hazard ratio of 0.24, with a 95% confidence interval of 0.18 to 0.33. The overall survival hazard ratio was 0.16, with an interval of 0.11 to 0.2514. The trial-level analysis of the same dataset ran the other way. The increase in pathological complete response frequency between treatment groups explained almost none of the variation in event-free survival, with a coefficient of determination of 0.03. For overall survival that coefficient was 0.2414.

The GeparNuevo result is what that failure looks like in a single trial. A non-significant pathological complete response difference of 9.2 percentage points accompanied an overall survival hazard ratio of 0.24 at three years7,8. The authors' multivariable analysis found a durvalumab effect independent of the response effect, and in the group that did not reach a complete response, three-year invasive disease-free survival was 76.3% against 69.7%8. Benefit accrued in the patients whose tumours were not eradicated, which is precisely the population a response-mediated mechanism cannot reach.

Residual disease is not one prognosis. It is a spectrum, which is why Residual cancer burden and the continuous measures in Residual cancer burden and continuous response measures carry more information than a binary result. Immune context adds a further axis. Among 39 evaluable patients with residual disease in GeparNuevo, estimated seven-year invasive disease-free survival was 92.3% where stromal tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes exceeded 10%, with a confidence interval of 56.6 to 98.9. Where they did not, it was 51.4%, with an interval of 29.2 to 69.79. The numbers are small and the intervals are wide. The direction is large enough to be worth measuring.

In practice

Report response as breast and nodes together. Eradication from the breast alone carries a weaker association with outcome than eradication from both14.

Use response to assign the next treatment, not to evaluate the last one. Assigning on observed response is what Post-neoadjuvant systemic therapy rests on, and it is a patient-level use that survives the surrogacy failure.

When a regimen raises pathological complete response and has no mature survival data, say that the survival question is open. The trial-level correlation between the two is close to nothing14.

Ask for stromal lymphocyte scoring on the residual disease specimen where it is available. It is prognostic and it costs a slide9,4.

5 · Post-neoadjuvant systemic therapy

Residual invasive disease after neoadjuvant treatment is the highest-risk state in early triple-negative breast cancer, and two randomised options exist.

CREATE-X randomised 910 patients with HER2-negative residual invasive disease after anthracycline, taxane or both to capecitabine or to no additional chemotherapy. Five-year disease-free survival was 74.1% with capecitabine and 67.6% without, with a hazard ratio of 0.70 and a 95% confidence interval of 0.53 to 0.92. Five-year overall survival was 89.2% against 83.6%, with a hazard ratio of 0.59 and an interval of 0.39 to 0.90. In the triple-negative subgroup, disease-free survival was 69.8% against 56.1%, with a hazard ratio of 0.58, and overall survival was 78.8% against 70.3%, with a hazard ratio of 0.52. Hand-foot syndrome occurred in 73.4% of patients on capecitabine15.

The control arm of that trial received neither carboplatin nor pembrolizumab. Its residual disease was therefore selected by a weaker regimen than the one used today, and the incremental value of capecitabine after a platinum-containing chemoimmunotherapy backbone has not been measured.

OlympiA randomised 1,836 patients with germline BRCA1 or BRCA2 pathogenic variants and high-risk HER2-negative early breast cancer to one year of olaparib or placebo16. At a median follow-up of 6.1 years the hazard ratio for invasive disease-free survival was 0.65, with a 95% confidence interval of 0.53 to 0.78. For overall survival it was 0.72, with an interval of 0.56 to 0.93. Six-year overall survival was 87.5% with olaparib and 83.2% with placebo, a difference of 4.4 percentage points17.

Caution

Olaparib and capecitabine have never been compared. The preference for olaparib in a germline carrier rests on a larger randomised population, a longer follow-up, a placebo control and a contemporary chemotherapy background. Those are reasons to prefer the evidence, not evidence of superiority. A patient told that olaparib is better should be told the comparison does not exist.

The third post-neoadjuvant question is whether the adjuvant pembrolizumab cycles do anything. KEYNOTE-522 gave up to nine adjuvant cycles to every patient, including those with a pathological complete response, so the trial cannot separate the neoadjuvant from the adjuvant contribution1. GeparNuevo gave no adjuvant checkpoint blockade at all and still showed a survival benefit8,9. OptimICE-pCR is testing omission after a complete response directly.

Molecular residual disease detection would in principle identify who needs post-neoadjuvant escalation. Whether acting on a positive result improves outcome is unresolved, and the evidence including the ctDNA-guided intervention trials is in Liquid biopsy and longitudinal monitoring.

6 · Immune-related toxicity in a curative-intent population

The calculation that governs immune toxicity here is not the one used in metastatic disease.

In KEYNOTE-522, 51.2% of patients in the control arm reached a pathological complete response without pembrolizumab1. A permanent endocrinopathy in a patient who would have been cured by chemotherapy alone is a different event from the same endocrinopathy in someone with incurable disease. The trial cannot identify which patients those were, which is the whole difficulty.

The rates are substantial and they are attributable. In NSABP B-59/GeparDouze, immune-related adverse events occurred in 27.6% of patients receiving atezolizumab and 11.4% receiving placebo. Grade 3 or higher treatment-emergent adverse events occurred in 75.3% against 73.4%, so almost the entire difference between the arms was immune-mediated rather than cytotoxic. Discontinuation in the neoadjuvant phase occurred in 25.5% of the atezolizumab group against 18.8% of the placebo group11. In GeparNuevo, thyroid dysfunction of any grade was the commonest immune-related event, in 47% of patients7.

Three structural features of these toxicities, developed in Immune-related toxicity and its management, matter most in this setting. Onset is often delayed and is not dose-related. Reversibility differs by organ, and endocrinopathy frequently does not reverse. And the same numerical grade carries different urgency in different organs, which is why the management algorithms branch by organ18.

In practice

Hold the immunotherapy and continue the chemotherapy when an immune-related event is suspected. Those are separable decisions and treating them as one costs the patient the cytotoxic backbone that most of the curative effect came from.

Check thyroid function before every cycle. It is the commonest event and the cheapest to find7.

Steroids for a confirmed grade 2 or higher event, by the organ-specific algorithm, rather than by grade alone18.

Say before the first dose that a minority of patients acquire a permanent endocrine deficiency, and that around half of patients in the control arm of the defining trial were cured without the drug1. That is the honest frame for consent in a curative setting.

Surveillance does not stop at the last dose. Events occur months after treatment ends, so the handover to primary care has to carry that instruction explicitly Rehabilitation and survivorship.

7 · De-escalation questions in the immunotherapy era

Three de-escalation questions are live. Each must clear the margin problem set out in Clinical trials and evidence interpretation, and specifically in Designing de-escalation trials and the non-inferiority margin problem, before it changes practice.

The first is whether the anthracycline can be removed. In NeoPACT, 115 patients with stage I to III disease received carboplatin, docetaxel and pembrolizumab for six cycles with no anthracycline. Pathological complete response was 58%, with a 95% confidence interval of 48 to 67, and the combined rate of residual cancer burden 0 or 1 was 69%, with an interval of 60 to 78. Grade 3 or higher immune-mediated adverse events occurred in 3.5%. Estimated three-year event-free survival was 86% overall, 98% in the group with a complete response and 68% in the group without19. SCARLET tests that regimen against the anthracycline-containing standard.

The second is whether adjuvant pembrolizumab adds anything after a complete response. OptimICE-pCR addresses it directly, and the reason the question is answerable at all is that GeparNuevo achieved a survival benefit with no adjuvant component8.

The third is whether any patient can omit checkpoint blockade entirely. That requires a predictive biomarker and The missing predictive biomarker explains why there is not one.

Caution

De-escalation in this disease is harder to prove than to propose. The cure rate is high, the events are relatively few, and a non-inferiority trial with a tolerable margin therefore needs either a very large sample or very long follow-up. NeoPACT is a single-arm phase 2 study of 115 patients reporting a response rate19. That is a reason to run SCARLET, not a reason to change practice before it reports.

8 · The missing predictive biomarker

Everyone with stage II or III triple-negative breast cancer receives checkpoint blockade because no test identifies who will benefit. That is a statement about measurement, not about biology.

PD-L1 does not select in this setting. KEYNOTE-522 enrolled without a PD-L1 criterion and established benefit in the unselected population1,6. In metastatic disease the same class works only above a combined positive score of 10, and loosens to nothing below it20. The assays themselves are not interchangeable, because the combined positive score and the immune cell score count different cells over different denominators, and that argument is in Predictive biomarkers and patient selection.

Tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes are prognostic and reproducibly scored, and they are not established as predictive of checkpoint benefit21,3,4. Prognostic and predictive are different properties, and the difference is defined in Analytical validity, clinical validity, and clinical utility defined.

Tumour mutational burden carries tumour-agnostic evidence from trials that included no breast cohort22, and a high burden is uncommon in this disease23. The eligible population is small before the question of whether the inference transfers is reached.

Two exploratory signals from the negative trial are worth naming without overreading. In NSABP B-59/GeparDouze, event-free survival appeared heterogeneous across subgroups, with benefit in patients presenting with clinical lymph node involvement and an interaction p value of 0.039. A messenger RNA-based subset analysis suggested benefit in basal-like immune-activated tumours11. Both are post hoc analyses of a trial that missed its primary endpoint.

Interplay

The reason no single marker predicts here is the reason Part VI gives for most biomarker failure in this disease. Immune response is a property of an organisation rather than of a molecule, and the spatial phenotypes in Inflamed, excluded, and desert phenotypes separate patients that any percentage computed on bulk tissue cannot. PD-L1 scored on a block reports abundance and discards arrangement, exactly as a HER2 percentage does in Spatial patterns, clustered, mosaic, and scattered. Immune pressure also selects among a mixed population in the way drug pressure does, so the compartment that presents antigen is removed first and the residual disease is enriched for the compartment that does not. That is why a marker measured before treatment cannot describe the tumour that treatment leaves behind.

See Integrative biological interplay

The honest close is narrow. Checkpoint blockade added to neoadjuvant chemotherapy improves survival in stage II and III disease6. The benefit is not uniform, since a large trial of the same class missed its endpoint11. No assay in clinical use identifies who is in which group. Until one exists, the field will keep treating everyone, and the permanent toxicities in Immune-related toxicity in a curative-intent population will keep falling on patients who were going to be cured anyway.
