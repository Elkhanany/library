Conjugates and chemotherapy in later-line hormone receptor-positive disease

Why four conjugate trials against physician's choice chemotherapy still leave the order of giving them unmeasured.

1 · Scope, and the point at which endocrine therapy is exhausted

This chapter covers what happens after endocrine-based therapy stops working in metastatic hormone receptor-positive, HER2-negative disease. It covers the three antibody-drug conjugates with randomised evidence in this population, the sequencing problem they create, and the place single agent chemotherapy still holds.

Endocrine therapy itself is in Endocrine therapy and receptor degraders in metastatic disease and CDK4/6 inhibition in CDK4/6 inhibition in metastatic disease, combinations and sequencing. Conjugate design, payload chemistry and resistance mechanism are in Antibody-drug conjugates and targeted delivery and are cited here rather than restated. Chemotherapy agent detail is in Cytotoxic chemotherapy. The visceral crisis decision is in Rapid progression and visceral crisis.

The phrase endocrine exhausted is a clinical judgment and not a test result. It is worth being explicit about what it usually means in practice. A patient has progressed on an aromatase inhibitor with a CDK4/6 inhibitor, then on at least one further endocrine-based line chosen by biomarker, and no remaining endocrine option matches a detectable alteration. Or the disease is moving fast enough that waiting to find out is not safe.

Both halves of that definition are soft. The first depends on which alterations were tested for and when. The second depends on a judgment about tempo that no scan reports.

Where a section carries a clinical question from the [Chapter Stories](stories.html) map, that question opens it. The story says what was being looked for and what the design cannot settle. A table says what else is in the cell. The prose argues the decision.

Four of those rows are the same experiment. A conjugate was randomised against the physician's choice of single-agent chemotherapy in disease out of endocrine options, and each lengthened the time to progression. That is the only claim the table supports as a class. Everything else in it varies. None of the variation is between one conjugate and another.

The first difference is where the patient was standing when she entered. Eligibility is written in units of prior chemotherapy rather than in units of biology. TROPiCS-02 enrolled the deepest population of the four and DESTINY-Breast06 the shallowest, with DESTINY-Breast04 and TROPION-Breast01 between them. Read down the population column and the four trials line up as a ladder. That ordering is an artefact of protocol writing rather than a tested sequence, and stacking the medians would compare populations rather than drugs.

The second difference is one of measurement. Two of the four required an assay result for the antibody's own target, and two required nothing of the kind. Trop-2 expression was never an entry criterion, so those trials enrolled whoever qualified on treatment history alone. The HER2-low trials enrolled on a staining category, which is a threshold on a continuous stain rather than a biological boundary HER2-low and ultralow reframed as a heterogeneity problem. A patient's eligibility moves with the assay in one pair of trials and not in the other.

The third difference is the endpoint. Only one of the four named overall survival a primary endpoint. That is the trial that failed it. Two of the others reported survival separately and one has not, because follow-up was short when the primary analysis was done. A blank survival cell and a tested negative survival result are different objects. The column cannot tell them apart.

The three remaining rows answer other questions and should not be read as further conjugate comparisons. SACI-IO HR+ randomised the addition of a checkpoint inhibitor to a conjugate rather than a conjugate against chemotherapy, and belongs to Unresolved questions and what reads out next. DAISY and ICARUS-BREAST01 are single-arm. One maps activity across the HER2 staining range, and the other carries the same payload class to a third antigen.

Two comparisons are missing. Neither can be supplied by longer follow-up. No row sets one conjugate against another. And no trial here randomised the decision that precedes everything in the table, which is whether to leave endocrine-based therapy at all. Every patient in every row had already been judged ready for a cytotoxic by the clinician who enrolled her.

2 · Sacituzumab govitecan in hormone receptor-positive disease

TROPiCS-02 is where this drug's position rests. The reason is the depth of what it randomised. Every patient had received endocrine therapy, a taxane and a CDK4/6 inhibitor, together with two to four prior chemotherapy regimens for metastatic disease8. The control arm was the menu of single agents such a patient would otherwise have been given.

The result to hold is that survival moved and not only the time to progression6. Overall survival gains are rare this far into the disease, which is why the trial changed practice despite absolute medians measured in months. Set a patient's expectations from those medians rather than from the hazard ratio.

Trop-2 expression did not select the responders. The survival benefit was consistent across expression subgroups measured by immunohistochemistry6. That sits awkwardly beside the biomarker analysis of the same drug in triple-negative disease, where progression-free survival tracked a Trop-2 expression gradient9. The two findings are usually reported as if they conflict.

They may not. The triple-negative analysis graded progression-free survival across three expression bands in a subset of assessable patients, and it was exploratory9. The hormone receptor-positive analysis reported overall survival by subgroup6. Different endpoint, different population, different analysis plan. The safest reading is that Trop-2 staining has never been shown to identify a group that should not receive the drug, which is the only question a clinician needs answered.

The toxicity is predictable and is managed rather than avoided. Neutropenia and diarrhoea dominate, and both are payload effects rather than target effects Toxicity mechanisms, interstitial lung disease, ocular, and mucosal. UGT1A1 genotype affects SN-38 clearance and is worth knowing before the first dose in a patient with a suggestive history.

What the trial cannot support is the use most clinics now want. Its population had exhausted several cytotoxic lines, so nothing in it establishes the drug ahead of the first of them. That question is being asked prospectively and is taken up in Unresolved questions and what reads out next.

3 · Trastuzumab deruxtecan in HER2-low and HER2-ultralow disease

Two randomised trials moved this drug progressively earlier in the same population, and the second also moved the eligibility boundary. DESTINY-Breast04 enrolled patients with HER2-low disease after one or two prior chemotherapy lines, and the large majority had hormone receptor-positive disease7. DESTINY-Breast06 enrolled after endocrine-based therapy and before any chemotherapy for metastatic disease, and it added a HER2-ultralow cohort4.

They agree on the thing that matters. Against the physician's choice of chemotherapy, at two different depths of prior treatment, the conjugate delayed progression. Where they part is on survival. That is a difference of maturity rather than of effect. The earlier trial has reported survival with long follow-up10. The later one had too few events at its primary analysis to say anything, which is what a trial reads out when it is moved forward into a population that lives longer.

The eligibility boundary is an assay boundary rather than a biological one. That argument is made in full in HER2-low and ultralow reframed as a heterogeneity problem and Antigen thresholds as eligibility criteria, the general lesson. Its practical form here is uncomfortable. HER2-ultralow means immunohistochemistry 0 with faint incomplete membrane staining in some cells, which is a category defined at the least reproducible end of the assay. Pathologist concordance is poorest exactly there, as Where heterogeneity sets a ceiling on precision medicine sets out. A patient's eligibility can therefore depend on who read the slide.

DAISY argues that the boundary is arbitrary from the other direction. It gave the same conjugate across three staining cohorts and confirmed responses occurred well above zero in immunohistochemistry 0 disease5. Within that cohort, response did not track the underlying ERBB2 transcript level either. Activity does not stop where the scoring category does. The study is single-arm, so it cannot say whether those patients would have done as well on chemotherapy, and the randomised ultralow evidence remains an exploratory cohort of one trial.

Caution

DESTINY-Breast06 is often read as showing that a conjugate should replace endocrine therapy once resistance appears. It shows something narrower. Its comparator was chemotherapy, and every patient enrolled had already been judged ready for chemotherapy. The trial therefore establishes that if the next line is cytotoxic, the conjugate is the better cytotoxic choice. It does not establish when to leave endocrine-based therapy, which is the decision that actually precedes it Endocrine therapy and receptor degraders in metastatic disease.

Interstitial lung disease is the toxicity that should shape the decision, because it is the one that can be fatal. Adjudicated interstitial lung disease or pneumonitis occurred in 11.3% of patients given the conjugate in DESTINY-Breast06 against 0.2% of those given chemotherapy4. In a patient with pre-existing lung disease, a low baseline oxygen saturation or prior thoracic radiotherapy, that asymmetry is a reason to think carefully about ordering. Surveillance and management are in Toxicity mechanisms, interstitial lung disease, ocular, and mucosal, and the difference between a grade 1 and a grade 5 event is mostly how early it was found.

4 · Datopotamab deruxtecan and the TROP2 question

TROPION-Breast01 carried two primary endpoints, progression-free survival and overall survival. It met the first and missed the second. The final survival analysis gave a hazard ratio for death of 1.01, with a confidence interval from 0.83 to 1.2211.

That combination is worth separating from the more common pattern of an unpowered secondary endpoint failing to confirm a progression-free survival result. Here survival was a stated primary endpoint that the trial was designed and powered to test. The trial answered its own question and the answer was no.

Three explanations have been offered and none has been excluded. Subsequent conjugate use was imbalanced, at 12.3% in the datopotamab arm against 24.0% in the chemotherapy arm, which would favour the control arm after progression11. Progression-free survival was assessed by blinded central review in an open-label trial, where the timing of scans and the decision to stop treatment are not blinded. Or the drug may delay progression in this setting without extending life.

The target itself does not resolve the question. Trop-2 is an address rather than a driver, chosen because it is differentially expressed on epithelium and not because tumours depend on it Antibody, linker, and payload design. Normal tissue expression is what limits the dose, which is why the dominant toxicities are mucosal and ocular rather than marrow-based. Two conjugates against the same antigen carrying the same payload class still differ in dominant toxicity. Linker chemistry and normal tissue handling separate them rather than the target Toxicity mechanisms, interstitial lung disease, ocular, and mucosal.

The practical consequence is a ranking argument rather than a prohibition. Where more than one conjugate is available and the disease is hormone receptor-positive, an agent that met a survival endpoint has a stronger claim than one that missed it. That inference crosses two trials with different entry criteria and different comparator menus. It should be labelled as such. Nobody has randomised the two drugs against each other, and no trial has selected patients for either one by Trop-2 staining.

5 · Sequencing conjugates and the cross-resistance problem

Advanced disease · HR+/HER2- · later

Antibody-drug conjugates after endocrine therapy

Does a second antibody-drug conjugate work after a first one has failed?

Rationale
The conjugates used here share a payload mechanism while differing in the antibody. If resistance is to the payload, changing the antibody buys nothing. If it is to the target, to its internalisation or to trafficking inside the cell, a different address might still work. Patients now arrive in later lines having already had a conjugate. The question has stopped being theoretical.
Experiment
A HER3-directed conjugate was given as a single arm after progression on CDK4/6 inhibition and a line of chemotherapy. Paired baseline and on-treatment biopsies were taken, to see how far the drug distributed inside the tumour and what the tumour did in response. A prospective study is now assigning a second conjugate after a first, in separate cohorts defined by which conjugate came before.
Finding
Redirecting the payload to a third antigen met its response endpoint in a population already treated with CDK4/6 inhibition and chemotherapy. The on-treatment tissue work associated efficacy with how far the conjugate distributed within the tumour and with an interferon response. At baseline the associations ran to where the receptor sat in the tissue and to the absence of an ESR1 mutation, with receptor level itself tracking only the progression endpoint.
Limitation
The HER3 study is single-arm. Its response rate has nothing to be read against and cannot rank HER3 delivery against another conjugate or against chemotherapy. That response is reported with a narrower confidence interval than the randomised trials beside it use, which is what a single-arm design of this kind specifies and not a sign of greater precision. The on-treatment biopsies come only from patients who stayed on the drug long enough to be rebiopsied. That is selection on the very outcome the biomarkers are being asked to explain. The authors state that every association is pending validation. The prospective sequencing study is still running. Its cohorts are non-comparative by design. It will report activity after a conjugate rather than the value of a sequence.

ICARUS-BREAST01 TRADE-DXd

Three conjugates are now used in this population. They engage two antigens and they all carry a topoisomerase I inhibitor payload. That is the whole problem in one sentence.

A fourth antibody makes the point sharper rather than softer. ICARUS-BREAST01 took a HER3-directed conjugate into patients who had progressed on a CDK4/6 inhibitor and one line of chemotherapy, and met its response endpoint2. Its target is a third antigen. Its payload is the same deruxtecan class as two of the three agents already in use. Adding antigens does not add payload classes. Payload class is what the preclinical work in Mechanisms of conjugate resistance shows carrying cross-resistance.

That response rate has nothing to be read against. The study is single-arm, so it cannot rank HER3 delivery against another conjugate or against the chemotherapy those patients would otherwise have had. Its interval is reported at 90% rather than 95%, which is what a single-arm design of this kind specifies and not a sign of greater precision. Comparing it with the intervals in the randomised rows above is comparing two different constructions.

Its correlative work is the more interesting part. It also carries a selection problem worth naming. Baseline associations ran to where the receptor sat in the tissue and to the absence of an ESR1 mutation, while receptor level itself tracked only the progression endpoint2. The on-treatment associations ran to how far the conjugate distributed inside the tumour and to an interferon response. Those came from biopsies taken only in patients who stayed on the drug long enough to be rebiopsied. That is selection on the very outcome the biomarkers are being asked to explain. The authors state that all of it awaits validation.

The mechanistic case for expecting cross-resistance, and the preclinical work separating target loss from payload resistance and from transporter change, is set out in Payload class, cross-resistance, and sequencing between conjugates and Mechanisms of conjugate resistance. Three findings from there govern this section and are not repeated. Resistance can arise at any of six steps. Only the first of those steps is measured by any assay in clinical use. And payload class, rather than the conjugate as a unit, is what preclinical models show carrying the cross-resistance.

The clinical evidence for a second conjugate is a retrospective series of 85 patients who received two in sequence, described in Payload class, cross-resistance, and sequencing between conjugates12. It is consistent with partial rather than complete cross-resistance. It is also 85 patients treated off protocol, which is the best evidence available for a decision made many times a week. TRADE-DXd is the first prospective test and it has not reported. Its cohorts are defined by which conjugate came first and are non-comparative by design, so it will estimate how much activity survives a conjugate rather than rank one order against another.

What follows for practice is a rationale and not a result, and the difference should be stated to patients. HER2-low or ultralow status determines whether trastuzumab deruxtecan is an option at all, so it is worth establishing before the conjugate decision rather than during it. Trials enrolled these agents at different depths of prior treatment, so the trial populations imply an ordering even though no trial compared orders. And a patient who progressed quickly on the first conjugate has, on the retrospective data, a poor prospect from the second.

Interplay

This is the clearest clinical instance of the ceiling argued in Where heterogeneity sets a ceiling on precision medicine. The decision is made on an antigen measurement taken from one block at one time, and that measurement answers only whether the antibody can bind. It says nothing about internalisation, lysosomal transport, efflux or repair capacity. A proportion of what is recorded here as conjugate failure is therefore allocation failure, and no improvement in the payload addresses it. Raising the ceiling requires a different kind of measurement rather than a better drug.

6 · Single-agent chemotherapy and when it remains the right choice

Advanced disease · HR+/HER2- · later

Antibody-drug conjugates after endocrine therapy

Once endocrine therapy is exhausted, does an antibody-drug conjugate beat the single-agent chemotherapy a clinic would otherwise give?

Rationale
The payload inside these conjugates is an ordinary cytotoxic. What the antibody changes is where that payload is delivered, not what it does to a dividing cell. The question was therefore whether a better address buys enough tumour exposure to beat giving the same class of drug systemically and accepting the toxicity that follows.
Experiment
Three randomised phase III trials gave a conjugate against physician's choice of single-agent chemotherapy in disease that was hormone receptor-positive and out of endocrine options. They do not all address the same antigen. They also set different requirements for how much chemotherapy a patient had to have had before entry. The control arm in each was drawn from the same short menu of single agents. That menu had been assembled by earlier trials that set those agents against physician's choice and against one another.
Finding
Each conjugate lengthened the time to progression against physician's choice chemotherapy. Overall survival did not follow in every case. In the trial that made progression-free survival and overall survival both primary, progression-free survival moved against chemotherapy. Overall survival did not.
Limitation
Physician's choice is a composite control rather than a drug. The trials that assembled that menu did not find its members interchangeable in the way the phrase implies. One of them showed a single agent lengthening overall survival against physician's choice in heavily pretreated disease. Another set one menu agent against another and separated them on neither co-primary endpoint. So the comparator is an average of agents whose relative merit was never settled, rather than the drug a particular clinician would have chosen for a particular patient. The three conjugate trials also demanded different amounts of prior chemotherapy before entry. They cannot be stacked into a sequence or read as a ranking. The dual-primary trial is the standing warning here, because it shows that a progression-free survival gain against chemotherapy in this setting does not convert into a survival gain on its own.

That table holds four questions rather than one. Reading it as a ranking of cytotoxics misreads all four. Two rows ask whether one named single agent beats another. One asks whether a single agent beats the menu a physician would otherwise have chosen from. Three ask whether endocrine therapy with a CDK4/6 inhibitor can be given instead of chemotherapy at all. One adds a PARP inhibitor to a chemotherapy backbone and belongs to the table below as much as to this one.

On the first question the answer is consistently negative. That is the finding to carry. Study 301 compared eribulin with capecitabine directly and separated them on neither co-primary endpoint18. CALGB 40502 / NCCTG N063H set nab-paclitaxel and ixabepilone against weekly paclitaxel in the first line, and neither experimental arm beat it17. The ixabepilone arm was clearly the worse of the two. Within the menu of single agents there is no demonstrated best drug. Choice is therefore driven by prior exposure, residual neuropathy, marrow reserve, renal and hepatic function, route and schedule. Those are patient variables, not trial variables.

EMBRACE looks like an exception and is not one. It showed that a single cytotoxic agent can extend survival after several previous chemotherapy regimens19. That was not the expectation at the time. It remains the reason eribulin holds its position. The difference from Study 301 is design rather than drug. EMBRACE set eribulin against a composite of whatever the physician would otherwise have chosen, with overall survival as the sole primary endpoint, in a deeply pretreated population. Study 301 set it against one named agent, earlier in the disease, with two co-primary endpoints to satisfy. Beating an average of agents is not the same test as beating a particular one.

The substitution question divides on design too. Young-PEARL / KCSG-BR15-10 and PEARL both compared palbociclib with endocrine therapy against capecitabine, and they disagree on progression-free survival16,15. The populations are not the same. One enrolled premenopausal women progressing on tamoxifen and added ovarian suppression, at phase 2 with a one-sided test. The other enrolled postmenopausal women resistant to an aromatase inhibitor, at phase 3, with co-primary endpoints in a cohort and in an ESR1 wild-type subset. They agree on the endpoint that does not depend on a scan schedule. Neither showed an overall survival difference against capecitabine20,21. Substituting endocrine therapy with a CDK4/6 inhibitor for a cytotoxic changes how the time is spent rather than how much of it there is. That is the honest way to offer it.

RIGHT Choice asks the same substitution question in aggressive disease, against combination rather than single-agent chemotherapy13. It is phase 2, its survival data are not yet reported, and its limits are discussed in Rapid progression and visceral crisis. It supports an older principle rather than replacing it. Combination chemotherapy raises response rate and toxicity together, while sequential single agents preserve function and allow more lines.

One group of patients should not reach that table at all. In germline carriers the physician's choice of chemotherapy was the comparator in two registration trials rather than the standard.

PARP inhibitor trials whose populations reach this chapter. All enrolled HER2-negative disease, so hormone receptor-positive patients sit inside every row. Read the population column first. The entry criteria differ more between these trials than the drugs do.

The decision rests on OlympiAD and EMBRACA, because they randomised a PARP inhibitor against the thing the clinic would otherwise have done26,25. They agree twice over. Each lengthened progression-free survival against the physician's choice of chemotherapy, and neither improved overall survival at its final analysis27,28. A germline result therefore changes which class the next line comes from. It also changes how the line is tolerated. Neither trial showed that it changes how long the patient lives. What those two trials did and did not establish is read in full in PARP inhibition for germline carriers.

The other four rows differ from those two by design rather than by drug. The population column is where the difference sits. BROCADE3 gave a PARP inhibitor with a platinum doublet rather than in place of chemotherapy. Its question is therefore addition and not substitution, and its survival data are not yet reported14. TBCRC 048 and Talazoparib Beyond BRCA moved eligibility off germline BRCA1 and BRCA2, both single-arm24,22. One took germline mutations in other homologous recombination genes in one cohort and somatic mutations, including somatic BRCA1 and BRCA2, in a second. The other took germline BRCA wild-type tumours carrying a mutation elsewhere in that pathway. MEDIOLA breast cohort added PD-L1 blockade to olaparib in a single arm, so nothing in it separates the contribution of the second drug from the first23.

Those two beyond-BRCA cohorts nonetheless agree on something the randomised trials never tested. Widening eligibility beyond germline BRCA1 and BRCA2 produces responses with some genes and none with others, with germline PALB2 the clearest of them24,22. A panel report naming any homologous recombination gene is not a PARP inhibitor indication. That agreement rests on two small uncontrolled cohorts. The randomised trial that would settle it does not exist.

Neither table holds the comparison a carrier in this chapter actually faces. No trial randomises a PARP inhibitor against a conjugate, in either order, and none of the conjugate trials stratified on germline status. The sequence is chosen on class reasoning and on tolerability.

In practice

Say what the line is for before starting it. Control of a symptom, protection of a threatened organ, and extension of time are different aims with different evidence behind them.

Choose the agent by what the patient can tolerate rather than by expected efficacy, because the comparative trials show no efficacy difference within the class.

Test for a germline BRCA1 or BRCA2 mutation before choosing the cytotoxic rather than after, since the result moves the patient into a different table.

Re-check performance status before each new line rather than at progression. A patient who has had three conjugates and two cytotoxics is a different physiological proposition from the trial populations above.

Ask about access explicitly. Conjugate availability is the binding constraint in most of the world, and single-agent chemotherapy is the realistic comparator there rather than the fallback Global breast cancer care.

Name the alternative of no further systemic therapy as an option, not as a failure. It is the right answer more often than the number of remaining licensed agents suggests Long-term disease control and the cost of staying on treatment.

7 · Unresolved questions and what reads out next

Advanced disease · HR+/HER2- · later

Antibody-drug conjugates after endocrine therapy

Does adding checkpoint blockade to a TROP2 conjugate improve on the conjugate alone?

Rationale
A topoisomerase payload kills in a way that releases tumour antigen and can draw immune cells into the tumour, which is the standard argument for putting a conjugate next to a checkpoint inhibitor. Hormone receptor-positive disease is the least immunogenic of the common subtypes. If a conjugate is what makes a tumour visible to the immune system, this is where an added effect should be easiest to attribute to the conjugate rather than to the tumour's own starting immunogenicity.
Experiment
A randomised phase II trial gave a TROP2 conjugate with or without a checkpoint inhibitor, in disease pretreated with endocrine therapy and at most one chemotherapy regimen. Progression-free survival was the primary endpoint. The PD-L1-positive population was a key secondary one. A phase III trial is now testing the same addition with a different TROP2 conjugate, ahead of any chemotherapy, against physician's choice chemotherapy. Elsewhere in this subtype, checkpoint blockade has been given either in single-arm combinations, where the partner drug cannot be separated from the checkpoint inhibitor, or in populations selected for a high mutational burden.
Finding
Adding the checkpoint inhibitor did not improve progression-free survival over the conjugate alone in the population unselected for PD-L1. The PD-L1-positive subgroup pointed in the favourable direction without reaching significance. Elsewhere in this subtype, checkpoint blockade has produced responses only where the population was selected for a mutational phenotype.
Limitation
The PD-L1 result is a key secondary analysis, confined to the patients who had evaluable tissue and scored positive on it. It did not reach significance. Its own authors describe it as a trend for larger randomised trials to test rather than as a result. Eligibility also allowed entry with no chemotherapy at all for metastatic disease, and much of the enrolled population came in on that basis. The trial therefore sits earlier in the course than the line at which this conjugate is registered in this subtype. A single line label is covering a mixed population. The single-arm combination that sits beside it cannot establish what its checkpoint inhibitor added, as its authors say themselves, so it supplies no counterweight in either direction. The mutational-burden study selected on a threshold that its own correlative subgroup was too small to fix. It says that selection matters without saying where the line falls. The randomised comparison here is phase II and was not sized to exclude the size of effect a phase III would chase. The phase III now putting the same addition ahead of chemotherapy is the trial that will settle it.
Next question
If neither a partner drug nor a measured biomarker improves on the conjugate, does the remaining lever become where in the sequence the conjugate is given?

SACI-IO HR+ MK-2870-010 NIMBUS MEDIOLA breast cohort

The checkpoint question has now been asked properly once and answered negatively. SACI-IO HR+ randomised pembrolizumab with a Trop-2 conjugate against the conjugate alone, and progression-free survival did not improve in the population unselected for PD-L11. The PD-L1-positive analysis pointed the favourable way without reaching significance. It is a key secondary endpoint, restricted to the patients with evaluable tissue who scored positive on a combined positive score of at least 1. Its own authors present it as a trend for larger trials to test.

Its population is the part to read carefully. Entry allowed no chemotherapy at all for metastatic disease, and close to half the patients came in on that basis1. The trial therefore sits earlier in the disease than the line at which this conjugate is registered in this subtype. One line label is covering two different clinical situations.

Nothing else in the subtype weighs against that result. MEDIOLA breast cohort combined a checkpoint inhibitor with olaparib in a single arm. Its authors say plainly that the design cannot show what the checkpoint inhibitor added23. NIMBUS produced responses only after selecting patients on tumour mutational burden. Its threshold subgroup is too small to fix where the cut-off should fall. Selection matters and nobody has measured where the line is.

SACI-IO HR+ also returned a useful null on measurement. Neither Trop-2 by immunohistochemistry, immunofluorescence or plasma epigenome-based assay, nor tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes, was associated with outcome, while a higher circulating tumour DNA fraction and PIK3CA mutations tracked worse progression-free survival1. Three ways of measuring the target all failed to select anyone, which is the same finding Sacituzumab govitecan in hormone receptor-positive disease reports from a different trial.

Conjugate trials in this population that have not reported.

ASCENT-07 tests sacituzumab govitecan against chemotherapy in endocrine-resistant disease with no prior chemotherapy for metastatic disease. That is the slot DESTINY-Breast06 now occupies, so a positive result would create a genuine choice between two conjugates at one decision point, with no trial comparing them.

MK-2870-010 does two things at once. It brings a third Trop-2 conjugate into that same slot, carrying a topoisomerase I inhibitor payload again, so it extends this chapter's problem rather than solving it. It also carries a checkpoint-inhibitor arm, which makes it the phase 3 test of the addition that SACI-IO HR+ could not exclude at phase 2.

TRADE-DXd is the one to watch for sequencing, for the reasons set out in Sequencing conjugates and the cross-resistance problem.

Three gaps will remain after all three report.

No trial randomises the choice between leaving endocrine-based therapy and continuing it, which is the decision that precedes everything in this chapter.

No assay in clinical use predicts which patient retains sensitivity after a topoisomerase I payload, and none of the running trials will produce one.

And no conjugate in this population carries a payload from a different class. Until one does, sequencing decisions will continue to rest on target expression, which answers one question out of six Mechanisms of conjugate resistance.
