Part VII · Detection, diagnosis, and biomarker assessment · Chapter 40

Liquid biopsy and longitudinal monitoring

Circulating tumour DNA tells you that residual disease is present. Nobody has yet shown that acting on it early makes a patient live longer.

1 · Biology of circulating tumor DNA, shedding, half-life, and detectability

Plasma always contains cell-free DNA. Most of it comes from haematopoietic cells. The tumour-derived fraction is a minority of that pool, and in early disease it is a very small minority.

Cell-free DNA has a short circulating half-life, measured in hours rather than days. That single property is what makes the assay useful. A plasma sample reports what is shedding now, not what was shedding last year. It is the opposite of an archival block.

Shedding is not proportional to tumour volume. It depends on cell turnover, on necrosis and apoptosis within the deposit, on vascular access, and on where the deposit sits. A proliferating liver metastasis contributes far more DNA per gram than a quiescent bone deposit does.

The site effect can be quantified. In a prospective cohort followed with serial plasma sampling, circulating tumour DNA detected 22 of 23 distant extracranial metastatic relapses, which is 96%. It detected 1 of 6 brain-only relapses, which is 17%1. The central nervous system is a separate compartment, and Cerebrospinal fluid analysis in leptomeningeal disease takes that up.

Detectability is also a property of the assay, and the two should never be reported as one. An ultrasensitive whole-genome-based platform tracking a median of 1,451 variants per patient detected circulating tumour DNA before treatment in 49 of 50 patients with early breast cancer, which is 98%. Thirty-nine per cent of all its detections sat below 100 parts per million of the cell-free DNA pool2. A less sensitive assay run on the same plasma would have called most of those patients negative.

Caution

Variant allele fraction is not the fraction of cancer cells carrying the variant. It is confounded by total shedding, by copy number at the locus, and by how much of the plasma pool is tumour-derived. Reading it as clonal fraction is the commonest analytic error in this literature, and Circulating tumor DNA as a heterogeneity integrator and its blind spots develops the point.

2 · Circulating tumor cells and extracellular vesicles

Circulating tumour cells were the first blood-based prognostic marker in this disease, and their history is the cautionary tale that governs the rest of this chapter.

In 177 patients with measurable metastatic breast cancer starting a new line of treatment, a count of 5 or more cells per 7.5 mL of whole blood at baseline separated two populations. Median progression-free survival was 2.7 months against 7.0 months. Median overall survival was 10.1 months against more than 18 months3. The count was an independent predictor in multivariable analysis.

Then the strategy was tested rather than assumed. SWOG S0500 enrolled 595 eligible patients starting first-line chemotherapy. Those whose counts remained elevated after 21 days were randomised to continue or to switch to an alternative cytotoxic agent. Median overall survival was 10.7 months in the continuation arm and 12.5 months in the switch arm, with a P value of .984. Across the three prognostic groups defined by the counts, median overall survival was 35, 23 and 13 months.

Read those two results together. The marker was strongly prognostic. Acting on it changed nothing. That is the exact shape of the question this chapter carries, and it was answered negatively for cells more than a decade before it was asked for DNA.

The cells themselves are biologically informative. Their epithelial and mesenchymal composition shifts dynamically during treatment5. Clusters are oligoclonal and carry greater metastatic potential than single cells6. The seeding biology belongs to Circulating tumor cells and the inefficiency of seeding, and the platform-dependence of counts to What is actionable today and what remains a research instrument.

Extracellular vesicles are a third analyte. Tumour-derived exosomes carry integrin repertoires that help determine which organ a tumour seeds7, which is developed in Premetastatic niche formation. No vesicle-based assay has established clinical validity in breast cancer, and none has a management decision attached to it.

3 · Molecular residual disease, tumor-informed and tumor-naive approaches

Molecular residual disease detection by plasma comes in two designs, and the difference is where the variant list comes from.

A tumour-informed assay sequences the resected primary, selects variants specific to that tumour, and builds a personalised assay to hunt for them. A tumour-naive or tissue-free assay uses a fixed panel or a methylation classifier, and needs no tumour tissue at all.

The tumour-informed evidence is the older and the larger. In 55 patients with early breast cancer receiving neoadjuvant chemotherapy, detection at a single postsurgical time point carried a hazard ratio for relapse of 25.1, with a 95% confidence interval of 4.08 to 130.5. Serial follow-up sampling gave a hazard ratio of 12.0, and a median lead time over clinical relapse of 7.9 months8. A larger validation cohort reproduced it. Among 101 women followed prospectively, detection during follow-up carried a hazard ratio for relapse of 25.2, with a 95% confidence interval of 6.7 to 95.61.

Sensitivity scales with how many variants are tracked. In 61 patients, a next-generation sequencing assay tracking up to 50 patient-specific variants gave a hazard ratio for relapse of 37.2, with a 95% confidence interval of 10.5 to 131.9. Median lead time over clinical relapse was 11.7 months9. A whole-genome-powered assay tracking a median of 1,451 variants identified residual disease in 11 of 11 patients who relapsed, and none of 64 patients in whom it was never detected relapsed during follow-up2.

The tissue-free comparison has now been done head to head within one cohort. Across 1,026 plasma samples from 159 women with triple-negative disease, a methylation-based tissue-free assay detected circulating tumour DNA in 54 patients, which is 34.0%. The hazard ratio for recurrence was 27.2, with a 95% confidence interval of 13.7 to 54.210. Against single-variant digital PCR the tissue-free assay was earlier, with median lead times of 7.9 against 5.8 months. Against a multivariant whole-exome-powered tumour-informed assay it was not, with lead times of 7.6 against 7.1 months. Among 41 patients detected by both, 12 were found earlier by the tumour-informed assay and 1 earlier by the tissue-free one.

The practical reading is narrow and useful. Tissue-free assays work when no tissue is available, and they beat a single-variant approach. The most analytically sensitive assays remain the ones that track many tumour-specific variants, and the number of variants tracked should be on the report.

4 · Lead time and the unresolved question of whether acting early helps

This is the section the chapter exists for, and the honest answer is uncomfortable.

Molecular residual disease detected in plasma is strongly prognostic across every subtype that has been examined. The hazard ratios in Molecular residual disease, tumor-informed and tumor-naive approaches are not marginal effects. They are among the largest prognostic separations reported for any biomarker in this disease.

Lead time is real and it is subtype-dependent. One multicentre study followed 83 patients with residual disease after neoadjuvant chemotherapy, using personalised assays against structural variants. Detection carried a hazard ratio for relapse-free interval of 29.2, with a 95% confidence interval of 6.9 to 124.011. Median lead time over clinical relapse was 13.9 months overall. It was 20.9 months in oestrogen receptor-positive HER2-negative disease, 14.2 months in HER2-positive disease, and 4.3 months in triple-negative disease. Sensitivity for locoregional or distant relapse was 25 of 27 patients, which is 92.6%. Among patients with a negative postsurgical sample, 5-year relapse-free interval was 72.6%, with a 95% confidence interval of 60.1% to 81.6%.

Those subtype differences are biology, not assay performance. Fast-growing disease gives less warning because the interval between detectable shedding and clinical relapse is shorter.

Now the negative. c-TRAK TN prospectively surveilled 161 women with moderate-risk and high-risk early triple-negative breast cancer, sampling three-monthly, and offered pembrolizumab to those who became positive. The detection rate by 12 months was 27.3%, which was 44 of 161, with a 95% confidence interval of 20.6% to 34.9%. Of the patients allocated to intervention, 72% already had metastases on staging imaging at the moment circulating tumour DNA was detected, which was 23 of 32. Of the five patients who actually started pembrolizumab, none achieved sustained clearance of circulating tumour DNA12. Seven patients relapsed with no prior detection at all.

Three separate lessons sit inside that result. Surveillance started too late, after adjuvant therapy had finished. Sampling was too infrequent for a subtype whose lead time is around four months11. And the intervention did not work on the disease it found.

In the advanced setting the same design has succeeded twice. PADA-1 monitored 1,017 women on first-line aromatase inhibitor and palbociclib for a rising ESR1 mutation in blood. Of those, 279 developed one, which is 27%, and 172 without synchronous progression were randomised. Median progression-free survival from randomisation was 11.9 months in those switched to fulvestrant and palbociclib, against 5.7 months in those continued. The stratified hazard ratio was 0.61, with a 95% confidence interval of 0.43 to 0.86 and a P value of 0.004013. SERENA-6 then confirmed the principle with a next-generation degrader, and Acting on evolution, intermittent dosing, adaptive therapy, pre-emptive switching develops that result rather than repeating it here.

The two settings are not the same experiment, and conflating them is the error to avoid. In advanced disease the patient is already on treatment, the molecular signal triggers a switch, and an effective next agent exists. After curative-intent treatment the patient is on no therapy or on endocrine therapy alone, and no agent has been shown to eradicate molecularly detected residual disease. DARE, LEADER and SURVIVE are testing that, alongside TRAK-ER and PERSEVERE. None has reported a survival result.

Guidelines reflect this. ASCO concluded that there are insufficient data to recommend routine use of circulating tumour DNA or circulating tumour cells to monitor response to therapy in metastatic disease14. Its 2026 follow-up and surveillance update identified no randomised trial evaluating different follow-up approaches by risk of relapse, and issued consensus-based rather than evidence-based recommendations15. Molecular residual disease surveillance is not recommended outside a trial.

Caution

Lead time by itself is not benefit. A test that moves the date of diagnosis forward without changing what happens next converts survival measured from diagnosis into a longer number without adding a day to anyone's life. That is lead-time bias, and it is the reason the surveillance question requires a randomised comparison of the action rather than a cohort showing the lead time. SWOG S0500 is the demonstration that a strongly prognostic blood marker can support no useful action at all4. Endpoints, surrogates, and the overall survival question treats the general form of this problem.

5 · Monitoring response and detecting emerging resistance

Serial plasma measures whether the treated population is shrinking. Whether that measurement is useful depends entirely on which variant is being tracked.

In PALOMA-3, the relative change in PIK3CA circulating tumour DNA after 15 days of treatment strongly predicted progression-free survival on palbociclib with fulvestrant. The hazard ratio was 3.94, with a log-rank P value of 0.0013. ESR1 dynamics in the same patients predicted clinical outcome only weakly16.

The explanation is clonal structure rather than assay failure. PIK3CA mutations in this population are typically truncal, so their abundance tracks the bulk of the disease. ESR1 mutations selected by prior aromatase inhibitor exposure are frequently subclonal, so their abundance tracks one compartment16. A falling ESR1 level can coexist with a growing ESR1 wild-type population.

The same samples show what the treatment selected. RB1 mutations emerged only in the palbociclib arm, in 6 of 127 patients17. Evolution of driver mutations was uncommon in patients who progressed early and common in those who progressed late. That argument belongs to CDK4/6 inhibition and the evolution of cell cycle escape and Emergence of ESR1, PIK3CA, RB1, CCNE1, and FAT1 alterations on treatment and is not repeated here.

Baseline plasma metrics carry independent prognostic weight in advanced hormone receptor-positive disease18. Prognosis is not the same claim as utility.

Caution

Molecular response is not a validated surrogate endpoint. A fall in circulating tumour DNA has not been shown to substitute for progression-free or overall survival in any registrational setting in breast cancer. Using it to justify continuing or stopping a treatment outside a trial imports an assumption that has not been tested14.

6 · Mutation tracking for ESR1 and PIK3CA to guide treatment change

Two alterations currently justify plasma genotyping in routine practice, and they justify it for different reasons.

ESR1 mutations are acquired under oestrogen deprivation. In 171 women with advanced disease, they were detected exclusively in oestrogen receptor-positive patients with prior aromatase inhibitor exposure. Patients with a detectable mutation had shorter progression-free survival on subsequent aromatase inhibitor based therapy than patients without one. The hazard ratio was 3.1, with a 95% confidence interval of 1.9 to 23.1 and a P value of 0.004119,20. That interval is wide enough that the point estimate should not be quoted alone. Testing before that exposure tests before the selection has happened. The biology is in Estrogen receptor signaling and endocrine resistance and the treatment consequences in Oral selective estrogen receptor degraders and their biomarker-defined benefit.

Plasma genotyping for these alterations is analytically mature. In plasmaMATCH, 1,034 of 1,051 registered patients returned a circulating tumour DNA result. Agreement between digital PCR and targeted sequencing was 96% to 99%, with kappa values of 0.89 to 0.93 across 800 samples. Sensitivity against mutations identified by tissue sequencing was 93%, with a 95% confidence interval of 83% to 98%21.

Finding the mutation is solved. What follows from finding it is not uniform. In the same trial, extended-dose fulvestrant in ESR1-mutant disease produced confirmed responses in 6 of 74 patients, which is 8%, and missed its target. Neratinib in HER2-mutant disease produced 5 responses in 20 patients, and capivasertib with fulvestrant in AKT1-mutant oestrogen receptor-positive disease produced 4 in 1821. The assay was equally good in all four cohorts. The drugs were not.

PIK3CA testing carries a specific asymmetry that should be stated explicitly. ASCO recommends testing tumour tissue or plasma to determine eligibility for a PI3K inhibitor, and recommends tissue testing if no mutation is found in plasma and tissue is available14. A positive plasma result is actionable. A negative plasma result is not conclusive, because it may only mean the tumour is not shedding.

In practice

Test plasma for ESR1 after aromatase inhibitor exposure, and at each progression on endocrine therapy. The mutation is acquired, so a negative result early does not stay negative19.

Test plasma for PIK3CA when a PI3K-directed option is on the table. If plasma is negative and tissue exists, test the tissue before concluding the tumour is wild type14.

Do not order plasma genotyping when no available drug or trial depends on the answer. The result will be interesting and it will not change anything.

7 · Cerebrospinal fluid analysis in leptomeningeal disease

The central nervous system is a separate compartment, and plasma reports it badly. That is a statement about anatomy rather than about assay quality.

Cerebrospinal fluid cytology is the conventional diagnostic standard, and its sensitivity is imperfect, which is why repeated lumbar puncture is often needed to confirm or exclude the diagnosis. Measuring the circulating tumour DNA fraction in cerebrospinal fluid by ultra-low-pass whole-genome sequencing performs better. In 30 patients under investigation for breast cancer leptomeningeal metastasis, a fraction of at least 0.10 was found in all 24 patients with confirmed disease, with a median fraction of 0.57. It was absent in the 6 patients without it, whose median fraction was 0.03, with a P value below 0.000122. Detection held despite negative cytology or borderline magnetic resonance imaging.

The compartment separation is visible in the same study. Plasma circulating tumour DNA was detected only in patients with extracranial disease progression or previous whole-brain radiotherapy22. A patient with leptomeningeal disease and controlled systemic disease can have an undetectable plasma result.

Serial measurement behaved like a response marker. Among 12 patients monitored during intrathecal treatment, suppression of the cerebrospinal fluid fraction was associated with longer survival, with a P value of 0.034. A rising fraction was detectable up to 12 weeks before clinical progression22.

Clinical management of the syndrome itself belongs to Leptomeningeal disease and whether delivery has to be intrathecal.

Caution

This test requires a lumbar puncture, so it is not a screening tool and it cannot be repeated freely. The whole-genome fraction method reports how much tumour DNA is present. It does not report which mutations are present, which is a different assay on the same fluid. Do not read a quantitative fraction as a genotype.

8 · Discordance between blood, tissue, and imaging

Three assays sample three different things. Blood integrates every shedding deposit. A biopsy characterises one deposit in detail. A scan sees every deposit and characterises none of them. A disagreement between them usually identifies which sampling frame failed, not which result was wrong.

Most blood-tissue discordance is temporal. In plasmaMATCH, sensitivity of plasma digital PCR against mutations identified in tissue was 93% overall, and 98% when the tissue biopsy was contemporaneous, with a 95% confidence interval of 87% to 100%21. The gap between those two numbers is the archive. A mutation absent from plasma but present in an old block may have been selected out, and a mutation present in plasma but absent from the block may have been selected in.

Shedding produces the second class of discordance. Low-volume disease, bone-only disease and intracranial disease all contribute less DNA than their apparent burden implies1,22. A negative plasma result in that setting is uninformative rather than reassuring.

Contamination from non-tumour clones produces the third class, and Clonal hematopoiesis and other false positives develops it23.

Plasma reports DNA and only DNA. A receptor conversion driven by transcriptional or post-translational change is invisible to it, which is why Receptor discordance and conversion still requires tissue.

Imaging adds a fourth axis. In 56 patients with HER2-positive metastatic disease, pretreatment zirconium-89 trastuzumab positron emission tomography was negative in 29%, and a mixed pattern within a single patient was found in 46%24. Nearly half of a population defined as HER2-positive by a tissue assay carried lesions that did not bind the antibody. Imaging-based heterogeneity, radiomics, PET metrics, and HER2 PET develops the imaging argument.

9 · Clonal hematopoiesis and other false positives

Most of the cell-free DNA in plasma comes from white blood cells, so most of the mutations found in plasma come from white blood cells too.

The magnitude was established by sequencing plasma alongside matched white blood cell DNA. Among 47 controls without cancer and 124 patients with metastatic cancer, 81.6% of the cell-free DNA mutations in controls and 53.2% of those in patients had features consistent with clonal haematopoiesis23. More than half of what a plasma assay calls in a cancer patient can come from the blood rather than the tumour.

Clonal haematopoiesis is common, it increases with age, and it is associated with adverse outcomes independent of cancer25,26. Cytotoxic chemotherapy and radiotherapy select for specific clones, particularly those carrying PPM1D and TP53 mutations27. That makes the problem worse in exactly the population under surveillance, because these patients have just finished chemotherapy.

The control is matched white cell sequencing. Its absence from an assay is a design choice, and it should be visible on the report.

Tumour-informed assays are largely immune to this failure, and that is their strongest argument. Their variant list comes from the resected tumour, so a haematopoietic clone that was never in the tumour cannot generate a positive result. A tumour-naive panel has no such protection.

Caution

A TP53 or PPM1D mutation appearing in the plasma of a patient who has recently received chemotherapy is more likely to be haematopoietic than tumour-derived27,23. Treating it as evidence of residual cancer, or as a target, is the specific error this section exists to prevent.

Other false positive sources are less discussed and equally real. Germline variants called as somatic, index hopping between multiplexed samples, and stochastic artefacts near the limit of detection all produce low-level positives. Each is more likely as the reported detection threshold falls, which means the most sensitive assays carry the greatest need for a specificity control.

10 · Implementation thresholds, regulation, and reimbursement

A limit of detection is a property of an assay at a stated plasma input and a stated variant count. It is not a property of the patient. A result reported as negative means no signal was found at that sensitivity, from that volume, on that day.

That makes reporting standards the practical issue. A circulating tumour DNA report should state six things: the assay design, whether it is tumour-informed or tissue-free, how many variants were tracked, the plasma volume, the limit of detection, and whether matched white cell sequencing was performed. A result without those fields cannot be compared to a result from a different laboratory, and serial results from different platforms cannot be compared at all.

The evidence position and the commercial position have diverged, and the tension should be stated rather than smoothed. ASCO found insufficient data to recommend routine circulating tumour DNA monitoring of treatment response in metastatic disease14. Its 2026 surveillance guideline found no randomised trials comparing follow-up strategies by relapse risk, and produced consensus recommendations instead15. Coverage and reimbursement decisions are made by different bodies against different standards, and assays are available and paid for in settings where no trial has shown that acting on the result helps.

Both facts are true at once. The test performs as advertised. The action that should follow a positive result has not been established.

In practice

Order molecular residual disease testing inside a trial. DARE, LEADER and SURVIVE are the designs that will answer the question, and enrolling a patient is more useful than testing her.

Outside a trial, ask what you would do with a positive result before drawing the sample. If the answer is earlier imaging and an earlier start of the same therapy you would have given anyway, the test has added anxiety and cost rather than information.

Do not present a negative result as evidence of cure. Dormant cells shed little, which makes a negative result specifically weak against the population that causes late recurrence Minimal residual disease as a measurable state.

Do use plasma genotyping at progression in advanced disease, where a detected ESR1 or PIK3CA alteration has a drug attached to it Mutation tracking for ESR1 and PIK3CA to guide treatment change.

If you are interpreting a report from a tumour-naive panel, check whether matched white cell sequencing was done before you accept a TP53 or PPM1D call as tumour-derived23.

Interplay

Liquid biopsy is where temporal heterogeneity becomes a measurement rather than an inference. Plasma integrates across deposits, so it sees convergent evolution well and divergent evolution poorly Convergent and divergent evolution and what each implies for sequencing. It samples shedding rather than presence, so it sees proliferating disease well and dormant disease poorly Cellular quiescence programs and the dormant state. Those two blind spots are not defects to be engineered away. They are the assay reporting faithfully on a biology that is itself heterogeneous in exactly those two dimensions. The unresolved clinical question follows directly. A test that detects the shedding, dividing fraction of residual disease will find the patients whose recurrence was going to be early, and those are the patients for whom an earlier start of existing therapy is least likely to be curative.
