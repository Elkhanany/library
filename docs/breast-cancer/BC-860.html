1 · Scope, and why pathological complete response carries most weight in this subtype

This chapter covers what is given before surgery in triple-negative disease. What follows surgery is in the companion chapter Adjuvant and post-neoadjuvant therapy in triple-negative disease. The framing argument is in Early triple-negative disease, with the neoadjuvant setting as a research instrument in The neoadjuvant setting as a scientific platform.

Pathological complete response matters more here than anywhere else in this book, and the reason is quantitative. In the pooled analysis of 12 trials and 11,955 patients, eradication of tumour from breast and nodes in triple-negative disease carried an event-free survival hazard ratio of 0.24, with a 95% confidence interval of 0.18 to 0.33. The overall survival hazard ratio was 0.16, with an interval of 0.11 to 0.251. No other readily available variable in this disease separates outcome that sharply.

The same analysis is why the endpoint cannot be used to judge a drug. At trial level, the increase in response frequency between treatment groups explained almost none of the variation in event-free survival1. The distinction between a patient-level prognostic marker and a trial-level surrogate is worked out in Endpoints, surrogates, and the overall survival question, and it governs every table below.

So read pathological complete response as an instruction about what to do next rather than as a verdict on what was just given. That is the use that survives the surrogacy failure, and the use on which Adjuvant and post-neoadjuvant therapy in triple-negative disease depends.

The master table for the whole chapter sits in Anthracycline and taxane backbones. Later sections narrow it by question, so a trial recurs when it bears on more than one of them.

The story below is the argument the rest of this chapter is evidence for, and it carries no figures at all. It states what each group of trials was built to find out, what came back, and what the design cannot settle, so the tables are read as answers to a question rather than as a list. Choosing what to add to preoperative chemotherapy, and what to take out

Treatment is given before surgery in this cell because the tumour answers back, sorting the population for everything that follows. So almost every question here is asked against pathological complete response, a measurement taken once at one operation. Almost every answer then has to survive the gap between that measurement and recurrence.

Does adding carboplatin to preoperative chemotherapy do more than raise pathological complete response?

Rationale
Triple-negative tumours are enriched for defective homologous recombination. Platinum works by making the interstrand crosslinks that this repair pathway exists to resolve. So a drug the rest of breast cancer has little use for should earn its place here. The preoperative setting is where that prediction could be read quickly.
Experiment
Randomised addition of carboplatin to a taxane and anthracycline backbone, in trials that differed in the backbone and in what else was randomised alongside it. The endpoint in each was pathological complete response. Later trials put the same addition against event-free or disease-free survival instead, giving the platinum before surgery, after it, or either way.
Finding
Carboplatin raised pathological complete response over the same backbone without it, in trial after trial. The trials that used survival rather than response as their endpoint found fewer events in the carboplatin arm. One trial that set carboplatin against a different partner drug on the same taxane backbone saw its response advantage gone by the time survival was counted.
Limitation
These trials vary the platinum and the backbone together, so the variable under test is not the platinum alone. They differ in which anthracycline was given, in whether a second experimental agent was randomised alongside in a factorial, and in how much of the planned chemotherapy survived the added toxicity. In the trial where the response advantage disappeared, treatment after the first twelve weeks depended on how the tumour had responded, so its survival comparison is confounded by what came next. One of the survival trials violated the proportional hazards assumption its summary estimate is computed under, so that estimate does not describe the whole of its follow-up. Pathological complete response is also prognostic within an arm rather than a demonstrated surrogate across arms. The older trial that doubled it by adding a preoperative taxane, and changed no survival curve, is the standing demonstration of the difference.

Does a homologous recombination deficiency assay, or a germline BRCA mutation, identify who needs the platinum?

Rationale
If platinum works because a crosslink goes unrepaired, tumours that can repair one should not need it. A test for the defect would let the drug be allocated rather than given to everyone. That matters here because platinum toxicity is what limits delivery of the rest of the regimen.
Experiment
Two randomised trials built around the marker rather than around the drug. One set single-agent platinum against a single-agent taxane, and asked whether a deficiency score above a fixed cut-point predicted response to the platinum specifically. The other set single-agent platinum against an anthracycline regimen in germline carriers. A third trial selected patients by the deficiency assay and put a PARP inhibitor in the platinum's place. A single-arm window study gave a PARP inhibitor to patients selected for none of this.
Finding
The deficiency score did not separate response to platinum from response to a taxane. In germline carriers, platinum produced no more complete responses than the anthracycline regimen those patients would have had anyway. Selecting on the assay did not make the platinum-free arm clear the response threshold set for it. Unselected tumours given a PARP inhibitor responded often enough to make the marker look less like a gate than a description.
Limitation
The biomarker trial was sized to test a predictive claim rather than to compare two drugs, so its arm-to-arm difference is not an efficacy result. A large share of its patients crossed to another preoperative regimen before surgery, and the assay could not be scored on all of them. Entry to the germline trial required a mutation rather than triple-negative disease, so hormone receptor-positive patients sit inside its answer. The assay-selected trial tested one arm against a fixed threshold rather than against its comparator, so its arms can be described and not compared. No trial here kept an assay-negative arm on the same treatment, which is the comparison that would show what the selection bought.

Can a PARP inhibitor stand in for the chemotherapy before surgery rather than be added to it?

Rationale
A PARP inhibitor kills cells that cannot repair a double-strand break. That is the same vulnerability the platinum is aimed at. If the oral drug does that work, the case for the cytotoxic backbone weakens. Its toxicity is of a different kind, which is the whole appeal in a disease being treated for cure.
Experiment
The designs run from addition to replacement. One trial randomised a PARP inhibitor on top of a platinum and taxane backbone, with a third arm carrying neither. One substituted the PARP inhibitor for the platinum in assay-selected patients. Two gave a PARP inhibitor as the only preoperative treatment, one for a full course in germline carriers, one as a short window before the chemotherapy in unselected disease. A further single-arm study pairs a PARP inhibitor with a checkpoint inhibitor and no chemotherapy at all.
Finding
Added to a platinum-containing backbone, the PARP inhibitor added nothing to it. Substituted for the platinum in selected patients, it did not clear the response threshold the trial had set itself, although it landed near the platinum arm. Given alone for a full course in germline carriers, it cleared tumours at a rate close to what combination chemotherapy achieves, and still fell short of its own prespecified bar. A short window of it, in patients unselected for a repair defect, produced objective responses in many of the tumours it was given to.
Limitation
The addition trial used a PARP inhibitor that traps the enzyme on DNA weakly, so a null result there is a result about one molecule rather than about the class. The monotherapy trials are single-arm, so their response rates have no concurrent comparator and get read against chemotherapy rates from other trials in other populations. One of them reports its response on an evaluable denominator rather than on everyone enrolled. A window of a few weeks followed by planned chemotherapy says nothing about whether that chemotherapy could have been left out. The chemotherapy-free combination has no PARP-only arm, so nothing in it separates the checkpoint inhibitor's contribution from the PARP inhibitor's.

Does adding a checkpoint inhibitor to preoperative chemotherapy lower recurrence, and does it have to be given before surgery?

Rationale
Triple-negative tumours carry more tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes and more neoantigen than the other subtypes. Chemotherapy releases antigen as it kills. A checkpoint inhibitor given while the primary tumour and its draining nodes are still in place should prime a wider response than one given to a patient who has neither left.
Experiment
Several randomised additions of a checkpoint inhibitor to preoperative chemotherapy. They differ in the drug, in whether the backbone carried a platinum, and in whether the checkpoint inhibitor continued after surgery. Set against them, two trials gave a checkpoint inhibitor only after surgery, with no preoperative phase at all.
Finding
Adding a checkpoint inhibitor raised pathological complete response over the same chemotherapy with placebo in most of these trials. In the trial that also continued it after surgery, the event-free survival advantage held. A large trial of a different checkpoint inhibitor, given across both halves of the pathway, missed its primary endpoint. Both trials that waited until after surgery were negative.
Limitation
The practice-defining trial varies more than the checkpoint inhibitor. Its backbone carries a platinum. Its treatment continues after surgery. Neither of those was randomised separately, so the trial cannot say which part is load-bearing. The trial that ran no post-surgical phase reported a survival separation its own response endpoint did not support, on few events. Programmed death-ligand 1 expression did not select here the way it does in metastatic disease, and the co-primary analysis in the marker-positive population did not cross its boundary. The assay and its cut-point differ between these trials in any case. The adjuvant-only trials differ in drug and in population as well as in timing, so the case for the preoperative window is drawn between trials rather than inside one. Whether the post-surgical half is needed at all is only now being randomised, in patients who cleared their tumour, and has not reported.

How much chemotherapy still has to sit underneath the checkpoint inhibitor?

Rationale
The cytotoxic part of the regimen carries the cardiac risk, the leukaemia risk and most of the acute toxicity. If the platinum and the immune agent are doing the work, the anthracycline is a cost with nothing on the other side of it. Taken further, the same argument asks whether any chemotherapy is needed in a tumour that already has an immune infiltrate.
Experiment
One randomised comparison of an anthracycline-free platinum and taxane regimen against the same drugs followed by an anthracycline, with response as the endpoint. A single-arm version of the short regimen with a checkpoint inhibitor added. One trial that kept the anthracycline but moved it after surgery, and randomised the checkpoint inhibitor on what was left in front. A randomised trial of the short regimen against the standard one, with event-free survival as its endpoint, is running. At the far end, a chemotherapy-free trial gave checkpoint blockade alone to tumours selected by a lymphocyte cut-point.
Finding
Anthracycline-free regimens reach response rates close to those of the anthracycline-containing ones. Where the anthracycline was deferred until after surgery, the checkpoint inhibitor's response advantage did not appear. Checkpoint blockade with no chemotherapy at all cleared some lymphocyte-rich tumours completely. It did so at the price of frequent immune endocrinopathy in patients who were otherwise well.
Limitation
All of these read out at the operation. The one trial randomising the two regimens against an outcome has not reported. The anthracycline-free comparison is small and was powered for response, which is the endpoint already shown in this cell to move without survival moving with it. The trial that deferred the anthracycline has published only a secondary response endpoint, so it cannot be used to argue that the anthracycline was the missing ingredient. The chemotherapy-free trial ran window cohorts whose endpoint is a laboratory measure of immune activation, and only its selected surgical cohort carried a response endpoint. A lymphocyte score read on one slide against a percentage cut-point is a scoring convention, and that trial selected on it. Substituting one taxane formulation for another raised response in an all-comers trial and did not replicate in a trial restricted to HER2-negative disease, which is the standing reminder that a backbone change is not portable between populations.

What can a preoperative trial settle about a new drug, and what has to wait for a randomised trial after surgery?

Rationale
The preoperative window is the fastest read in this disease. The tumour is still in place, the endpoint arrives at the operation, and paired biopsies make the mechanism visible. So it is the natural place to try a conjugate, a targeted addition or a physical primer, well before anyone commits to a trial with survival as its endpoint.
Experiment
An adaptive platform runs candidate agents against a shared chemotherapy control and graduates them on a Bayesian probability of success in a future trial. A conjugate was given alone in a single arm, with treatment after those cycles left to the treating physician. A JAK inhibitor was added to preoperative chemotherapy in inflammatory triple-negative disease, with a pharmacodynamic conversion as the primary endpoint. Preoperative radiation dose was randomised as an immune primer, with immunological endpoints. The randomised conjugate trials in this disease all sit after surgery, in patients with residual disease.
Finding
Conjugate monotherapy cleared a minority of untreated tumours. That is enough to say the class is active before surgery. It is not enough to say it belongs there. The JAK inhibitor hit its pharmacodynamic target while response in that rare population stayed low.
Limitation
A graduation probability is a prediction about a trial not yet done rather than a result. The conjugate trial is single-arm and response-guided, and what patients received afterwards was at their physician's discretion, so its event-free survival describes a pathway rather than a drug. The inflammatory trial enrolled very few patients, its response figure rests on a handful of events, and its run-in sits inside the randomisation rather than before it, so its two comparisons overlap. The radiation trial's endpoints are immunological and its dose comparisons are exploratory, so they do not rank doses for efficacy. None of the randomised conjugate trials asks a preoperative question, so there is no randomised basis for moving one in front of surgery.

Neoadjuvant trials in triple-negative disease. The arms column is where the backbone differences live.

Two structural facts constrain how the arms in that table should be read.

Giving the same chemotherapy before rather than after surgery does not change survival. In the individual patient data meta-analysis of ten trials, fifteen-year distant recurrence was 38.2% with neoadjuvant treatment against 38.0%, with a rate ratio of 1.02. Local recurrence was higher, at 21.4% against 15.9%, with a rate ratio of 1.3724. Those trials predate modern surgical planning and response-adapted local therapy. The point that survives is that the timing buys information and conservative surgery rather than a survival advantage in itself.

Dose density belongs to the same inheritance, and its evidence is in Adjuvant chemotherapy regimens and dose density. Several trials here use a dose-dense anthracycline phase and others do not, which is one more reason cross-trial response rates are not comparable.

The live backbone question is whether the anthracycline is needed at all when a platinum and a checkpoint inhibitor are both present. NeoPACT and NeoTRIP were built on that premise from opposite directions4,6. That question is taken up in The other immunotherapy trials and why they read out differently, because in practice it is inseparable from the immunotherapy question.

The part that surprises people is that the benefit is not confined to germline carriers. In the homologous recombination analysis of GeparSixto, carboplatin raised response sharply in deficient tumours and modestly in non-deficient ones. The interaction test was not significant, at a p value of 0.327, with disease-free survival improving in both groups25. The authors' conclusion on that point was explicit. Deficiency predicted response to the regimen as a whole, and not benefit from carboplatin specifically.

The apparent contradiction with the metastatic evidence dissolves once the designs are compared. In advanced disease, carboplatin was tested against docetaxel, where germline carriers responded better to the platinum than to the taxane26. That is a substitution design, which can expose differential sensitivity. The neoadjuvant trials added carboplatin to a full anthracycline and taxane backbone. Every patient still received the drugs a BRCA-associated tumour is already sensitive to, so the increment attributable to platinum is measured on top of them. An addition design cannot reveal the same contrast, whatever the germline result says.

The survival evidence is mixed, and the mixture is itself informative. Long-term follow-up of CALGB 40603 found no event-free survival benefit from carboplatin despite the response gain27. Post hoc analysis of BrighTNess pointed the other way on the same question28. GeparSixto and PEARLY were both positive on the survival endpoints they reported. None of these trials was powered for the question it is being asked.

Caution

Carboplatin is already inside the regimen that most stage II and III patients now receive12. No trial has removed it from a chemoimmunotherapy backbone, so its contribution in that context is unmeasured. The question is not whether to add platinum. It is whether the platinum that is already there is doing anything, and that trial does not exist.

Pembrolizumab-containing neoadjuvant regimens in triple-negative disease.

KEYNOTE-522 settled one thing completely and left several others open. Adding pembrolizumab to neoadjuvant chemotherapy and continuing it after surgery improves overall survival in stage II and III triple-negative disease29. An overall survival benefit in a curable population is rare, which is why the regimen is standard rather than optional.

Four things it did not settle are worth stating precisely, because each is regularly treated as though it had been.

It did not establish which phase does the work. Pembrolizumab was given before and after surgery to every patient12, and Adjuvant immunotherapy and the question stratified by response takes that up.

It did not establish the contribution of carboplatin, which both arms received.

It did not establish any benefit below stage II. Enrolment required stage II or III disease, leaving a node-negative 1.2 cm tumour unrepresented.

It did not establish which individual patients benefit. The trial enrolled without a PD-L1 criterion by design, and Biomarkers for immunotherapy benefit, and why PD-L1 fails here explains why no marker has since filled the gap.

The effect size was also predicted in advance by a platform trial. I-SPY2 pembrolizumab arm graduated pembrolizumab on a Bayesian estimate of success in a future phase 3 trial11. That prediction proved right, which is a point in favour of the platform design rather than evidence about the drug. A graduation is a probability statement about a trial that has not been run Biomarker-selected, basket, umbrella, and platform trials.

Every randomised neoadjuvant immunotherapy trial in triple-negative disease, plus the single-arm and ongoing studies.

Reading that spread as a statement about checkpoint blockade is the standard error. The trials differ on at least five axes at once, and no axis was isolated.

The agent differs: pembrolizumab binds PD-1, while atezolizumab and durvalumab bind PD-L1 and leave the PD-L2 interaction intact.

The chemotherapy backbone differs. KEYNOTE-522 and NSABP B-59/GeparDouze used a platinum and an anthracycline, IMpassion031 used an anthracycline without a platinum, and NeoTRIP used a platinum with the anthracycline deferred until after surgery.

Continuation after surgery differs. KEYNOTE-522, NSABP B-59/GeparDouze and IMpassion031 continued the checkpoint inhibitor postoperatively, while GeparNuevo stopped at surgery.

The endpoint each trial was powered for differs. IMpassion031 and GeparNuevo were powered for pathological complete response, while NSABP B-59/GeparDouze and NeoTRIP were powered for event-free survival, which is a much harder endpoint to move.

The enrolled population differs. NeoTRIP took high-risk and locally advanced disease, and in a pooled reconstruction of five of these trials its four-year recurrence rate was the highest30.

The dissociation between response and survival is the finding that matters. GeparNuevo missed on response and separated on survival15,31. IMpassion031 hit its response endpoint, while its survival comparisons remained descriptive and non-significant8,32. That is the trial-level surrogacy failure of Endpoints, surrogates, and the overall survival question appearing inside one drug class.

The anthracycline-free question sits here rather than in Anthracycline and taxane backbones for the same reason. NeoPACT reported a response rate in a single arm with no randomised comparator4. NeoTRIP used an anthracycline-free neoadjuvant phase and has not reported its primary endpoint. SCARLET is the randomised test against the KEYNOTE-522 regimen. Until it reports, the omission of an anthracycline remains a hypothesis.

Caution

Comparing pathological complete response rates across these trials is not informative. The populations, the backbones and the response definitions were not held constant. A lower rate in one trial than another is at least as likely to reflect who was enrolled as what was given.

The setting where PD-L1 does select. Every trial here restricted or stratified entry by an assay result.

Five differences separate the two settings, each cumulative with the others rather than competing.

The first difference is that PD-L1 positive is not the output of one assay. In advanced disease, samples scored by three antibodies gave immune cell positivity of 46.4% with SP142, 74.9% with SP263 and 73.1% with 22C3, and 80.9% were positive by 22C3 combined positive score. Analytical concordance with SP142 was about 69%, and no inter-assay equivalence was found38. KEYNOTE-522 used the combined positive score and IMpassion031 and NeoTRIP used the SP142 immune cell score. Part of what looks like a setting effect is an assay effect, with the general form of that problem set out in Predictive biomarkers and patient selection.

The second is that the tissue being scored is not the same tissue. Early disease is scored on a treatment-naive core of an intact primary. Advanced disease is often scored on an archival primary or on a metastasis. Immune cell positivity was 63.7% in primary tumours and 42.2% in unmatched metastatic lesions in one series scored with the SP142 assay39. Lymphocyte counts, PD-L1 and a coordinated set of interferon-regulated and antigen presentation genes are all lower in metastases40.

The third follows from the second, because the dynamic range of the marker differs. If the early population is shifted towards the positive end of the scale, the negative group is smaller and less extreme. A cut-point applied to a compressed distribution separates less, whatever the underlying biology.

The fourth is the combination partner that the checkpoint inhibitor is added to. In the early setting the checkpoint inhibitor is added to a curative multi-agent regimen containing a platinum. Cytotoxic therapy changes the infiltrate and PD-L1 expression itself41, and DNA damage activates innate sensing42. A strong combination partner compresses the difference between subgroups defined by a marker.

The fifth is that the statistical claim is weaker than it sounds. KEYNOTE-522 was not designed to test a PD-L1 interaction, and its PD-L1 negative subgroup was small12. Benefit regardless of PD-L1 means that no interaction was demonstrated. It does not mean that no interaction exists.

Within the early setting the marker has not been inert either. The PD-L1 positive co-primary population of IMpassion031 did not cross its significance boundary8. In NeoTRIP, PD-L1 was the strongest factor associated with response in multivariable analysis, with an odds ratio of 2.08, in a trial where the drug itself did not improve response6. A marker associated with response and not with the treatment effect is prognostic rather than predictive, a distinction defined in Analytical validity, clinical validity, and clinical utility defined.

Three other candidates are worth tracking and none is ready.

Tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes are prognostic and reproducibly scored under the convention in Tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes, biology and standardized scoring43,44. The first randomised signal that they might also be predictive of checkpoint benefit comes from A-BRAVE, where avelumab improved outcome only above a baseline stromal score of 30%. In the residual disease stratum the interaction p value was 0.01945. That is a biomarker analysis of a trial that missed its primary endpoint. Residual cancer burden was not predictive in the same analysis.

Tumour-specific class II major histocompatibility expression was associated with response to atezolizumab and not to chemotherapy alone in NeoTRIP46. It requires quantitative imaging mass cytometry that no routine laboratory runs.

Tumour mutational burden carries tumour-agnostic evidence from studies that had no breast cohort47. High burden is also uncommon in this disease48.

Interplay

The reason a percentage of PD-L1 staining fails to predict is the reason given in Part VI for most biomarker failure in this disease. What a checkpoint inhibitor releases is a relationship between cell types, not a property of one cell. A score computed on a block reports abundance and discards arrangement, exactly as a HER2 percentage does in Spatial patterns, clustered, mosaic, and scattered, and the spatial phenotypes in Inflamed, excluded, and desert phenotypes separate patients that no abundance measure can. There is a second point specific to the neoadjuvant setting. Treatment is being given to an intact primary and its draining nodes, which is where priming happens Systemic immunity, lymph node priming, and immune memory. A marker measured on a core taken before any of that describes the starting state of a system whose behaviour depends on what the first two cycles do to it.

7 · De-escalation after pathological complete response and the immune toxicity cost

After a pathological complete response the residual risk is low and it is front-loaded. In a pooled reconstruction of five neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy trials, most recurrences occurred within 24 months of randomisation, in patients with and without a complete response30. A de-escalation decision taken after surgery is therefore a decision about the next two years rather than the next ten.

The cost side of that trade is specific to this setting. More than half the patients in the control arm of KEYNOTE-522 reached a complete response without pembrolizumab12. A permanent endocrinopathy in one of those patients is a different event from the same endocrinopathy in incurable disease. The structural features that make these toxicities unlike chemotherapy toxicity are in Immune-related toxicity and its management and Immune-related toxicity in a curative-intent population.

Two arguments support omission after a complete response, and neither of them is a result. GeparNuevo produced a survival benefit with no adjuvant checkpoint phase at all31. And a complete response identifies a group whose absolute risk leaves little room for an adjuvant drug to work.

The obstacle to answering it is arithmetic rather than conceptual. Three-year event-free survival after a complete response on this regimen is high, so a non-inferiority trial needs either a very large sample or a margin wide enough to permit real harm. The margin problem behind that arithmetic is worked through in Designing de-escalation trials and the non-inferiority margin problem.

In practice

Do not stop adjuvant pembrolizumab early on the grounds that the tumour responded. That is the question OptimICE-pCR is asking, and it has not answered it.

Stopping it is defensible when a significant immune-related event occurs after a complete response. The remaining absolute benefit is small and unproven. The toxicity in front of you is neither.

Separate the two drugs when an immune event appears during the neoadjuvant phase. Hold the checkpoint inhibitor and continue the chemotherapy Immune-related toxicity in a curative-intent population.

Do not de-escalate local therapy on response alone. Nodal management after a complete response is a separate randomised question NSABP B-5149.

SCARLET asks whether the anthracycline can be removed from a pembrolizumab and platinum regimen. A non-inferior result would remove an anthracycline, and with it the cardiac and leukaemic risks, from a young population treated with curative intent.

NeoTRIP has yet to report the event-free survival endpoint it was powered for6. Its response result, reported as a secondary endpoint, was negative. A positive survival result would add a second example of the dissociation that GeparNuevo produced, and would strengthen the case that response is the wrong readout for this drug class.

OptimICE-pCR asks whether the adjuvant checkpoint phase can be dropped after a complete response. That question is discussed in De-escalation after pathological complete response and the immune toxicity cost and again in Adjuvant immunotherapy and the question stratified by response.

ASCENT-05/OptimICE-RD and TROPION-Breast03 concern the other half of the response split and are covered in Adjuvant and post-neoadjuvant therapy in triple-negative disease.

Four questions in this chapter have no trial running at all.

Nobody is removing carboplatin from the chemoimmunotherapy backbone, so its contribution there stays unmeasured.

Nobody is testing checkpoint blockade in stage I disease, where the regimen is increasingly used by extrapolation.

No biomarker-driven omission trial is running, because Biomarkers for immunotherapy benefit, and why PD-L1 fails here explains that there is no marker to build one on.

And no trial is comparing the neoadjuvant regimens against each other. Every comparison a clinician makes between KEYNOTE-522 and its alternatives is a cross-trial comparison, with all the confounding set out in The other immunotherapy trials and why they read out differently.
