1 · Scope, and why this subtype made the neoadjuvant platform work

Early disease · HER2+ · neoadjuvant

How much chemotherapy a HER2-positive tumour still needs

Is a pathological complete response worth raising, or is it only a marker of the tumour the patient already had?

Rationale
Preoperative and postoperative chemotherapy were compared head to head in operable disease. The order changed nothing about survival. So the reason to treat first is not more cure. It is downstaging of the breast and the axilla. It is also information, because in HER2-positive disease the response is graded on the surgical specimen. That grade separates outcomes steeply.
Experiment
The order of the same chemotherapy was randomised in operable disease of every subtype. Within HER2-positive disease, trastuzumab was added to a neoadjuvant anthracycline and taxane backbone in locally advanced and inflammatory tumours, with event-free survival rather than a pathological endpoint as the primary measure. A platform trial with a shared chemotherapy control then followed patients by whether they reached a complete response. Separately, residual invasive disease found at surgery was used as the entry criterion for a trial that randomised what was given afterwards.
Finding
The order of treatment does not change survival. What the specimen shows does. Patients who reach a complete response have few events. Those left with residual disease have many. Acting on that residual disease with a conjugate lowers recurrence against continuing the antibody. Adding trastuzumab to neoadjuvant chemotherapy raised event-free survival against the same chemotherapy alone, which is the one trial here that measured cure rather than the specimen.
Limitation
The association between response and outcome is measured within patients rather than between treatments. A drug that raises the proportion reaching a complete response has not thereby been shown to lower recurrence. The clearest counterexample sits in this cell. The lapatinib and trastuzumab pair raised pathological complete response against trastuzumab before surgery, then failed to improve disease-free survival against trastuzumab when the same combination was tested adjuvantly. The trial that fixed the equivalence of the two orders predates HER2 testing and used chemotherapy nobody now gives. It licenses treating first in general rather than this backbone in particular. The trial that added trastuzumab before surgery enrolled locally advanced and inflammatory disease, which is not the operable population in which the order question was settled.

If the endpoint is worth raising at all, what raises it?

Almost every methodological advantage of the neoadjuvant setting is larger here than anywhere else in breast cancer.

The tumour is in place, so response is measured rather than inferred. Response arrives in months rather than years, at rates high enough for a few hundred patients to separate two regimens. And response carries more prognostic information in this subtype than in any other. The pooled analysis of 12 trials and 11,955 patients found that association strongest in HER2-positive, hormone receptor-negative disease treated with trastuzumab. There the event-free survival hazard ratio, comparing patients who reached a complete response with those who did not, was 0.15. Across the whole pooled population it was 0.48, and for overall survival 0.361. Nowhere else in breast cancer does the specimen carry that much information about an individual.

That is why the questions asked in this cell are subtractive ones. Most patients here are cured by chemotherapy given with HER2 antibodies. Most of what the cure costs them comes from the chemotherapy rather than from the antibodies. Treating first does not itself add cure, because randomising the order of the same chemotherapy in operable disease changed neither disease-free nor overall survival2. That trial predates HER2 testing and used chemotherapy nobody now gives, so it licenses treating first in general rather than this backbone in particular. The reasons to treat first are downstaging of the breast and the axilla, and information.

The information is the part that changed practice. The specimen grades the response and the grade routes what follows, because residual invasive disease is the entry criterion for post-neoadjuvant intensification Post-neoadjuvant intensification for residual disease. Subtraction is therefore testable here in a way it is not after surgery, because a tumour that fails to respond announces itself before the operation. Chemotherapy-free and de-escalated neoadjuvant regimens records what happens when it announces itself too late.

Two consequences follow and they pull in opposite directions.

The first is productive. This is where HER2-directed drug development happens on a signal that is obtainable quickly and cheaply. Most of the agents in Targeted therapy and Antibody-drug conjugates and targeted delivery were first tested against this endpoint.

The second is a trap. A measurement that predicts outcome for an individual patient is not thereby a measurement that predicts the effect of a drug on outcome. Dual blockade and what the pathological complete response gains bought works that distinction through on this subtype's own trials. Its general statistical form belongs to Clinical trials and evidence interpretation and Endpoints, surrogates, and the overall survival question, and the decision architecture that brings a patient here at all belongs to Deciding whether to treat before surgery.

This chapter covers what is given before surgery. What is given afterwards is in Adjuvant and post-neoadjuvant therapy and Post-neoadjuvant intensification for residual disease. Hormone receptor-positive HER2-positive disease has a lower response rate and its own chapter in Early triple-positive disease, while scoring conventions, including residual cancer burden, are in Pathological assessment after neoadjuvant treatment.

2 · Chemotherapy backbones with trastuzumab

How much chemotherapy a HER2-positive tumour still needs, continued

Can the anthracycline come out of the neoadjuvant backbone, and what should stand in its place?

Rationale
An anthracycline costs cardiac function and carries a small leukaemia risk. It is given alongside an antibody that itself depresses left ventricular function. The pivotal adjuvant trial that ran a trastuzumab regimen with and without an anthracycline had already found the two close. That made the omission worth testing where the readout is fast.
Experiment
A cardiac safety trial ran three dual-blockade regimens before surgery, one of them carrying no anthracycline, with the pathological endpoint secondary. A randomised trial then varied the anthracycline alone, on a fixed taxane, carboplatin and dual-blockade backbone, with response as the endpoint and event-free survival on follow-up. Separately, carboplatin was added to a taxane and anthracycline backbone in a trial enrolling both triple-negative and HER2-positive disease. A further trial set weekly nab-paclitaxel with dual blockade against docetaxel and carboplatin with dual blockade, neither arm carrying an anthracycline. A non-inferiority trial then kept a three-weekly taxane and dual blockade in both arms and randomised the carboplatin. The taxane formulation was isolated on its own in an all-comers trial where both arms kept the anthracycline.
Finding
Dropping the anthracycline did not cost event-free survival against keeping it, on a backbone that was otherwise identical. In the cardiac safety trial the anthracycline-free regimen produced responses in the same range as the two regimens containing one. Symptomatic left ventricular systolic dysfunction appeared there only in the arms that carried an anthracycline. Adding platinum raised response in triple-negative disease and added nothing in the HER2-positive stratum of the same trial, with a formal test of interaction separating the two. Removing carboplatin from an anthracycline-free taxane and dual-blockade regimen was non-inferior for complete response and caused severe toxicity less often. Among anthracycline-free regimens, weekly nab-paclitaxel with dual blockade raised response against docetaxel and carboplatin with dual blockade. Substituting nab-paclitaxel for solvent-based paclitaxel also raised response in the all-comers trial that changed nothing else.
Limitation
The trial that varied the anthracycline alone was sized on the pathological endpoint. Its event-free survival comparison is a follow-up analysis on few events. The absence of a difference there is not a demonstration of equivalence. The cardiac safety trial put response second and never compared its arms formally, so its three rates sit beside each other rather than against each other. The comparison that favoured nab-paclitaxel in HER2-positive disease changed two things at once, the taxane formulation and the carboplatin. It cannot say which change produced the gain. It also enrolled Asian patients only. The trial that isolates the formulation kept the anthracycline in both arms and enrolled every subtype, so it settles that question outside this cell rather than inside it. The platinum question was first answered in a stratum of a trial powered for the triple-negative population. The trial later powered for it in HER2-positive disease gave a higher docetaxel dose in the arm without carboplatin. Its tolerability gain therefore cannot be credited to the omission alone. It was sized on the specimen rather than on survival, and it enrolled patients in mainland China only.

If the anthracycline is dispensable, is the rest of the cytotoxic backbone dispensable too?

Of the six trials in this table, only one was built to test the anthracycline itself. One more was built and powered to test what the regimen that replaces it still needs.

Start with what is not in dispute. NOAH is the only row whose primary endpoint was survival rather than the specimen. Adding trastuzumab to an anthracycline and taxane backbone improved event-free survival against the same chemotherapy alone3. It enrolled locally advanced and inflammatory disease, where the event rate was high enough to show that. A pooled analysis of two randomised trials found that concomitant trastuzumab doubled the rate of complete response in breast and nodes, with a relative risk of 2.07, and lowered the risk of relapse, without a significant increase in cardiac events4. Every other row argues about what to put beside the antibody rather than about whether to give it.

The anthracycline is the contested part, and the rows disagree about it for reasons of design rather than of drug.

TRAIN-2 is load-bearing, because it exchanged three cycles of an anthracycline regimen for three more cycles of taxane and carboplatin, with the same dual blockade in both arms, then followed patients for event-free survival5. Its primary endpoint, pathological complete response, had already reported without a meaningful advantage for the anthracycline6. Read its interval rather than its point estimate. The hazard ratio for an event-free survival event ran from 0.50 to 1.63. That excludes neither a substantial benefit from omitting the anthracycline nor a substantial harm. No non-inferiority margin was set in advance. The trial was sized on the specimen, and the survival comparison is a follow-up analysis on few events.

So the case for omission does not rest on demonstrated equivalence. It rests on a harm measured in the same randomisation. An ejection fraction fall of 10 points or more to below 50% occurred in 7.7% of patients given an anthracycline and in 3.2% of those who were not5. Designing de-escalation trials and the non-inferiority margin problem sets out why a measured harm beside an unmeasured efficacy risk is a stronger argument than a null result would be.

TRYPHAENA is quoted alongside it and should not be. It was powered as a cardiac safety study. Its response rates were secondary and its three arms were never compared formally7. Three rates sitting beside each other are a compatibility result. Its cardiac readout is the part worth quoting, and it points the same way as the randomised cardiac difference in TRAIN-2.

The remaining three rows vary the cytotoxic partner rather than the anthracycline. HELEN-006 set weekly nab-paclitaxel with dual blockade against docetaxel and carboplatin with dual blockade, neither arm carrying an anthracycline. The nab-paclitaxel arm responded more often8. It changed the taxane formulation and the carboplatin at once, so it cannot say which change produced the gain. It also enrolled Asian women only, which fixes the comparator rather than widening it. GeparSixto added carboplatin to a taxane and anthracycline backbone in a trial enrolling triple-negative and HER2-positive disease together. Platinum raised response in the triple-negative population and added nothing in the HER2-positive stratum, with a formal interaction test separating the two9. That stratum was not what the trial was powered for, so the null there is weak evidence rather than none.

neoCARHP is the trial that was powered for the platinum question in this subtype. It kept a three-weekly taxane and dual blockade in both arms and randomised the carboplatin, against a prespecified non-inferiority margin for complete response10. Omission met that margin with less frequent severe toxicity than the platinum regimen caused. It therefore tests the carboplatin, one of the two changes HELEN-006 made at once, without the other. The separation is not complete, because the docetaxel dose was higher in the arm without carboplatin. Part of the tolerability gain may belong to the taxane dose rather than to the omission. The trial enrolled women in mainland China, most with stage II disease, and has not reported survival10.

What the table cannot show is a survival comparison for any of the three rows that varied the cytotoxic partner. HELEN-006 and neoCARHP have not reported one, and GeparSixto's survival result belongs to its triple-negative population rather than to the HER2-positive stratum beside it. Nothing here randomises the taxane schedule outside Asia. Nothing randomises the anthracycline inside heavy nodal disease, which is where an interval as wide as TRAIN-2's leaves the most room.

In practice

Under dual blockade, treat anthracycline omission as the default question rather than the exception. The toxicity evidence is randomised and the efficacy evidence shows no signal of loss.

Do not describe the omission as proven non-inferior. Describe it as a trade of an unmeasured and probably small efficacy risk against a measured cardiac one.

Keep the anthracycline in reach for very high-volume nodal disease. Nobody randomised the question inside that group, and events are common enough there for the width of TRAIN-2's interval to matter.

3 · Dual blockade and what the pathological complete response gains bought

How much chemotherapy a HER2-positive tumour still needs, continued

Does adding a further drug to trastuzumab and a taxane, or replacing trastuzumab, raise pathological complete response?

Rationale
Trastuzumab binds one epitope. It does not prevent ligand-driven dimerisation. Pertuzumab blocks the dimerisation face. A small-molecule inhibitor blocks the kinase from inside the cell. Engineering the Fc region raises affinity for the activating receptor on immune effector cells. Releasing the brake on T cells asks the same question from the other side, which is whether one antibody leaves HER2 signalling, or immune killing, on the table.
Experiment
A second HER2-directed drug was added to trastuzumab on a taxane backbone in separate randomised trials of pertuzumab, of lapatinib and of pyrotinib, each reporting a pathological endpoint. One trial substituted an Fc-engineered antibody for trastuzumab alongside pertuzumab and paclitaxel. Another substituted lapatinib for trastuzumab throughout an anthracycline and taxane backbone. It followed survival after every patient had received trastuzumab after surgery. One added a checkpoint inhibitor instead of a second HER2-directed drug, with co-primary analyses in all comers and in the PD-L1-positive subset. One added a pan-PI3K inhibitor to trastuzumab and paclitaxel against placebo.
Finding
Pertuzumab added to trastuzumab and docetaxel raises the proportion reaching a complete response against trastuzumab and docetaxel, in the trial that established the pair and in the trial that repeated it. Pyrotinib added to that same backbone does the same against placebo. Lapatinib is not consistent. Added to trastuzumab on a taxane it raised response against trastuzumab in one trial and fell short of significance in another. The kinase inhibitors buy what they gain with diarrhoea. Improving the antibody rather than adding to it did not raise response against trastuzumab in the same regimen. Nor did adding a checkpoint inhibitor, in either of its co-primary populations. Lapatinib in place of trastuzumab lowered response. Survival did not differ significantly between the two pathways. The PI3K inhibitor did not raise response.
Limitation
The pathological endpoint is not scored the same way across these trials. Some require only the breast to be clear. Others require the nodes as well. The rates therefore cannot be set side by side, even where the regimens nearly match. The backbones differ too, from docetaxel alone, to paclitaxel or docetaxel after an anthracycline, to a dose-dense anthracycline followed by paclitaxel. The trial that repeated the pertuzumab result enrolled an Asian population, which fixes the comparator rather than widening it. The third lapatinib trial, the one that gave a taxane with no anthracycline, has published long-term survival and gene expression rather than the pathological comparison it was designed around. It cannot be counted on either side of the lapatinib question. The Fc-engineering result exists only as conference and registry material, with no primary publication. Its unsuccessful comparison cannot be read at the level of detail the others allow. The PI3K trial closed at a fraction of its planned size because of liver toxicity, so its negative result is weak. The lapatinib substitution trial gave every patient trastuzumab after surgery, for longer in the lapatinib arm, so its survival comparison is between two pathways rather than two drugs.

Adding a second drug to trastuzumab on a taxane raises the proportion of patients reaching a pathological complete response. That is the class finding and most of these rows carry it. What the table cannot do is put the rates on one axis.

Compare the endpoint column before the result column. NeoSphere, NeoALTTO, NSABP B-41, CALGB 40601 and NeoPHOEBE scored the breast alone. PEONY, PHEDRA and IMpassion050 required the nodes to be clear as well. GeparQuinto / GBG 44, HER2-positive cohort also required both to be free of in situ disease. The backbones differ by more than the endpoint does, from docetaxel alone, to paclitaxel after doxorubicin and cyclophosphamide, to docetaxel after epirubicin and cyclophosphamide, to a dose-dense anthracycline followed by paclitaxel, to six weeks of antibody before any chemotherapy at all. Four of the nine ran a placebo control. The rest were open-label.

The lapatinib rows show what that does to a comparison. Added to trastuzumab and a taxane, lapatinib raised breast response decisively in NeoALTTO, then missed significance in NSABP B-41 and again in CALGB 40601. The drug and the dose were not the variable. The control arms were. The trastuzumab control reached 29.5% in NeoALTTO, against 52.5% in NSABP B-41 and 46% in CALGB 40601. NeoALTTO gave six weeks of antibody alone and no anthracycline before surgery. B-41 gave four cycles of doxorubicin and cyclophosphamide first. A second HER2-directed drug has most room where the control arm leaves most residual disease, which is a statement about control arms rather than about lapatinib.

GeparQuinto / GBG 44, HER2-positive cohort asked the lapatinib question from the other side. It gave lapatinib in place of trastuzumab, throughout epirubicin and cyclophosphamide followed by docetaxel, and complete responses fell11. Lapatinib was also harder to give. A third of the lapatinib group discontinued treatment. That settles the substitution before surgery. It does not settle what the lower rate cost, which is the question below.

The row the pertuzumab decision rests on is NeoSphere, because it is the randomised comparison against trastuzumab and docetaxel, which is what the clinic would otherwise give. PEONY repeated that comparison with a stricter endpoint, independent central review and a placebo control12. It enrolled Asian women, so it fixes the comparator rather than widening the population. PHEDRA asked the same question with a kinase inhibitor and answered it the same way. The gain was bought with diarrhoea, grade 3 or worse in 44.4% of the pyrotinib group against 5.1% of the placebo group13.

Among the trials that added a drug, IMpassion050 is the only unambiguous negative. What it added was not HER2-directed. Atezolizumab changed nothing in either co-primary population, and the PD-L1-positive analysis pointed the wrong way14. The five grade 5 events at the primary analysis all occurred in the atezolizumab arm. NeoPHOEBE also added a drug aimed elsewhere, the pan-PI3K inhibitor buparlisib. Its negative is ambiguous for a plainer reason. Liver toxicity stopped accrual at a fifth of the planned size. The buparlisib arm also received less of its trastuzumab and paclitaxel15. A trial that cannot deliver its drug cannot say what the drug does. Its receptor-positive subgroup is taken up in Reciprocal cross-talk and what it predicts for treatment. MARGOT / TBCRC 052 put an Fc-engineered antibody in place of trastuzumab beside pertuzumab and paclitaxel. It has no primary publication and no tabulated result, so it settles nothing in either direction.

Now follow the response gains to their outcomes, because that is the reason this section exists.

NeoSphere was followed for five years. Five-year progression-free survival was 86% in the pertuzumab, trastuzumab and docetaxel group and 81% in the trastuzumab and docetaxel group16. There were roughly 107 patients per arm, so the trial could never have measured an outcome difference of that size. The trial that generates a response difference is never the trial that can price it.

NeoALTTO is the cleaner case, because its drug pair was then tested adjuvantly at scale. ALTTO randomised more than eight thousand patients and missed its disease-free survival threshold against trastuzumab alone17. The same pair raised the specimen endpoint before surgery and did not raise disease-free survival after it.

CALGB 40601 is the same failure running the other way. Its primary response comparison did not reach significance18. Its survival comparison, from the same randomisation and at more than seven years, did, for relapse-free and for overall survival19. A reader who stopped at the specimen would have concluded the drug did nothing.

GeparQuinto shows the endpoint failing on a deficit rather than a gain. The arm with fewer complete responses did not do significantly worse on disease-free or overall survival at long-term follow-up20. Every patient went on to adjuvant trastuzumab. The lapatinib arm received HER2-directed treatment for longer in all, so the survival comparison is between two whole pathways rather than two drugs. Within the trial, a complete response separated overall survival in the trastuzumab arm and not in the lapatinib arm. A complete response did not carry the same prognostic weight whichever drug produced it, which is what ARTemis found with bevacizumab Pathologic complete response as an endpoint, what it does and does not predict.

Put the four together and the endpoint is not biased in a fixed direction. It can overstate an outcome gain and it can understate one, which is what makes correcting for it impossible. Across 12 trials, the correlation between a treatment's increase in pathological complete response and its effect on event-free survival had a coefficient of determination of 0.03. For overall survival it was 0.24, and the authors judged neither sufficient to validate the endpoint1. Endpoints, surrogates, and the overall survival question sets out why that is the quantity a surrogate has to satisfy.

Caution

None of this argues against dual blockade, since pertuzumab produces a real adjuvant benefit in node-positive disease Dual blockade in the adjuvant setting and the neoadjuvant response gain pointed in the right direction. What fails is the inference from the size of a response gain to the size of an outcome gain. A regimen that raises pathological complete response has not thereby been shown to improve survival by any particular amount.

The overall survival column would settle these questions and it is mostly blank. No trial here randomised a second HER2-directed drug before surgery with survival as its primary endpoint, and none compared two second drugs against each other. A response improvement licenses a hypothesis about outcome. It licenses a change of regimen only where response itself changes the next treatment, which in this subtype it does Post-neoadjuvant intensification for residual disease.

4 · Chemotherapy-free and de-escalated neoadjuvant regimens

How much chemotherapy a HER2-positive tumour still needs, continued

Can conventional chemotherapy be left out altogether and the HER2 block given alone?

Rationale
Some HER2-positive tumours depend on the receptor to the point of addiction. In the trial that established the antibody pair, the arm given both antibodies without a taxane still produced complete responses in a minority of patients. If those tumours can be identified, the cytotoxic drug is being given to them for nothing.
Experiment
Chemotherapy-free dual blockade was given for a fixed window before surgery, with endocrine therapy added for oestrogen receptor-positive disease, across a series of single-arm and randomised phase II trials. One randomised the length of that window, a shorter course against a longer one. One asked whether the intrinsic subtype called from the diagnostic biopsy predicts who responds. One tested formally whether early responders given dual blockade alone could be spared the taxane.
Finding
Chemotherapy-free blockade produces complete responses in a minority. That minority is larger with the longer window than with the shorter one. It is larger in oestrogen receptor-negative than in receptor-positive disease. It is larger in tumours called HER2-enriched than in the rest. The trial that tested omission formally did not show non-inferiority against the taxane. On that test the taxane was not dispensable, even in early responders.
Limitation
Most of these trials have no chemotherapy arm and no survival endpoint. Nothing in them can support equivalence to chemotherapy. The duration trial combined a phase II design in its experimental arm with a pick-the-winner design and was explicitly not powered to compare the two windows. The gap between them is a numerical difference that was never tested. The trial that did test omission set early responders in the chemotherapy-free arm against every patient in the taxane arm, which compares a selected group with an unselected one. It also enrolled hormone receptor-negative disease only. The subtype trial had no comparator. A subtype that identifies responders to chemotherapy-free blockade has not thereby been shown to identify patients who can forgo chemotherapy. The single-arm windows and the duration trial all used lapatinib with trastuzumab rather than the antibody pair now given, so their rates do not transfer to the regimen a clinic would actually use.

That is a failure mode peculiar to the preoperative window. The tumour is still in the patient, so an underpowered regimen does not merely fail to shrink it. Where these trials say what follows surgery, a patient with residual disease went on to receive chemotherapy, a conjugate, or both27,30,31. A reader who takes these rows as evidence that chemotherapy can be dropped has read a response-adapted strategy as a subtraction.

Anthracycline omission is the de-escalation with randomised survival evidence behind it5. It is a different proposition from this one and should be asked first.

5 · Response-adapted strategies and imaging-guided escalation

How much chemotherapy a HER2-positive tumour still needs, continued

Can the response to the first weeks of treatment decide who needs chemotherapy, rather than the decision being made for everyone in advance?

Rationale
A baseline assay predicts. A short exposure tests. A tumour whose metabolic activity collapses under antibody alone has demonstrated the dependence an assay was trying to infer. The same logic applies at the other end of the course, because a complete response at surgery is the strongest evidence a tumour can give that what it received was enough.
Experiment
One trial randomised a response-adapted strategy against chemotherapy with dual blockade, giving antibodies alone and adding chemotherapy only to patients whose tumours failed to respond on imaging. One tested formally whether the metabolic change measured early in the first cycle predicts pathological complete response, against a prespecified accuracy threshold. Others adapted at the far end, using magnetic resonance imaging to decide when enough chemotherapy had been given, or withholding further chemotherapy from patients whose specimen showed a complete response.
Finding
Imaging separates responders from non-responders early. The adapted groups have done well on the endpoints reported so far. Adherence to withholding chemotherapy after a complete response was high where it was measured against a feasibility target. The formal test of predictive accuracy missed its prespecified threshold. The signal underneath it was real, since tumours without a substantial metabolic fall rarely reached a complete response.
Limitation
The randomised response-adapted trial reported its recurrence endpoint against a fixed threshold rather than against its own comparator arm. That arm was small by design. So the strategy was measured against an expectation rather than against chemotherapy. The trials that adapt on the pathological response are single-arm. Their recurrence and survival endpoints have since been reported and are excellent, and that changes nothing about the inference. With no comparator, an excellent outcome after a complete response describes the prognosis of a responder rather than the effect of stopping. The trial that tested metabolic prediction required oestrogen receptor-negative disease. It says nothing about the receptor-positive majority, where complete responses are least common and the decision is hardest.

Response-adapted designs differ in when they look, and the timing decides what they can claim.

Two of the five rows look while the tumour is still in the patient. PHERGain uses metabolic imaging after two cycles to decide whether chemotherapy is added before surgery. Imaging responders without a pathological complete response receive chemotherapy after surgery. TRAIN-3 uses magnetic resonance imaging to decide when enough chemotherapy has been given, sending the patient to surgery once the scan is clear34. The other three look at the specimen. DAPHNe, CompassHER2 pCR and PHERGain-2 all withhold further chemotherapy from patients whose surgery showed a pathological complete response.

Where they agree is unremarkable and worth saying once. Every adapted group in the table has done well on the endpoint reported so far, and adherence to withholding chemotherapy was high wherever it was measured against a feasibility target35. That is what a cohort selected by its own good response should look like.

Where they diverge is in what the adaptation is for. Early adaptation escalates, adding chemotherapy to patients who fail to respond. Late adaptation de-escalates, removing chemotherapy from patients who already have. The second inherits the strongest evidence in the subtype, because the same logic run in the other direction is what licenses post-neoadjuvant intensification Post-neoadjuvant intensification for residual disease. The first does not.

Early adaptation is also escalation on a surrogate of a surrogate. Metabolic response early in treatment is not pathological response at surgery, and the two are separate measurements with separate error. TBCRC026 tested that relation formally, scoring the metabolic change on day 15 of the first cycle against a prespecified accuracy threshold. A real signal that misses a prespecified accuracy bar is not a licence to route treatment on it. That study required oestrogen receptor-negative disease, so it says nothing about the majority in whom the decision is hardest.

PHERGain is the only randomised row and the strategy currently rests on it. State what it established precisely, because it is usually overstated. Its three-year invasive disease-free survival was measured against a fixed threshold rather than against its randomised comparator, and that comparator arm held 71 patients by design32. Grade 3 or higher treatment-related adverse events occurred in 33% of the response-adapted group against 62% of the chemotherapy group. That is a real toxicity advantage attached to a strategy nobody has measured against chemotherapy.

CompassHER2 pCR is much the largest row, and its response rates by receptor status already say where the strategy will be usable37. A fall of 10% or more in global quality of life at one year affected 42.8% of patients on a regimen carrying no conventional cytotoxic at all31. 6 · Conjugates in the neoadjuvant setting

How much chemotherapy a HER2-positive tumour still needs, continued

Can an antibody-drug conjugate replace conventional chemotherapy before surgery?

Rationale
A conjugate delivers a cytotoxic to cells that display the target. In a tumour selected for displaying that target, it ought to reproduce the cell kill of chemotherapy while sparing everything else much of the exposure. The neoadjuvant setting reads that claim out in months rather than years.
Experiment
The substitution was made randomly before surgery, conjugate with pertuzumab against docetaxel, carboplatin and dual blockade, with pathological complete response as the endpoint. In hormone receptor-positive disease the same conjugate was randomised against trastuzumab with endocrine therapy. The substitution was also tested after surgery, conjugate with pertuzumab against a taxane with dual blockade, both arms following an anthracycline. A single-arm study of conjugate with pertuzumab defined heterogeneous and non-heterogeneous tumours prospectively, from biopsies taken at more than one site in each tumour rather than from a single score. A newer conjugate was then tested not as a replacement for chemotherapy but as the opening of the regimen, against an anthracycline opening, with the same antibody and taxane triplet completing both.
Finding
Substituting the conjugate for chemotherapy before surgery lowered response against that chemotherapy, even though severe adverse events were far less frequent than on the chemotherapy it replaced. The same substitution after surgery did not improve freedom from invasive disease against the taxane with dual blockade. The heterogeneity study accounts for part of the shortfall, because tumours carrying regions without the target essentially did not respond, while tumours uniform for it responded well. The newer conjugate, placed before the taxane triplet rather than instead of it, raised response against the anthracycline opening.
Limitation
The heterogeneity comparison was planned prospectively. Its groups were still not randomised. Only a small minority of evaluable tumours were heterogeneous, so the response rate in that group rests on very few patients. The hormone receptor-positive conjugate trial used trastuzumab with endocrine therapy as its control, a comparator with no cytotoxic content at all. Beating it says nothing about replacing chemotherapy. The adjuvant comparison is a different question in a different setting, since both of its arms also received an anthracycline. The newer conjugate was tested in high-risk disease only. Its event-free survival is barely mature. Its winning arm still contains a taxane. These are different questions about conjugates rather than repeated confirmations of one.

7 · Unresolved questions and what reads out next

Both awaited trials test omission rather than addition, and each will settle less than its size suggests. Both are single-arm by design, so neither can measure what the omitted treatment was worth.

CompassHER2 pCR will report recurrence-free survival in patients who reached a pathological complete response and then had chemotherapy omitted37. A low rate will make omission defensible without measuring the loss, because no arm kept the chemotherapy. PHERGain-2 will report a recurrence-free interval after a fully chemotherapy-free preoperative phase in a selected population, where the same limitation applies to a smaller sample31.

Whether the response advantage in DESTINY-Breast11 becomes an event-free survival advantage. That needs follow-up rather than a new trial, and the current maturity is too low to support a decision Conjugates in the neoadjuvant setting.

This chapter treats response as a property of a tumour and a drug. Part VI argues it is also a property of a population of cells with unequal antigen. A tumour scored HER2-positive on one core may contain a compartment that is not, and that compartment is what survives to become residual disease Consequences for pathologic complete response in the neoadjuvant setting. Read that way, the response rates in these tables are partly measurements of how uniform each enrolled population happened to be. That is not speculation here, because the one trial that biopsied several sites per tumour found response and antigen uniformity tracking each other40. It also explains why a bystander-capable payload changes the arithmetic in Conjugates in the neoadjuvant setting and a receptor-blocking antibody does not.
