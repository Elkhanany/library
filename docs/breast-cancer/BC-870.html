Part XII · Metastatic systemic therapy by subtype and line · Chapter 68

Endocrine therapy and receptor degraders in metastatic disease

ESR1 mutation defeats oestrogen deprivation and not receptor degradation, which is why the degrader trials split where they do.

1 · Scope, and the sequence this chapter assumes

This chapter covers endocrine therapy for metastatic hormone receptor-positive, HER2-negative disease. It covers the two backbones, the acquired alteration that separates them, the oral receptor degraders, the first proteolysis-targeting chimera result, and the pathway inhibitors that are added to an endocrine partner.

Three things are deliberately left to neighbouring chapters. CDK4/6 inhibition is in CDK4/6 inhibition in metastatic disease, combinations and sequencing, including which inhibitor to choose and whether to continue it. Conjugates and chemotherapy are in Conjugates and chemotherapy in later-line hormone receptor-positive disease. The clinical framing of the four biomarker branches is in Metastatic hormone receptor-positive, HER2-negative disease, which this chapter expands rather than repeats. Receptor biology is in Estrogen receptor signaling and endocrine resistance and agent pharmacology in Endocrine therapy.

The sequence assumed here is the common one. Most patients arrive having had an adjuvant aromatase inhibitor, then a first-line aromatase inhibitor with a CDK4/6 inhibitor. Almost every endocrine decision in this chapter is therefore made in a tumour already selected by oestrogen deprivation. That is not a detail of ordering. It determines which alterations are present and which drugs still have a target.

The tables are generated from the trial registry when the book is built. Four slices appear below: first-line regimens, therapy after progression, the pathway combinations, and the trials still running. A new readout enters the registry once and appears in every table whose filter matches it. The prose does not repeat a figure the table carries, except where the argument is about the figure itself.

2 · Aromatase inhibitors and fulvestrant as backbones

Two backbones exist and they fail differently. Oestrogen deprivation removes the ligand, using an aromatase inhibitor with ovarian suppression where ovaries are functioning. Receptor-directed therapy leaves the ligand alone and acts on the protein. That distinction is the argument of this whole chapter, so it is worth stating before any trial.

Only two randomised trials address the backbone question directly. FALCON compared fulvestrant with anastrozole in patients who had never received endocrine therapy1. CONFIRM compared two fulvestrant doses and settled the schedule now in use2.

First-line regimens. In each of these the endocrine backbone is present and the comparison is about what is added to or substituted into it.

The FALCON population has largely disappeared. Endocrine therapy-naive advanced disease now means de novo metastatic presentation without prior treatment, or relapse many years after tamoxifen alone. Most patients presenting today carry adjuvant aromatase inhibitor exposure.

The trial is still worth reading for its final result, because the progression-free survival advantage did not become a survival advantage8. The cleanest available comparison of the two backbones says that in an unselected, endocrine-naive population the choice does not change how long a patient lives. A signal persisted in the 208 patients with non-visceral disease, where median overall survival was 65.2 months against 47.8 months, with a hazard ratio of 0.85 and a confidence interval from 0.60 to 1.208. That is a subgroup with an interval crossing one. Read it as a hypothesis.

The fulvestrant dose question is pharmacology rather than biology. The drug is given intramuscularly, reaches steady state slowly, and needs a day-15 loading dose in the first cycle. CONFIRM established that 250 mg was underdosing2. A patient who received the lower dose historically has not had an adequate trial of the drug.

The practical position is that the backbone is now chosen by two things. The first is what the tumour has already seen. The second is whether an ESR1 mutation is detectable.

3 · ESR1 mutation and what it changes

ESR1 mutation is acquired under aromatase inhibitor exposure and is not found in patients who have never received one. The evidence for that, including how prevalence differs between the adjuvant and metastatic settings, is set out in Endocrine therapy and the emergence of ESR1 alterations and is not repeated here.

Three consequences follow and they should be held separately.

The mechanism is ligand independence. A mutant receptor adopts an active conformation without oestrogen, so lowering oestrogen removes nothing that the tumour still needs. The receptor protein remains present and remains transcriptionally active. A drug that acts on the protein therefore still has a target.

The failure is specific. ESR1 mutation predicts failure of oestrogen deprivation, not failure of endocrine therapy as a class. This is the organising fact of the chapter and it is frequently misread. Patients whose disease carries the alteration are often described as endocrine-resistant and moved to chemotherapy, which discards the one branch where the mechanism and the available drugs line up.

The mutation describes a population rather than a tumour. It is subclonal and frequently polyclonal within a single patient. Its prevalence rises across successive endocrine lines Evolution across successive lines and the cost of each switch.

The measurement side is separable from all of that. Detection is a plasma assay, not a tissue assay. Archival primary tissue will be negative because the selection had not yet happened, so a negative result on an old block carries no information. In plasma, sensitivity at low variant allele fractions is the limiting factor. Disease that sheds little DNA can give a false negative. The respective blind spots of tissue and plasma are developed in Liquid biopsy and longitudinal monitoring.

Caution

A detectable ESR1 mutation is not a marker of general endocrine resistance, and it is not a prognostic curiosity either. Its only established use is narrow and specific. It says that continued oestrogen deprivation has lost its target, and that a receptor-directed agent has not. Reading it as a reason to abandon endocrine therapy inverts the result.

Interplay

This is the one place in the book where the evolutionary argument of Part VI converts cleanly into a prescription. Treatment applies a selection, the selection is convergent at the level of a single gene, the gene is detectable in blood, and a drug class exists whose mechanism survives that particular escape. Every step in that chain is required. Where any one is missing, as with cyclin E1 in CDK4/6 inhibition in metastatic disease, combinations and sequencing or with antigen loss in Antibody-drug conjugates and targeted delivery, the same evolutionary reasoning produces knowledge and no action.

Endocrine therapy after progression. Every randomised oral SERD trial sits here, alongside the pathway combinations argued in the next section.

EMERALD met its primary endpoint in both the whole population and the ESR1-mutant population, with a larger effect in the latter15. EMBER-3 met it in the mutant population and missed it in the whole population9. SERENA-2 was a dose-finding phase 2 against fulvestrant11.

The two negative trials carry more weight than either positive one, because the split appears in them without being designed for. acelERA missed its primary endpoint in all patients. Of 232 patients evaluable for circulating tumour DNA, the 90 with a detectable ESR1 mutation had a hazard ratio for progression of 0.60, against 0.88 in the 142 without one12. AMEERA-3 also missed, with a hazard ratio slightly above one overall and 0.9 in the 120 of 280 patients carrying a baseline ESR1 mutation13. Two trials that failed reproduced the same pattern as the two that succeeded. That is what makes the pattern hard to explain away as selective reporting.

Caution

The control arm in every one of these trials is endocrine monotherapy, usually fulvestrant, given to patients who have already progressed on endocrine therapy. Median progression-free survival in those control arms runs from roughly two to five months. A hazard ratio measured against a nearly inert comparator establishes that a drug is active. It does not establish where the drug belongs relative to a CDK4/6 inhibitor combination, a pathway inhibitor or a conjugate. No oral degrader has yet shown an overall survival benefit in any of these trials.

Two trials moved the decision earlier by acting on a molecular change rather than a scan. PADA-1 monitored patients on a first-line aromatase inhibitor with palbociclib and, on a rising ESR1 mutation without progression, switched the endocrine partner to fulvestrant7. SERENA-6 used the same trigger and switched to camizestrant while continuing the same inhibitor3. The design argument, and its limits, are developed in Acting on evolution, intermittent dosing, adaptive therapy, pre-emptive switching.

The operational cost of that strategy is easy to underestimate. It requires plasma testing every two to three months in patients who are well and responding. It then commits the service to acting on a molecular result while the scan is still reassuring.

5 · Targeted degradation and the first PROTAC result

A proteolysis-targeting chimera is a bifunctional molecule. One end binds the oestrogen receptor. The other recruits an E3 ubiquitin ligase. Bringing the two together marks the receptor for ubiquitination and proteasomal destruction. The molecule is then released and can act again, so the mechanism is catalytic rather than stoichiometric. A conventional degrader binds the receptor and destabilises the molecule it is bound to.

VERITAC-2 tested that chemistry against fulvestrant in a randomised phase 3 trial. Its split is the teaching case of the chapter. Vepdegestrant significantly prolonged progression-free survival in the ESR1-mutant population. In the full randomised population it did not10.

The comparator is what makes this informative. Fulvestrant is itself a receptor degrader. The result is therefore degrader against degrader, so it cannot be explained by saying that degradation beats antagonism. Two readings remain and the trial does not separate them. Mutant disease may be more dependent on the amount of receptor protein present, so more complete degradation matters more. Or fulvestrant may simply perform worse against a mutant receptor, given its incomplete receptor occupancy at the licensed dose. Both predict the observed split.

The wider point is the one to carry away. Three chemically distinct approaches to the receptor, the intramuscular degrader, the oral degraders and now the chimera, produce the same biomarker-defined pattern. What selects the responding patient is the resistance mechanism, not the chemistry used against it. A new molecule in this class should be expected to reproduce the split until a trial shows otherwise.

Two limits apply as they do to the whole class. No survival data exist. No trial has compared this agent with a CDK4/6 inhibitor combination at the same point in the sequence.

Pathway inhibition added to an endocrine partner. INAVO120 is a first-line trial and so does not appear in the table above.

SOLAR-1 is the design that made PIK3CA mutation a predictive marker rather than a prognostic one, because it enrolled a marker-negative cohort and reported it17. That argument belongs to trial methodology and is worked through in Biomarker-selected, basket, umbrella, and platform trials. What matters clinically is the consequence. A drug licensed on the strength of a marker-negative comparison is on firmer ground than one licensed on an entry criterion.

CAPItello-291 widened the biomarker to include AKT1 and PTEN alterations, and it enrolled without selecting14. It showed benefit in the whole population as well as in the altered group. That is a weaker basis for a predictive claim than SOLAR-1 provides, for a mechanical reason. The overall population contains the altered patients, so an overall effect is expected whenever the altered subgroup benefits. Separating the marker's predictive value requires the marker-negative analysis, which in this trial was exploratory.

INAVO120 selected the most resistant population in the group, requiring relapse during or within 12 months of adjuvant endocrine therapy, and it used a triplet4. It is the only trial in this section with a significant survival benefit20. Whether that reflects the population, the triplet or the drug cannot be separated from a single trial.

BOLERO-2 predates biomarker selection entirely19. Everolimus with exemestane delayed progression in unselected patients and did not extend life21. It remains useful where sequencing is unavailable and the mechanism is otherwise unaddressed.

In practice

Test for PIK3CA, AKT1 and PTEN alterations at progression on first-line therapy, not later. The result determines three of the four options in this section.

Use plasma first and tissue second. A plasma-negative PIK3CA result in low-burden or low-shedding disease should be followed by tissue before the option is discarded Liquid biopsy and longitudinal monitoring.

Read PTEN carefully. It is usually reported as loss of immunohistochemical staining, which is a different kind of claim from a sequenced mutation and is less reproducible between laboratories.

Plan the metabolic monitoring before the first prescription, not after the first fasting glucose. Hyperglycaemia with a PI3K-alpha inhibitor is an on-target effect and is predictable. The discontinuation rate it produced in SOLAR-1 is in PI3K, AKT, and mTOR directed therapy and its toxicity burden and is the number to hold when consenting a patient.

Ask who else is managing the patient. These agents need a plan for glucose, rash, diarrhoea and stomatitis that survives a weekend, and that plan belongs in the notes rather than in the consultation.

Endocrine and pathway trials that have not reported.

Three trials ask the same question and it is the question that would rewrite this chapter. SERENA-4, VERITAC-3 and persevERA each test an oral receptor degrader against an aromatase inhibitor as the first-line backbone, with palbociclib in both arms. All three enrol without selecting for ESR1 mutation, which is the correct design, because the alteration is rare before treatment. A positive result would move the degraders from salvage to backbone. It would also make the pre-emptive switching strategy redundant, since there would be nothing to switch from.

CAPItello-292 and VIKTORIA-1 ask whether pathway inhibition belongs alongside continued CDK4/6 blockade rather than after it. VIKTORIA-1 randomises separately by PIK3CA status, so it can say something about the marker as well as the drug.

INAVO121 compares two PI3K-alpha inhibitors directly. It is the only trial in this area that will produce a within-class comparison. Within-class comparisons are what the field consistently lacks.

EvoPAR-Breast01 removes first-line CDK4/6 inhibition altogether in germline or somatic BRCA1, BRCA2 or PALB2 disease, replacing it with a PARP1-selective inhibitor and an oral degrader.

Three questions have no trial attached to them and should be named. Nothing compares an oral degrader with a conjugate at the same decision point. Nothing randomises the order in which endocrine-based and conjugate therapy are given, which is the decision made in Conjugates and chemotherapy in later-line hormone receptor-positive disease. And no oral degrader trial is powered for overall survival, so the class may reach routine use without a survival result at all.
