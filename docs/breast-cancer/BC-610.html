Young adults, fertility, and pregnancy

Decisions taken in weeks that a patient lives with for forty years.

1 · Breast cancer in adolescents and young adults

The adolescent and young adult boundary is administrative. Registries draw it at 15 to 39 years, and nothing in the biology changes at either edge. The category is useful because the consequences of treatment run for decades rather than because the tumours are a class.

Young-onset disease is enriched for higher stage at presentation, higher grade, and for triple-negative and HER2-positive phenotypes. The incidence trend and its interpretation are set out in The rise of young-onset disease. What is less often quoted is where the survival deficit actually sits.

One multicentre cohort examined 17,575 women with stage I to III disease, of whom 1,916 were aged 40 years or younger. Median follow-up was 6.4 years. After adjustment, breast cancer mortality was higher in the younger group, with a hazard ratio of 1.4 and a 95% confidence interval of 1.2 to 1.7. Stratified by subtype, the excess was significant in luminal A disease. The hazard ratio there was 2.1, with an interval of 1.4 to 3.2. It was also significant in luminal B disease, with a hazard ratio of 1.4 and an interval of 1.1 to 1.9. It was borderline in triple-negative disease and absent in HER2-positive disease1.

Caution

The intuitive account is that young women do badly because they get aggressive subtypes. This dataset says the opposite. Once subtype is held constant, the age effect concentrates in the luminal tumours, which are the ones with the best average prognosis. Age is doing something inside hormone receptor positive disease that subtype assignment does not capture.

Three tasks belong at diagnosis and are time-critical. Germline testing changes surgical and systemic decisions, and the indications are in Inherited susceptibility and familial breast cancer. Fertility referral must precede the first dose, which is the subject of Fertility preservation before treatment. And the conversation about pregnancy after treatment starts now, because it constrains the endocrine plan described in Adjuvant chemotherapy and the genomic assay decision.

2 · Fertility preservation before treatment

Two different problems hide under one heading. One is banking gametes or embryos, which preserves the material. The other is protecting the ovary during chemotherapy, which preserves function. They are not substitutes for each other, and a patient offered only the second has been offered half of what exists2.

Cryopreservation of mature oocytes or embryos after controlled ovarian stimulation is the established method. Ovarian tissue cryopreservation is now an option rather than an experimental procedure. Random-start protocols allow stimulation to begin at any point in the cycle, which removes most of the scheduling delay that used to make referral feel incompatible with starting treatment.

Ovarian protection with a gonadotropin-releasing hormone agonist has been tested directly. In POEMS/SWOG S0230, premenopausal women with operable hormone receptor negative disease received standard chemotherapy with or without goserelin. Among 135 patients with complete primary endpoint data, ovarian failure at 2 years occurred in 8% of the goserelin group and 22% of the chemotherapy-alone group. The odds ratio was 0.30, with a 95% confidence interval of 0.09 to 0.973. An individual patient level meta-analysis of five trials and 873 patients found premature ovarian insufficiency in 14.1% with the agonist and 30.9% without it. The adjusted odds ratio was 0.38, with an interval of 0.26 to 0.57. Post-treatment pregnancy occurred in 10.3% and 5.5% respectively4.

Caution

Most of this evidence uses amenorrhoea or a postmenopausal follicle-stimulating hormone level as the endpoint. Those are measurements of menstrual and hormonal status. They are not measurements of the capacity to conceive. Anti-Mullerian hormone and antral follicle count measure reserve, which is a third quantity again. A patient told that her ovarian function was protected has been told something narrower than she will hear.

In practice

Refer before the first dose, not after the first cycle. The window is the interval between diagnosis and treatment, and it closes.

Offer cryopreservation and ovarian suppression as separate decisions. A patient may reasonably take one, both, or neither.

Ask what the patient wants, not what her age predicts. Uptake is poor and the commonest reason is that the conversation did not happen.

3 · Pregnancy-associated and postpartum breast cancer

Pregnancy-associated breast cancer is a phrase that collapses two different situations. One is diagnosis during gestation. The other is diagnosis in the years after delivery. Only the first is a treatment problem in the obstetric sense. The second is a biological problem, and it is the more consequential of the two.

Parity confers long-term protection and short-term risk. A pooled analysis of 15 prospective studies found that risk in parous women remained above that of nulliparous women for more than 20 years after childbirth. Crossover to reduced risk was observed for oestrogen receptor positive disease and not for receptor negative disease. The pattern was more pronounced with a family history, with older age at first birth, and with more births. Breastfeeding did not modify it5.

The mechanism is postpartum involution, developed in Pregnancy, lactation, and postpartum involution and Pregnancy, postpartum involution, and tissue remodeling. Involution is a programmed tissue remodelling event with the features of wound healing, including collagen deposition and COX-2 dependent inflammation, and it promotes progression of ductal carcinoma in situ in model systems6. The tumour is not more aggressive by grade. The tissue it grows in is transiently permissive.

The clinical correlate is a stage-independent excess of metastasis. In a cohort study, diagnosis within 10 years post partum was associated with increased risk of metastasis. The excess was greatest in stage I and stage II disease, was present in both receptor positive and receptor negative cancers, and persisted in receptor positive cases for up to 15 years after diagnosis. Postpartum cases showed more lymphovascular invasion and more nodal involvement, and no increase in Ki67 index7.

The measurement caveat matters here more than usual. The window chosen to define postpartum disease determines the answer. Five years and ten years are different definitions, and studies using them are not directly comparable. Neither is a biological boundary.

Interplay

Postpartum disease is the cleanest available demonstration that host tissue state, rather than tumour genotype, can carry prognostic weight. The tumours are not separated from others by the receptor and grade measurements of Receptor assessment and the measurement problem. The stroma they arose in is different. This is the microenvironment argument of Functional states of the microenvironment arriving through an obstetric history.

4 · Treatment during pregnancy and lactation

The governing principle is that the pregnancy is usually continued and the cancer usually treated. Termination has not been shown to improve oncologic outcome, and the decision belongs to the patient rather than to the algorithm.

Diagnosis is delayed by the pregnant breast. Ultrasound is the first imaging test, mammography with abdominal shielding is acceptable, and core biopsy is unaffected by gestation. Staging avoids computed tomography and isotope bone scan where alternatives exist.

Surgery is safe in any trimester. Sentinel node mapping with technetium radiocolloid is acceptable, and blue dye is avoided. Radiotherapy is deferred until after delivery in almost all cases.

Chemotherapy is contraindicated in the first trimester because organogenesis is occurring. Anthracycline and taxane regimens are given in the second and third trimesters. Delivery is planned with an interval after the last cycle sufficient for maternal and fetal marrow recovery. Chemotherapy is withheld beyond about 35 weeks so that spontaneous labour does not occur at the neutrophil nadir.

Three drug classes are contraindicated throughout. Endocrine therapy is teratogenic. HER2-directed antibodies cause oligohydramnios through their effect on fetal renal epithelium. Checkpoint inhibitors have no safety data in pregnancy and a mechanism that predicts harm.

The outcome data are more reassuring than the mechanism suggests, and the reason is instructive. A multicentre case-control study compared 129 children whose mothers had cancer during pregnancy with matched controls. Of the exposed children, 96 had received chemotherapy in utero. There was no significant between-group difference in cognitive development on the Bayley scales, and cardiac evaluation at 36 months was normal. Gestational age at birth correlated with cognitive outcome in both groups. Prematurity predicted worse cognition, and that effect was independent of cancer treatment8.

Caution

The clinical instinct is to deliver early and then treat freely. This dataset says that iatrogenic prematurity, not cytotoxic exposure, is the measurable harm to the child. Elective early delivery to start treatment is a decision that trades a documented risk for a hypothetical one.

Lactation is not compatible with cytotoxic therapy, endocrine therapy, or HER2-directed antibodies. Breastfeeding from a previously irradiated breast is often unsuccessful for mechanical reasons, and the contralateral breast is usually adequate.

5 · Pregnancy after breast cancer and interrupting endocrine therapy

The clinical conflict is arithmetic. Adjuvant endocrine therapy runs for 5 to 10 years, and the patients who most want a pregnancy are the ones for whom that interval consumes the remaining fertile years.

POSITIVE addressed the question directly. Women aged 42 years or younger with stage I to III hormone receptor positive disease, who had received 18 to 30 months of adjuvant endocrine therapy, interrupted treatment to attempt pregnancy. Among 516 women the median age was 37 years and 93.4% had stage I or stage II disease. Of 497 followed for pregnancy status, 368 had at least one pregnancy and 317 had at least one live birth. At 1638 patient-years of follow-up there had been 44 breast cancer events against a prespecified safety threshold of 46. The 3-year incidence of breast cancer events was 8.9% in the interruption group and 9.2% in an external control cohort9.

The follow-up duration must be stated every time this result is quoted. Median follow-up was 41 months. Hormone receptor positive disease recurs on a hazard curve that stays meaningfully above zero for two decades, which is the argument in Annual hazard of recurrence and the long tail of hormone receptor-positive disease. A 41-month result addresses the fear that interruption causes an early relapse. It cannot address whether interruption shifts risk into years 10 to 20, because the trial has not been running that long.

Three further limits belong with the number. The design was a single group with an external control rather than a randomised comparison. The population was selected, both by stage and by having already completed 18 to 30 months of therapy without recurrence. And the protocol resumed endocrine therapy after the pregnancy attempt, so the result describes an interruption rather than an abandonment.

Observational data on pregnancy after breast cancer are consistent and must be read carefully. A systematic review and meta-analysis found that survivors were less likely to conceive than the general population, with a relative risk of 0.40 and a 95% confidence interval of 0.32 to 0.49. Caesarean section, low birth weight, preterm birth, and small for gestational age were all more frequent, particularly after chemotherapy. Congenital abnormality was not increased. Compared with survivors who did not conceive, those who did had better disease-free survival, with a hazard ratio of 0.66, and better overall survival, with a hazard ratio of 0.5610. A later analysis restricted to hormone receptor positive disease found no disease-free survival difference, with a hazard ratio of 0.96 and an interval of 0.75 to 1.24. Overall survival in that analysis favoured the pregnancy group, with a hazard ratio of 0.4611.

Caution

The apparent survival advantage of pregnancy is almost certainly selection. Women conceive when they are well, are still well at the point of conception, and have already survived the interval in which early recurrence occurs. Presenting that hazard ratio to a patient as a benefit of pregnancy misrepresents it. The defensible statement is that pregnancy has not been shown to be harmful, and that is a different sentence.

In practice

Raise this before the first prescription, not at the two-year review. The plan for interruption shapes which endocrine regimen is chosen and whether ovarian suppression is used.

Quote the follow-up. Say that the reassurance covers roughly three years and that the trial continues.

Resume endocrine therapy afterwards. That was the protocol, and the result does not license stopping.

Fertility does not return because treatment stopped. Reassess reserve rather than assume it.

6 · Parenting, relationships, and long-horizon survivorship

A woman treated at 34 has a survivorship horizon measured in decades, and every late effect in Rehabilitation and survivorship applies to her for longer than it applies to anyone else.

Premature menopause is the dominant late effect and it arrives abruptly. Chemotherapy-induced ovarian failure and ovarian suppression produce in months what normally takes years. The consequences are bone loss, vasomotor symptoms, genitourinary atrophy, sexual dysfunction, and a cardiovascular risk profile shifted forward by decades. The management belongs to Sexual health and menopausal symptoms and Bone health and cardiovascular risk after treatment, with one modification. Systemic hormone replacement, which is the standard answer in the general population, is not available here.

Adherence is the quiet failure. In a cohort of 8,769 women, 32% had discontinued adjuvant endocrine therapy by 4.5 years, and only 72% of those who continued were fully adherent. Women under 40 years had the highest risk of discontinuation, with a hazard ratio of 1.51 and a 95% confidence interval of 1.23 to 1.8512. The population with the most to gain from prolonged therapy is the population least likely to complete it.

Second primary risk is also age-weighted. In a population cohort of women with early invasive breast cancer, contralateral breast cancer by 20 years was 3.1 percentage points more common than expected. The absolute excess was greater in younger women than in older ones13. That shapes the surveillance argument in Second primaries and long-term health maintenance.

The non-medical load is not a soft topic. Young patients carry dependent children, active careers, mortgages, and partners who become caregivers. Work disruption and out-of-pocket cost fall on the years of lowest accumulated wealth, which is the mechanism in Access, affordability, and financial toxicity. Fear of recurrence and body image after mastectomy at 30 are covered in Distress, anxiety, depression, and fear of recurrence and Body image and identity.

In practice

Ask about children by name and age. It changes what the patient is optimising for and it is not recorded anywhere in the chart.

Treat adherence as a clinical outcome with its own follow-up, not as patient behaviour.

Document who is at home. The partner of a 35-year-old patient is doing childcare, income, and caregiving at once.
