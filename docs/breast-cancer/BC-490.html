Early hormone receptor-positive, HER2-negative disease

Two risk axes that are routinely collapsed into one, and every decision that turns on telling them apart.

1 · Clinical and genomic risk assessment

Risk in this subtype runs on two axes that are routinely collapsed into one. The anatomic axis is tumour size and nodal burden. The biological axis is grade, proliferation and the output of a multigene assay. They correlate weakly and they answer different questions.

This chapter is about how to approach the patient. It covers what to measure, what to give, to whom, for how long, and what to do once treatment stops. The randomised trial record it draws on lives next door and is not repeated here. Adjuvant endocrine therapy is in Adjuvant endocrine therapy in hormone receptor-positive disease. Adjuvant CDK4/6 inhibition is in Adjuvant CDK4/6 inhibition. Preoperative systemic therapy in this subtype is in Neoadjuvant therapy in hormone receptor-positive disease. Adjuvant PARP inhibition is in Adjuvant PARP inhibition for germline carriers. Where a decision below rests on one of those records, the text points forward rather than restating it.

The two axes are not academic, because they have been used to define eligibility for different treatments. monarchE enrolled on clinicopathological criteria1. NATALEE enrolled on anatomic stage instead2. Neither used a genomic assay to select. The population column of the table in Adjuvant CDK4/6 inhibition, patient selection and open questions is where the difference is legible.

The anatomic axis also does most of the work in predicting late events. Size and nodal status still stratify distant recurrence risk between years 5 and 20. The figures are in Late recurrence risk and surveillance implications3. Nothing in routine use has superseded them.

Two measurement cautions belong with all of this. Ki-67 is the least reproducible of the routine variables, and the scoring conventions that make it usable are set out in4 and5. And a genomic assay reports a risk estimate conditioned on the treatment the validation cohort received, which is developed in Prognostic and predictive assays and somatic profiling. Tools that combine the recurrence score with clinicopathological features report a different quantity again6.

2 · Selection of endocrine therapy by menopausal status

Menopausal status is not a patient characteristic in this decision. It is a statement about where oestrogen is being made, and therefore about which blockade will work.

In postmenopausal women an aromatase inhibitor outperforms tamoxifen. A patient-level meta-analysis of 31,920 women found that recurrence rate ratios favoured aromatase inhibitors while the treatments differed, at 0.70 with a 95% confidence interval of 0.64 to 0.77, and not significantly thereafter. Ten-year breast cancer mortality across all comparisons was reduced, with a rate ratio of 0.86 and an interval of 0.80 to 0.947. The same analysis priced the trade. Ten-year endometrial cancer incidence was 0.4% against 1.2% with tamoxifen, and five-year bone fracture risk was 8.2% against 5.5%.

In premenopausal women the ovary is the dominant source, so an aromatase inhibitor alone does not work. It requires ovarian suppression, and the comparison then changes character. A patient-level meta-analysis of 7,030 women receiving ovarian suppression found that an aromatase inhibitor reduced breast cancer recurrence against tamoxifen, with a rate ratio of 0.79 and an interval of 0.69 to 0.90. The absolute reduction in five-year recurrence risk was 3.2 percentage points, from 10.1% to 6.9%. Breast cancer mortality did not differ, with a rate ratio of 1.01 and an interval of 0.82 to 1.24. Fractures were more common, at 6.4% against 5.1%8.

The randomised trials underneath both estimates are tabulated in Adjuvant endocrine therapy in hormone receptor-positive disease, where the agent question and the duration question are separated. The shape of the result recurs through this chapter. Recurrence falls, mortality has not yet followed, and a named toxicity rises. The agents themselves are covered in Endocrine therapy.

The randomised trials of ovarian suppression. Read the experimental-versus-control column first, because these four ask three different questions.

Two questions live under one heading and the table holds the trials for both. Whether to add suppression to tamoxifen was asked in SOFT. Whether an aromatase inhibitor is the better partner once suppression is in place was asked in SOFT/TEXT. ASTRRA and ABCSG-12 are the other two rows, and the trial-level argument across all four is made in Ovarian function suppression and who benefits.

The subgroups are where the decision lives. The table carries none of them. In HER2-negative disease the 12-year overall survival advantage of exemestane with suppression was 2.0 percentage points, and 3.3 points in those who had received chemotherapy. It reached 4.0 points in women under 35, 4.5 points in tumours larger than 2 cm and 5.5 points in grade 3 disease9. Those five numbers span the whole range of what this treatment is worth.

One inversion inside SOFT is worth more attention than it gets. Among women who remained premenopausal after chemotherapy, eight-year overall survival was 89.4% with tamoxifen and suppression and 87.2% with exemestane and suppression11. The better partner on recurrence was not the better partner on survival in the group with most to gain. The numbers are small and the comparison was not randomised against that question.

In practice

Suppression is a risk-stratified decision, not a menopausal-status decision. The women who gained most were young, high grade, node-positive or chemotherapy-treated9.

In a woman with a small, grade 1, node-negative tumour the absolute gain is around a point and the toxicity is not proportionally smaller. Say that number out loud before prescribing.

Remaining premenopausal after chemotherapy is itself a selection marker, because it identifies the women in whom ovarian function was never removed11.

If suppression is used, the aromatase inhibitor is the better partner on recurrence and has not been shown to be better on survival8,9. Fractures rise, so bone management starts at the same visit.

4 · Genomic assays and the decision to omit chemotherapy

This is the chapter's own question. The story below states it in the form the trials were built to answer. It is followed by a second question that the same group of investigators asked next, which is whether the tumour's response to treatment allocates better than any baseline assay.

Early disease · HR+/HER2- · adjuvant

Choosing the intensity and the duration of endocrine treatment

Can the decision to give chemotherapy be made on biology rather than on anatomy?

Rationale
Node count and tumour size measure how long a cancer has been growing, not how much it depends on oestrogen. A genomic assay measures the second, which is what predicts whether cytotoxic treatment adds anything to endocrine treatment.
Experiment
Randomised trials that assigned chemotherapy by a genomic score rather than by stage, in node-negative and then node-positive disease, with survival as the endpoint.
Finding
A large fraction of this cell can omit chemotherapy without losing disease control, and the exceptions cluster by age and menopausal status rather than by score alone.
Limitation
The assays were validated on populations treated with the endocrine therapy of their era, and the age effect is confounded with ovarian function, so the benefit attributed to chemotherapy may partly be the benefit of chemotherapy-induced menopause.
Next question
If a score taken once before treatment can allocate chemotherapy, can the tumour's own response to a short course of endocrine therapy allocate it better?

TAILORx RxPONDER MINDACT

Can the response to treatment be used to allocate treatment, rather than the baseline alone?

Rationale
A baseline assay predicts; a short course of endocrine therapy tests. A tumour whose proliferation collapses under two weeks of an aromatase inhibitor has demonstrated the dependence the assay was trying to infer.
Experiment
Response-adapted designs that give a brief endocrine exposure, measure the change in proliferation, and route patients to chemotherapy or to endocrine treatment on what they see.
Finding
Response separates the population, and the endocrine-responsive group does well on endocrine treatment alone.
Limitation
A dynamic marker predicts outcome in a cohort that has already been selected on it, so the comparison is against a historical expectation rather than against the same patients treated the other way.
Next question
Does adding CDK4/6 inhibition to the induction course change who looks responsive, and is that change real or an artefact of a drug that suppresses proliferation in everyone?

The prospective trials that tested assay-guided and response-guided chemotherapy omission.

All five rows ask whether chemotherapy can be left out, and all five should be read alongside the non-inferiority methods in Clinical trials and evidence interpretation and specifically Designing de-escalation trials and the non-inferiority margin problem. TAILORx settled the node-negative midrange score. MINDACT settled the clinically high-risk, genomically low-risk discordance. WSG-ADAPT HR+/HER2− added a three-week endocrine window to the score and allocated on both. WSG ADAPTcycle is still running and asks whether a CDK4/6 inhibitor can take the place of the chemotherapy the assay would otherwise mandate.

RxPONDER is the one that did not settle its question. The reason sits in the interaction rather than in either arm. Chemotherapy benefit differed by menopausal status, with an interaction p value of 0.00821. The two strata point in opposite directions. A single hazard ratio for that trial would describe nobody.

One sentence in that report matters more than the headline. The relative chemotherapy benefit did not increase as the recurrence score increased21. In node-positive premenopausal women the assay identified who was at risk. It did not identify who benefited from chemotherapy.

Caution

A trial that declares endocrine therapy non-inferior has not shown that chemotherapy does nothing. It has shown that the difference is smaller than a margin chosen in advance. The MINDACT intention-to-treat comparison makes the point concretely, because a 2.6 percentage point difference at eight years sits inside a design whose primary question was a single-arm rate20. The general form of this error is set out in Designing de-escalation trials and the non-inferiority margin problem.

Caution

The premenopausal RxPONDER result is widely read as showing that chemotherapy works in young node-positive women with low genomic risk. The flat relationship between score and benefit is compatible with a different reading, which is that some of what chemotherapy delivered was ovarian suppression21. The trial did not randomise suppression, so it cannot separate the two. A patient offered chemotherapy on this evidence should be told that ovarian suppression is the alternative hypothesis, not a consolation prize.

The randomised trials that built the adjuvant backbone. Three questions are interleaved here, and the experimental-versus-control column is what separates them.

The eight trials answer three questions that are habitually read as one.

Whether a taxane should follow an anthracycline was asked by CALGB 9344 and NSABP B-28, and which taxane on which schedule by ECOG E1199. Whether the interval between cycles carries an independent effect was asked by CALGB 9741 and GIM2. Whether the anthracycline can come out altogether was asked by US Oncology 9735, ABC trials and WSG-PlanB. Those three disagree.

The disagreement is the useful part. Four cycles of docetaxel and cyclophosphamide beat four cycles of an anthracycline combination outright in US Oncology 973527. Six cycles of the same doublet were then set against a taxane and anthracycline sequence twice. ABC trials could not declare non-inferiority and WSG-PlanB could25,24. The two trials set their margins on different scales, one on the hazard ratio and one as a difference in percentage points of disease-free survival. The verdict turns on the scale as much as on the estimate.

Two structural points are specific to this subtype. The absolute gain from anthracycline-containing over taxane-based regimens is small in endocrine-responsive disease, and the trial-level comparisons that establish it are in32. And schedule carries an independent effect, established across 37,298 women in 26 trials33.

The practical consequence is that dose density is available without adding an agent. In a subtype where the marginal benefit of chemotherapy is contested, changing the schedule is a cheaper lever than changing the drugs.

The four reported adjuvant CDK4/6 trials and the one still running. The population column carries most of the difference between them.

Four trials, four designs. They differ in agent, in duration at one, two and three years, in schedule between continuous and intermittent dosing, and in the population enrolled. No trial varies one of those while holding the others fixed, so nothing in this evidence identifies which variable explains the split. Adjuvant CDK4/6 inhibition does that comparison axis by axis. Cross-trial comparison and what actually differs between the populations is where the outcomes are set side by side.

Two properties of the evidence matter at the point of prescribing. The first is that NATALEE has reported three data cuts at different amounts of follow-up, so its absolute rates are not comparable across reports. The hazard ratio is the stable quantity. It has sat between 0.716 and 0.75 in every analysis the trial has published36,2,37,38. The second is that monarchE is the only one of the four with a mature overall survival result39.

Caution

Class effect is the word doing the damage here. The three agents have different target selectivity, different schedules and different tolerability, and the two positive trials used the two agents that were not palbociclib. That is consistent with a drug difference. It is equally consistent with a duration difference, an adherence difference or a population difference. Reading a positive result for abemaciclib as support for palbociclib runs against two randomised trials that looked for exactly that and did not find it34,35.

In practice

Use the agent in the population in which it was tested, for the duration for which it was tested. That is the only reading these four trials support.

For node-positive high-risk disease meeting the monarchE criteria, two years of abemaciclib buys an absolute overall survival difference of 1.8 percentage points at seven years, alongside a 6.5-point recurrence difference39. Quote both absolute numbers rather than the hazard ratios, which are what a patient will otherwise hear.

For stage II to III disease including selected node-negative tumours, three years of ribociclib has a recurrence benefit with immature survival data38. Say that the survival question is open rather than implying it is answered.

Where a patient meets both sets of criteria there is no randomised comparison. Choose on tolerability, duration and what the patient will take, and say that is the basis.

Do not substitute palbociclib for either agent on grounds of access or familiarity. In the adjuvant setting it has been tested twice and has failed twice34,35.

The only randomised adjuvant PARP inhibitor trial in early breast cancer. Its population spans two subtypes with different entry criteria.

OlympiA enrolled both hormone receptor-positive and triple-negative disease, with risk criteria that differed between the two40. That matters for transfer. A hormone receptor-positive carrier entered on stage and nodal count. A triple-negative carrier usually entered on residual disease after neoadjuvant chemotherapy. The trial was not designed to separate them. The triple-negative half of the same argument is in Post-neoadjuvant systemic therapy.

Two secondary observations matter for the consent conversation and neither is in the table. There were fewer new BRCA-associated breast and ovarian or fallopian tube cancers with olaparib. And adverse events of special interest were not increased, with myelodysplastic syndrome or acute myeloid leukaemia in 0.4% of patients on olaparib against 0.7% on placebo41. The leukaemia signal that was anticipated from the metastatic experience has not appeared at six years.

The mechanism is developed in DNA repair, replication stress, and genomic instability and the agents in Targeted therapy. Germline testing should precede the adjuvant decision rather than follow it, and the testing strategy is in Inherited susceptibility and familial breast cancer.

Caution

Sequencing olaparib with adjuvant CDK4/6 inhibition in a dually eligible patient is unresolved and should be presented that way. OlympiA did not permit a CDK4/6 inhibitor and monarchE did not require germline testing, so neither trial observed the combination or the sequence. Choosing one, choosing the other, or giving them in series are three strategies with no comparative evidence behind any of them. A patient who is offered a definite order should be told that the order is a judgement.

8 · Extended endocrine therapy and duration

The case for extending endocrine therapy rests on the residual hazard quantified in Late recurrence risk and surveillance implications. The case against rests on toxicity and on adherence. A patient-level meta-analysis of 12 trials in 22,031 postmenopausal women who had already completed at least five years of endocrine therapy now prices both sides. The individual trials underneath it are tabulated in Extended adjuvant therapy and the shape of the late benefit.

Allocation to further aromatase inhibition reduced recurrence by 27%, with a rate ratio of 0.73 and a 95% confidence interval of 0.67 to 0.8042.

The subset that matters most in current practice is the one already treated with an aromatase inhibitor. There, five further years reduced recurrence with a rate ratio of 0.71, taking the risk from year 5 to year 15 from 15.2% to 11.6%. Distant recurrence fell with a rate ratio of 0.73, from 8.6% to 6.6%. Breast cancer mortality fell non-significantly, with a rate ratio of 0.90, an interval of 0.70 to 1.15 and a p value of 0.40, from 5.0% to 4.4%42.

Because the proportional reduction was similar across tumour characteristics, the absolute benefit scales with baseline risk. Ten years against five years took node-positive risk from 20.1% to 16.3% and node-negative risk from 11.8% to 9.1%42. Five-year fracture risk rose with a rate ratio of 1.35, from 3.4% to 4.6%.

In practice

Extended therapy is a recurrence decision with an unproven mortality effect. Present it that way rather than as a survival intervention42.

Anchor the offer to nodal status, because the proportional benefit is flat and the absolute benefit is not. Between years 5 and 15, ten years of an aromatase inhibitor against five spares about 3.8 percentage points of recurrence risk in node-positive disease and about 2.7 points in node-negative disease42.

Put the fracture number in the same sentence as the recurrence number. Roughly one additional fracture per 83 women over five years is the price42.

A patient who has tolerated five years badly is unlikely to tolerate ten. Non-adherence in these placebo-controlled trials ran at 39.0% on further treatment and 37.6% on placebo42, which is the honest baseline for what extension means in practice.

9 · Late recurrence risk and surveillance implications

This subtype recurs on a timescale that outlasts most follow-up schedules and most trials. The annual hazard of recurrence does not fall to zero after five years, and the pattern across 24 years of follow-up is set out in43 and44.

The size of that residual risk is now precisely quantified. Among 62,923 women with oestrogen receptor-positive disease who were disease-free after five years of scheduled endocrine therapy, recurrences occurred at a steady rate from year 5 to year 20. Distant recurrence risk over that window was 13% for T1N0 disease, 20% for T1N1-3, 34% for T1N4-9, 19% for T2N0, 26% for T2N1-3 and 41% for T2N4-93.

Grade added moderately within a stage stratum. For T1N0 disease the risk over years 5 to 20 was 10% for low-grade, 13% for moderate-grade and 17% for high-grade tumours3. Progesterone receptor and HER2 status were not predictive of late distant recurrence in that dataset.

Two implications follow, and only one of them is comfortable.

The comfortable one is that the original pathology report retains predictive power for two decades. Size and nodal status are not superseded by anything currently in routine use.

The uncomfortable one is that no surveillance strategy has been shown to convert that knowledge into benefit. Detecting asymptomatic distant recurrence earlier has not improved survival, and the evidence against surveillance intensity is in Rehabilitation and survivorship. Circulating tumour DNA detects molecular relapse before imaging does, and whether acting on that lead time helps is unresolved and is the subject of Liquid biopsy and longitudinal monitoring. Late recurrence is a dormancy problem, and its biology is in Dormancy, residual disease, and late recurrence.

Interplay

Heterogeneity in this subtype is mostly temporal rather than spatial, which is why the chapter reads as a series of duration decisions. The adjuvant setting applies years of selective pressure to a population that cannot be sampled, and what emerges at year eight has been shaped by that pressure exactly as the metastatic sequences in Endocrine therapy and the emergence of ESR1 alterations are. Extended endocrine therapy and adjuvant CDK4/6 inhibition are not simply longer treatment. They are longer selection, and the ESR1 and RB1 escape routes that define the metastatic setting CDK4/6 inhibition and the evolution of cell cycle escape are being selected for during the years in which the patient is called disease-free.

10 · Adherence and long-term tolerability

Every hazard ratio in this chapter is an intention-to-treat estimate over a population in which a large minority stopped taking the drug. That is not a footnote to the effect size. It is part of what the effect size means.

In a cohort of 8,769 women with stage I to III hormone-sensitive breast cancer who filled at least one prescription, 32% had discontinued endocrine therapy by 4.5 years. Among those who continued, 72% were fully adherent by a medication possession ratio of 80% or more. Combining the two, 49% took adjuvant endocrine therapy for the full duration on the intended schedule. Women under 40 had the highest discontinuation risk, with a hazard ratio of 1.51 and a 95% confidence interval of 1.23 to 1.8545.

The randomised evidence agrees. In the placebo-controlled extended-therapy trials, non-adherence was 39.0% on further aromatase inhibition and 37.6% on placebo42. Almost as many patients stopped an inert tablet as stopped an active one, which says that the burden is the regimen as much as the pharmacology.

The toxicities that drive this are known and are quantified elsewhere in the chapter. Fractures rise with aromatase inhibition in postmenopausal women7 and with ovarian suppression in premenopausal women8. Endometrial cancer and thrombosis rise with tamoxifen7. Arthralgia and vasomotor symptoms are the commonest reasons given for stopping, and the symptom burden is developed in Endocrine therapy and Rehabilitation and survivorship.

In practice

Ask about adherence at every visit and ask in a form that permits a true answer. Half of patients do not complete the prescribed course45.

Treat the symptom rather than shortening the course wherever that is possible. A switch between agents, or a dose reduction with evidence behind it46, keeps a patient on treatment that stopping does not.

Flag women under 40 as the highest-risk group for discontinuation and follow them accordingly45.

When quoting a benefit from any trial in this chapter, remember it was achieved under the same adherence that the patient in front of you will have. The number is not an estimate of perfect compliance and should not be presented as one.
