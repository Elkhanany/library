Part XI · Hormone receptor-positive, HER2-negative disease · Chapter 58

Early disease

Which decisions in this subtype rest on a randomised comparison against usual care, and which rest on a subgroup, a margin or a population nobody enrolled.

1 · Clinical and genomic risk assessment

Risk in this subtype runs on two axes that are routinely collapsed into one. The anatomic axis is tumour size and nodal burden. The biological axis is grade, proliferation and the output of a multigene assay. They correlate weakly and they answer different questions.

This chapter is about how to approach the patient. It covers what to measure, what to give, to whom, for how long, and what to do once treatment stops. The randomised trial record it draws on lives next door and is not repeated here. Adjuvant endocrine therapy is in Adjuvant endocrine therapy. Adjuvant CDK4/6 inhibition is in Adjuvant CDK4/6 inhibition. Preoperative systemic therapy in this subtype is in Neoadjuvant therapy. Adjuvant PARP inhibition is in Adjuvant PARP inhibition for germline carriers. Where a decision below rests on one of those records, the text points forward rather than restating it.

The two axes are not academic, because they have been used to define eligibility for different treatments. monarchE enrolled on clinicopathological criteria1. NATALEE enrolled on anatomic stage instead2. Neither used a genomic assay to select. The population column of the table in Adjuvant CDK4/6 inhibition, patient selection and open questions is where the difference is legible.

The anatomic axis also does most of the work in predicting late events. Size and nodal status still stratify distant recurrence risk between years 5 and 20. The figures are in Late recurrence risk and surveillance implications3. Nothing in routine use has superseded them.

Two measurement cautions belong with all of this. Ki-67 is the least reproducible of the routine variables, and the scoring conventions that make it usable are set out in4 and5. And a genomic assay reports a risk estimate conditioned on the treatment the validation cohort received, which is developed in Prognostic and predictive assays and somatic profiling. Tools that combine the recurrence score with clinicopathological features report a different quantity again6.

2 · Selection of endocrine therapy by menopausal status

Menopausal status is not a patient characteristic in this decision. It is a statement about where oestrogen is being made, and therefore about which blockade will work.

In postmenopausal women an aromatase inhibitor outperforms tamoxifen. A patient-level meta-analysis of 31,920 women found that recurrence rate ratios favoured aromatase inhibitors while the treatments differed, at 0.70 with a 95% confidence interval of 0.64 to 0.77, and not significantly thereafter. Ten-year breast cancer mortality across all comparisons was reduced, with a rate ratio of 0.86 and an interval of 0.80 to 0.947. The same analysis priced the trade. Ten-year endometrial cancer incidence was 0.4% against 1.2% with tamoxifen, and five-year bone fracture risk was 8.2% against 5.5%.

In premenopausal women the ovary is the dominant source, so an aromatase inhibitor alone does not work. It requires ovarian suppression, and the comparison then changes character. A patient-level meta-analysis of 7,030 women receiving ovarian suppression found that an aromatase inhibitor reduced breast cancer recurrence against tamoxifen, with a rate ratio of 0.79 and an interval of 0.69 to 0.90. The absolute reduction in five-year recurrence risk was 3.2 percentage points, from 10.1% to 6.9%. Breast cancer mortality did not differ, with a rate ratio of 1.01 and an interval of 0.82 to 1.24. Fractures were more common, at 6.4% against 5.1%8.

The randomised trials underneath both estimates are tabulated in Adjuvant endocrine therapy, where the agent question and the duration question are separated. The two estimates do not have the same shape. Breast cancer mortality fell alongside recurrence in the postmenopausal comparison and did not under ovarian suppression. A named toxicity rises in both. The agents themselves are covered in Endocrine therapy.

The randomised trials of ovarian suppression. Read the experimental-versus-control column first, because these four ask three different questions.

Four rows, three questions. Only two of these trials randomised suppression at all. SOFT set tamoxifen plus suppression, and exemestane plus suppression, against tamoxifen alone. ASTRRA set two years of goserelin added to tamoxifen against tamoxifen alone. SOFT/TEXT and ABCSG-12 gave a gonadotropin-releasing hormone agonist to everybody and randomised the partner drug instead.

Where the four agree is narrower than it looks. Adding ovarian suppression to tamoxifen lowers recurrence in premenopausal hormone receptor-positive disease, and in SOFT and ASTRRA it also moved overall survival. Upgrading the partner drug on top of suppression is a separate claim. The two trials that tested that upgrade disagree on recurrence, and the one that reports overall survival for the comparison finds no difference. The trial-level argument across all four is made in Ovarian function suppression and who benefits.

The apparent disagreement is between SOFT/TEXT and ABCSG-12, and it is not a disagreement about aromatase inhibitors. Exemestane beat tamoxifen on disease-free survival in the joint SOFT and TEXT analysis. Anastrozole did not beat tamoxifen in ABCSG-12. Four things differ besides the drug. ABCSG-12 treated for three years and SOFT and TEXT for five. ABCSG-12 enrolled a largely chemotherapy-naive and lower-risk population. Its primary report came before most late events in this subtype have occurred12. And its final analysis was powered around a second randomisation, to zoledronic acid, which the row reports and which belongs to the adjuvant bisphosphonate argument rather than to this one13. Reading the two rows as a verdict on anastrozole against exemestane is the error the arms column exists to prevent.

ASTRRA diverges by population rather than by design. It enrolled women aged 45 or younger who had already had chemotherapy and were confirmed still premenopausal on follicle-stimulating hormone and bleeding history at six-monthly intervals10. That is a prospective enrichment of precisely the SOFT stratum in which suppression looked worthwhile. Its agreement with SOFT is therefore close to circular, and its effect size should not be transported to a woman selected any other way. What it does add is that the effect survives being looked for on purpose, and it holds at eight years14.

The decision rests on SOFT. It randomised suppression against no suppression in a premenopausal population that was not pre-selected on recovered ovarian function. That is the population a clinic actually faces, which is what makes the row load-bearing.

The subgroups are where the decision lives, and the table carries none of them. In HER2-negative disease, which was 86.0% of the joint population, the 12-year overall survival advantage of exemestane with suppression was 2.0 percentage points. It was 3.3 points in the women who had received chemotherapy9. The gain tracks baseline risk rather than the drug, so a single average for this comparison is not usable in clinic.

One inversion inside SOFT is worth more attention than it gets. Exemestane with suppression gave the best eight-year disease-free survival of the three arms and did not give the best eight-year overall survival11. The arm that beat tamoxifen alone on survival was tamoxifen with suppression. The trial was not powered to rank the two suppression arms against each other on survival. Read the recurrence ordering as a survival ordering and you are reading something the design did not test. Final outcomes from both trials are now on the record15.

Three things nobody randomised are missing from every row. Duration of suppression is one, at two years in ASTRRA, three in ABCSG-12 and five in SOFT and TEXT, never varied inside a trial. Method is the second. ABCSG-12 and ASTRRA specified a gonadotropin-releasing hormone agonist, and no trial here set one method of suppression against another, so oophorectomy and radiation ablation are inferred rather than tested. The third is confirmation of suppression. ASTRRA measured ovarian function repeatedly and the others did not, which means the treatment being compared is biochemically the same drug and not reliably the same state.

In practice

Suppression is a risk-stratified decision, not a menopausal-status decision. Receipt of chemotherapy marks the subgroup with the larger twelve-year survival gain9.

In a woman with a small, grade 1, node-negative tumour the absolute gain sits at the bottom of that range and the toxicity is not proportionally smaller. Quote the absolute difference before prescribing rather than the hazard ratio.

Remaining premenopausal after chemotherapy is itself a selection marker, because it identifies the women in whom ovarian function was never removed11.

If suppression is used, the aromatase inhibitor is the better partner on recurrence and has not been shown to be better on survival8,9. Fractures rise, so bone management starts at the same visit.

4 · Genomic assays and the decision to omit chemotherapy

Early disease · HR+/HER2- · adjuvant

Choosing the intensity and the duration of endocrine treatment

Can the decision to give chemotherapy be made on biology rather than on anatomy?

Rationale
Node count and tumour size measure how long a cancer has been growing, not how much it depends on oestrogen. A genomic assay measures the second, which is what predicts whether cytotoxic treatment adds anything to endocrine treatment.
Experiment
Randomised trials that assigned chemotherapy by a genomic score rather than by stage, in node-negative and then node-positive disease, with survival as the endpoint.
Finding
A large fraction of this cell can omit chemotherapy without losing disease control, and the exceptions cluster by age and menopausal status rather than by score alone.
Limitation
The assays were validated on populations treated with the endocrine therapy of their era, and the age effect is confounded with ovarian function, so the benefit attributed to chemotherapy may partly be the benefit of chemotherapy-induced menopause.
Next question
If a score taken once before treatment can allocate chemotherapy, can the tumour's own response to a short course of endocrine therapy allocate it better?

Can the response to treatment be used to allocate treatment, rather than the baseline alone?

Rationale
A baseline assay predicts; a short course of endocrine therapy tests. A tumour whose proliferation collapses under two weeks of an aromatase inhibitor has demonstrated the dependence the assay was trying to infer.
Experiment
Response-adapted designs that give a brief endocrine exposure, measure the change in proliferation, and route patients to chemotherapy or to endocrine treatment on what they see.
Finding
Response separates the population, and the endocrine-responsive group does well on endocrine treatment alone.
Limitation
A dynamic marker predicts outcome in a cohort that has already been selected on it, so the comparison is against a historical expectation rather than against the same patients treated the other way.
Next question
Does adding CDK4/6 inhibition to the induction course change who looks responsive, and is that change real or an artefact of a drug that suppresses proliferation in everyone?

The prospective trials that tested assay-guided and response-guided chemotherapy omission.

Four reported trials and one still running, and they converge on one finding. In node-negative or limited node-positive hormone receptor-positive disease, a large fraction of patients can be treated with endocrine therapy alone without a measurable loss of distant disease control over the follow-up so far accrued. That is the finding. Everything else in the table is a difference in how it was established.

Those differences are design, population and endpoint, not assay performance. TAILORx randomised chemotherapy against no chemotherapy inside a midrange score band in node-negative disease, and was powered as a non-inferiority comparison25. MINDACT randomised only the patients whose clinical and genomic risk disagreed, and its primary question was a single-arm rate tested against a fixed boundary, with the randomised comparison secondary27. RxPONDER moved to one to three positive nodes and capped the score at 2524. WSG-ADAPT HR+/HER2− compared two prospectively defined groups rather than two randomised ones, because allocation followed biology22. The assays differ too, with a 70-gene signature in MINDACT and a 21-gene score elsewhere, on thresholds that are not interchangeable. And the endpoint differs, because a distant metastasis-free survival endpoint and an invasive disease-free survival endpoint do not count the same events. The second counts second primaries and non-breast deaths, which dilutes a chemotherapy effect in an older population and inflates it in none.

The decision in node-negative disease rests on TAILORx. It is the only row that randomised the thing a clinic would otherwise do against the thing it would otherwise omit. It did so in the population where the question is actually asked, with enough events to bound the difference.

RxPONDER is the one that did not settle its question, and the reason sits in the interaction rather than in either arm. Chemotherapy benefit differed by menopausal status, with an interaction p value of 0.00824. The two strata point in opposite directions. A single hazard ratio for that trial would describe nobody. One sentence in that report matters more than the headline. The relative chemotherapy benefit did not increase as the recurrence score increased. In node-positive premenopausal women the assay identified who was at risk. It did not identify who benefited from chemotherapy.

Read alongside TAILORx, where the residual chemotherapy signal also sat in women aged 50 or younger, that is the same finding twice. MINDACT looked in the same place, reporting an exploratory analysis by age in its update23. Age and menopausal status travel together, and no trial in this table randomised ovarian suppression. The benefit attributed to chemotherapy in young women cannot be separated from the benefit of chemotherapy-induced menopause.

WSG-ADAPT HR+/HER2− answers the second question and answers it in a form that needs care. Three weeks of preoperative endocrine therapy separated the population, with central Ki-67 read before and after. The endocrine-responsive midrange group then did as well on endocrine therapy alone as the low-score control group22. The comparison was between two arms defined by the marker, so nobody with an endocrine-responsive midrange tumour was treated the other way. The response rate also depends on which drug was given, at 78.1% on an aromatase inhibitor against 41.1% on tamoxifen. Endocrine response as measured here is a property of the test as much as of the tumour.

WSG ADAPTcycle is still running. It asks whether ribociclib with an aromatase inhibitor can take the place of the chemotherapy that a recurrence score and an endocrine response test would otherwise mandate. That is the only substitution question in the table. Its reported concordance work is the part to read now. Local and central Ki-67 disagreed on the endocrine-response call often enough that 13.1% of patients called responders locally were non-responders centrally26. A response-guided strategy is only as portable as the assay that defines response.

All five rows should be read alongside the non-inferiority methods in Clinical trials and evidence interpretation and specifically Designing de-escalation trials and the non-inferiority margin problem. Three things the table cannot give you are worth naming. Nobody randomised assay use against no assay use, so the value of testing itself is inferred from the outcomes of the tested. Nobody enrolled four or more positive nodes into an omission question. And the overall survival column is where the gap shows, because the node-positive trial has not reported it and this subtype recurs for twenty years Late recurrence risk and surveillance implications.

Caution

A trial that declares endocrine therapy non-inferior has not shown that chemotherapy does nothing. It has shown that the difference is smaller than a margin chosen in advance. The MINDACT intention-to-treat comparison makes the point concretely, because its eight-year distant metastasis-free survival favoured chemotherapy inside a design whose primary question was a single-arm rate against a fixed boundary23. The general form of this error is set out in Designing de-escalation trials and the non-inferiority margin problem.

Caution

The premenopausal RxPONDER result is widely read as showing that chemotherapy works in young node-positive women with low genomic risk. The flat relationship between score and benefit is compatible with a different reading, which is that some of what chemotherapy delivered was ovarian suppression24. The trial did not randomise suppression, so it cannot separate the two. A patient offered chemotherapy on this evidence should be told that ovarian suppression is the alternative hypothesis, not a consolation prize.

The randomised trials that built the adjuvant backbone. Three questions are interleaved here, and the experimental-versus-control column is what separates them.

The eight trials answer three questions that are habitually read as one, and they agree on one thing across all three. Every increment here is a few percentage points of disease-free survival, and none of the modern comparisons found a regimen that was clearly wrong.

Whether a taxane should follow an anthracycline was asked by CALGB 9344 and NSABP B-28. Both found the same relative reduction in disease-free survival events and diverged on survival, which CALGB 9344 improved and NSABP B-28 did not34,33. Nothing about the taxane differs between them. CALGB 9344 also randomised three doxorubicin doses and found no dose effect, which is the more durable result in that row. Its unplanned subgroup analysis put the recurrence hazard ratio at 0.72 in oestrogen receptor-negative and 0.91 in receptor-positive tumours34. That single comparison does more to explain the position of taxanes in this subtype than either trial's headline.

Inside that same question sits the choice of taxane and schedule, which ECOG E1199 asked directly. The answer is a pairing rather than a class. Weekly paclitaxel beat three-weekly paclitaxel on disease-free and overall survival. Docetaxel every three weeks also beat three-weekly paclitaxel on disease-free survival. Weekly docetaxel did not32. A trial with four arms is the only design that could have shown that. Its price was grade 2 to 4 neuropathy in 27% of the weekly paclitaxel arm against 20% of the control.

Whether the interval between cycles carries an independent effect was asked by CALGB 9741 and GIM2, twelve years apart, on different backbones. Both are positive on disease-free and overall survival. Both also carried a second randomisation that added nothing, to concurrent against sequential scheduling in CALGB 9741 and to fluorouracil in GIM235,30. The schedule effect is the only claim in this table reproduced on two independent backbones, which is why it is more secure than any single agent addition.

Whether the anthracycline can come out altogether was asked by US Oncology 9735, ABC trials and WSG-PlanB. Those three appear to disagree and largely do not, because they did not use the same control arm. US Oncology 9735 compared four cycles of docetaxel and cyclophosphamide with four cycles of doxorubicin and cyclophosphamide, which is an anthracycline regimen containing no taxane31. It is evidence that a taxane doublet beats an old doublet. It is not evidence about the modern comparator.

ABC trials and WSG-PlanB both used that modern comparator, setting six cycles of docetaxel and cyclophosphamide against a taxane and anthracycline sequence. Both returned small differences, and the two point estimates do not favour the same arm. Their verdicts differ because their margins were set on different scales. ABC set an inferiority boundary on the hazard ratio at 1.18 and returned 1.202, so non-inferiority could not be declared29. PlanB set a margin of 4.4 percentage points of disease-free survival and cleared it28. The trials also differ in follow-up at the time of the verdict and in how their randomised populations were assembled, because PlanB removed its lowest genomic risk stratum from randomisation altogether. ABC has since reported with longer follow-up36. Read the margin before reading the conclusion.

Two structural points are specific to this subtype, and one of them is about the table itself. Six of these eight trials enrolled without regard to receptor status, so they appear here on the all-comers wildcard rather than because they enrolled this population. Their estimates are averages over a population in which hormone receptor-positive disease was the part least likely to benefit. The absolute gain from anthracycline-containing over taxane-based regimens is small in endocrine-responsive disease, and the trial-level comparisons that establish it are in37. Schedule, by contrast, carries an independent effect established across 37,298 women in 26 trials38.

The practical consequence is that dose density is available without adding an agent. In a subtype where the marginal benefit of chemotherapy is contested, changing the schedule is a cheaper lever than changing the drugs.

The four reported adjuvant CDK4/6 trials and the one still running. The population column carries most of the difference between them.

Four reported trials, two positive and two negative, and one still running that asks a different question. Treating them as a single class question is the most consequential error available in this chapter.

The agreement is thin and should be said plainly. All four reported trials added a CDK4/6 inhibitor to adjuvant endocrine therapy, used invasive disease-free survival as the primary endpoint, and were large enough to detect the effect they were designed for. monarchE and NATALEE separated on that endpoint. PALLAS and PENELOPE-B did not, and neither has separated on survival with longer follow-up41,42.

The two negative trials used palbociclib, which is the observation that drives the class-effect reading. It is not the only thing that differs. Duration runs from one year in PENELOPE-B through two in monarchE and PALLAS to three in NATALEE. Schedule differs between abemaciclib given continuously twice daily, ribociclib at a reduced dose three weeks on and one week off, and palbociclib for three weeks in four. No trial varies one of those while holding the others fixed.

Population is where the split is usually explained, and the explanation does not survive contact with the table. PALLAS enrolled stage II to III disease with no high-risk qualifier, which is a lower-risk population than monarchE required39. PENELOPE-B enrolled residual invasive disease after taxane-containing neoadjuvant chemotherapy with a CPS-EG score of 3 or more, which is a higher-risk population than either positive trial enrolled40. The two failures therefore bracket the two successes on risk rather than sitting below them. A population account has to explain both ends and explains neither.

PENELOPE-B also differs on an axis the filter has to name explicitly. It is a post-neoadjuvant trial, so its patients had already demonstrated chemotherapy resistance in vivo. That is a biologically different starting point from an adjuvant population selected on pathology alone. It is the one difference in this table that is about the tumour rather than about the protocol.

The decision rests on monarchE. It is the only one of the five that has shown an overall survival advantage43, and its entry criteria are written in variables a pathology report already contains. NATALEE is load-bearing for the wider stage II population and for node-negative disease, on recurrence alone. It has reported at successive data cuts with different amounts of follow-up, so its absolute rates are not comparable across reports. The hazard ratio for an invasive disease-free survival event is the quantity that has stayed stable across them44,2,45,46.

WSG ADAPTcycle is in this table because of its modality and belongs to the argument in Genomic assays and the decision to omit chemotherapy. It is the only row asking whether a CDK4/6 inhibitor can replace chemotherapy rather than follow it.

Two things the table cannot show. The result column reports different timepoints in every row, so reading down it compares three-year rates with four-year and seven-year rates. And no patient has ever been randomised between abemaciclib and ribociclib in this setting, which is the comparison a clinic makes weekly. Adjuvant CDK4/6 inhibition does the axis-by-axis comparison. Cross-trial comparison and what actually differs between the populations sets the outcomes side by side.

Caution

Class effect is the word doing the damage here. The three agents have different target selectivity, different schedules and different tolerability, and the two positive trials used the two agents that were not palbociclib. That is consistent with a drug difference. It is equally consistent with a duration difference, an adherence difference or a population difference. Reading a positive result for abemaciclib as support for palbociclib runs against two randomised trials that looked for exactly that and did not find it39,40.

In practice

Use the agent in the population in which it was tested, for the duration for which it was tested. That is the only reading these four trials support.

For node-positive high-risk disease meeting the monarchE criteria, two years of abemaciclib buys an absolute overall survival difference of 1.8 percentage points at seven years, alongside a 6.5-point recurrence difference43. Quote both absolute numbers rather than the hazard ratios, which are what a patient will otherwise hear.

For stage II to III disease including selected node-negative tumours, three years of ribociclib has a recurrence benefit with immature survival data46. Say that the survival question is open rather than implying it is answered.

Where a patient meets both sets of criteria there is no randomised comparison. Choose on tolerability, duration and what the patient will take, and say that is the basis.

Do not substitute palbociclib for either agent on grounds of access or familiarity. In the adjuvant setting it has been tested twice and has failed twice39,40.

Choosing one, choosing the other, or giving them in series are three strategies with no comparative evidence behind any of them. A patient who is offered a definite order should be told that the order is a judgement.

8 · Extended endocrine therapy and duration

The case for extending endocrine therapy rests on the residual hazard quantified in Late recurrence risk and surveillance implications. The case against rests on toxicity and on adherence. A patient-level meta-analysis of 12 trials in 22,031 postmenopausal women who had already completed at least five years of endocrine therapy now prices both sides49. The individual trials underneath it are tabulated in Extended adjuvant therapy and the shape of the late benefit.

Allocation to further aromatase inhibition reduced recurrence, with a rate ratio of 0.73 and a 95% confidence interval of 0.67 to 0.8049.

The subset that matters most in current practice is the one already treated with an aromatase inhibitor. There, five further years reduced recurrence with a rate ratio of 0.71, taking the risk from year 5 to year 15 from 15.2% to 11.6%. Distant recurrence fell with a rate ratio of 0.73, from 8.6% to 6.6%. Breast cancer mortality fell non-significantly, with a rate ratio of 0.90, an interval of 0.70 to 1.15 and a p value of 0.40, from 5.0% to 4.4%49.

Because the proportional reduction was similar across tumour characteristics, the absolute benefit scales with baseline risk. Ten years against five years took node-positive risk from 20.1% to 16.3% and node-negative risk from 11.8% to 9.1%49. Five-year fracture risk rose with a rate ratio of 1.35, from 3.4% to 4.6%.

In practice

Extended therapy is a recurrence decision with an unproven mortality effect. Present it that way rather than as a survival intervention49.

Anchor the offer to nodal status, because the proportional benefit is flat and the absolute benefit is not. Quote the node-positive and node-negative recurrence risks between years 5 and 15 rather than the rate ratio49.

Put the fracture risk in the same sentence as the recurrence risk. The same analysis prices both, and the five-year fracture rate is what a patient will weigh against the recurrence she cannot see49.

A patient who has tolerated five years badly is unlikely to tolerate ten. Non-adherence in these placebo-controlled trials ran at 39.0% on further treatment and 37.6% on placebo49, which is the honest baseline for what extension means in practice.

9 · Late recurrence risk and surveillance implications

This subtype recurs on a timescale that outlasts most follow-up schedules and most trials. The annual hazard of recurrence does not fall to zero after five years, and the pattern across 24 years of follow-up is set out in50 and51.

The size of that residual risk is now precisely quantified. Among 62,923 women with oestrogen receptor-positive disease who were disease-free after five years of scheduled endocrine therapy, recurrences occurred at a steady rate from year 5 to year 20. Distant recurrence risk over that window was 13% for T1N0 disease, 20% for T1N1-3, 34% for T1N4-9, 19% for T2N0, 26% for T2N1-3 and 41% for T2N4-93.

Grade added moderately within a stage stratum. For T1N0 disease the risk over years 5 to 20 was 10% for low-grade, 13% for moderate-grade and 17% for high-grade tumours3. Progesterone receptor and HER2 status were not predictive of late distant recurrence in that dataset.

Two implications follow, and only one of them is comfortable.

The comfortable one is that the original pathology report retains predictive power for two decades. Size and nodal status are not superseded by anything currently in routine use.

The uncomfortable one is that no surveillance strategy has been shown to convert that knowledge into benefit. Detecting asymptomatic distant recurrence earlier has not improved survival, and the evidence against surveillance intensity is in Rehabilitation and survivorship. Circulating tumour DNA detects molecular relapse before imaging does, and whether acting on that lead time helps is unresolved and is the subject of Liquid biopsy and longitudinal monitoring. Late recurrence is a dormancy problem, and its biology is in Dormancy, residual disease, and late recurrence.

Interplay

Heterogeneity in this subtype is mostly temporal rather than spatial, which is why the chapter reads as a series of duration decisions. The adjuvant setting applies years of selective pressure to a population that cannot be sampled. What emerges at year eight has been shaped by that pressure exactly as the metastatic sequences in Endocrine therapy and the emergence of ESR1 alterations are. Extended endocrine therapy and adjuvant CDK4/6 inhibition are not simply longer treatment. They are longer selection, and the ESR1 and RB1 escape routes that define the metastatic setting CDK4/6 inhibition and the evolution of cell cycle escape are being selected for during the years in which the patient is called disease-free.

10 · Adherence and long-term tolerability

Every hazard ratio in this chapter is an intention-to-treat estimate over a population in which a large minority stopped taking the drug. That is not a footnote to the effect size. It is part of what the effect size means.

In a cohort of 8,769 women with stage I to III hormone-sensitive breast cancer who filled at least one prescription, 32% had discontinued endocrine therapy by 4.5 years. Among those who continued, 72% were fully adherent by a medication possession ratio of 80% or more. Combining the two, 49% took adjuvant endocrine therapy for the full duration on the intended schedule. Women under 40 had the highest discontinuation risk, with a hazard ratio of 1.51 and a 95% confidence interval of 1.23 to 1.8552.

The randomised evidence agrees. In the placebo-controlled extended-therapy trials, non-adherence was 39.0% on further aromatase inhibition and 37.6% on placebo49. Almost as many patients stopped an inert tablet as stopped an active one, which says that the burden is the regimen as much as the pharmacology.

The toxicities that drive this are known and are quantified elsewhere in the chapter. Fractures rise with aromatase inhibition in postmenopausal women7 and with ovarian suppression in premenopausal women8. Endometrial cancer and thrombosis rise with tamoxifen7. Arthralgia and vasomotor symptoms are the commonest reasons given for stopping, and the symptom burden is developed in Endocrine therapy and Rehabilitation and survivorship.

In practice

Ask about adherence at every visit and ask in a form that permits a true answer. Half of patients do not complete the prescribed course52.

Treat the symptom rather than shortening the course wherever that is possible. A switch between agents, or a dose reduction with evidence behind it53, keeps a patient on treatment that stopping does not.

Flag women under 40 as the highest-risk group for discontinuation and follow them accordingly52.

When quoting a benefit from any trial in this chapter, remember it was achieved under the same adherence that the patient in front of you will have. The number is not an estimate of perfect compliance and should not be presented as one.
