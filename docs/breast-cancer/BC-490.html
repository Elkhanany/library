Part X · Adjuvant and neoadjuvant therapy by subtype · Chapter 53

Early hormone receptor-positive, HER2-negative disease

Four adjuvant CDK4/6 trials, two positive and two negative, and what that does to the word class.

1 · Clinical and genomic risk assessment

Risk in this subtype runs on two axes that are routinely collapsed into one. The anatomic axis is tumour size and nodal burden. The biological axis is grade, proliferation and the output of a multigene assay. They correlate weakly and they answer different questions.

The distinction is not academic, because the two axes have been used to define eligibility for different treatments. monarchE enrolled on clinicopathological criteria. Eligible patients had four or more positive nodes, or one to three nodes with a tumour of 5 cm or more, histological grade 3, or central Ki-67 of 20% or above1. NATALEE enrolled on anatomic stage instead, covering stage IIA with nodal involvement or with node-negative disease plus additional risk factors, stage IIB and stage III2. Neither used a genomic assay to select.

The anatomic axis also does most of the work in predicting late events. One meta-analysis covered 88 trials and 62,923 women who were disease-free after five years of endocrine therapy. The risk of distant recurrence between years 5 and 20 tracked size and nodal status closely. It ran from 13% for T1N0 disease to 41% for T2 disease with four to nine positive nodes3.

Two measurement cautions belong with all of this. Ki-67 is the least reproducible of the routine variables, and the scoring conventions that make it usable are set out in4 and5. And a genomic assay reports a risk estimate conditioned on the treatment the validation cohort received, which is developed in Prognostic and predictive assays and somatic profiling. Tools that combine the recurrence score with clinicopathological features report a different quantity again6.

2 · Selection of endocrine therapy by menopausal status

Menopausal status is not a patient characteristic in this decision. It is a statement about where oestrogen is being made, and therefore about which blockade will work.

In postmenopausal women an aromatase inhibitor outperforms tamoxifen. A patient-level meta-analysis of 31,920 women found that recurrence rate ratios favoured aromatase inhibitors while the treatments differed, at 0.70 with a 95% confidence interval of 0.64 to 0.77, and not significantly thereafter. Ten-year breast cancer mortality across all comparisons was reduced, with a rate ratio of 0.86 and an interval of 0.80 to 0.947. The same analysis priced the trade. Ten-year endometrial cancer incidence was 0.4% against 1.2% with tamoxifen, and five-year bone fracture risk was 8.2% against 5.5%.

In premenopausal women the ovary is the dominant source, so an aromatase inhibitor alone does not work. It requires ovarian suppression, and the comparison then changes character. A patient-level meta-analysis of 7,030 women receiving ovarian suppression found that an aromatase inhibitor reduced breast cancer recurrence against tamoxifen, with a rate ratio of 0.79 and an interval of 0.69 to 0.90. The absolute reduction in five-year recurrence risk was 3.2 percentage points, from 10.1% to 6.9%. Breast cancer mortality did not differ, with a rate ratio of 1.01 and an interval of 0.82 to 1.24. Fractures were more common, at 6.4% against 5.1%8.

The shape of that result recurs through this chapter. Recurrence falls, mortality has not yet followed, and a named toxicity rises. The agents themselves are covered in Endocrine therapy.

3 · Ovarian function suppression and who benefits

Ovarian suppression is the clearest example in early breast cancer of a treatment whose absolute benefit is concentrated in a minority of the people offered it.

In the combined analysis of SOFT/TEXT, 4,690 premenopausal women received five years of exemestane with ovarian suppression or tamoxifen with ovarian suppression. At a median follow-up of 13 years the 12-year disease-free survival was improved by 4.6 percentage points, with a hazard ratio of 0.79 and a 95% confidence interval of 0.70 to 0.90. Distant recurrence-free interval improved by 1.8 percentage points, with a hazard ratio of 0.83. Overall survival was 90.1% against 89.1% and did not differ, with a hazard ratio of 0.93 and an interval of 0.78 to 1.119.

The subgroups are where the decision lives. In HER2-negative disease the 12-year overall survival advantage of exemestane with suppression was 2.0 percentage points, and 3.3 points in those who had received chemotherapy. It reached 4.0 points in women under 35, 4.5 points in tumours larger than 2 cm and 5.5 points in grade 3 disease9.

The separate question of whether to add suppression to tamoxifen at all was answered in the same programme. Eight-year disease-free survival was 78.9% with tamoxifen alone, 83.2% with tamoxifen and suppression, and 85.9% with exemestane and suppression. Eight-year overall survival was 91.5%, 93.3% and 92.1%. Among women who remained premenopausal after chemotherapy the survival figures were 85.1%, 89.4% and 87.2%10.

In practice

Suppression is a risk-stratified decision, not a menopausal-status decision. The women who gained most were young, high grade, node-positive or chemotherapy-treated9.

In a woman with a small, grade 1, node-negative tumour the absolute gain is around a point and the toxicity is not proportionally smaller. Say that number out loud before prescribing.

Remaining premenopausal after chemotherapy is itself a selection marker, because it identifies the women in whom ovarian function was never removed10.

If suppression is used, the aromatase inhibitor is the better partner on recurrence and has not been shown to be better on survival8,9. Fractures rise, so bone management starts at the same visit.

4 · Genomic assays and the decision to omit chemotherapy

Three randomised trials asked whether a multigene assay can identify patients who do not need chemotherapy. All three are de-escalation trials, and all three should be read alongside the non-inferiority methods in Clinical trials and evidence interpretation and specifically Designing de-escalation trials and the non-inferiority margin problem.

In TAILORx, 10,273 women with node-negative disease were enrolled and the 6,711 with a recurrence score of 11 to 25 were randomised. Endocrine therapy alone was non-inferior to chemoendocrine therapy, with a hazard ratio of 1.08 and a 95% confidence interval of 0.94 to 1.24. At nine years invasive disease-free survival was 83.3% with endocrine therapy and 84.3% with chemoendocrine therapy, and overall survival was 93.9% and 93.8%. Chemotherapy benefit varied with the combination of score and age, and some benefit appeared in women aged 50 or younger with a score of 16 to 2511.

RxPONDER extended the question to one to three positive nodes and a score of 25 or below. Among 5,083 women the effect differed by menopausal status, with an interaction p value of 0.008. In postmenopausal women five-year invasive disease-free survival was 91.9% with endocrine therapy and 91.3% with chemoendocrine therapy, giving a hazard ratio of 1.02 and an interval of 0.82 to 1.26. In premenopausal women it was 89.0% and 93.9%, with a hazard ratio of 0.60 and an interval of 0.43 to 0.8312.

One sentence in that report matters more than the headline. The relative chemotherapy benefit did not increase as the recurrence score increased12. In node-positive premenopausal women the assay identified who was at risk. It did not identify who benefited from chemotherapy.

MINDACT used a 70-gene signature and a separate clinical risk estimate. Among 6,693 enrolled patients, those at high clinical and low genomic risk who received no chemotherapy had a five-year distant metastasis-free survival of 95.1%, above the prespecified 92% boundary. The same discordant group was also randomised. In that intention-to-treat comparison, eight-year distant metastasis-free survival was 92.0% with chemotherapy and 89.4% without, with a hazard ratio of 0.66 and an interval of 0.48 to 0.9213,14.

Caution

A trial that declares endocrine therapy non-inferior has not shown that chemotherapy does nothing. It has shown that the difference is smaller than a margin chosen in advance. The MINDACT intention-to-treat comparison makes the point concretely, because a 2.6 percentage point difference at eight years sits inside a design whose primary question was a single-arm rate14. The general form of this error is set out in Designing de-escalation trials and the non-inferiority margin problem.

Caution

The premenopausal RxPONDER result is widely read as showing that chemotherapy works in young node-positive women with low genomic risk. The flat relationship between score and benefit is compatible with a different reading, which is that some of what chemotherapy delivered was ovarian suppression12. The trial did not randomise suppression, so it cannot separate the two. A patient offered chemotherapy on this evidence should be told that ovarian suppression is the alternative hypothesis, not a consolation prize.

5 · Chemotherapy selection when it is indicated

Once chemotherapy is chosen, the remaining questions are which regimen and on what schedule. Both are settled by meta-analysis rather than by any single trial, and the detail belongs to Cytotoxic chemotherapy.

Two structural points are specific to this subtype. The first is that the absolute gain from anthracycline-containing over taxane-based regimens is small in endocrine-responsive disease, and the trial-level comparisons that establish it are in15. The second is that schedule carries an independent effect. More frequent administration or sequential rather than concurrent scheduling reduces recurrence, established across 37,298 women in 26 trials16. Weekly paclitaxel improves disease-free and overall survival against three-weekly administration17.

The practical consequence is that dose density is available without adding an agent. In a subtype where the marginal benefit of chemotherapy is contested, changing the schedule is a cheaper lever than changing the drugs.

6 · Adjuvant CDK4/6 inhibition, patient selection and open questions

Four randomised trials have added a CDK4/6 inhibitor to adjuvant endocrine therapy. Two are positive and two are negative. Treating the four as a single class question is the most consequential error available in this chapter.

monarchE randomised 5,637 patients with node-positive high-risk disease to endocrine therapy with or without two years of abemaciclib, which is dosed continuously. At the preplanned interim analysis the hazard ratio for invasive disease-free survival was 0.75, with a 95% confidence interval of 0.60 to 0.931. At a median follow-up of 76.2 months the picture is mature. Seven-year invasive disease-free survival was 77.4% with abemaciclib and 70.9% without, an absolute difference of 6.5 percentage points, with a hazard ratio of 0.734 and an interval of 0.657 to 0.820. Seven-year overall survival was 86.8% against 85.0%, an absolute difference of 1.8 points, with a hazard ratio of 0.842, an interval of 0.722 to 0.981 and a p value of 0.02718. Fewer patients were alive with metastatic disease in the abemaciclib arm, at 6.4% against 9.4%.

NATALEE randomised 5,101 patients across a broader population that included selected node-negative disease. They received a nonsteroidal aromatase inhibitor with or without three years of ribociclib at 400 mg, given three weeks on and one week off. The prespecified interim analysis gave a hazard ratio of 0.75 and three-year rates of 90.4% against 87.1%19. The headline figures used here are from the final preplanned invasive disease-free survival analysis, at a July 2023 cutoff and a median follow-up of 33.3 months. That analysis gave a hazard ratio of 0.749, with an interval of 0.628 to 0.892, and three-year rates of 90.7% against 87.6%2. The absolute difference there is 3.1 percentage points.

Two later data cuts follow, and each recomputes its own estimates. At a median follow-up of 44.2 months the hazard ratio was 0.72, with three-year rates of 90.8% against 88.1% and four-year rates of 88.5% against 83.6%20. At a median follow-up of 55.4 months the hazard ratio was 0.716, with an interval of 0.618 to 0.829. Within that analysis the absolute difference grew from 2.7 percentage points at three years to 4.5 points at five years. The node-negative subgroup gave a hazard ratio of 0.606 with an interval of 0.372 to 0.986. Overall survival remains immature, with a hazard ratio of 0.800 and an interval of 0.637 to 1.00321.

Absolute rates are not comparable across those three cuts, because each is estimated on a different amount of follow-up. The hazard ratio is the stable quantity here. It sits between 0.716 and 0.75 in every analysis the trial has reported.

PALLAS randomised 5,761 patients with stage II to III disease to endocrine therapy with or without two years of palbociclib, given on days 1 to 21 of a 28-day cycle. Invasive disease-free events occurred in 253 of 2,884 patients on palbociclib and 263 of 2,877 on endocrine therapy alone. Four-year invasive disease-free survival was 84.2% against 84.5%, with a hazard ratio of 0.96, an interval of 0.81 to 1.14 and a p value of 0.6522.

PENELOPE-B randomised 1,250 patients with residual invasive disease after neoadjuvant chemotherapy to one year of palbociclib or placebo added to endocrine therapy. The stratified hazard ratio for invasive disease-free survival was 0.93, with a 95% repeated confidence interval of 0.74 to 1.1723.

Four trials, four designs. They differ in agent, in duration at one, two and three years, in schedule between continuous and intermittent dosing, and in the population enrolled. No trial varies one of those while holding the others fixed, so nothing in this evidence identifies which variable explains the split.

Caution

Class effect is the word doing the damage here. The three agents have different target selectivity, different schedules and different tolerability, and the two positive trials used the two agents that were not palbociclib. That is consistent with a drug difference. It is equally consistent with a duration difference, an adherence difference or a population difference. Reading a positive result for abemaciclib as support for palbociclib runs against two randomised trials in 7,011 patients that looked for exactly that and did not find it22,23.

In practice

Use the agent in the population in which it was tested, for the duration for which it was tested. That is the only reading these four trials support.

For node-positive high-risk disease meeting the monarchE criteria, two years of abemaciclib has a mature overall survival benefit of 1.8 percentage points at seven years18. Quote that absolute number alongside the 6.5-point recurrence difference.

For stage II to III disease including selected node-negative tumours, three years of ribociclib has a recurrence benefit with immature survival data21. Say that the survival question is open rather than implying it is answered.

Where a patient meets both sets of criteria there is no randomised comparison. Choose on tolerability, duration and what the patient will take, and say that is the basis.

Do not substitute palbociclib for either agent on grounds of access or familiarity. In the adjuvant setting it has been tested twice and has failed twice22,23.

7 · Adjuvant PARP inhibition for germline carriers

OlympiA randomised 1,836 patients with germline BRCA1 or BRCA2 pathogenic variants and high-risk HER2-negative early breast cancer to one year of olaparib or placebo after chemotherapy. The population included both hormone receptor-positive and triple-negative disease, with risk criteria that differed between the two24.

At the third prespecified interim analysis, with a median follow-up of 6.1 years, the benefit was sustained across all three time-to-event endpoints. The hazard ratio for invasive disease-free survival was 0.65, with a 95% confidence interval of 0.53 to 0.78. For distant disease-free survival it was 0.65, with an interval of 0.53 to 0.81. For overall survival it was 0.72, with an interval of 0.56 to 0.93. Six-year overall survival was 87.5% with olaparib and 83.2% with placebo, a difference of 4.4 percentage points with an interval of 0.9 to 6.725.

Two secondary observations matter for the consent conversation. There were fewer new BRCA-associated breast and ovarian or fallopian tube cancers with olaparib. And adverse events of special interest were not increased, occurring in 6.3% of patients on olaparib against 9.3% on placebo, with myelodysplastic syndrome or acute myeloid leukaemia in 0.4% against 0.7%25. The leukaemia signal that was anticipated from the metastatic experience has not appeared at six years.

The mechanism is developed in DNA repair, replication stress, and genomic instability and the agents in Targeted therapy. Germline testing should precede the adjuvant decision rather than follow it, and the testing strategy is in Inherited susceptibility and familial breast cancer.

Caution

Sequencing olaparib with adjuvant CDK4/6 inhibition in a dually eligible patient is unresolved and should be presented that way. OlympiA did not permit a CDK4/6 inhibitor and monarchE did not require germline testing, so neither trial observed the combination or the sequence. Choosing one, choosing the other, or giving them in series are three strategies with no comparative evidence behind any of them. A patient who is offered a definite order should be told that the order is a judgement.

8 · Extended endocrine therapy and duration

The case for extending endocrine therapy rests on the residual hazard quantified in Late recurrence risk and surveillance implications. The case against rests on toxicity and on adherence. A patient-level meta-analysis of 12 trials in 22,031 postmenopausal women who had already completed at least five years of endocrine therapy now prices both sides.

Allocation to further aromatase inhibition reduced recurrence by 27%, with a rate ratio of 0.73 and a 95% confidence interval of 0.67 to 0.8026.

The subset that matters most in current practice is the one already treated with an aromatase inhibitor. There, five further years reduced recurrence with a rate ratio of 0.71, taking the risk from year 5 to year 15 from 15.2% to 11.6%. Distant recurrence fell with a rate ratio of 0.73, from 8.6% to 6.6%. Breast cancer mortality fell non-significantly, with a rate ratio of 0.90, an interval of 0.70 to 1.15 and a p value of 0.40, from 5.0% to 4.4%26.

Because the proportional reduction was similar across tumour characteristics, the absolute benefit scales with baseline risk. Ten years against five years took node-positive risk from 20.1% to 16.3% and node-negative risk from 11.8% to 9.1%26. Five-year fracture risk rose with a rate ratio of 1.35, from 3.4% to 4.6%.

In practice

Extended therapy is a recurrence decision with an unproven mortality effect. Present it that way rather than as a survival intervention26.

Anchor the offer to nodal status, because the proportional benefit is flat and the absolute benefit is not. Between years 5 and 15, ten years of an aromatase inhibitor against five spares about 3.8 percentage points of recurrence risk in node-positive disease and about 2.7 points in node-negative disease26.

Put the fracture number in the same sentence as the recurrence number. Roughly one additional fracture per 83 women over five years is the price26.

A patient who has tolerated five years badly is unlikely to tolerate ten. Non-adherence in these placebo-controlled trials ran at 39.0% on further treatment and 37.6% on placebo26, which is the honest baseline for what extension means in practice.

9 · Late recurrence risk and surveillance implications

This subtype recurs on a timescale that outlasts most follow-up schedules and most trials. The annual hazard of recurrence does not fall to zero after five years, and the pattern across 24 years of follow-up is set out in27 and28.

The size of that residual risk is now precisely quantified. Among 62,923 women with oestrogen receptor-positive disease who were disease-free after five years of scheduled endocrine therapy, recurrences occurred at a steady rate from year 5 to year 20. Distant recurrence risk over that window was 13% for T1N0 disease, 20% for T1N1-3, 34% for T1N4-9, 19% for T2N0, 26% for T2N1-3 and 41% for T2N4-93.

Grade added moderately within a stage stratum. For T1N0 disease the risk over years 5 to 20 was 10% for low-grade, 13% for moderate-grade and 17% for high-grade tumours3. Progesterone receptor and HER2 status were not predictive of late distant recurrence in that dataset.

Two implications follow, and only one of them is comfortable.

The comfortable one is that the original pathology report retains predictive power for two decades. Size and nodal status are not superseded by anything currently in routine use.

The uncomfortable one is that no surveillance strategy has been shown to convert that knowledge into benefit. Detecting asymptomatic distant recurrence earlier has not improved survival, and the evidence against surveillance intensity is in Rehabilitation and survivorship. Circulating tumour DNA detects molecular relapse before imaging does, and whether acting on that lead time helps is unresolved and is the subject of Liquid biopsy and longitudinal monitoring. Late recurrence is a dormancy problem, and its biology is in Dormancy, residual disease, and late recurrence.

Interplay

Heterogeneity in this subtype is mostly temporal rather than spatial, which is why the chapter reads as a series of duration decisions. The adjuvant setting applies years of selective pressure to a population that cannot be sampled, and what emerges at year eight has been shaped by that pressure exactly as the metastatic sequences in Endocrine therapy and the emergence of ESR1 alterations are. Extended endocrine therapy and adjuvant CDK4/6 inhibition are not simply longer treatment. They are longer selection, and the ESR1 and RB1 escape routes that define the metastatic setting CDK4/6 inhibition and the evolution of cell cycle escape are being selected for during the years in which the patient is called disease-free.

10 · Adherence and long-term tolerability

Every hazard ratio in this chapter is an intention-to-treat estimate over a population in which a large minority stopped taking the drug. That is not a footnote to the effect size. It is part of what the effect size means.

In a cohort of 8,769 women with stage I to III hormone-sensitive breast cancer who filled at least one prescription, 32% had discontinued endocrine therapy by 4.5 years. Among those who continued, 72% were fully adherent by a medication possession ratio of 80% or more. Combining the two, 49% took adjuvant endocrine therapy for the full duration on the intended schedule. Women under 40 had the highest discontinuation risk, with a hazard ratio of 1.51 and a 95% confidence interval of 1.23 to 1.8529.

The randomised evidence agrees. In the placebo-controlled extended-therapy trials, non-adherence was 39.0% on further aromatase inhibition and 37.6% on placebo26. Almost as many patients stopped an inert tablet as stopped an active one, which says that the burden is the regimen as much as the pharmacology.

The toxicities that drive this are known and are quantified elsewhere in the chapter. Fractures rise with aromatase inhibition in postmenopausal women7 and with ovarian suppression in premenopausal women8. Endometrial cancer and thrombosis rise with tamoxifen7. Arthralgia and vasomotor symptoms are the commonest reasons given for stopping, and the symptom burden is developed in Endocrine therapy and Rehabilitation and survivorship.

In practice

Ask about adherence at every visit and ask in a form that permits a true answer. Half of patients do not complete the prescribed course29.

Treat the symptom rather than shortening the course wherever that is possible. A switch between agents, or a dose reduction with evidence behind it30, keeps a patient on treatment that stopping does not.

Flag women under 40 as the highest-risk group for discontinuation and follow them accordingly29.

When quoting a benefit from any trial in this chapter, remember it was achieved under the same adherence that the patient in front of you will have. The number is not an estimate of perfect compliance and should not be presented as one.
