Part XI · Hormone receptor-positive, HER2-negative disease · Chapter 59

Adjuvant chemotherapy and the genomic assay decision

Which decisions in this subtype rest on a randomised comparison against usual care, and which rest on a subgroup, a margin or a population nobody enrolled.

1 · Clinical and genomic risk assessment

Risk in this subtype runs on two axes that are routinely collapsed into one. The anatomic axis is tumour size and nodal burden. The biological axis is grade, proliferation and the output of a multigene assay. They correlate weakly and they answer different questions.

This chapter is about how to approach the patient. It covers what to measure, what to give, to whom, for how long, and what to do once treatment stops. The randomised trial record it draws on lives next door and is not repeated here. Adjuvant endocrine therapy is in Adjuvant endocrine therapy. Adjuvant CDK4/6 inhibition is in Adjuvant CDK4/6 inhibition. Preoperative systemic therapy in this subtype is in Neoadjuvant therapy. Adjuvant PARP inhibition is in Adjuvant PARP inhibition for germline carriers. Where a decision below rests on one of those records, the text points forward rather than restating it. The exception is adjuvant chemotherapy, whose randomised record was built in trials that enrolled every subtype. Those trials are tabulated once, in Chemotherapy selection when it is indicated, and argued there and in Cytotoxic chemotherapy.

The two axes are not academic, because they have been used to define eligibility for different treatments. monarchE enrolled on clinicopathological criteria1. NATALEE enrolled on anatomic stage instead2. Only NATALEE accepted a high-risk genomic assay result, and only as one way for grade 2, node-negative stage IIA disease to qualify3. The population column of the table in Scope, and why four trials of one drug class gave different answers is where the difference is legible.

The anatomic axis also does most of the work in predicting late events. Size and nodal status still stratify distant recurrence risk between years 5 and 20. The figures are in Late recurrence risk and surveillance implications4. Nothing in routine use has superseded them.

Two measurement cautions belong with all of this. Ki-67 is the least reproducible of the routine variables, and the scoring conventions that make it usable are set out in5 and6. And a genomic assay reports a risk estimate conditioned on the treatment the validation cohort received, which is developed in Prognostic and predictive assays and somatic profiling. Tools that combine the recurrence score with clinicopathological features report a different quantity again7.

2 · Genomic assays and the decision to omit chemotherapy

Early disease · HR+/HER2- · adjuvant

Choosing the intensity and the duration of endocrine treatment

Can the decision to give chemotherapy be made on biology rather than on anatomy?

Rationale
Node count and tumour size measure how long a cancer has been growing, not how much it depends on oestrogen. A genomic assay measures the second, which is what predicts whether cytotoxic treatment adds anything to endocrine treatment.
Experiment
Randomised trials that assigned chemotherapy by a tumour assay rather than by stage, in node-negative and then node-positive disease, with survival as the endpoint. The first used two invasion proteins measured in tumour tissue. The rest used genomic scores.
Finding
A large fraction of this cell can omit chemotherapy without losing disease control, and the exceptions cluster by age and menopausal status rather than by score alone.
Limitation
The assays were validated on populations treated with the endocrine therapy of their era, and the age effect is confounded with ovarian function, so the benefit attributed to chemotherapy may partly be the benefit of chemotherapy-induced menopause. The earliest trial randomised only the high-risk patients who agreed to it. Its chemotherapy benefit reached significance per protocol but not by intention to treat.

Can the response to treatment be used to allocate treatment, rather than the baseline alone?

Rationale
A baseline assay predicts; a short course of endocrine therapy tests. A tumour whose proliferation collapses under two weeks of an aromatase inhibitor has demonstrated the dependence the assay was trying to infer.
Experiment
Response-adapted designs that give a brief endocrine exposure, measure the change in proliferation, and route patients to chemotherapy or to endocrine treatment on what they see.
Finding
Response separates the population, and the endocrine-responsive group does well on endocrine treatment alone.
Limitation
A dynamic marker predicts outcome in a cohort that has already been selected on it, so the comparison is against a historical expectation rather than against the same patients treated the other way.

What did the cohorts that built the genomic assays actually randomise?

Rationale
A genomic assay that allocates chemotherapy has to be validated on material from patients who were randomised to receive it or not. The archival cohorts that made the recurrence score possible were trials run for a different purpose, and their design constrains what the score can be read to establish.
Experiment
Retrospective assay of banked tumour from randomised adjuvant trials. Tamoxifen against placebo in node-negative receptor-positive disease, chemotherapy added to tamoxifen in the same population, and chemoendocrine therapy against endocrine therapy alone in node-positive postmenopausal disease.
Finding
The score is prognostic in the endocrine-treated arms and predicts chemotherapy benefit in the randomised comparisons, with the benefit concentrated at the high end of the range. The node-positive cohort established that the same relationship holds when nodes are involved.
Limitation
These are retrospective analyses of prospectively randomised trials, which is stronger than a cohort and weaker than a trial designed to answer the question. The chemotherapy regimens are decades old and no longer given, so a benefit measured against them may not transfer. The assay was developed on some of the same material it is validated against.

Each of the three contributed a different piece, and none of them was designed to. The randomised comparison in NSABP B-14 was tamoxifen against placebo, so the assay work used one arm of it and could show only that the score is prognostic under tamoxifen11. NSABP B-20 randomised chemotherapy added to tamoxifen, so it could ask about an interaction, and reported one12. SWOG-8814 / INT-0100 carried the same structure into postmenopausal node-positive disease with an anthracycline regimen13. The parent trial had randomised chemotherapy followed by tamoxifen against tamoxifen alone, and chemotherapy given before tamoxifen against the two given together8. Prognostic and predictive are different claims, and only the second and third rows were built in a way that could support the predictive one.

What all three share matters more than what separates them. Each measured the score in banked blocks from a minority of the enrolled patients, and tissue availability is not a random event. Each conditioned the score on tamoxifen alone as the endocrine backbone, because that was the treatment of the era. The score was later shown to be prognostic under anastrozole, in node-negative and node-positive disease, in TransATAC14. That established risk under an aromatase inhibitor, not chemotherapy benefit. Nobody has recalibrated the chemotherapy thresholds against an aromatase inhibitor or against tamoxifen with ovarian suppression, which is what the patient in front of you will receive. That is a measurement problem rather than a biological one, and it propagates into every prospective trial below, all of which inherited the thresholds.

The same archival route produced the score's other associations. In node-negative NSABP trials it was associated with locoregional recurrence as well as distant recurrence15. Prognostic estimates were better when the score was combined with clinicopathological information than when it was combined with the online Adjuvant calculator16. Registry and prospective cohorts then allocated treatment by the score. In PlanB, 348 patients with a score of 11 or less omitted chemotherapy, and their three-year disease-free survival was 98%17. In a registry of node-positive disease with micrometastases or one to three positive nodes, outcomes with a score below 18 supported endocrine therapy alone18.

Four reported trials and one still running, and they converge on one finding. In node-negative or limited node-positive hormone receptor-positive disease, a large fraction of patients can be treated with endocrine therapy alone without a measurable loss of distant disease control over the follow-up so far accrued. That is the finding. Everything else in the table is a difference in how it was established.

Those differences are design, population and endpoint, not assay performance. TAILORx randomised chemotherapy against no chemotherapy inside a midrange score band in node-negative disease, and was powered as a non-inferiority comparison22. MINDACT randomised only the patients whose clinical and genomic risk disagreed, and its primary question was a single-arm rate tested against a fixed boundary, with the randomised comparison secondary24. RxPONDER moved to one to three positive nodes and capped the score at 2521. WSG-ADAPT HR+/HER2− compared two prospectively defined groups rather than two randomised ones, because allocation followed biology19. The assays differ too, with a 70-gene signature in MINDACT and a 21-gene score elsewhere, on thresholds that are not interchangeable. And the endpoint differs, because a distant metastasis-free survival endpoint and an invasive disease-free survival endpoint do not count the same events. The second counts second primaries and non-breast deaths, which dilutes a chemotherapy effect in an older population and inflates it in none.

The decision in node-negative disease rests on TAILORx. It is the only row that randomised the thing a clinic would otherwise do against the thing it would otherwise omit. It did so in the population where the question is actually asked, with enough events to bound the difference.

RxPONDER is the one that did not settle its question, and the reason sits in the interaction rather than in either arm. Chemotherapy benefit differed by menopausal status, with an interaction p value of 0.00821. The two strata point in opposite directions. A single hazard ratio for that trial would describe nobody. One sentence in that report matters more than the headline. The relative chemotherapy benefit did not increase as the recurrence score increased. In node-positive premenopausal women the assay identified who was at risk. It did not identify who benefited from chemotherapy.

Read alongside TAILORx, where the residual chemotherapy signal also sat in women aged 50 or younger, that is the same finding twice. MINDACT looked in the same place, reporting an exploratory analysis by age in its update20. Age and menopausal status travel together, and no trial in this table randomised ovarian suppression. The benefit attributed to chemotherapy in young women cannot be separated from the benefit of chemotherapy-induced menopause.

WSG-ADAPT HR+/HER2− answers the second question and answers it in a form that needs care. Three weeks of preoperative endocrine therapy separated the population, with central Ki-67 read before and after. The endocrine-responsive midrange group then did as well on endocrine therapy alone as the low-score control group19. The comparison was between two arms defined by the marker, so nobody with an endocrine-responsive midrange tumour was treated the other way. The response rate also depends on which drug was given, at 78.1% on an aromatase inhibitor against 41.1% on tamoxifen. Endocrine response as measured here is a property of the test as much as of the tumour.

WSG ADAPTcycle is still running. It asks whether ribociclib with an aromatase inhibitor can take the place of the chemotherapy that a recurrence score and an endocrine response test would otherwise mandate. That is the only substitution question in the table. Its reported concordance work is the part to read now. Local and central Ki-67 disagreed on the endocrine-response call often enough that 13.1% of patients called responders locally were non-responders centrally23. A response-guided strategy is only as portable as the assay that defines response.

All five rows should be read alongside the non-inferiority methods in Clinical trials and evidence interpretation and specifically Designing de-escalation trials and the non-inferiority margin problem. Three things the table cannot give you are worth naming. Nobody randomised assay use against no assay use, so the value of testing itself is inferred from the outcomes of the tested. Nobody enrolled four or more positive nodes into an omission question. And the overall survival column is where the gap shows, because the node-positive trial has not reported it and this subtype recurs for twenty years Late recurrence risk and surveillance implications.

Caution

A trial that declares endocrine therapy non-inferior has not shown that chemotherapy does nothing. It has shown that the difference is smaller than a margin chosen in advance. The MINDACT intention-to-treat comparison makes the point concretely, because its eight-year distant metastasis-free survival favoured chemotherapy inside a design whose primary question was a single-arm rate against a fixed boundary20. The general form of this error is set out in Designing de-escalation trials and the non-inferiority margin problem.

Caution

The premenopausal RxPONDER result is widely read as showing that chemotherapy works in young node-positive women with low genomic risk. The flat relationship between score and benefit is compatible with a different reading, which is that some of what chemotherapy delivered was ovarian suppression21. A later serum analysis in women younger than 55 found the benefit confined to those whose anti-Mullerian hormone showed normal ovarian reserve25. The trial did not randomise suppression, so it cannot separate the two. A patient offered chemotherapy on this evidence should be told that ovarian suppression is the alternative hypothesis, not a consolation prize.

3 · Chemotherapy selection when it is indicated

Choosing the intensity and the duration of endocrine treatment, continued

Which chemotherapy regimen, for the minority of this subtype who need one?

Rationale
The adjuvant chemotherapy backbone was built before subtype was used to allocate it. Agents were added one at a time to a regimen that already worked. Each addition was tested in a population enrolled without regard to receptor status. The record a clinic now uses for this subtype was therefore built largely in patients who were not selected to be in it.
Experiment
Sequential randomised additions and substitutions on an anthracycline backbone. A taxane added after doxorubicin and cyclophosphamide, the interval between cycles shortened with growth factor support, the anthracycline replaced by a taxane and cyclophosphamide, and one taxane and schedule compared against another. Before them, a single alkylating agent was set against placebo and an anthracycline put in place of methotrexate. Beside them, a taxane was put in place of fluorouracil and set against an anthracycline course of the same length. A third drug was added to the full course. Dose was pushed further, by higher single-agent intensity and by high-dose chemotherapy with stem-cell support. A taxane-based regimen was tried in older patients.
Finding
Adding a taxane improved outcomes against a shorter anthracycline control, and in place of fluorouracil. In place of further anthracycline cycles it did not. Shortening the interval improved outcomes in three trials and in the patient-level meta-analysis, although neither trial that compressed an anthracycline regimen alone reached significance. One four-cycle anthracycline-free regimen matched a taxane and anthracycline combination while a six-cycle version of the same idea did not. Weekly paclitaxel and three-weekly docetaxel each beat three-weekly paclitaxel. No third drug added to the full course met its primary endpoint.
Limitation
Almost none of this was randomised within this subtype, and the subtype composition of each trial differs. A regimen comparison that enrolled a mixed population reports an average over subtypes whose sensitivity to chemotherapy is not the same, so a result that holds overall may not hold here. The non-inferiority margins were set separately and are not comparable. Several trials changed two things at once, the interval with the sequence or a drug with the dose of another. They cannot say which did the work.

The thirteen trials answer three questions that are habitually read as one, and they agree on one thing across all three. Every increment here runs to single percentage points of disease-free or event-free survival, and none of the modern comparisons found a regimen that was clearly wrong.

Whether a taxane should follow an anthracycline was asked by CALGB 9344 and NSABP B-28. Both found the same relative reduction in disease-free survival events and diverged on survival, which CALGB 9344 improved and NSABP B-28 did not37,36. Nothing about the taxane differs between them. CALGB 9344 also randomised three doxorubicin doses and found no dose effect, which is the more durable result in that row. Its unplanned subgroup analysis put the recurrence hazard ratio at 0.72 in oestrogen receptor-negative and 0.91 in receptor-positive tumours37. That single comparison does more to explain the position of taxanes in this subtype than either trial's headline.

Two later rows ask the same question against controls of the same length as the experimental arm. The answer depends on what the taxane displaced. GEICAM 9805 put docetaxel in place of fluorouracil in high-risk node-negative disease and lowered recurrence, consistently across hormone receptor status, although its survival analysis rested on few deaths32. TACT set docetaxel after four cycles of FEC against eight cycles of anthracycline-based chemotherapy, so docetaxel took the place of the second half of an anthracycline-based course. It found no difference33. Why that control matters is argued in Regimen selection and scheduling. In this subtype the more useful line is TACT's exploratory one. Docetaxel benefit was most apparent in oestrogen receptor-negative, HER2-positive tumours, with lesser or no apparent benefit elsewhere. The investigators proposed that receptor-positive, HER2-negative disease gains little from a taxane33. That is the CALGB 9344 subgroup again, in a second trial, and still a hypothesis.

Inside that same question sits the choice of taxane and schedule, which ECOG E1199 asked directly. The answer is a pairing rather than a class. Weekly paclitaxel beat three-weekly paclitaxel on disease-free and overall survival. Docetaxel every three weeks also beat three-weekly paclitaxel on disease-free survival. Weekly docetaxel did not35. A trial with four arms is the only design that could have shown that. Its price was grade 2 to 4 neuropathy in 27% of the weekly paclitaxel arm against 20% of the control.

Whether the interval between cycles carries an independent effect was asked by five of these trials. Only two answer it cleanly. CALGB 9741 and GIM2, twelve years apart, on different backbones, are both positive on disease-free and overall survival. Both also carried a second randomisation that added nothing, to concurrent against sequential scheduling in CALGB 9741 and to fluorouracil in GIM238,31. AGO ETC (iddEPC) is positive on both endpoints too. It also changed the sequence along with the interval28. GONO-MIG-1 and TACT2 found no significant difference26,30. Why those two do not overturn the effect is argued in Dose intensity, dose density, and dose modification. So is why AGO ETC cannot isolate it. The schedule effect is still the claim in this table with the broadest support, because the patient-level meta-analysis pooled the flat trials with the positive ones and kept the gain39.

For this subtype the row to read is GONO-MIG-1's. Its subgroup analysis found the gain in hormone receptor-negative tumours and none in receptor-positive ones. Its own test found no significant difference in effect by receptor status26. The meta-analysis found the proportional reduction similar in oestrogen receptor-positive and -negative disease, with a somewhat smaller absolute gain in the first39. A single trial's receptor split is not a reason to withhold the schedule here.

TACT2's second randomisation belongs to the argument about ovarian function in Genomic assays and the decision to omit chemotherapy. Capecitabine in place of CMF after epirubicin was non-inferior on time to recurrence. Permanent amenorrhoea was far less common with capecitabine than with CMF in women who were premenopausal at entry30. Its investigators drew no conclusion about whether that difference mattered for recurrence. It shows only that two regimens of similar efficacy can differ sharply in the menopause they cause.

Whether the anthracycline can come out altogether was asked by US Oncology 9735, ABC trials and WSG-PlanB. Those three appear to disagree and largely do not, because they did not use the same control arm. US Oncology 9735 compared four cycles of docetaxel and cyclophosphamide with four cycles of doxorubicin and cyclophosphamide, which is an anthracycline regimen containing no taxane34. It is evidence that a taxane doublet beats an old doublet. It is not evidence about the modern comparator.

ABC trials and WSG-PlanB both used that modern comparator, setting six cycles of docetaxel and cyclophosphamide against a regimen containing a taxane and an anthracycline. Both returned small differences, and the two point estimates do not favour the same arm. Their verdicts differ because their margins were set on different scales. ABC set an inferiority boundary on the hazard ratio at 1.18 and returned 1.202, so non-inferiority could not be declared29. PlanB set a margin of 4.4 percentage points of disease-free survival and cleared it27. The trials also differ in follow-up at the time of the verdict and in how their randomised populations were assembled, because PlanB removed its lowest genomic risk stratum from randomisation altogether. ABC has since reported with longer follow-up40. Read the margin before reading the conclusion.

Two structural points are specific to this subtype, and one of them is about the table itself. Eleven of these thirteen trials enrolled without regard to receptor status. So did every trial in the next table. They appear here because this chapter is where the book tabulates the all-comers adjuvant chemotherapy record, not because they enrolled this population. Their estimates are averages over a population in which hormone receptor-positive disease was the part least likely to benefit. The absolute gain from anthracycline-containing over taxane-based regimens is small in endocrine-responsive disease, and the trial-level comparisons that establish it are in41. Schedule, by contrast, carries an independent effect established across 37,298 women in 26 trials39.

The practical consequence is that dose density is available without adding an agent. In a subtype where the marginal benefit of chemotherapy is contested, changing the schedule is a cheaper lever than changing the drugs.

The rest of the all-comers record asked other questions, and the second table holds it. Two rows come from before the backbone. NSABP B-05 set a single alkylating agent against placebo after mastectomy42,43, and NCIC CTG MA.5 put epirubicin in place of methotrexate in premenopausal node-positive disease44. Three added a third drug to a full anthracycline and taxane course. FinXX and US Oncology 01062 added capecitabine45,46, and SUCCESS-A added gemcitabine47. Two pushed dose further. SWOG S9313 (INT-0137) raised the intensity of each drug at an unchanged total dose48. WSG AM-01 gave tandem high-dose chemotherapy with stem-cell support to patients with nine or more involved nodes49. ICE II / GBG 52 tested a taxane-based regimen in patients aged 65 or older, with tolerability as its endpoint50. Chemo-N0 asked the question of Genomic assays and the decision to omit chemotherapy a generation early, allocating CMF in node-negative disease by two invasion proteins measured in tumour tissue51.

All-comers adjuvant chemotherapy trials outside the backbone's three questions: what came before it, what was added to it, how far dose was pushed, older patients, and selection by a tumour marker.

NSABP B-05, MA.5, SWOG S9313 and Chemo-N0, which enrolled between 1972 and 1998, and WSG AM-01 have no taxane in either arm.

None of the three additions met its primary endpoint, which is the finding to carry into clinic. The two capecitabine trials each reported a survival difference as a secondary endpoint. What that can and cannot mean is argued in Neoadjuvant and adjuvant applications. For this subtype the more useful result in US Oncology 01062 is a measurement. Among its oestrogen receptor-positive, HER2-negative patients, 70% of them node-positive, about a quarter had a central Ki-67 below 10%. Only 17 of those had a disease-free survival event by seven years46. Its investigators concluded that such patients should not be enrolled in adjuvant trials measured by five-year disease-free survival. That is a statement about trial design. It is also, from inside a chemotherapy trial, a description of the tumour the assays in Genomic assays and the decision to omit chemotherapy were built to find.

What neither table can show is a regimen comparison in this subtype alone. Apart from the two HER2-negative trials, ABC and PlanB, the regimen record for hormone receptor-positive, HER2-negative disease is an average taken over everyone.
