Options exercise is governed by the [[Options Clearing Corporation]] (OCC), the central counterparty for every listed US options contract. When a holder [[exercises|exercise]] an option, the OCC assigns the obligation to a short holder, whose [[assignment]] then settles in shares or in cash. The rules of that process, and their timing, decide what you hold on Monday morning.

## The expiration timeline

For standard monthly equity options, which expire on the third Friday of the month, the timeline runs as follows:

- **Thursday close (T − 1).** With every open position still alive, [[wholesalers|wholesaler]] post final-hour quotes and [[pin risk]] peaks for short options near their strikes.
- **Friday close (T, the expiration).** The close is about 16:00 ET for equities and 16:15 ET for SPX and XSP, and the final settlement print is computed. Equity options settle to the official 4:00 PM close. [[SPX weekly options|SPXW]] and XSP weekly options settle to the 4:15 PM close, as PM-settled options. SPX monthlies, the standard third-Friday contracts, settle to the Friday-morning [[SOQ|special opening quotation]], an opening-print auction with its own quirks.
- **Friday evening: automatic exercise.** Under OCC Rule 805, the OCC automatically exercises any long option that closes more than $0.01 in the money. The holder can stop it only by filing a "do not exercise" instruction with the broker by the cutoff, typically 5:30 PM ET on Friday. This is the source of most "I got assigned overnight" surprises: the $0.01 threshold is unforgiving.
- **Saturday: assignment notification.** The OCC notifies clearing firms of the net exercises. The firms allocate the assignments to their short holders by one of two methods, random or first in, first out (FIFO), which each broker discloses.
- **Monday morning: settlement.** Assigned equity options settle as stock for cash through the Depository Trust Company (DTC), while cash-settled index options settle as wire transfers.

## Early exercise of American options

For [[American-style options|American-style option]], such as single stocks, SPY, [[QQQ]] and IWM, the holder can exercise on *any* trading day before expiration. The OCC accepts exercise notices throughout the day and assigns them to short holders, typically overnight, for settlement the next business day.

A holder exercises early only when it pays, and two economic conditions make it rational (Chapter 5F):

- **An American call on a dividend-paying stock** is worth exercising just before the [[ex-dividend date]] if the dividend exceeds the call's remaining time value. The holder captures the dividend at the cost of the time value.
- **A deep in-the-money (ITM) American put** is worth exercising when the interest on its strike exceeds its remaining time value. The holder receives the strike's cash now and reinvests it at the risk-free rate.

With \(K\) the strike, \(r\) the risk-free rate and \(T\) the time to expiry in years, the put's condition reads

\[ K \, r \, T > \text{time value} \]
{#eq:putex}

and the call's condition is that the dividend exceeds the time value, the [[extrinsic value]] of Chapter 5B.

::: worked Early assignment from the short side
**Step 1: a short put.** You are short 5 American puts struck at $50 on a stock at $30, with 3 months left, a rate of 5% and $0.25 of time value. By [eq:putex], \(50 \times 0.05 \times 0.25 = 0.625\), which is more than $0.25, so expect early assignment, which would have you buy 500 shares at $50 for $25,000 at once.

**Step 2: a short call before a dividend.** You are short 10 deep in-the-money calls on a stock that goes ex-dividend tomorrow with a $1.00 dividend, and each call has $0.30 of time value. The dividend exceeds the time value, so expect assignment tonight. You are then short 1,000 shares on the ex-dividend date and owe the $1,000 dividend.
:::

For [[European-style options|European-style option]], such as SPX, XSP, NDX, VIX and RUT, all cash-settled indexes, early exercise is impossible by contract design. The contract permits exercise only at the official expiration print.

## The "expired worthless" edge case

A short holder occasionally sees an option close $0.005 in the money, just under the OCC's $0.01 threshold for automatic exercise. The option is recorded as "expired", and the short holder keeps the full premium. But the underlying may keep trading after hours, sometimes pushing through the strike far enough that the long holder *exercises manually*, which is allowed up to the 5:30 PM cutoff. The short holder, having priced the position as "expired", wakes up Monday to an unwanted assignment.

The defense is to **close any short option trading within $0.05 of the strike in the final hour before expiration**. The OCC's threshold is too coarse to rely on, since a long holder can still exercise by hand in the window after the close. The cheapest insurance is closing the short.

## SOQ settlement for SPX monthlies

Standard monthly SPX options, expiring on the third Friday of every month, settle to the **Special Opening Quotation** (SOQ). The SOQ is a Friday-morning opening-print auction that produces a single index level for settlement.

The SOQ has historically been a source of pin-risk surprises. The opening-print auction can produce settlement values materially different from Thursday's close, especially in high-volatility regimes.

::: worked A short call settled by the SOQ
**Step 1: Thursday.** You are short an SPX 6,000 call, and SPX closes Thursday at 5,998, leaving the call 2 points out of the money.

**Step 2: Friday morning.** The SOQ prints at 6,005, so the short call settles 5 points in the money. It costs \(5 \times \$100\), which is $500 per contract, a loss that did not exist at Thursday's close.
:::

The defense is to **close SPX monthly options on Thursday afternoon, not Friday morning**. This is the single most important operational rule for trading SPX monthlies. Weekly SPX options, PM-settled to the Friday 4:15 PM close, do not carry this SOQ risk: only the monthlies do.

## Cash settlement against physical settlement

Under [[cash settlement]], used by SPX, XSP, NDX, RUT and VIX options, an assignment moves only cash. Your account receives or pays the difference between the strike and the settlement level, times the multiplier. No shares change hands, so there is no weekend gap risk on the underlying. A short SPX 5,900 put settled at 5,850, for example, pays out \((5{,}900 - 5{,}850) \times \$100\), which is $5,000, and the position is closed.

Under [[physical settlement]], used by SPY, QQQ, IWM, AAPL, NVDA and every single stock, shares actually move:

- **A long call exercised:** you receive 100 shares at the strike.
- **A short put assigned:** you receive 100 shares at the strike, paid for from your account.
- **A short call assigned:** you deliver 100 shares, which are sold short from your account if you do not already own them.

The shares show up Monday morning, fully exposed to the weekend gap (Chapter 5F).

Cash settlement is structurally cleaner for systematic income strategies, which is the central operational reason this book prefers XSP to SPY for retail premium-selling, alongside the Section 1256 tax advantage (Chapter 2E).

::: practice
In expiration week, sort every short option by how it settles and when. Close SPX monthlies on Thursday afternoon. Close any short option within $0.05 of its strike in the final hour on Friday. Before an ex-dividend date, check each short in-the-money American call's time value against the dividend. For each physically settled short, multiply by 100 shares a contract to know how much stock, and how much cash, could arrive on Monday.
:::

::: warn When it fails
The rules above remove the predictable surprises, not every one. An American option can be exercised on any day, including in conditions that are not rational for the holder. Closing a short near the strike assumes there is a market to close into. In fast markets the final hour's spread can be wide. Cash settlement removes delivered shares, not the settlement print, and the SOQ can still differ from every level you saw.
:::

::: rule
- **Close SPX monthly options by Thursday afternoon**, unless you accept the SOQ print.
- **Close any short option within $0.05 of the strike** in the final hour before expiration.
- **Expect early assignment** on a short American put when \(K r T\) exceeds its time value, and on a short American call when the next dividend exceeds its time value.
- **Prefer cash-settled European index options**, such as XSP and SPX, for systematic income strategies.
:::

::: warn Common mistake: trusting "expired"
A short option that closes a fraction of a cent in the money, or just out of it, can still be exercised by hand after the close. The broker's "expired" label on Friday evening is not final until the exercise cutoff has passed and Monday's statement arrives.
:::

::: warn Common mistake: holding an SPX monthly into Friday
A trader treats the monthly SPX option like a weekly and holds it to the Friday close. It settles instead at Friday morning's SOQ, which can sit on the other side of the strike from Thursday's close.
:::

::: recap
- The OCC runs exercise and assignment: automatic exercise above $0.01 in the money on Friday evening, assignment notices on Saturday, and settlement on Monday.
- American options can be exercised early, unlike European index options: puts when \(K r T\) exceeds their time value, and calls before a dividend larger than their time value.
- An option recorded as expired can still be exercised by hand before the 5:30 PM cutoff. Close shorts within $0.05 of the strike in the final hour.
- SPX monthlies settle to the Friday-morning SOQ, so close them on Thursday. Weekly SPX options settle to the Friday close.
- Cash settlement moves only cash and removes the weekend gap on delivered shares, which is why the book prefers XSP to SPY.
:::

::: check
?? Your long SPY call closes Friday $0.02 in the money, and you would rather not buy the shares. What happens, and what can you do?
!! The OCC exercises it automatically, since it closed more than $0.01 in the money, and you receive 100 shares a contract on Monday. To prevent that, file a "do not exercise" instruction with your broker before the cutoff, typically 5:30 PM ET.

?? You are short an American put struck at $80 on a stock at $55, with 2 months left, a 4.5% rate and $0.20 of time value. Should you expect early assignment?
!! Yes: by [eq:putex], \(80 \times 0.045 \times 2/12 = 0.60\), which exceeds the $0.20 of time value, so the holder gains by exercising now.

?? You are short a monthly 6,000 SPX put when SPX closes Thursday at 6,003, and the SOQ then prints at 5,991. What do you owe?
!! The put settles \(6{,}000 - 5{,}991 = 9\) points in the money, so you pay \(9 \times \$100\), which is $900 a contract. At Thursday's close it was out of the money.

?? Why does a short XSP put carry no weekend gap risk at expiry, while a short SPY put does?
!! XSP is cash-settled: the settlement fixes the payment on Friday, and no shares arrive. An assigned SPY put delivers 100 shares a contract, which you hold through the weekend and which open Monday at whatever price the gap brings.
:::
