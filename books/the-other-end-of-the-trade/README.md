# The Other End of the Trade

SPX options read from the dealer's side of the trade. This is the Education curriculum of the
Dealer Mechanics Lab (`Elkhanany/dealer-lab`, `src/content/education`), imported into the
library: the same twelve sections, in the library's frame, type, colours and navigation, with
every interactive widget of the app rewritten as a library figure.

## What is here

```
source/          the app's twelve markdown files, verbatim. Refreshed by --import, never edited here.
outline.json     which ## subsection becomes which chapter, and the commit source/ came from
figures/         one file per widget: the figure's markup and its script
  _lib.js        what every figure shares: the app's own maths, tapes and indicators, and
                 drawing helpers over NMT.Plot
src/_landing.html  the front door, hand-written
src/*.html       the chapters, generated by tools/spx.py. Do not edit them.
curriculum.json  generated by tools/spx.py
book.json        title, theme and the part blurbs on the front door
```

## How the app becomes a book

`tools/spx.py` does the whole conversion and changes presentation only. `--verify` proves
it: every one of the source's words reaches the book, in order, and nothing is added to the
prose.

- **Parts** are the app's sections, labelled as the app labels them in its own navigation
  (Part I · Foundation to Part XII · Discipline). A part's subtitle is the italic line under
  its title, and the text before its first subsection opens its first chapter under "About
  this part".
- **Chapters** are the `##` subsections, numbered as the source numbers them (1A, 10A.5,
  10H.2), because the prose cites them that way about two hundred times. "Section 8A" in the
  text is a link to chapter 8A. A title's words after the dash become the subtitle.
- **The Strategy Atlas** is the exception. Its tier subsections run from 75 to 1,100 words,
  so they are gathered into one chapter per tier (11.1 to 11.4), each subsection a section of
  it, and the atlas continues the source's own 11.0 numbering to 11.7 for the appendices.
- **Blocks** map onto components the library already has: a blockquote is an aside, a table
  is the house data table (it scrolls sideways inside itself, labels every cell on a phone,
  and bleeds into the margin on a wide screen), and a fenced block is a formula block.
- **Figures** replace `[INTERACTIVE: id]` markers. A marker inside a sentence (one in 9A)
  names the figure and links to it, rather than mounting a second copy as the app did.

## Where the import departs from the app

- 8D writes `MA(|returns|)` inside a table cell, which the app's markdown reads as two cell
  breaks and renders as a torn row. Here a pipe inside parentheses is kept as text.
- The EV bet builder's second preset was captioned "EV ≈ +$0.0125/toss" in the app; its own
  numbers (25% at +$35, 75% at −$1) give +$8.00, as the prose says. The figure labels its
  presets by name only.
- The CNDR-versus-SPX figure draws the app's stand-in series, which the app's data file
  describes as synthetic. The figure says so in its caption.
- Figures never move until the reader asks. The app's animations start from a button here,
  and honour reduced motion.

## Changing the book

The prose belongs to the app. Change it there, then:

```bash
python3 tools/spx.py --import ../dealer-lab   # copy the curriculum; record the commit in outline.json
python3 tools/spx.py                          # regenerate curriculum.json and src/
python3 tools/spx.py --verify
```

A new `##` subsection has to be given a chapter in `outline.json`; the tool fails on one that
belongs to none. A new widget needs a figure in `figures/`: copy the shape of an existing one,
keep the rules at the top of `_lib.js`, and try it with `python3 tools/spxfig.py <id>`.
