# The Other End of the Trade

SPX options read from the dealer's side of the trade, and the discipline of trading them. The
book is the library's rewrite of two texts by the same author:

- the Education curriculum of the Dealer Mechanics Lab (`Elkhanany/dealer-lab`,
  `src/content/education`), and
- the SPX/XSP Edge Book (`Elkhanany/spx-book`), which came first.

It is written for a reader who is new to markets and knows algebra and introductory
statistics. It teaches everything else where it is first needed, keeps the maths exact, ends
each idea in what it means for a real account, and follows the Google developer documentation
style guide. Every interactive widget of the app is a library figure, 54 in all.

## What is here

```
outline.json       the parts and chapters: numbers, titles, what each chapter is rewritten
                   from, the figures it places, and the commits the sources were taken at
chapters/          the text, one file per chapter, in the house markdown. This is the book.
glossary/          the glossary, one JSON file per part. The R1 page is built from it.
notes/             per chapter: its sources, and what the rewrite dropped, changed, could not
                   settle or asks the author
CONVENTIONS.md     the writers' brief: reader, voice, chapter shape, markdown, notation, facts
source/            the two sources as they were imported. Read-only.
figures/           one file per figure: its markup and its script
  _lib.js          what every figure shares: the app's own maths, tapes and indicators, and
                   drawing helpers over NMT.Plot
FIGURES.md         how a figure is written
src/_landing.html  the front door, written by hand
src/*.html         the chapters, generated by tools/spx.py. Do not edit them.
curriculum.json    generated: the parts and chapters, for the library's build
FIGURE-IDEAS.md    generated: every place a chapter marks where a new figure would help
QUESTIONS.md       generated: every question the rewrite leaves for the author
book.json          title, theme, and the part blurbs on the front door
```

## Where the book came from

The Edge Book came first. The curriculum's Discipline section grew out of it: that section is
the Edge Book's framework (the retail haircut, the conviction score, sizing and kill criteria)
rewritten for swing trades. The rest of the curriculum is its own, from returns and risk
through dealer hedging to the tactical toolkit and the strategy atlas. The two texts share
almost no sentences.

The first version of this book imported the curriculum word for word. This version is a
rewrite that draws on both texts:

- The curriculum gives Parts II to IX, XI and XII, Chapter 1A, and most of Part XIII.
- The Edge Book gives most of Part I from 1B on: the shape of returns, the Sharpe-ratio trap,
  drawdown, leverage and margin, correlation and liquidity, and the four sources of edge
  (Chapter 1G).
- The Edge Book also gives Part X, one chapter per edge with its evidence, including calendar
  effects (10G) and what not to trade (10I). It gives the portfolio and kill criteria (13E and
  13G) and the Reference part.
- Where both cover a topic (1B to 1D, 13A to 13C, 13F), the chapter gives it once and keeps
  every number from both.

`outline.json` names each chapter's sources, and `python3 tools/spx.py --sources <slug>` prints
them. `source/` keeps one slip of the app's text as imported: `10-tactical.md` repeats the
second half of section 10A.5, which Chapter 11B uses once.

## The rewrite

`CONVENTIONS.md` is the brief every chapter was written to. In short:

- **The reader** knows algebra and introductory statistics, and is at home with clinical trials
  and evidence. Logarithms, compounding, expectation, distributions and the calculus ideas are
  taught where they are first needed.
- **The maths** is exact. Every symbol is defined, derivations are built step by step (the long
  ones in a box the reader opens), and every formula is worked once with numbers.
- **Every idea is practical.** It ends in what it means for a real account: costs, taxes,
  margin, sizing, and a rule for when to act.
- **The wording** follows the Google developer documentation style guide and the library's
  house-style rules, with two exceptions for this book: it speaks to the reader as "you", and
  it uses American spelling.

What the rewrite keeps, adds and fixes:

- **It keeps every fact.** Each number, date, name, study and hedge in a chapter's sources is
  in the chapter, or in its notes file with the reason it was dropped. `python3 tools/spx.py
  --facts <slug>` checks this.
- **It adds teaching, not facts.** Definitions, derivations, worked examples, toy numbers that
  say they are toys, and clinical analogies are new. Statistics, studies, dates and prices are
  not.
- **Disagreements stay disagreements.** The Edge Book rejects systematic iron condors and
  trading around gamma-exposure levels for retail accounts; the curriculum teaches both. Where
  the sources differ, the chapters state both positions and link each other.
- **It fixes errors.** Arithmetic, signs and units that were wrong in a source are corrected in
  the text. Each chapter's notes file lists its corrections under "Changed".
- **It leaves questions.** What the sources do not settle (an unsourced statistic, two numbers
  that disagree) is in the chapter's notes under "Questions for the author", and all of them
  are gathered in `QUESTIONS.md`.

## What the rewrite corrected

Every correction the rewrite made to a source is in `CORRECTIONS.md`, by chapter, so that it
can be carried back to the app and to the Edge Book. Each chapter's text was checked twice:
by its writer, and by a second, independent check that recomputed every worked example,
formula and answer and ran the figures' own code. The corrections that change a number or a
conclusion a reader would act on:

- **Compounding (1A).** A +50% gain followed by a −50% loss costs 25% a pair, not 13.4%, and
  repeating the pair grinds wealth toward zero without ever reaching it.
- **Tail events (1B).** A 3-sigma day comes about once every 2.9 years, not twice a decade.
- **Utility (1C).** The log-utility example's expected log return is +0.0501, positive, where
  the source called it negative.
- **Risk-adjusted return (1D).** The ruin formula's exponent is 2μ/σ² − 1, the Sortino ratio
  exceeds the Sharpe ratio even for a symmetric strategy, and the Sharpe ratio is return per
  unit of risk, where the source had it the other way up.
- **Taxes and contract size (2D, 2E).** The like-for-like tax gap between SPY and XSP at the
  top bracket is 10.2 points, not 12 to 14. XSP's tax advantage is against SPY, not SPX. An
  SPX option carries $100 a point, or $500,000 of notional at 5,000.
- **Kelly sizing (3D).** The source's example put dollars per share where fractions of
  capital belong; full Kelly for that edge is five times the account, and quarter-Kelly
  keeps about 44% of the growth rate, rather than giving 44% up.
- **Options (5B to 5D).** The synthetic option chain broke put-call parity and is rebuilt. An
  at-the-money 30-day SPX call carries about $680 of vega a contract, not $10. With the strike
  below spot, d₁ and d₂ are positive.
- **Dealers (6B, 6D, 7A, 7B).** SPY's dollar gamma is a tenth of SPX's, not a hundredth. What
  stays flat is gamma times the price squared, not gamma. Four signs and directions in the
  vanna and charm examples are corrected.
- **The whole market (8A, 8B, 9A).** Dealers cannot be short calls bought from customers. The
  feedback coefficient grows as market depth falls, not with it. The vanna loop's opening
  hedge sells tens of thousands of shares, not 410. The VIX scales with the square root of the
  options' prices, not linearly.
- **Edges (10E, 10F).** Carry is (S − F)/F, positive in backwardation. The betting-against-beta
  weights had their betas swapped, so the portfolio was not beta-neutral.
- **Strategies (12C, 12D, 12H).** A long calendar is short gamma at its strike. A naked put's
  loss is bounded. Cash-settled SPX options assign no stock.
- **Discipline (13D, 13E, 13H).** The trade at C = 0.282 is a pass under the 0.3 threshold,
  and a 0.497 score is not high conviction. The vega monitor's three buttons set a target
  vega.

What the sources leave open (an unsourced statistic, two figures that disagree, a claim the
arithmetic does not support) is in `QUESTIONS.md`, with the chapter each question comes from.

## How a chapter becomes a page

`tools/spx.py` turns `chapters/<slug>.md` into `src/<slug>.html`, and `outline.json` into
`curriculum.json`. The library's own build (`tools/make.py`) does the rest. The house markdown
is described in full in `CONVENTIONS.md` and at the top of `tools/spx.py`:

- `\( ... \)` and `\[ ... \]` are maths, typeset by KaTeX. A dollar sign is always money: the
  book sets `math_delimiters` to `brackets` in `book.json`, so KaTeX never reads `$` as maths.
- A display is numbered by chapter, (2A.1) and on, and `{#eq:name}` under it names it for
  `[eq:name]` in the text.
- `::: kind Title` opens a callout: a worked example, a derivation to open, a rule, a warning,
  a clinical analogy ("Familiar ground"), a recap, or "Check yourself".
- `[[term]]` marks a glossary term, whose definition shows on a card. "Chapter 5C" and
  "Part V" become links.
- `[FIGURE: id]` places `figures/<id>.html`. `[FIGURE IDEA: ...]` shows nothing on the page
  and is collected in `FIGURE-IDEAS.md`.
- A table is the house data table. A pipe inside maths, code or parentheses is part of its
  cell, so 8D's `MA(|r|)` stays in one column.

## Where the figures depart from the app

How things are shown:

- Figures never move until the reader asks. The app's animations start from a button here,
  and honor reduced motion.
- The Greek surface (5C), drawn with three.js in the app, is a wireframe the reader turns
  with a view-angle and a tilt slider. Tables the app tinted cell by cell (the Mag 7
  correlations in 11L, the DTE matrix in 11M) are drawn on a canvas, since a figure adds no
  CSS.
- The CNDR-versus-SPX figure (12C) draws the app's stand-in series, which the app's data file
  describes as synthetic, and says so in its caption. Its dates sit on a time axis; the app
  spaced its points evenly whatever their dates.

Where a widget contradicted its own maths or the prose, the figure follows the maths:

- 1B, skew simulator: the slider was labeled skewness, but it sets the skew-normal's shape
  parameter, and is labeled shape. The caption said the Sharpe ratio barely moves; the
  readouts show it moving. The caption now says a thinner spread over the same tail.
- 1C, EV bet builder: the second preset was captioned "EV ≈ +$0.0125/toss"; its own
  numbers (25% at +$35, 75% at −$1) give +$8.00, as the prose says. The presets are
  labeled by name only.
  Its loss slider stepped by $0.10, so it could not hold its own $1.25 default and
  opened at $1.30; it steps by $0.05.
- 1D, deflated Sharpe calculator: the app put an annual Sharpe ratio into a per-day standard
  error and fixed the spread of Sharpe ratios across trials at 1. The figure follows Bailey
  and López de Prado in one set of units: it takes a number of daily returns and an annual
  Sharpe ratio, and converts between them. Its N slider steps by 2, so 252 sits on its grid.
- 2E, tax drag: the long-term rate was 20% in every bracket, and state tax fell only on the
  40% taxed as short-term. The long-term rate now follows the bracket (0%, 15% or 20%), and
  state tax falls on the whole gain.
- 3B, AR(1) explorer: |φ| ≥ 0.85 was "near-non-stationary" on both sides, with "variance
  scales faster than linearly". Its own paths narrow as φ goes toward −1, so the figure
  reads that end as the strong mean reversion it draws.
- 5A, position picker: a short call's breakeven was put at K − P and a short put's at
  K + P. A short position breaks even where the long one does, where its payoff crosses zero.
- 5C, Greek surface: the axis labeled strike varies spot, with the strike fixed at 100. It
  is labeled spot.
- 5D and 7B, higher-order Greeks: color was computed as (−Γ/2T)(1 + d₁d₂/σ√T), negative at
  every spot, while the widget's card and the chapter call it positive and large at the money. The
  figure draws ∂Γ/∂t, (Γ/2T)(1 + d₁(2rT − d₂σ√T)/σ√T), checked against a numerical
  derivative. The heatmap labeled strike × DTE varies spot, and says so. The notes put
  charm's peak at the money and volga's at 10–15 and 85–90 delta; the curves peak a short
  way either side of the strike, and at about 8–12 and 92–95 delta.
- 5F, pin risk: moving the strike could leave spot outside its own slider's range and off
  the chart; spot now stays in range. "Pin probability" is the gauge 1 − 2|Δ − 0.5|, and
  the caption says so.
- 5G, IV rank against percentile: the reading had "top-heavy" and "bottom-heavy" the wrong
  way round. A history of low days with a few spikes gives a percentile above the rank.
- 6D, gamma hedge simulator: the heading gave a long option's P&L,
  ½Γ·S²·[(dS/S)² − σ²·dt], for a dealer who is short; the caption gives the short sign.
  Kept as the app has them: theta accrues a trading day per bar while time to expiry falls a
  calendar day, and the calls expire at bar 30 of 50, which the caption says.
- 6D, dollar-gamma units: the sanity check named fixed parameters but printed the live
  value; the caption gives the fixed $46.2k. At zero gamma the short-call convention read
  "long γ" (−0 ≥ 0); the label now comes from the convention.
- 7A, hedging friction: each schedule's hedge and costs were kept but left out of the P&L,
  so the four curves were one. The figure adds the delta the stale hedge leaves open and the
  cost of each rebalance; hedging on every bar gives the app's curve.
- 8B, effective vol: the variable in σ/(1 − κ·G) was labeled GEX, but it is the prose's
  G, the negation of published GEX, and is labeled so. "Trigger stress (GEX flips + to −)"
  moved it the wrong way, from +0.6 to −0.6, and damped the vol it meant to amplify; it now
  runs G from −0.6 to +0.6. "Damped" covered up to 1.67× amplification; the middle band is
  split at zero. The marker for the current G never drew, and now does.
- 9A, vanna feedback loop: short puts are long delta, so the opening hedge is short stock,
  not "long ~410 shares"; the dealer's delta shift reads longer, as step 3 says; step 4
  sells the readout's 11,000 shares, not 110; the diagram lights each station when the
  step reaches it, not one step early.
- 9A, VIX strip builder: kept as the app computes it. The smile moves with the forward, so
  the SPX shock barely moves the index (18.507, 18.505, 18.503), and the caption says so.
- 9B, gamma by DTE: the note put the 4-hour peak at "roughly 8.5×" the 30-day one; the
  maths gives 13.5× (the chapter's table, 13.4×). The slider labeled log scale was linear,
  and is now logarithmic.
- 11C and 11F, RSI (in `_lib.js`): with no losses in the window the app set RS to 100, so a
  run of pure gains read 99.0. The RSI follows Wilder's definition: 100 with no losses, and
  50 with neither gains nor losses.
- 11E, stage classifier: kept as the app computes it. Its fallback rules classify every bar
  whatever the ADX, so the ADX gate changes no stage, which the chapter says.
- 11F, multiple timeframes: the higher timeframe's trend was read from the SMA five bars
  back, but 350 bars make 14 higher-timeframe bars, which leave a 10-bar SMA five values,
  so the read was "—" on every seed. The figure reads four bars back.
- 11G, composite scorecard: the composite was shown rounded beside a bucket chosen on the
  unrounded value, so 9.6 read "+10 Neutral". It is shown to one decimal, cut toward zero.
- 11I, pre-earnings IV ramp: the verdict said the binary outcome went against whenever
  exiting beat holding, including when the move was up; each clause now appears only when
  true, and a gap under $0.50 reads as similar outcomes.
- 11L and 11M keep the app's data where it differs from the prose: the calm correlations
  run 0.35–0.70 against the text's 0.5–0.8, and the DTE buckets start LEAPS at 180 days
  against the text's 365. The DTE matrix's caption says so, and the Mag 7 caption calls
  both of its matrices illustrative.
- 12A, strategy selection tree: kept word for word, including where its rules differ from
  the chapter's matrix. The time-horizon answer feeds no rule, which the caption says;
  bearish, rich IV and a negative catalyst give the trend list, not the matrix's diagonal
  put spread and bear call spread; bullish, cheap IV and a positive catalyst leave out the
  call debit spread.
- 13A, retail haircut: the verdict tested the Sharpe ratio against 0.5 while naming the
  "~0.40" buy-and-hold baseline, so 0.462 was called below 0.40; it tests against 0.40.
  Total drag read "NaN%" at a paper Sharpe of 0; it is computed as 1 − h₁h₂h₃h₄.
- 13C, position-size clarifier: dragging the entry below the stop kept the old stop, which
  floored the stop distance at $0.01 and sized thousands of contracts; the stop now moves
  with the entry. At a size of zero the risk ratio reads "—".
- 13C, position-size calculator: "notional at stop" was the planned risk × 50, a notional
  only when the stop is 2% from the price, and is labeled "notional at a 2% stop".
- 13C, behavioral checklist: the note's example of a factor at 0.2 was poor sleep, which
  its own rubric scores 0.3 at under five hours; the caption uses a same-day loss.
- 13H, vega monitor: the flat, half and full hedge buttons set the target vega to none,
  half or all of the book's, so "full hedge" always bought nothing. They are labeled
  target vega: flat, half, full. "Other" used a beta of 1.00 no reader could set, not the
  table's 0.50; the overlay said buy even when the target called for selling; the DTE
  input fed no computation and is gone.

## Changing the book

The book is written here now; the app's text no longer feeds it. Edit `chapters/<slug>.md`,
and the glossary and notes beside it, then run the checks every chapter was written to:

```bash
python3 tools/spx.py --lint 5c        # markup, maths, glossary, links, figures
python3 tools/spx.py --facts 5c       # numbers and names in the sources that 5C lacks
python3 tools/prosecheck.py the-other-end-of-the-trade 5c    # sentence lengths
python3 tools/stylecheck.py the-other-end-of-the-trade       # the Google rules
python3 tools/spx.py                  # regenerate src/, curriculum.json, FIGURE-IDEAS.md
                                      # and QUESTIONS.md
python3 tools/spx.py --verify         # every word of the markdown reaches the page
```

A glossary term is defined once in the whole book: `spx.py` fails on a term that two files
in `glossary/` define. A new chapter needs an entry in `outline.json`. A new figure needs a
file in `figures/`: copy the shape of an existing one, keep the rules in `FIGURES.md`, and try
it with `python3 tools/spxfig.py <id>`.
