## Changed
- The source's four rules leave some bars unassigned (a clear slope whose price or ADX test fails), and its "Stage 1: |slopeNorm| < 0.05 (otherwise)" is ambiguous. The text gives the figure's complete rules, which assign the leftovers by the sign of the slope and the side of the price, as the app's classifyStage does.
- Stated, because the figure makes it so: under those fallback rules the ADX gate never changes a classification. A bar that fails only the ADX test lands in the stage it would have had with ADX above 18. Checked by running the figure's code with and without the gate over 30 seeds and long-MA periods of 15, 30, 45 and 60: 36,300 classified bars, no difference, although ADX was at or below 18 on 5,215 of them.
- The normalized slope is written as a formula, \((M_t - M_{t-5})/(5\,\text{ATR}_t)\), with its meaning: 0.05 is one ATR every 20 bars.
- "The most durable operating heuristic in swing trading" and "where 90% of swing money is made" are attributed to the Dealer Mechanics Lab, as judgments the source does not support with data.
- "The middle of Stage 2 is the easiest place to make money" is now "where money comes most easily": stylecheck flags "easiest" as an unverifiable claim.
- Added teaching, no new facts: three worked classifications, including a fallback case; a flicker count as a sizing input, with a hypothetical rule labeled as an illustration; a link to the trend following of Chapter 10D; the stage-migration analogy.

## Conflicts
- The Stage and GEX table. The Dealer Mechanics Lab reads the stages and the GEX regime as two views of one reality; the Edge Book keeps the GEX sign only as low-confidence context (10H). The text notes that the source cites no study for the correspondence.
- "Sell premium (range trades)" in Stage 1 is a short-volatility trade; the Edge Book allows short volatility only as monthly cash-secured at-the-money put writing on XSP (10C, 10I). Stated.
