## Conflicts
- Two limits in the source do not agree: a "reasonable upper bound" of $500 to $1,500 per volatility point for a $250,000 to $1 million account, and a "stress threshold" at 1% of NAV per volatility point ($2,500 to $10,000 a point on those accounts, 1.7 to 6.7 times the top of the band). The text states both, shows the arithmetic, and uses the dollar band as the working limit because it binds first. The spread-purchase hedge's condition, "below 0.5% of NAV per vol point", has the same scale.
- The Lab's VIX betas are ranges (QQQ 0.85-0.95, IWM 0.80-0.90, large caps 0.60-0.85, mid and small caps 0.40-0.65); the figure uses single values (0.90, 0.85, 0.75 for NVDA, 0.70 for AAPL, 0.65 for MSFT, 0.50 for others). Both are given.
- Chapter 13B (from dl:12B) says the sizing factor checks that "the book's total vega and delta stay within portfolio limits (Chapter 13H)". This chapter, like its source, sets limits on vega only; delta enters only in the choice between VIX calls and SPX puts as a hedge.
- Failure mode 2 sizes hedges at 30% to 50% of the gross exposure; hedge 1 sizes VIX calls at half. Both are given; they meet at 50%.

## Questions for the author
- The two vega limits: were the NAV percentages meant per 10 volatility points? At 0.1% of NAV per point, a $1 million account's threshold would be $1,000 a point, inside the $500 to $1,500 band, and "0.5% of NAV" would become $500 a point, the band's floor.
- "Several hundred SPX vega units": is the unit dollars per volatility point, as the text assumes?
- Chapter 13B points here for delta limits on the book. Should this chapter give one, or should 13B's pointer name vega only?
