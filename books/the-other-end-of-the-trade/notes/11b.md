## Figures
- No figure is placed. Six figure ideas.
- pooled-conditional-reversal (new, from a figure idea): works the chapter's two-regime example exactly. After a down week, the next day's mean is the calm effect on calm days and the stressed effect on stressed days, and down weeks come on 35% of calm days and 60% of stressed ones. Bayes' theorem gives the share of down weeks that fall in stress, (1 − c) × 0.60 / (c × 0.35 + (1 − c) × 0.60) for a calm share c, and the pooled bar weights the two effects by it. Bars show the pooled, calm-only and stressed-only means, with the round-trip cost as a dashed line. Controls: the calm share of days (50% to 95%, default 80%), the round-trip cost (0% to 0.20%, default 0.05%), the calm effect (0% to +0.30%, default +0.10%) and the stressed effect (−0.40% to +0.10%, default −0.15%). First state: bars at +0.025%, +0.10% and −0.15%; stress: 20% of days, 30% of down weeks; pooled after cost = −0.025%; calm only after cost = +0.05%; verdict "Conditioning pays." These reproduce the worked example "A pooled average hides the regime": 0.12/0.40 = 0.30, 0.070% − 0.045% = 0.025%, and 0.10% before costs and 0.05% after them.
