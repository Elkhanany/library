## Changed
- Kelly made to agree with Chapter 3D. dl:12C says that "Section 2E's worked example shows the per-trade chain end-to-end", pointing to an example that, before Chapter 3D corrected its units, made Kelly look more conservative than the loss budget. With the correction, full Kelly for that edge (60% at $4/$3 on a $50 share) holds five times the account and quarter-Kelly 6,250 shares, five times the loss budget's 1,250. The text now says so: the two rules converge only for mild edges (quarter-Kelly equals the 1% budget at a 52% win rate at even payoffs, and is 1.5% at 53%), and for a strong edge the loss budget binds by a wide margin. The source's convergence claims ("52-55% win rate ... 4-10% range"; "quarter-Kelly dollar bet sits near the dollar loss the loss-budget rule produces") are kept, stated as Chapter 3D states them.
- dl:12C: "the Kelly utility function is concave — the penalty for over-betting exceeds the gain from under-betting". Stated as Chapter 3D corrected it: betting 1.3 or 0.7 times Kelly both keep 91% of the growth, but the chance of ever halving is 69% against 28%. The source's "30% standard error (typical for backtest-derived estimates)" is kept at its hedge.
- dl:12C: "if your loss-budget-derived positions across the open book imply ≥30% capital at simultaneous risk, you are effectively past half-Kelly". Kept as the rule, with the arithmetic that supports it: five uncorrelated strategies at a 55% edge reach half-Kelly at 25%. Added: if the open trades are correlated they are one bet, whose full Kelly at that edge is 10%, so 30% is three times Kelly, where by Chapter 3D the growth rate is negative; and the source's per-strategy addition ("five uncorrelated strategies ... ~12.5%") holds only for uncorrelated bets.
- dl:12C's "Loss-budget is robust to this: it caps loss regardless of edge correctness" is kept, with Chapter 3D's limit: below a 52% win rate at even payoffs, 1% at risk exceeds quarter-Kelly.
- dl:12C's "Common mistake B: interpreting '1% risk' as '1% notional'" repeats the wording of mistake A while describing the opposite error (buying $500k of underlying without a stop). The text describes B by its consequence: sizing to the notional and forgetting the stop, so that a 5% move costs $25,000.
- Added teaching: the ratio of the two readings of "1%" is \(P/d\), price over stop distance, which is 100 for a stop 1% below the price and 50 for 2% (the source's "50-100×"); the delta-one assumption behind \(L_{\max} = \$20 \times 100\) for an XSP contract; the gap test of both figures scales with the stop distance, so the text adds that a 3% gap on the $1.5 million notional of a $2 stop costs $45,000, nine times the budget; the subadditivity of expected shortfall behind [eq:portfolio], with a toy example of a fourth sleeve that does not fit; the recovery gain after a 25% drawdown (33%); the further fall from an 18% to a 25% drawdown (8.5%); a definition of the margin cushion (equity above the requirement, as a share of equity), worked on Chapter 1E's example; the count check that 1 to 3 positions a week is 4 to 13 a month; the readiness score as a product ([eq:readiness]).
- spx:13.5 sizes the put sleeve with "$10,000 (Chapter 11, Example 10)" and rounds 5 down to 4 "for buffer". Kept; the text adds that Chapter 13E's own estimate, about $10,800, gives 4.6 and the floor takes it to 4 directly, as Chapter 13E says.
- Two VIX/VIX3M thresholds (1.00 and 1.05) appear in spx:13's examples. The text keeps each example's number and points to Card 02 of Chapter 13F, which layers them (halt new entries above 1.00 for 3 closes; reduce 50% above 1.05).
- spx:13.6 "MTM down 3× initial premium" is read as a mark-to-market loss of three times the premium collected.
- One clinical analogy: written invalidation as a statistical analysis plan locked before unblinding.
- Tables replace lists for the steps (spx:13.1), the failing observations, the trigger list and the kinds of invalidation.

## Conflicts
- Strategy-level kill rules. dl:12C gives the Compression Breakout a 7-day time stop and IBS + RSI mean reversion "21-EMA reclaim achieved OR time stop at 3 days". dl:10G (Chapter 11H) and dl:12E (Chapter 13F) give 8 trading days, and for IBS + RSI a 2-day time stop with a target at the first close above the entry day's high or 1R; Chapter 11H also closes a Trend Pullback when the 30-bar SMA's slope turns negative. The text sets the two versions side by side in one table and links Chapters 11H and 13F. The writers of Part XI raised the same point.
- Days with no qualifying setup: 60% to 70% here (dl:12C), 70% to 80% in Chapter 11N (dl:10L). Both stated, with the link; Chapter 11N states it from its side.
- Trend sleeve review: spx:13.8 reassesses after "5+ year sustained negative absolute returns", while Chapter 10D (spx:6.3) reassesses after three consecutive negative years. Both stated; both review rather than exit.
- Drawdown discipline: Chapter 3D's illustration uses a 30% close-and-reset rule; this chapter's portfolio rule halts at 25%. Both stated.
