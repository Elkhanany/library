## Conflicts
- IBS with RSI(2). This source lists them among five measures of one quantity; the Dealer Mechanics Lab's setup section (old 10G, now 11H) calls their combination "genuine, not multicollinear". The chapter states both, with 11H's reason, and gives the reader the correlation test that settles it.
- Oscillator signals. The Dealer Mechanics Lab uses RSI(2) extremes as entry signals in trending markets; the Edge Book calls a single oscillator reading "too noisy for sizing decisions" (10H). Both are stated.
- GEX in the dealer notes (RSI(2) as a gamma-scalping setup, ADX against the GEX sign, bandwidth against positive GEX, Donchian breakouts against short gamma). The Edge Book treats the GEX sign as low-confidence context (10H); stated at the RSI and ADX notes.

## Questions for the author
- ADX above 25 "corresponds empirically to negative-GEX regimes": is there a study or a dataset behind this? The text says the source cites none.
- "Donchian breakouts often coincide with mechanical institutional rebalancing flow at quarter ends": the Edge Book treats quarter-end rebalancing as a small effect (10G, 10I). Is there evidence for the coincidence?
- `pandas.ewm(span=n).mean()` defaults to `adjust=True`, which weights the first values differently from the recursive EMA until the history is long. The text keeps the source's code as given. Should it say `adjust=False` for both?
- "The 23-indicator menagerie": the source names 16 plus "several others". The text keeps "some 23".
