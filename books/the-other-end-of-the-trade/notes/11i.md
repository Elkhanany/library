## Conflicts
- The IV ramp and the option premium. The Dealer Mechanics Lab says the swing "captures the structural IV bid" and rides the drift "while the IV ramp inflates the option premium". Its own mechanism, a fixed event becoming a larger share of the option's life, raises the quote and lowers the premium, as Chapter 11K also works out. The figure's call gains because its straight-line ramp from ambient to peak raises the call's total variance, which amounts to the market pricing a larger event every day (at the defaults, an event move of about 22% by T-day). The text states the claim and the condition under which it holds.
- Adams and Neururer (2020): cited by the Dealer Mechanics Lab for through-earnings trades in general, described by the Edge Book (10I) as a finding about naive single-name short volatility. Both are stated.
- The conviction label at C = 0.300: "medium-high, take the trade" in dl:10H against Chapter 13B's medium band at reduced size. Both are stated.
- The Trend Pullback overlay here (RSI(2) below 15, the 21-EMA) against Chapter 11H's trigger (RSI(2) below 10 or IBS below 0.20, the 10- or 21-EMA). The text points out the difference.
- dl:12D's Example 8 (Chapter 13D) draws the lesson "Event-week single-name positions need defined-risk structures, not naked equity", while this chapter makes shares the default for a trade that exits before the report. The text points to 13D without reconciling them; see the questions.

## Questions for the author
- Condition 5, "Forward IV not cheap": as written it says that if forward IV is below 70% of ambient, the exit before earnings is mandatory, but the exit is mandatory in every case. Is it meant as a filter that excludes such candidates, or as a reminder?
- Is there evidence that the priced size of the event rises into reports, beyond the mechanical rise of the quote? Without it, the "structural IV bid" is available to long options only when the event is repriced.
- "A single bad earnings outcome can wipe out 10-20 successful pre-earnings momentum trades": at what average win and loss? With the example's 1.2R win and a 15% gap against a 3.5% stop (4.3R), one loss erases about 3.6 wins.
- The NVDA timeline: which day count was intended?
- The ORATS composite of the at-the-money straddle and the first two OTM strangles: with what weights?
- The figure's verdict says that "across many trials, the negative skew of single-name earnings moves makes exit-T-1 dominant in expectation". The figure computes one path, and neither source gives evidence on the skew of earnings moves. The text reports it as the verdict's claim and proposes a many-path figure.
- How should 13D's Example 8 (defined risk, not shares, in event weeks) be read beside this chapter's shares-by-default with an exit before the report?
- Length. About 4,140 words of running prose (about 5,310 with maths, lists and figure ideas), about 2.4 times the source's 1,730 words of prose, above the brief's 1.2 to 1.5. First cuts, if wanted: the IV-ramp worked example (Chapter 11K has a similar one), the surface-trade step of the NVDA example, one check question.
