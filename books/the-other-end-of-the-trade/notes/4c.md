## Conflicts
- The variance risk premium for retail accounts. This chapter's source (dl:4C) calls it "the single most important source of edge available to retail traders". The Edge Book (spx:6.2, the source of Chapter 10C) is more guarded. It reads the apparent alpha of premium selling as "largely just compensation for accepting negative skew" (Jurek and Stafford, 2015), finds naive VRP harvesting unattractive after risk pricing (Bakshi, Crosby and Gao, 2024), and applies a retail haircut that takes the academic Sharpe ratio of 0.65 to 0.20 to 0.40 for a self-managed account, as Chapter 1D does. The chapter states both positions and links Chapters 1D, 10C and 13A.
- The size of the premium, and its units. dl:4C: "roughly 3-5 percentage points of annualized variance". spx:6.2 (Chapter 10C): a mean of about 4 volatility points, from the VIX at 19.3% against realized volatility of 15.1% over 1990 to 2018. Read as volatility points, the two agree. Read literally as variance, 3 to 5 points (0.03 to 0.05) is two to three times the Edge Book's gap in variance, \(0.193^2 - 0.151^2 = 0.0144\). The chapter keeps dl:4C's wording, gives the Edge Book's, and shows the difference between the units.
- Consistent across chapters: the PUT Index's Sharpe ratio (0.6 to 0.7 here; about 0.65 in Chapter 1D) and skew (about −2.1 here; −2.09 in Chapter 1B).
