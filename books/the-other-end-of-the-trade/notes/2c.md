## Changed
- Fixed: "the average fill price might be 5 cents worse than the top-of-book ask — about 0.5 bps of slippage on a $580 stock". Five cents on $580 is 0.86 bps. The text says "about 0.9 basis points" and shows the division.
- "the last fifteen minutes of every trading day has a different microstructure character" (dl:2D) and "the last 30 minutes of the trading day are dominated by mechanical, non-fundamental order flow" (dl:3B) are combined as "the last 15 to 30 minutes". The retail advice about market orders keeps the source's "last 15 minutes".
- Added teaching, on hypothetical numbers: the spread and the mid (eq:spread) on the source's ladder; the percentage spread, with an SPY and an SPX option at 0.4% each; a bid-ask bounce with a constant mid that a model would read as mean reversion; the average fill and slippage (eq:avgfill), with ten contracts through the book; the VWAP benchmark as a formula, and one-line definitions of TWAP and implementation shortfall; a round trip made and taken, at the source's fee rates; a queue in which a trade prints at your price without filling you; a break-even spread against informed traders, \(h = \pi\delta\) (eq:halfspread), derived from the source's "the spread captured exactly compensates for adverse selection losses, plus a margin"; the three order types on the ladder; an auction cleared from a published imbalance; one-line definitions of top of book, fat finger and net asset value.
- Familiar ground: the bid-ask bounce as regression to the mean in a noisy biomarker.
- Fixed: the intraday momentum paragraph credited Baltussen, Da, Lammers and Martens (2021) with "the first half-hour of return predicts the last half-hour of return ... across 60+ futures contracts" (dl:2D) and set that beside Chapter 6C's rest-of-day predictor as two descriptions of one paper. They are two papers. Gao, Han, Li and Zhou (2018, *Journal of Financial Economics*) found in SPY, 1993 to 2013, that the first half-hour predicts the last; Baltussen and co-authors used the rest-of-day return (previous close to the last half-hour) across more than 60 futures, 1974 to 2020. Only the second ties the pattern to short-gamma hedging (option dealers and leveraged funds); Gao and co-authors proposed late-informed trading and infrequent rebalancing. The text now gives each paper its own predictor, sample and explanation, attributes "the mechanism is the same in every market" to the Dealer Mechanics Lab, and points to Chapter 6C for the limits of the evidence (research: GaoHanLiZhou2018, BaltussenDaLammersMartens2021; evidence.md item 30).

## Conflicts
- SPY's depth. dl:3D puts 500,000 to 2 million share-equivalents at the NBBO. dl:2D's walking example has "a top-of-book ask of 1,000 shares" and a 100,000-share order that walks several levels. The text keeps both and says the example assumes a thin top of book, since at dl:3D's depth a 100,000-share order would fill at the top.
- The intraday momentum paper. dl:2D says Baltussen, Da, Lammers and Martens (2021) show "the first half-hour of return predicts the last half-hour of return"; dl:6D (Chapter 6C) describes the predictor as the return over the rest of the day. Settled by the literature search: the rest-of-day predictor is Baltussen and co-authors', and the first half-hour predictor is Gao, Han, Li and Zhou's (2018). See Changed.
- The auction's size. dl:2D: "Approximately 10-15% of daily NYSE volume". dl:3B: about 5% to 7% in 2010, 10% to 12% in 2020, and estimates from 12% to 18% in 2025. The text gives both; it does not guess which year dl:2D's figure describes.
- SPX option spreads: "$0.10 to $0.30 wide" (dl:2D, on the bounce) and "often $0.10 to $0.50 on liquid SPX strikes" (dl:3D). Each is kept in its own context.

## Questions for the author
- Inverted markets. The source's rationale, "traders who want immediate execution badly enough are willing to pay for it explicitly, while liquidity is harder to attract", does not say which side pays for what: on an inverted venue the resting order pays and the taker is paid. The text keeps the source's sentence. Should the mechanism be spelled out?
- "When your resting order fills, the trade was almost certainly initiated by someone with marginally more information": kept at its strength, though the break-even model the text adds lets any fraction of traders be informed.
