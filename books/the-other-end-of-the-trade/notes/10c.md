## Conflicts
- The VRP's worth to a retail account. The Dealer Mechanics Lab (Chapters 4C and 5E) calls it the most important source of edge available to retail traders. The Edge Book (this chapter, and Chapters 1D and 13A) is more guarded: Jurek and Stafford's reading of premium-selling alpha as payment for skew, Bakshi, Crosby and Gao's finding that a naive harvest is unattractive after risk pricing, a retail Sharpe ratio of 0.20 to 0.40, and a sleeve capped at 10% to 15%. The chapter states both and links 1D, 4C, 5E and 13A.
- Iron condors. The Lab teaches them (Chapter 12C) and counts condor writing among short-premium strategies with positive long-run returns (Chapter 5E); the Edge Book rejects running them systematically (Chapter 10I). Stated in the chapter, with 12C's own report of the CNDR record.
- The size of the premium in variance. Chapter 4C (dl:4C) puts it at "roughly 3 to 5 percentage points of annualized variance"; this source's 19.3% against 15.1% gives 1.44 points of variance, or 4.2 volatility points. Chapter 4C's notes raise the question; this chapter uses its own source's numbers and units.
- The PUT index appears in other chapters over other windows: Chapter 1E gives about 9.5% a year with a maximum drawdown of about 33% for 1990 to 2024 (Calmar 0.29 against SPX's 0.18 with its 55% drawdown). This chapter's 1990 to 2018 figures (9.54% against 9.80%, −33% against −51%, Calmar 0.29 against 0.19) are consistent with them.
