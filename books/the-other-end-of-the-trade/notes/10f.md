## Changed

With those weights the portfolio's beta is \(\beta_L/\beta_H - \beta_H/\beta_L\), which is negative whenever \(\beta_L < \beta_H\), so the subscripts are swapped. ## Questions for the author
- Momentum's tax drag (spx:7.2): an ETF such as MTUM usually passes little of its turnover to holders as taxable gains, because it can shed low-basis shares through in-kind redemptions. Is "best held in tax-advantaged accounts" about a self-run momentum portfolio, or about the ETF? The chapter keeps the source's claim.
- "Add 0.2-0.4 of Sharpe to the equity sleeve": is this the information ratio of the tilts, or a rise in the sleeve's own Sharpe ratio? Read literally, the second needs 3% to 6% a year of extra return, above the 0.5% to 1.5% the same chapter expects.
- Quality "did not crash through the 2017-2022 value drawdown", while value's drawdown is dated 2010 to 2020 in spx:7.3. Which window is meant for quality's test?
- AQR Equity Market Neutral and AQR Absolute Return are multi-factor funds rather than pure value portfolios. Are their results (−38% by December 2020; +43.5% in 2022, +18.5% in 2023) meant as evidence about value specifically?
- "QUAL captures perhaps 60-70% of the academic factor premium", "Long-only captures roughly half the long-short Sharpe", and "Bali et al. (2017)": what are the sources? The reading list (R3) will need them.
- Jegadeesh and Titman (1993): over which sample are the "UMD ~1%/month, t≈3, Sharpe ~0.5" figures?

## Figures
- The outline places no figure in this chapter. Eight figure ideas are marked: a factor sorting machine, years of data needed for a given t-statistic, a quality score builder, momentum's hidden beta through a crash, value's return split into spread and fundamentals, the security market line with BAB built from it, an equity sleeve builder, and the Sharpe ratio of a factor mix with its ceiling.
- factor-sorting-machine (new, from a figure idea): builds the long-short factor of eq:factor from fifty made-up stocks over 240 months. Each month every stock gets a new trait score z ~ N(0, 1) and returns a made-up market (0.6% a month on average, 4.5% volatility) plus the trait link times z plus noise of its own of 8% a month; the portfolio buys the top group by score and sells the bottom group short in equal dollars, so the market cancels. The top chart is the first month's sort, with the true link dashed and each group's average return marked; the bottom chart is the growth of $1 in the long-short portfolio and in the market, on a log scale, with the longest stretch from a peak to its recovery shaded. Controls: the trait link (0% to 0.50% a month per standard deviation of score, default 0.15%), the stocks in each group (5, 10 or 25, default 10) and a new-stocks button (seed 52, then 53 and on). The true Sharpe ratio comes from the settings and the expected gap in score between the two groups (order statistics of 50 normal draws: 3.411, 2.744 and 1.571 standard deviations for groups of 5, 10 and 25). First state: true Sharpe ratio = 0.40 (0.398); t = 2 takes 25 years, eq:tstat's (2/SR)², which reproduces the chapter's 0.4 × √25 = 2; this run: Sharpe 0.40, t = 1.81; longest below a peak: 6.3 years. The run is typical: across 300 seeds at the defaults the median t is 1.86, 44% of runs reach t = 2, and the median longest stretch is 6.4 years.
