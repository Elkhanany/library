## Changed
- Fixed: the ruin formula (dl:1D), "P(ruin to fraction f) ≈ f^(2μ/σ²)", with μ and σ "the drift and volatility of the levered strategy". For geometric Brownian motion with arithmetic drift μ the exact probability is f^(2μ/σ² − 1); the source's form holds only if μ is the drift of log wealth, μ − σ²/2. The text gives the exact form with a derivation and keeps "the ratio 2μ/σ² gets divided by k", which is true. The difference matters at high leverage: at four times, a Sharpe-0.5 strategy with 20% volatility halves with probability 84% (exact) against 42% (source form).
- Quantified: "a Sharpe-0.5 strategy levered 4× has effective μ/σ² that produces ruin probabilities approaching unity over long horizons". The exponent is 2 SR/(kσ) − 1, so the outcome depends on the strategy's volatility as well as its Sharpe ratio. At 20% volatility and four times, the chance of ever losing 90% is 56%; from 25% volatility up, four times reaches levered volatility of 2 SR and ruin is certain in the model. The text works the 20% case and states the exact ceiling, levered volatility below twice the Sharpe ratio.
- Fixed: "Sortino is higher than Sharpe for any strategy with positive skew and lower than Sharpe for negative-skew strategies" (dl:1D). With the downside deviation measured below zero or the risk-free rate, the Sortino ratio exceeds the Sharpe ratio even for a symmetric strategy (1.09 against 0.5 for a normal with a 5% mean and 10% volatility). The text states the relation that holds, which also fits the source's PUT and SPX facts: the more of a strategy's volatility is on the upside, the more its Sortino exceeds its Sharpe.
- Fixed: spx:2.5's worked example. "Strategy B ... 95% of years return between $4-7, but in 5% of years the strategy loses $50-100 in a single bad month. Same mean ($5), same standard deviation ($10)". A 5% chance of losing $50 or more already gives a standard deviation above $12, and those ranges give a mean near $1.50. The text keeps what the example is for (mean $5, standard deviation $10, a Sharpe ratio of 0.5, a worst year about four times A's, a recovery that may be impossible) with numbers that satisfy it: about $6.00 in 99 years of 100 and a loss of about $94.50 in the hundredth. So "5% of years" became "1 year in 100". Strategy A's "Worst year historically: −$25" is kept (a three-sigma year); its "Recovery time after worst year: ~3 years" became "about five years" at its $5 average, which is what the example's own numbers give.
- Changed: "XIV ... Calmar ~undefined (max DD ~96% destroyed the strategy)". The ratio is defined; the point is that it means little for a product that its drawdown ended. The text says "hardly meaningful".
- Changed: tail beta near 1.0 means "your 'diversifier' ... fully correlates with everything else" (dl:1A). A beta of 1 means moving one for one with the market, which is not the same as a correlation of 1; the text says "moves one for one".
- Kept at strength, scoped: spx:2.6's "the entire hedge fund industry is just selling insurance, not generating alpha" is kept as the blunt reading of the result, with "in aggregate", because Jurek and Stafford study broad indices. The text uses dl:1A's "largely disappears" (spx:2.6 says "vanish").
- The PSR uses N(·) for the source's Φ and n observations for the source's T (T is time to expiry in this book), and is split into its standard error and the PSR itself so that each display fits a phone.
- The deflated Sharpe ratio: the source describes it as the probability that the observed Sharpe ratio is distinguishable from zero given the number of trials, non-normality and length. The text keeps that and adds the mechanism: the PSR evaluated at the expected maximum Sharpe ratio of K null trials. The expression in the grind box is Bailey and López de Prado's (2014), from memory of the paper, not from the sources; please check it.

- "Most published 'anomaly' Sharpe ratios in the academic literature shrink to indistinguishable-from-zero after deflation" is attributed by dl:1A to Bailey and López de Prado. Worth checking: the usual citation for the multiple-testing critique of published factors is Harvey, Liu and Zhu (2016).
- "A strategy with Sharpe of 4.0 (typical of intraday market-making)": kept as stated. Is there a source?
- spx:2.8 attributes "ES₉₅ for monthly returns of ATM PUT is roughly −8%" to Bondarenko's 2019 data; the headline numbers the sources quote from that paper do not include it. Keep the attribution?
- Recovery arithmetic overlaps with 1E (another writer), which derives D/(1 − D) and tabulates 20% to 60%. This chapter refers there and adds only the 70% to 90% rows and dl:1A's narrative.
- The worked long-equity/short-put example now uses a 1-in-100-year loss rather than a 5%-of-years one (above). If you prefer 5% of years, the loss that keeps the mean at $5 and the standard deviation at $10 is about $38.60, with gains of about $7.30, and B's worst year is then only 1.5 times A's.
