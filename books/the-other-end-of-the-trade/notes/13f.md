- The swing cards against Chapter 13C's kill rules (dl:12C): the Compression Breakout's time stop is 8 days on the card and in 11H, 7 days in 13C; the IBS and RSI setup's is 2 days on the card and in 11H, 3 days in 13C, where the exit is a reclaim of the 21-EMA. Stated in the same table.

- Card 09 permits an optional 1.0/0.75 rotation in a tax-deferred account; 13E's Example 5 advises against rotation in either account; Chapter 10G cuts equity 20% to 30% in odd weeks by ETF rotation. Stated, with a pointer to 13E.
- Card 10 keeps the size small for 8 quarters; 13E's Example 6 lets it grow after 4 good quarters. Stated.
- F3 cuts leveraged exposure by 50% and halts new positions; Chapter 10H's liquidity rule also cuts short volatility by 50% and equity beta by 25%. F4 trims put-writing by 10% to 15% on negative GEX; 10H gives the sign only 10% to 15% of one filter's weight. Both stated.
- Card 02's net return of 3% to 5% a year against 13E's Example 2 (2% to 4%); Card 02's layered kill rule (halt new above 1.00, cut 50% above 1.05) against Example 2's single trigger at 1.05 and against 13G's Level 1 rule. Stated here and in 13E and 13G.

## Questions for the author
- Card 02's S1 loss of −$3,500: was it a first-order estimate? It is below what the put's intrinsic value allows.
- Card 04 names RSST as the carry vehicle; Chapter 10D (from spx:6.3) describes RSST as US stocks plus managed futures. Is there a carry fund you meant, or is RSST's futures book the intended exposure?
- The FOMC overlay's skip rule: is "stop distance × 1.5 exceeds 2× normal portfolio risk" meant as the dollar loss at the widened stop at the size you would hold? Under the overlay's own half size, that loss is 0.75 of normal, so the rule bites only for unusually wide stops or unhalvable positions.
- Card 02's net 3% to 5%: a return on the collateral? The text reads it so, as it reads 13E's Example 2.
