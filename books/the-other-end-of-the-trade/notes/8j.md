- level-test-planner (new, from a figure idea): the worked example "How many touches a test needs" as a planner. It computes the chapter's n = (z_{α/2}√(p₀q₀) + z_β√(p₁q₁))²/(p₁ − p₀)² with the example's z values, 1.96 for 5% two-sided and 0.84 for 80% power. It multiplies n by the design effect 1 + (m − 1)ρ and converts touches to sessions and then to years at 252 sessions a year. There are m touches in each session that touches the level, and the level is touched on one session in k. A dashed curve gives the count for each group when the base rate is estimated from as many control touches (the two-proportion formula with the pooled rate). Controls: base rate (50% to 90%, default 80%), hoped-for edge (1 to 10 points in half points, default +5, so 85%), touched on (every session to 1 session in 20, default 1 in 5), touches per session (1 to 6, default 1) and correlation ρ (0 to 0.8, default 0.2). First state (figshot): "touches needed = 470", "× design effect 1.0 = 470", "sessions = 2,350 ≈ 9.3 years", "from controls too: 905 of each", and "About 9.3 years. The level is touched about 50 times a year. Halve the edge to 2.5 points and the test takes about 39 years." These reproduce the example's about 470 touches, about 900 of each when the controls are estimated too, and 2,350 sessions, over nine years. Set 3 touches a session to reproduce step 4: "× design effect 1.4 = 658", the text's about 660. It takes 1,097 sessions (4.4 years), because each session that touches the level now gives three touches, worth 2.1 independent ones.
- many-definitions (new, from a figure idea): multiple testing for level definitions, the level-testing version of the deflated Sharpe ratio (Chapter 1D). The sessions are 50 made-up sessions of 780 steps of a walk that moves one point up or down. The walk runs on from one session to the next, with levels every 25 points and nothing at them. A touch is price reaching a level from at least 3 points away, as in level-test-sim. A definition holds if price moves its bounce target X back before it moves its break point Y through. Each touch is followed until it is decided, so the base rate is exactly Y/(X + Y). There are 100 definitions: the level test's default (5 and 20) first, then 99 other pairs from 2 to 20 points in a fixed shuffled order. A definition's edge is its hold rate on the shared touches minus Y/(X + Y). The solid line is the best edge among the first K definitions, and the dashed line is that same rule scored on 50 fresh sessions. Controls: definitions tried, K (1 to 100, default 20), and new sessions (the seed starts at 174 and steps by one). First state (figshot): 501 touches; "best of 20: +3.4 points", "bounce 11, break 17", "holds 64.1%, chance 60.7%" and "fresh sessions: −0.4 points". The verdict reads "An edge from nothing. Nothing happens at these levels, yet the best of 20 definitions beats its own base rate by 3.4 points over 501 touches. Frozen and run on 50 fresh sessions, the same rule falls short of its base rate by 0.4 points." At K = 1 the default rule is at +1.0 points (81.0% against 80.0%) and −1.1 on fresh sessions. At K = 100 the best is bounce 8 and break 19, at +4.5 points (74.9% against 70.4%) and −1.0 on fresh sessions. Over 1,000 samples of 50 sessions, about 521 touches each, the best edge averages +0.04 points at K = 1. It averages +2.7 at K = 5, +3.8 at 20, +4.4 at 50 and +4.8 at 100. The same rules on fresh sessions average between −0.06 and +0.01 points, with standard errors of about 0.09. The best of 20 reaches 4 points in 44% of samples, the check question's case. About 500 touches is the sample the worked example asks for to tell 85% from 80%. With twenty tries on that sample, luck alone supplies most of that 5-point edge.
