## Changed
- Fixed: spx:2.2, "Sell a 90-delta short put for $0.50 of premium ... You win the $0.50 about 90% of the time." A put that expires worthless about 90% of the time is roughly a 10-delta put; a 90-delta put is deep in the money. The text describes the put by its win rate and calls it roughly 10-delta.
- Fixed: dl:1C's log-utility example. "A trade that has +$10,000 expected value on a $100,000 portfolio but a 5% chance of losing $80,000 has ... expected log return that is negative." With those numbers the winning branch gains $14,737 and the expected log return is +0.0501, positive, worth a sure $5,140. The text keeps the $80,000 case with the right arithmetic (log utility halves its value) and adds a $95,000 case, same dollar expectation, whose expected log return is −0.0127: the sign change the source meant to show.
- Fixed: dl:1C, "The payoff diagram of a short iron condor is identical to the payoff diagram of a long lottery ticket — limited risk, limited reward, in opposing geometries". "Identical" and "opposing" contradict each other. The text compares the short iron condor with its mirror image, the long iron condor.
- Changed: dl:1A, "Premium selling can be Scenario A or Scenario B depending on strike selection, sizing ..." Scenario A is the insurance buyer, which a premium seller cannot be; the point is that premium selling can have positive or negative expected value. The text says that.
- Changed: dl:1A, "That ratio, times the win rate, is what determines whether you're in Scenario A or Scenario B." The sign of the expected value comes from comparing the loss-to-win ratio with p/(1 − p), not from a product. The text gives the breakeven check and works it for 85% (5.7 times) and 92% (11.5 times).
- spx:2.2's "$4-4.50 ... mean = +$0.05": +$0.05 at $4 and zero at $4.50. The table gives both.
- dl:1C "It is fantastic" became "it is an excellent bet" (no hype).
- The "Scenario A→B transition" (dl:1A) is written as "the slide from a profitable premium seller into Scenario B", for the same reason as above.
- "The equity skew" (dl:1A) is glossed in one sentence as the richer pricing of index puts than calls, with a pointer to Chapter 5G.
- Translated references: "previewed in 1A" → Chapter 1B; "Section 12" → Part XIII; "Section 5" → Part V; "Kelly criterion, which we'll meet in 2E" → Chapter 3D.
- Added teaching, no new facts: the breakeven win rate formula (L + c)/(G + L) and its values for each bet; the waiting-time arithmetic (0.95 to the 12th is 54%); a grind box showing that the expected log return is the long-run growth rate (law of large numbers); the $1,000 example in log utility on $50,000 and $5 million; the table of the put's expected value against its average loss; the surrogate-endpoint analogy (response rate against overall survival).

## Questions for the author
- "These two examples are foundational in quant trading curricula and worth committing to memory" (dl:1C) is kept as the source's claim. Keep?
