- The chapter places no figure. It marks 7 figure ideas: capital against risk, a risk-contribution calculator, the stress table as a live grid, the gut check as a path, three levels of kill criteria along a crash, where the variance of P&L comes from, and selectivity as a funnel over a year.
- risk-contribution-split (new, from a figure idea): three foundation sleeves, equity at 16% volatility, the VRP at 10% and trend at a volatility the reader sets (6% to 24%, default 12%), drawn as two stacked bars: their shares of the dollars and their shares of the risk, \(\text{RC}_i = w_i(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\mathbf{w})_i/\sigma_p\) [eq:rc]. The dollar split is the worked example's 60/40 of equity and trend, equal dollars, or the equal-risk weights, solved numerically (1/σ when the sleeves are uncorrelated). Two sliders set trend's and the VRP's correlations with equity (−0.3 to 0.9), and the correlation between trend and the VRP is taken as their product, as if they moved together only through equity. First state, 60/40 at zero correlation: \(\sigma_p\) = 10.7%, equity 80% of the risk (8.6 points) and trend 20% (2.1 points), the worked example. A correlation of −0.2 gives 86% and 14% with \(\sigma_p\) = 9.8%, the self-check. Equal risk gives 25%, 34% and 41% of the dollars, the heuristic. Equal dollars gives 51%, 29% and 20% of the risk, and the VRP's share climbs to 31% at a correlation with equity of 0.9.
