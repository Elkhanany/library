## Conflicts
- Within the Edge Book, the classification of calendar effects. spx:1 (Chapter 1G) lists seasonality (Halloween, January) among behavioral edges and "pin risk / OPEX dynamics" under microstructure. spx:5.4 (Chapter 10A) calls the overlays "mostly institutional frictions (Source 3) and some behavioral (Source 2)", says "Halloween is a mystery", and its matrix puts OPEX under microstructure, "partial". spx:8.4 files OPEX as "Microstructure / institutional (Sources 3, 4)" and spx:8.5 gives Halloween "no clean mechanism". The chapter picks one filing, says so, and flags Halloween's as provisional.
- Within the Edge Book, how to run the FOMC cycle. spx:8.2's decision rule is ETF rotation, reducing equity 20-30% in odd weeks. spx:11, Example 5 (Chapter 13E) concludes: "Don't run a separate FOMC rotation strategy. The expected alpha doesn't survive implementation costs even in tax-deferred accounts at retail scale," and times only discretionary entries to even weeks. The chapter states both and makes 13E's reading the default for a taxable account.
- Between the two sources, the FOMC meeting. The Dealer Mechanics Lab (dl:5D, dl:7B, dl:10I, dl:12E; Chapters 5D, 7B, 11K, 13F) teaches a post-announcement vanna-unwind ramp, "0.3-0.5%" of the SPX move "with high reliability across years", traded in the 30 to 90 minutes after the announcement at up to 0.5% of the portfolio at risk, and halves new swing entries from two days before the meeting to one day after. The Edge Book tests neither; it treats the FOMC calendar as an overlay and rejects pre-FOMC drift (Chapter 10I). The chapter states both and links 5D, 7B, 11K, 13F and 10I. The two do not strictly contradict each other: the Lab's trade is intraday and event-driven, the Edge Book's overlay weekly.
- Between the two sources, OPEX: no disagreement. The Lab chapters (2A, 5D, 5F, 9C) describe the charm flow into OPEX Fridays, pinning and the settlement print, and treat pinning as real but small; the Edge Book says awareness only. The chapter links them.
- spx:8.3 sizes the end-of-quarter trade at "0.5% of portfolio at risk per trade"; spx:11, Example 6 (Chapter 13E) at "0.5% of portfolio at risk per leg". The chapter keeps "per trade" and points to 13E's worked quarter.

## Questions for the author
- "NBER WP 33554 (2024) 'The Unintended Consequences of Rebalancing'": the authors are not named. The reading list (R3) will need them.
- "~17 bps" next-day effect: per quarter-end, per day, or per unit of divergence?
- "Aggregated across $5T in 60/40 mandates": which source gives the $5 trillion?
- "FOMC even-week tilt: +0.10-0.30 Sharpe" against spx:5.4's "most overlays add 10-30 bps of Sharpe": Chapter 10A (written in parallel) raises the same question of scale. The toy in this chapter, with equal daily volatility, gives a relative rise of 13% for a 25% odd-week cut, about 0.05 on an equity Sharpe ratio of 0.4, below the quoted range unless the odd weeks are cut much further.
- "Transaction cost discipline: if costs exceed 100 bps annualized, simplify": against an overlay worth 10 to 40 bps a year, a cost of up to 100 bps would already make it lose money. Is 100 bps meant as a hard ceiling, or should the trigger be a fraction of the expected gain?
- The Cieslak sample: "1994-~2014 in their analysis". Please confirm the end year.
- Stivers and Sun (2013): "~30 bps/week excess return" is for option-active large caps; the toy then holds SPY. Is SPY's own OPEX-week return in the paper?
- 180 bps per FOMC cycle over about eight meetings a year is about 14% a year from the even weeks alone, roughly twice the equity premium of Chapter 10B, so the odd weeks cannot have returned "about 0" over the same years unless the full-year total is far above the index's. Which figure should give way? (Raised by the second check of the maths.)
