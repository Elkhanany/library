## Changed
- "Where this fits" (spx:0) described the Edge Book's order: Chapter 1 (four sources), Chapter 2 (why Sharpe lies), Chapter 3 (haircut). In this book the Sharpe material comes first, so the paragraph now reads: Chapter 1G sorts edges into four sources, Chapter 1D "has already shown" why the Sharpe ratio flatters short volatility, and Chapter 13A uses the haircut equation.
- The Sharpe ratio in the six equations is written \((\mu - r)/\sigma\), the brief's notation, rather than the source's \(E[R - r_f]/\sigma_R\). They mean the same here: no log returns appear, so the brief's plain \(r\) applies.
- The expected-value equation keeps the source's letters (\(p\), \(G\), \(L\), \(c\)), which are also those of dl:1C, the source of Chapter 1C. To avoid a clash two chapters apart, Chapter 1E writes leverage as \(k\) (as dl:1D does), not \(L\).
- The source's conviction threshold has \(\text{ES}_{95}\) and a general \(\text{ES}_\alpha\); both are kept, with \(\text{VaR}_\alpha\) glossed as the loss beyond which the worst tail begins.
- Added teaching: the correlation formula (eq:rho) with a hypothetical covariance of −0.006; the two-asset portfolio variance (eq:pvar) with its derivation in a grind box; a hypothetical 60/40 portfolio (20% and 10% volatility) at four correlations; the covariance matrix and \(\mathbf{w}^\top\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\mathbf{w}\) (eq:wsw); the Sharpe ratio of an equal mix of two strategies, \(\text{SR}\sqrt{2/(1+\rho)}\) (eq:srp), which makes "individual Sharpes don't aggregate" concrete; a hypothetical spread of $4.90 to $5.10 widened 10 times.
- Two "Familiar ground" callouts: restriction of range (why a jump in tail correlation is not an artifact of conditioning, since for jointly normal returns conditioning on the tail pulls correlation toward zero) and per-protocol against intention-to-treat (a backtest at mid prices against live fills).
- The VIX and VIX3M are glossed in one sentence (30-day and three-month implied volatility of SPX options) and pointed to Chapter 9A.
- "conflating them produces garbage thinking" (spx:0.9) is softened to "confused thinking".
- Checked: the six equations' expected shortfall, E[L | L > VaR_α], counts a loss as a positive number, while Chapter 1B (and the glossary) define VaR₉₅ and ES₉₅ on returns, as negative numbers, and the conviction threshold of equation 6 needs the positive sign. The text now says that L is the return with its sign reversed, which makes ES_α Chapter 1B's expected shortfall written as a positive number.
