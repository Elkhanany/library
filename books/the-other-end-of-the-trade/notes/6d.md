## Changed
- Fixed: "For SPY at $600 spot: approximately $460 per contract per 1% (same Γ, 100× smaller notional)." An at-the-money SPY option does not have the same gamma per dollar as the SPX option: gamma scales as 1/S, so SPY's is ten times larger (0.0128 against 0.00128). Dollar gamma, ΓS² × 0.01, therefore scales with S, and one SPY contract carries 0.0128 × 600² × 0.01 × 100 = $4,620 per 1%, a tenth of the SPX contract, not a hundredth. The dollar-gamma figure agrees: at SPX 6,000 with the multiplier set to 10 it gives $4.62k. The chapter says "about $4,600", explains the scaling, and makes it the "Common mistake" callout.
- Fixed: the gamma hedge simulator's heading gave a long option's P&L, ½Γ·S²·[(dS/S)² − σ²·dt], for a dealer who is short. The chapter gives the short dealer's sign, eq:shortpnl, as the figure's caption does, with the theta income and the rebalancing cost named as the caption names them.
- The long-gamma mirror keeps the source's round numbers (delta about 0.52, gamma 0.0013, a change of 0.078, then 0.60 and 0.44; 52,000, 60,000 and 44,000 share-equivalents; about 8,000 shares). - "$464/share × 100 = $46,400" is kept, beside the figure's $46.20k (caption: "about $46.2k ... the $46,000 of the text").
- "We'll see in Section 9B that current 0DTE market structure has dealers in aggregate long gamma" is "Chapter 9B shows that the 0DTE market structure has dealers long gamma in aggregate on most days"; "current" is dropped as time-anchored.
- Volmageddon is dated February 2018, as Chapter 5D (dl:5D) dates it, and GME is glossed as GameStop, which dl:5F names. The "gamma flip" is defined in one sentence (the index level at which dealers' net gamma changes sign) and marked with its glossary entry, whose home is Chapter 8A.

## Conflicts
- The dollar-gamma figure's default convention, "0DTE-era (call short)", signs call open interest negative, so a reader who loads only calls sees "Short γ, destabilizing". dl:6E and dl:9B say dealers are net long gamma in 0DTE on most days. The two are compatible: in the 0DTE-era convention of dl:8A dealers are also long puts, so the net can be positive. The chapter says the convention button is an assumption about which side of the calls the dealer holds and points to Chapter 8A. It avoids the self-contradiction listed among the known errors for Chapter 8A.
- The Edge Book reads the size of this force more skeptically (see notes/6a.md). The chapter points to Chapter 6A for that reading, and its rule callout sends the reader to Chapters 10H and 10I for how to use the sign of dealer gamma.
