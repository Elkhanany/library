## Changed
- Fixed: the top-bracket tax comparison. The source gives "37% ordinary income, 23.8% long-term capital gains including the 3.8% Net Investment Income Tax" and then a 1256 blend of "approximately 27-28%" against "approximately 40-41%" for short-term gains, "roughly 12-14 percentage points". On its own inputs the blend is 0.6 × 23.8% + 0.4 × 40.8% = 30.6%; the 27% to 28% is the blend without the surtax (0.6 × 20% + 0.4 × 37% = 26.8%), while the 40% to 41% includes it (37% + 3.8%). Like for like, the saving is 10.2 points either way, 60% of the gap between the short-term and long-term rates. The text keeps the quoted figures as quoted, labels them, and works the like-for-like arithmetic in a table.
- The downstream numbers follow. "Approximately 100 basis points" on an 8% put-write is kept as what the quoted gap gives; like for like it is about 80 (4.74% against 5.55% after tax). "100 bps × 10 years = roughly 10% more terminal wealth, before accounting for the compounding of the saved taxes themselves (which adds another few percent)" is kept as a rough rule, and the text adds the computed decade: $1 grows to $1.59 on SPY and $1.72 on XSP, an 8% larger account and a gap of 13% of the starting capital.
- The SPY against XSP example keeps all its numbers ($300 a contract, $3,000 a month, 9 wins and 3 losses, about $36,000, $15,000 and $21,000; about $12,400 and $15,200 after tax; about $2,800 more). A fifth step adds the like-for-like results: $12,432 against $14,574, or $13,230 against $15,372, a gap of $2,142 either way.
- "XSP delivers roughly 10% more after-tax return on the same gross premium captured": on the source's own example XSP's after-tax result is 22% above SPY's, and like for like 17%, so "10% more" cannot mean the ratio. The text reads it as keeping about 10 more cents of each dollar of pre-tax profit, which is the like-for-like 10.2 points.
- The "five-product, three-account decision tree" recommends "Sized XSP strangles or condors" for portfolio margin. The Edge Book rejects systematic iron condors for retail accounts; the text states both positions, as Chapter 1D does, and points to Chapters 12C, 1D and 10I.
- Added teaching, on hypothetical numbers where invented: settlement and T+1 defined; the cash held against a cash-secured XSP put; the Reg-T call point (eq:regtcall), derived from the two percentages, with $20,000 of stock on $10,000; the day trade defined; a portfolio-margin scenario grid for a short XSP strangle, valued at expiry for simplicity, showing the requirement rise 63% and 125% as the grid widens to ±20% and ±25%; the leverage of one ES contract at $15,000 and $22,000 of margin, and the 5% fall that takes the margin; the blended rate (eq:blend) and the saving identity (eq:saving); after-tax compounding (eq:aftertax); a wash sale and a 1256 roll side by side; the year-end mark on a 1256 position; the March 2020 moves as percentages (−28.5% at the close, nearly 32% to the intraday low, the VIX up almost fivefold); FINRA, ZN and CL glossed in a phrase.
- "every gain and every loss flows through to ordinary tax computation in the year it occurred": "ordinary" is dropped, since a reader could take it for ordinary-income treatment, which 1256 gains do not get. The text says "flows through to the tax computation in the year it occurred".
- Familiar ground: the twelve-month rule for portfolio margin as surgical credentialing with proctored cases.

## Conflicts
- The portfolio-margin minimum. dl:3C: "$125,000+ equity (FINRA minimum)". dl:11.6 (Chapter 12G): "typically $125,000, sometimes $150,000-200,000 depending on broker". Both are given.
- ES initial margin. dl:3C: "around $15,000-$22,000 depending on conditions". dl:11.6: "roughly $13,000-15,000 of initial SPAN margin". Both are given, and the text points to 12G.
- Condors and strangles under portfolio margin: the Dealer Mechanics Lab recommends them; the Edge Book (Chapter 10I) rejects systematic condors for retail. Stated as a disagreement.

## Questions for the author
- The corrected tax gap (10.2 points like for like, not 12 to 14) also changes Chapter 2A's summary, which now quotes both. Is the author content with presenting the quoted figures beside the corrected ones, or should the quoted ones go?
- The iron-condor comparison under portfolio margin: "both wings 5% out-of-the-money ... portfolio margin might require only the loss that would occur in a ±15% scenario — perhaps 1/3 the Reg-T number". If the short strikes sit 5% out of the money, a 15% move passes through them and the scenario loss is close to the spread's full width, the Reg-T number. The one-third holds only for wide wings. Which structure is meant? The text keeps the sentence with its hedges ("might", "perhaps").
- The tax figure holds the long-term rate at 20% at every bracket, leaves out the surtax and charges state tax only on the short-term share, as its caption says. When the ordinary rate, federal plus state, is below 20%, it therefore shows 1256 gains taxed more heavily than SPY's; the text warns the reader to read it where the ordinary rate is well above 20%. Should the figure's long-term rate follow the bracket? And do states tax 1256 gains on the 60/40 split, as the figure assumes?
- "Most retail accounts with $2,000+ equity qualify for Reg-T margin" and "The first PM account every trader opens is operated incorrectly": kept at the source's strength. Is there a source for either?
- Do the Pattern Day Trader thresholds need a date, given the rule's history of proposed changes?

- Length. The chapter runs about 4,900 words without its figure ideas, about 4,200 of them running prose, against dl:3C's 1,940: 2.1 to 2.5 times, above the brief's 1.2 to 1.5. Most of the excess is the tax arithmetic that the correction needs (the like-for-like table, the saving identity, the computed decade), the scenario grid and the three short tax examples. First cuts, if it should be shorter: the year-end mark example, the first common mistake and one check question.
