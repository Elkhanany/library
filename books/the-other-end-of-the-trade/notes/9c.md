## Conflicts
- GEX. The Dealer Mechanics Lab puts the sign of GEX first in strategy selection, treats the gamma flip as a regime boundary and pairs GEX with the term structure. The Edge Book (spx:1's misconception box, Chapter 1G; spx:9.3 and card F4, Chapter 10H; spx:10.5, Chapter 10I) keeps only the sign, as a low-confidence filter worth 10% to 15% of one filter's weight, ignored when it disagrees with the term structure or realized volatility, and used for modest sizing changes; it rejects trading GEX levels. Its three reasons (model dependence, the post-2022 0DTE structure, Cboe's 2025 estimate of about 0.2% of daily liquidity) are given. The chapter states both in "Where the Edge Book disagrees" and returns to it in the mental model, the backtest paragraph, the practice callout, the rule and the recap. Both sources agree on not trading levels, on the sign as context rather than signal, and on skepticism of gamma-squeeze stories.
- Premium selling in calm regimes. The classifier's Range notes call "iron condors and credit spreads still profitable"; the Edge Book rejects systematic iron condors (spx:10.6, Chapter 10I). Stated in the same section.
- Five regimes against four: Chapter 9A's table and the classifier; mapped here.

- The claim that a regime-aware backtest "persists out-of-sample more reliably than most other technical signals" has no cited study. Is there one?
