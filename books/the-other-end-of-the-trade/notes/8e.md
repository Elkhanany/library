## Conflicts
- Golez and Jackwerth's mechanism. research/theory.md reads it as charm plus customer flows acting together, "as in the simulator's c term". research/evidence.md, from summaries of the paper's results, reads it more specifically: the time decay of hedges on its own produces anti-pinning, and investors' reselling and early exercise of in-the-money options outweigh it at the serial expirations. The text gives the abstract's words for the mechanism and the more specific reading as "a summary of the paper's results". If the paper's body says otherwise, the paragraph beginning "A summary of the paper's results goes further" and the third check question need revising.
- Skylit's King Node against the theory and evidence: its sign-blind definition, its end-of-week anchor and its "prefer to settle" wording against the models (sign decides attraction or repulsion; drift only near expiry; nobody chooses the pin) and the evidence (small, sign-dependent effects at expiry; no test of the largest absolute node as a target). Skylit's two definitions of the King (largest absolute; strongest positive) are both quoted.
- Chapter 9C (from dl:9C) says pinning is "stronger in single stocks than in the index, and stronger in names with lower liquidity". This chapter neither repeats nor contradicts that. The sources it uses measure the two in different units, basis points per stock against notional futures value, so they give no direct comparison. The two chapters agree that pinning is a tiebreaker and that the 16.5 does not convert into SPX points.
