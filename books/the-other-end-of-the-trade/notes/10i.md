## Conflicts
- Systematic iron condors. The Edge Book rejects them on the CNDR record and allows only the occasional discretionary condor. The Dealer Mechanics Lab teaches the condor as an intermediate neutral structure (Chapter 12C), counts "iron condor writing" among the short-premium strategies with positive long-run returns (Chapter 5E), and picks a defined-risk condor at 0.25% of portfolio risk in its strategy matrix (Chapter 12A), while its own do-not-trade card rejects systematic condors on the same record (Chapter 13F). The chapter states each position and names the open question 10A names: whether a condor chosen for the regime escapes the index's record. Chapter 5E's claim of positive long-run returns and the CNDR record cannot both describe systematic condor writing, and the book should say which it means.
- GEX levels. The Edge Book rejects level scalping and keeps only the sign. The Lab's dashboard reads the call wall above spot as potential resistance and a magnet for pinning, the put wall below as potential support and the flip distance as a regime marker (Chapter 11A); its conviction score rewards or penalizes a trade by the walls (Chapter 13B); Chapter 8B expects range trading to win with positive GEX. The Lab's own card rejects level-by-level scalping (Chapter 13F). The chapter states both, and notes that the Edge Book lists open-interest concentration as descriptive, not predictive.
- VIX derivatives. The Edge Book rejects VIX ETPs and points volatility exposure to SPX and XSP options. The Lab uses long-dated VIX calls as the cleanest hedge for a short-vega book (Chapters 13H and 12F). VIX calls are not ETPs, so the rejection does not strictly cover them; the chapter says so.
- The Cboe 2025 figure: "0.2% of SPX daily liquidity" (Edge Book) against "0.2% of futures liquidity" (dl:12E, Chapter 13F). Settled by the literature search: the 2025 note says SPX daily liquidity; futures liquidity is the base of Cboe's 2023 note (0.04% to 0.17%). See the notes of 1G and 10H.
- The VXX roll decay: 5% to 15% a year in normal times (Edge Book) against about 10% (the Lab, Chapter 9A). Chapter 9A already notes that the range brackets the Lab's figure.
