## Changed
- Fixed: "Party A has converted their fixed-rate funding into floating-rate exposure; Party B has done the reverse." Party A pays fixed and receives floating, which turns floating-rate funding into a fixed rate, not the other way round. The text now says so: A, with floating-rate funding, ends up paying a fixed 4.5%; B, with fixed-rate funding, does the reverse.
- Fixed: "A collar is long stock + long put + short call → equivalent to a long call spread + a put exposure." At expiry, long stock with a long put at K1 and a short call at K2 is worth exactly cash of K1 plus a K1 to K2 call spread; no put exposure remains. The worked example checks it at three prices (80, 100, 130 with strikes 95 and 110), and Chapter 5F's put-call parity gives the same result.
- Clarified: "The futures price K is set such that this 'cash-and-carry' trade has zero expected P&L." The cash-and-carry's result is fixed on the day it is entered, so arbitrage sets it to zero outright, not only on average. The grind box derives \(F_0 = S_0 e^{(r-q)T}\) and says so.
- Fixed: "when interest rates are positive, ES trades at a premium to SPX that decays to zero". By the carry formula the premium needs the rate to exceed the dividend yield, so the text reads "positive and above the dividend yield".
- Fixed: "notional is not exchanged; it's only used to compute the floating payments" is "used only to compute the payments": the notional also sets the fixed leg (4.5% of $10 million for a quarter is the $112,500).
- Notation. The source's \(K\) for "the contract price" of a future is kept in the payoff, because the figure strikes all three families at \(K = 100\). The price a future trades at today is \(F_0\). Option premiums are \(C\) and \(P\). In the swap, the notional is \(M\) (the brief reserves \(N\) for the normal distribution function), the fixed rate \(c\) and the floating rate \(L\).
- "FX" is spelled out once as foreign exchange; "P&L" is "profit and loss (P&L)" once.
- Glossary: gamma, theta, vega and breakeven use the Part V entries (glossary/V-a.json), straddle and variance swap those of V-b.json, and the basis, tick and Section 1256 marks use the Part II entries "futures basis", "tick size" and "Section 1256 contract". This chapter's new entries (glossary/IV.json) are initial margin, variation margin, forward contract, cost of carry, swap, interest rate swap and structured product.
- Added teaching, hypothetical where it uses numbers: daily settlement on one ES contract (6000, 6030, 5985); the cost-of-carry formula with a grind derivation; the basis at a 4% rate, a 1.5% yield and three months (37.6 points, about $1,880 a contract); the swap's net payment [eq:swap] at SOFR of 4% and 5%; a swap as a string of forwards; the breakevens \(K + C\) and \(K - P\), matched to the figure; Jensen's inequality [eq:jensen] with two spreads of outcomes (90 or 110, then 80 or 120), the chord form of convexity that Chapter 4A states in words; why a linear hedge stays right and a convex one does not; the slope rule for taking apart any payoff made of straight segments; the straddle's payoff \(|S_T - K|\); a 5% fall as $15,000 a contract against the low end of the initial margin.
