## Dropped
- "Calendar", "Iron": capitalized at the start of table cells in the source ("Calendar/diagonal (slow-drift vehicles...)", "Iron condor → Iron butterfly"); written in lower case inside the cells. The facts checker reports both.
- "Cards 1-7 favor cash-settled SPX/XSP equivalents for the same view when account size permits": no cards 1 to 7 exist in the atlas or in the Dealer Mechanics Lab's setup cards (old 12E has four setup cards, two overlays and seven do-not-trade cards, none numbered). The text keeps the rule without the numbers: an index view favors the cash-settled SPX or XSP version of a structure when account size permits. See the questions below.
- The arrows in the "Prefer" column ("Equity → Long call spread (45 DTE) → ...") are written as lists separated by semicolons, in order of preference, and "Equity" is written "Shares", defined once above the tables.

## Changed
- The single table is split into six tables by kind of view (directional, mean reversion, range and volatility, around earnings, income and hedging, specialist), keeping every row, every preference in its order and every avoid with its reason. Each group gets a short paragraph on the logic behind its preferences, drawn from the source's own reasons in the avoid column.
- "Long call spread" is written "call debit spread", the name used in Chapter 12C, where the entry notes that it is also called a long call spread.
- The PEAD row marks the term with the glossary entry "post-earnings announcement drift" (Chapter 1G).
- Old references translated: Section 3C (the 1256 treatment) to Chapter 2E; "12B conviction or 12B Real Value (single names)" to Chapter 13B; "12C pipeline" to Chapter 13C.
- Added teaching, no new facts: a worked example of why a long call is a poor vehicle for a modest drift, priced with Black-Scholes at a hypothetical 16% implied volatility and 30 days (the 6,000 call at about 110 points, the 6,150 call at about 52, the spread at about 58; breakevens 6,110 and 6,058; P&L at 1%, 2% and 3% drifts; the call wins only above about 6,202). It makes the source's avoid reason for the naked long call, "theta + vega risk for marginal directional gain", checkable. A practice callout, a "When it fails", a decision rule, a common mistake, a recap and four self-check questions.
- One "Familiar ground" analogy: the cross-index as a differential diagnosis, with the avoid column as relative contraindications, matching the source's note that the avoid column is "structural, not absolute".

## Conflicts
- Iron condors: the range-bound row ranks the iron condor first and the crush row lists it second. The Edge Book rejects systematic iron condors for retail accounts (spx:10.6, Chapter 10I). The text states both after the range and volatility table and links Chapters 10I and 12C.

## Questions for the author
- "Cards 1-7": which cards were meant? If the first seven rows of the cross-index, the rule reads naturally as "for the directional and mean-reversion views, prefer SPX or XSP"; the text states it for index views generally.
- The modest bullish drift row prefers a call debit spread at 45 DTE for a view of 2 to 4 weeks; the worked example uses 30 days for simplicity. Should the example use 45?
