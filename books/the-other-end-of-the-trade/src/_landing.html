How market makers move markets

The Other End
of the Trade

Every option has a dealer on the other side of it. The dealer hedges in the underlying to stay neutral, and that hedging is forced, structural and measurable. This book teaches it from the ground up, for a reader who knows algebra and some statistics: returns and risk, the products, options and the Greeks, one dealer's hedge and then the whole market's. Then it shows how to trade with that knowledge.

Every option ever traded created an obligation for someone to transact in the underlying. The market is, in part, the integral of those obligations.

How it is arranged

Thirteen parts and a reference section, meant to be read in order. Each chapter lists what you need to know first and ends with questions to check yourself.

In order

Foundations, mechanism, practice

Parts I to V lay the ground: returns and risk, markets, models, derivatives and options. Parts VI to IX are the mechanism, from one dealer's hedge to the whole market. Part X takes the known sources of return one by one. Parts XI to XIII turn it all into trading: swing setups, a strategy atlas and the discipline that keeps an edge alive.

Figures

Made to be played with

Fifty-four interactive figures, each with its controls under it. A figure illustrates the passage beside it, and the text stands on its own without one.

Not advice

Education and analysis

Nothing here is a recommendation to trade. Where a number is a rule of thumb rather than a fitted estimate, the text says so, and no figure prints a probability of profit.

How it is built

The book is written from two sources: the Education curriculum of the Dealer Mechanics Lab and the SPX/XSP Edge Book. Both were rewritten for a reader new to markets, with the maths taught where it is first needed. Every number, study and caution in the sources was kept, or its removal recorded in the book's notes.

A term with a dotted underline opens its definition. Every term is also in the glossary, and every formula is on one page in the reference section. The figures draw in the book's colors, follow its light and dark themes, and need no network.

The whole book installs to a home screen and reads with the network switched off. Nothing on any page is fetched from anywhere.

Part of A Library. For education only, and not investment advice. The Black-Scholes model assumes idealized markets, and real markets are messier.
