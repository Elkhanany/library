Part VIII · Aggregation · Chapter 8D

Gamma squeeze identification

Preconditions, signals, when not to chase it

The case studies in 8C describe what a squeeze looks like after it ignites. This subsection is the operational counterpart: how to identify a setup before the feedback runs, what live indicators distinguish a real squeeze from a noisy uptrend, and the conditions under which you should explicitly avoid chasing one despite the headline pattern.

1 · The three-precondition framework, restated as a checklist

From the case studies — Volmageddon 2018, March 2020, GME 2021, August 2024 — three preconditions consistently co-occur. Any one alone is insufficient.

PreconditionWhat it meansHow to measure
Structural concentrationEither dealer book is heavily short-gamma in one direction, or a meaningful share of float is shorted (single-name)Net dealer GEX sign + magnitude; FINRA short interest reports for single-names
Mechanical buying pressureA flow source that requires purchases at progressively higher prices — short covering, dealer delta-hedging, leveraged-ETF rebalancingVolume composition + dealer-side flow inference + leveraged-ETF size in the name
Liquidity constraintCounterparty inventory or capital can't expand to absorb the flow — wide bid-ask, broker margin calls, exchange circuit logicBid-ask spread widening; broker outage signals; price-paths showing gap-up rather than continuous expansion

All three present → squeeze conditions exist. Whether the squeeze triggers is a question of catalyst: news, options-expiry positioning, retail coordination.

2 · SqueezeMetrics-style indicator overlay

The four univariate indicators from the SqueezeMetrics framework (P, V, G, D) map onto the precondition framework as live, computable signals. The signal-to-precondition mapping:

IndicatorComputationSqueeze interpretationMaps to
P (price-trend)MA(returns) / MA(|returns|) over 21 days, range [−1, +1]P > +0.4 = strong directional bias upward → mechanical-buying narrative plausibleMechanical buying pressure
V (vol-trend)mad − MA(mad) — recent absolute moves vs their own MAV > 0 expanding into a strong P > 0 = vol rising into the trend; this is the rare pattern that distinguishes a squeeze from a quiet uptrendLiquidity constraint (vol expanding = absorption breaking down)
G (gamma-ratio)call_gamma / (call_gamma + put_gamma) at constant 20% vol, OI-weightedG > 0.6 = call-heavy book; combined with rising spot this means dealers absorb increasing upside delta → short-gamma feedback liveStructural concentration
D (dark-ratio)FINRA dark-pool short volume / total dark volume, 5-day MAD rising while P also rising = hidden accumulation underneath a visible uptrend → the squeeze is fueled and obscuredHidden order flow / mechanical buying

All four normalize to roughly [−1, +1] via tanh on a rolling window so they can be plotted on a common axis. The interesting joint signals are rare — most days, the four diverge. When they align (P high, V high, G high, D high), the structural setup for a squeeze is in place; that's the moment to check the catalyst, not bet ahead.

3 · Single-name vs index

The mechanism is identical; the structural conditions differ in kind.

AspectIndex gamma squeeze (Vol/March-20/Aug-24)Single-name gamma squeeze (GME)
Required short-interest concentrationNone (mechanism runs through dealer hedging alone)Critical — typically > 20% of float, often > 50%
Required catalystVol shock (idiosyncratic or macro)Coordinated retail call-buying + a narrative
SpeedHours to daysDays to weeks
AsymmetrySymmetric (positive or negative GEX can blow out)Strongly asymmetric (long side; rare to see short-side squeezes)
Liquidity constraint operates throughMarket-maker capital + market depthBrokerage capital + DTCC margin
Resolution mechanismVol-fade after dealer rebalancing capacity restoredRetail attention fade + short covering complete + broker restrictions break the loop

Most published "gamma squeeze" commentary conflates the two. The trades are different, the risk profiles are different, and the resolution paths are different. Tag every squeeze read explicitly as single-name or index before sizing.

4 · Operational decision rule — when to chase, when to fade, when to step aside

Three rules borrowed from the case-study discipline in this section:

  1. Don't chase what's already running. Once GME hit $200, the squeeze was visible. The dealer hedging that drove $20 → $200 had largely completed. The path from $200 → $483 was retail FOMO and short covering, not gamma. The risk-reward was much worse.

  2. Liquidity constraints break loops abruptly. Broker restrictions, exchange halts, and clearinghouse margin all act as discrete shock absorbers that can collapse the feedback in minutes. Position sizing for squeeze trades should explicitly assume 50% of intraday peak is the realistic exit price under broker-restriction scenarios.

  3. Cover before the catalyst clears. Squeezes resolve when the structural condition resolves: short interest covered, dealer hedge inventory normalized, retail attention faded. Whichever resolves first defines the exit. Wait for the one you can actually observe (broker restrictions, dealer-side rebalancing footprints in the tape, short-interest reports) rather than the one you'd prefer (price exhaustion you can't predict ahead of time).
