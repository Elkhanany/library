## Verdict

Part III reads as one argument, and the argument arrives: a reader who takes only these 72
passages is carried from a measured coincidence about two kinds of mass to the statement that a
growing universe has no conserved energy for anything to belong to, and the last chapter's
handover to Part IV is the most deliberate ending the plain layer has yet produced. The thread
drops in exactly three places — the 3.6→3.7 seam, where the part changes from building machinery
to spending it and no passage says so; 3.9.5, where the whole observational fact that the universe
expands is never stated in the plain layer at all; and 3.8.8, which promises to name the assumption
carrying the weight of the singularity theorems and then names it only in the main text. The three
new chapters read as one hand with each other and very nearly as the same hand as 3.1–3.6, with a
single measurable tell — the colon, used at three times the rate of the six earlier chapters — while
three of the four motifs fall silent across 3.7 and 3.8 and one of them, "falling apart into
independent pieces", has now sounded once in seventy-two passages.

## §4 Voice drift

Three agents, three sittings. It shows in exactly one measurable and one impressionistic way, and
otherwise it does not show at all — which is a better result than the previous review got for a
single chapter written by a single agent.

**The measurable tell: the colon.** Counting every colon in the 72 passages:

| chapter | 3.1 | 3.2 | 3.3 | 3.4 | 3.5 | 3.6 | **3.7** | **3.8** | **3.9** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| colons | 3 | 2 | 6 | 6 | 8 | 4 | **14** | **11** | **14** |
| per passage | 0.4 | 0.3 | 0.8 | 0.8 | 0.8 | 0.6 | **1.8** | **1.4** | **2.0** |

Three times the rate of the six earlier chapters, and the same construction every time: a claim,
then a colon, then the expansion of it. *"Static means two further things at once: there is another
such direction…"*; *"it is wrong by a factor of three: it puts the circular orbit inside the
radius…"*; *"a first-order statement: the square of the fractional growth rate equals the
density…"*; *"an accurate report of where the subject stands: the curvature near anything a
laboratory contains…"*. Individually each is fine — the plan does not forbid the colon and the book
uses it. As a habit it is a different hand's rhythm, and read in sequence after 3.1–3.6 it is the one
thing that changes audibly. Notably the tell is *shared* by all three new chapters, which means the
three sittings produced one voice, not three; 3.7, 3.8 and 3.9 do not diverge from each other at all
on any measure I could construct. The replacements in §2 remove five colons (3.7.2 ×2, 3.7.4, 3.8.8
×2), bringing 3.7 to 11 and 3.8 to 9. That is enough. Do not hunt the rest — several are load-bearing.

**The impressionistic tell: the new chapters write to the top of the length range.** Mean words per
passage: 3.1–3.6 range from 230 to 233; 3.7, 3.8, 3.9 sit at 230, 232, 230. No drift there at all,
in fact — the form discipline is identical and near-perfect. Every one of the 72 passages is two or
three paragraphs and between 220 and 235 words.

**The 2.4 test, applied to the three new chapters.** The previous review caught chapter 2.4 by four
tells. All four come back clean:

- *A bare equation.* None. Zero display equations, zero bare symbols, zero formulae anywhere in
  3.7–3.9. `MATHPLAN-3.7-3.9.md` is full of them and not one leaked.
- *An authorial "we".* One instance in 23 passages: 3.8.3's *"the polar language the problem forces
  on us"*. That is the same construction as 3.5.2's *"the objects the previous section forced on
  us"*, so it is an echo of the existing voice rather than a departure, and it reads as the book's
  impersonal "us" rather than an authorial one. Leave it.
- *Em-dash pile-ups.* Zero em-dashes in all 23 new passages. The whole of Part III has one, in 3.4.1.
- *Banned words.* `simply`, `of course`, `obviously`, `clearly`, `imagine`: none. `just` appears
  twice, both in 3.8.2 (*"a ray aimed to pass just outside the peak"*, *"A ray aimed just inside"*),
  and both are positional rather than the dismissive intensifier the plan bans. Do not change them;
  see §5.
- Also checked: zero rhetorical questions, zero exclamation marks, zero second-person flattery.
  `you` appears three times in 3.9 and none in 3.7 or 3.8, against twelve in 3.3.

**The seams between the three new chapters.** 3.7.8→3.8.1 and 3.8.8→3.9.1 are both good, and 3.8.8→
3.9.1 is the best chapter seam in the part: 3.8.8 ends on a theory reporting the edge of its own
domain, 3.9.1 opens *"Every geometry in this part so far has been built around a lump. The largest
object there is has no outside, so the method must be turned inside out"*, which names the turn
rather than restating the previous passage. 3.7.8→3.8.1 is weaker but adequate, because 3.8.1's
second clause (*"Every path in the last few chapters was labelled by the reading of a clock carried
along it"*) does the reaching. **The seam that fails is 3.6→3.7, which is a seam between an old
chapter and a new one, not between two new ones** — Finding 1.

**Verdict on voice: the three new chapters read as one hand, and as very nearly the same hand as
3.1–3.6. The colon is the only thing that gives them away, and five deletions take care of it.**

## §5 What is already working, and should not be "fixed"

A later pass with a checklist will be tempted by several things here. They are deliberate.

**The ending is deliberate, and it does exactly what it was asked to do.** 3.9.7 was set the job of
telling the reader in advance that Parts IV–VI happen on flat spacetime and that gravity does not
return until 7.1. It does it, and it does it without a citation: *"The next three parts are done on
flat spacetime, and gravity does not reappear for twenty-five chapters. That is not the book
abandoning what it has built. It is an accurate report of where the subject stands: the curvature
near anything a laboratory contains is far below the precision of any experiment ever performed on a
particle, so flat spacetime is not a convenience but a measured approximation with a stated error."*
Then it names the three places where that stops being legitimate. That last sentence is the best
closing sentence the plain layer has produced, because it turns a four-part absence into a stated
limitation with an error bar, which is the essay's whole method applied to the essay's own structure.
Do not soften it and do not add a summary after it.

**The 3.9.6 / 3.9.7 pairing is the point of the ending, not a collision.** Two consecutive passages
open with the book auditing itself: here is the oldest debt, paid; here is the one debt, deliberately
unpaid. §3 flags it as C16 because a mechanical reading of the rule would; it should stay. Finding 7
changes 3.9.6's opening words for a different reason and leaves the pairing intact.

**Motif C is in the best health it has ever been, and it got there in the new chapters.** The
previous review found "nearly everything is an approximation" absent from Part I and *inverted* in
Parts II and III, used four times out of five as a boast about exactness. In 3.7–3.9 it is stated
positively four times and once as an explicit book-wide habit: 3.8.3's *"the honest move is the one
this book has made in every part: expand about something already understood and keep the first
correction"*, with the small quantity named and the discarded term named (*"an error that is the
correction to the correction"*); 3.7.8's *"This is a perturbative result, legitimate because the
correction is one part in ten million even for the innermost planet, and worthless otherwise"*; and
3.9.7's *"a measured approximation with a stated error"*. That is the drumbeat working. Leave it.

**The local-versus-global thread is the quietest and best-built thing in Part III.** 3.1.5's product
of size and time, 3.2.1's patches, 3.4.8's cone, 3.5.4's hole measured by calculus, 3.9.2's cylinder,
and finally 3.9.6's *"What does survive is the local statement, exactly and always: nothing is
created or destroyed at any point. Adding that up over a large region is the step that fails."* Six
passages across nine chapters, each adding something, no repetition. Finding 4 improves one address
in it and changes nothing else.

**3.7.3's middle paragraph is the closest thing in Part III to a box describing machinery, and it
survives the test.** *"Take the equation belonging to the time direction and the one belonging to the
radial direction, weight each by the other's factor, and add."* That is algebra narration and the
plan warns against it. It stays, because the passage's first sentence has already said why the
narration matters (*"a system in that position has no business being consistent. It is, and not by
luck"*) and its last two sentences deliver the meaning nothing else in the chapter delivers:
*"Nothing in the empty-space equations knows what a mass is… it is at that moment, and not earlier,
that a mass enters at all."* Every one of the 72 passages can be placed on the §2 table's Part III
row; the only genuine drift off it is 3.9.3's static-universe sentence, which Finding 10 repairs.

**3.8.2's two uses of `just`.** Positional, not dismissive: a ray aimed just outside the peak escapes
and one aimed just inside does not, and the whole content of the passage is that the dividing aim is
a single number. No substitute is as precise. The plan's ban is on the minimising intensifier.

**3.8.7's rocket comparison.** *"An accelerating rocket in empty space has a boundary of the same
local kind, worked out in full earlier. The honest difference is that the rocket's boundary belongs
to the rocket and dissolves when it stops accelerating, and this one belongs to nobody."* This is the
plan's analogy rule executed exactly: a tight mapping, reached back to by picture, and the break
named in the same breath. It is the best single analogy in Part III.

**3.9.5's balloon.** Same discipline, and the harder case, because the balloon is the analogy the
reader arrives with. *"The rubber balloon gets the important part right and one thing badly wrong…
But the rubber sits in a room, and the room is what makes expanding into anything mean anything.
There is no room."* Three words at the end, and they close a picture the reader has carried since
3.2.1's *"Spacetime has no room around it and nothing outside from which it could be viewed"*. That
reach is not signposted and does not need to be.

**Form discipline.** Seventy-two passages, every one two or three paragraphs, every one between 220
and 234 words, no display equations, no bare symbols, one em-dash, no rhetorical questions, no
exclamation marks. Nothing in §2 asks for a new passage, and nothing should.
