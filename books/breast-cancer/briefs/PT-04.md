## Thesis

Organized by pathway. Every chapter closes on the resistance mechanisms that Part X will act
on, so biology and clinical failure are never decoupled. Write each chapter so the clinical
payoff in Part X reads as a direct consequence rather than a coincidence.

---

### BC-130 · Hallmarks and pathway logic

**Carries** where the hallmark framework misleads in breast cancer specifically.

**Threads** Subtype-specific dependencies. Pathway redundancy and feedback as the reason single
node inhibition fails.

---

### BC-140 · Estrogen receptor signaling and endocrine resistance

**Carries** that ESR1 alterations are acquired, not de novo, and what that implies for when you
look for them.

**Threads** The cistrome and pioneer factors, FOXA1 and GATA3. Non-genomic signaling including
GPER. ESR1 mutations, fusions, and amplification. ER and HER2 cross-talk as a bidirectional
plane rather than a one-way arm. A working taxonomy of endocrine resistance.

**Anchors** ESR1 mutations rise from roughly 8 to 10% at first line to 33 to 39% by third line.
That gradient is the whole argument for serial ctDNA in [[BC-390]] and for the
{{trial:serena6}} paradigm [@turner2026serena6]. ER signaling as an active immune-evasion
programme is the bridge to [[BC-230]].

**Controversy** Whether adaptive rewiring, cistromic reprogramming, and clonal lock-in occur in
that order.

**Cross-refs** [[BC-150]] [[BC-230]] [[BC-300]] [[BC-430]] [[BC-560]]

### BC-150 · HER family signaling

**Carries** that amplification and activating mutation are different diseases.

**Threads** Dimerization and ligand dependence. The ERBB2 amplicon and extrachromosomal DNA
architecture that generates instability, which sets up [[BC-280]] directly. HER3 and neuregulin.
p95HER2 and epitope loss.

**Anchors** Extrachromosomal amplification and breakage-fusion-bridge behave measurably
differently, which is the mechanistic origin of HER2 heterogeneity rather than a description of
it [@goyette2026].

**Cross-refs** [[BC-280]] [[BC-460]] [[BC-570]]

### BC-160 · Cell cycle regulation

**Threads** The cyclin D, CDK4/6, and Rb axis. Escape through Rb loss, cyclin E amplification,
and CDK6 upregulation. Rb as a transcriptional coactivator under CDK4/6 inhibition. CDK2 and
CDK7 as the next nodes.

**Anchors** {{trial:paloma3}} showed RB1 mutations emerging exclusively under palbociclib, which
is the cleanest available demonstration of therapy as selective pressure [@palomA3rb1].

**Cross-refs** [[BC-300]] [[BC-345]] [[BC-490]] [[BC-560]]

### BC-170 · PI3K, AKT, mTOR, and related networks

**Threads** Isoform biology and the therapeutic window. Feedback reactivation. Hippo and FAT1.

**Anchors** The clinical payoff is {{trial:capitello291}} and {{trial:inavo120}}. Inavolisib is
the first PI3K-pathway agent with an overall survival benefit, at 34.0 against 27.0 months
[@jhaveri2025inavo], which reframes this chapter's section on pathway convergence and
combination logic from theory into a demonstrated result.

**Controversy** Whether the toxicity burden of this class is acceptable outside the
biomarker-selected population.

**Cross-refs** [[BC-450]] [[BC-560]]

### BC-180 · DNA repair, replication stress, and genomic instability

**Threads** HRD as a measurable phenotype through scars, signatures, and functional assays.
PARP trapping and synthetic lethality. Reversion mutations. The cGAS-STING bridge to immunity,
which is the link to [[BC-210]].

**Anchors** {{trial:olympia}} [@olympia2026], {{trial:olympiad}}, {{trial:embraca}}, and
{{trial:tbcrc048}} for the extension beyond germline BRCA.

### BC-190 · Metabolism, proteostasis, and stress responses

**Threads** Glycolytic and oxidative phenotypes. Mitochondrial biogenesis and OXPHOS dependence.
