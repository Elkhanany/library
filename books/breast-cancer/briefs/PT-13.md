## Thesis

Separate biology from access at every step and refuse to let race stand in for either. Race,
ethnicity, and genetic ancestry are three different variables. The rigor of this part depends
entirely on keeping them apart, and its credibility depends on refusing the easy attribution.

---

### BC-690 · Disparities across the breast cancer continuum

**Carries** the decomposition of an outcome gap into biology, access, and delivery.

**Threads** Race, ethnicity, and ancestry as separate variables. Structural racism and social
determinants as measurable exposures, including redlining and residential segregation.
Health-related social needs and their collection. Multilevel frameworks. Subtype distribution
and young-onset disease across populations. Ancestry-associated tumor biology, stated carefully.
Screening uptake, diagnostic delay, and time to treatment. Guideline-concordant receipt and
completion. Trial representation. Disaggregation of aggregated populations. How disparities
research can itself cause harm.

**Anchors** Black women in the United States experience roughly 36 to 41% higher breast cancer
mortality than White women despite similar or lower incidence, and the gap has widened for
breast cancer even as overall cancer disparities narrowed. West African ancestry correlates with
higher TNBC rates, more TP53 and fewer PIK3CA alterations, and greater chromosomal instability.
Present those as ancestry-associated, then immediately note that the PIK3CA difference, roughly
34% against 42%, has direct implications for PI3K-inhibitor eligibility. That is the exact point
where biology and access become entangled rather than separable, and it is the best teaching
moment in the part. Black patients are roughly 2.3 to 5.9% of breast cancer trial participants
against about 12.7% of the population, with especially severe underrepresentation in PARP
inhibitor trials, even though willingness to enroll does not differ by race when trials are
offered. That last clause does a lot of work and should not be omitted.

**Controversy** Whether ancestry-associated tumor biology reflects genetics or the biological
embedding of social adversity, and the harm of attributing access-driven outcome gaps to biology.

### BC-710 · Global breast cancer care

**Threads** The epidemiologic transition. Late-stage presentation and the determinants of delay.
Resource-stratified guidelines. Pathology infrastructure as the rate-limiting step. Essential
medicines. Radiotherapy and surgical capacity. Workforce. Capacity building and twinning. Data
sovereignty and avoiding extractive research.

**Anchors** Frame global care with the WHO Global Breast Cancer Initiative 60-60-80 targets,
which are at least 60% stage I or II at diagnosis, workup within 60 days, and at least 80%
treatment completion [@who_gbci]. Use the Breast Health Global Initiative four-tier resource
stratification of basic, limited, enhanced, and maximal [@bhgi]. Pair with the survival spread
planted in [[BC-020]].
