## Thesis

Decision-focused, subtype by subtype. Mechanism lives in Part VIII. The neoadjuvant setting is
framed as a scientific platform, not as a question of treatment timing.

---

### BC-480 · Atypia and in situ disease

**Threads** Risk stratification and genomic assays in DCIS. Surgery, radiation, and endocrine
risk reduction. Active surveillance. Communicating a diagnosis that may not need treating.

**Anchors** {{trial:comet}}, {{trial:loris}}, and {{trial:lord}} test whether some DCIS needs no
treatment, which is the direct experimental answer to the question posed in [[BC-050]].

**Controversy** What these trials can and cannot answer given their enrollment and follow-up.

**Cross-refs** [[BC-050]] [[BC-400]] [[BC-670]]

### BC-490 · Early hormone receptor-positive, HER2-negative disease

**Carries** that CDK4/6 inhibitors are not interchangeable in the adjuvant setting.

**Threads** Clinical and genomic risk assessment. Endocrine selection by menopausal status.
Ovarian suppression. Genomic assays and chemotherapy omission. Adjuvant CDK4/6 inhibition.
Adjuvant PARP inhibition. Extended therapy. Adherence.

**Anchors** Two positive adjuvant CDK4/6 trials with different populations. {{trial:monarche}},
abemaciclib for two years in high-risk node-positive disease, shows a significant OS benefit at
7 years, 86.8% against 85.0% with HR 0.84, and a widening 7-year IDFS gap of 77.4% against
70.9%. {{trial:natalee}}, ribociclib for three years in a broader stage II to III population
including selected node-negative disease, shows a 5-year IDFS benefit with HR 0.72 and
still-immature OS. {{trial:pallas}} and {{trial:penelope-b}} with palbociclib were negative,
which is the teaching point. Adjuvant olaparib improves six-year OS, 87.5% against 83.2% with
HR 0.72, in germline BRCA carriers [@olympia2026].

**Controversy** Sequencing olaparib with adjuvant CDK4/6 inhibition in dually eligible patients
is unresolved and should be presented as genuinely unresolved.

**Cross-refs** [[BC-070]] [[BC-160]] [[BC-380]] [[BC-430]] [[BC-670]]

### BC-500 · Early HER2-positive disease

**Threads** Stage and risk-adapted selection. Neoadjuvant backbone and dual blockade. Pathologic
response as a decision point. Post-neoadjuvant therapy. De-escalation in small node-negative
tumors. Triple-positive disease as its own algorithm. Cardiac surveillance.

**Anchors** {{trial:katherine}} established T-DM1 for residual disease with a mature OS benefit,
7-year OS 89.1% against 84.4% with HR 0.66, but no reduction in CNS recurrence, which is worth
stating because it shapes expectations. {{trial:destiny-b05}} now shows trastuzumab deruxtecan
superior to T-DM1 for high-risk residual disease, 3-year IDFS 92.3% against 83.5% with HR 0.47,
at the cost of roughly 10% interstitial lung disease [@db05]. {{trial:aphinity}} confines the
adjuvant pertuzumab benefit to node-positive disease. Neoadjuvant {{trial:destiny-b11}} raises
pCR over ddAC-THP, 67.3% against 56.3%, while conjugate monotherapy was inferior, and EFS is
immature, which invites the pCR caution developed in [[BC-540]].

**Controversy** Whether a pCR gain with immature EFS should change practice.

**Cross-refs** [[BC-280]] [[BC-460]] [[BC-540]] [[BC-640]]

### BC-510 · Early triple-negative disease

**Carries** that the OS benefit of neoadjuvant immunotherapy exceeds what the pCR gain predicts,
which is the strongest evidence in the book that pCR is an imperfect surrogate.

**Threads** Neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy. Platinum and BRCA status. Residual disease
adaptation. Immune toxicity in a curative-intent population. The missing predictive biomarker.

**Anchors** {{trial:keynote522}} is the standard for stage II to III. pCR 63% against 55.6%,
five-year EFS 81.3% against 72.3% with HR 0.63, and a five-year OS benefit of 86.6% against
81.7% with HR 0.66 that is driven by better outcomes in residual-disease patients and is
independent of PD-L1 [@schmid2024k522os]. {{trial:geparnuevo}} showed an OS benefit despite a
non-significant pCR gain, reinforcing the same point. {{trial:b59}} missed its EFS endpoint, so
the class effect is not automatic. {{trial:create-x}} benefit may concentrate in RCB-III and is
of uncertain value after platinum and immunotherapy. For germline BRCA carriers adjuvant
olaparib is preferred over capecitabine.

**Controversy** Whether adjuvant pembrolizumab adds anything after pCR, which
{{trial:optimice}} addresses, and whether the anthracycline can be dropped, which
{{trial:scarlet}} addresses.

**Figure worth featuring** The KEYNOTE-522 overall survival Kaplan-Meier with the subgroup
forest plot, because it shows both the effect and its independence from PD-L1 status in one
place.

**Cross-refs** [[BC-180]] [[BC-210]] [[BC-470]] [[BC-540]]

### BC-540 · The neoadjuvant setting as a scientific platform

**Carries** the surrogate-endpoint argument for the whole book.

**Threads** pCR as an endpoint and what it does and does not predict. Residual cancer burden and
continuous measures. Response-adapted and de-escalation designs. Window-of-opportunity studies.
Paired sampling under treatment pressure. Neoadjuvant therapy as an observable experiment in
tumor evolution.

**Anchors** The KEYNOTE-522 OS-exceeding-pCR result and the GeparNuevo dissociation are the two
worked examples [@schmid2024k522os]. {{trial:destiny-b11}} is the counterexample where pCR is
positive and EFS is immature.
