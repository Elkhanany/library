## Thesis

The conceptual core. Placed after biology and before management so every clinical chapter can
assume it. Each chapter must separate what is biology from what is measurement. The measurement
problem is the recurring discipline of this part.

### BC-260 · A framework for heterogeneity

**Carries** the taxonomy and the honest through-line, which is that most measured heterogeneity
is not yet actionable.

**Threads** Interpatient, intratumoral, temporal, and intermetastatic. Linear, branched,
neutral, and punctuated evolution. Fitness landscapes and bottlenecks. The measurement problem.

**Controversy** When heterogeneity is a curiosity and when it changes a decision. This is the
section the rest of the part is judged against. Do not soften it.

### BC-270 · Spatial heterogeneity

**Threads** Regional variation within the primary. Multifocal and multicentric disease.
Receptor and immune variation across space. Sampling limitations.

**Anchors** Breast tumors show higher spatial intratumoral heterogeneity than colon, lung,
brain, or esophageal cancers. Integrated spatial proteomics reveals a paradox worth a figure:
receptor heterogeneity falls with grade while proteomic heterogeneity rises, and the proteomic
measure independently predicts worse disease-free survival [@mardamshina2025]. That dissociation
is the strongest argument in the book that heterogeneity is not one thing.

### BC-280 · HER2 heterogeneity

**Carries** the genetic against protein-level distinction, kept explicit from the first
paragraph to the last. Most published confusion collapses the two, and the chapter's value is
that it does not.

**Threads** Definitions and thresholds, including the ten percent convention. Spatial patterns
described as clustered, mosaic, and scattered. Low-level amplification and its association with
heterogeneity. Amplicon topology and extrachromosomal DNA. Frequency and its dependence on
detection method. Consequences for response and for pCR. HER2 loss as acquired resistance.
Implications for conjugate delivery and the bystander effect. HER2-low reframed. What to do in
clinic on Monday.

**Anchors** HER2 intratumoral heterogeneity occurs in roughly 16 to 40% of HER2-positive
tumors. In the neoadjuvant T-DM1 and pertuzumab trial, pCR was 0% in heterogeneous against 55%
in homogeneous tumors [@filho2021], and larger series confirm an independent negative
predictive effect. HER2 loss is now an established acquired resistance mechanism to
trastuzumab deruxtecan itself, and preclinical models show HER2-high and HER2-low subclonal
cooperation driving conjugate resistance, with USP9X inhibition as a candidate sensitizer
[@goyette2026]. {{trial:daisy}} shows activity across the expression range including IHC 0,
which is what makes the bystander argument clinical rather than theoretical.

**Controversy** Whether heterogeneity should be an eligibility criterion or a stratification
factor, and what threshold would be defensible.

**Figures worth commissioning** The clustered, mosaic, and scattered spatial patterns as one
panel. The 0% against 55% pCR split.

### BC-300 · Temporal heterogeneity and clonal evolution

**Carries** therapy as selective pressure, developed agent class by agent class.

**Threads** Natural history and clonal selection before treatment. Endocrine therapy and ESR1.
CDK4/6 inhibition and cell cycle escape. HER2-directed therapy and loss of dependence.
Conjugate exposure and antigen loss. Immunoediting. Genetic evolution against reversible cell
state change. Convergent and divergent evolution. Acting on evolution.

**Anchors** {{trial:paloma3}} showed RB1 mutations emerging exclusively under palbociclib.
ESR1 mutations rise from 8 to 10% at first line to 33 to 39% by third line. APOBEC3 mutagenesis
generates the resistance hotspots themselves [@gupta2025apobec], which links this chapter back
to [[BC-060]]. {{trial:serena6}} is the proof that acting on evolution before radiographic
progression can help, roughly doubling PFS at 16.8 against 9.2 months [@turner2026serena6].

**Controversy** Whether the SERENA-6 paradigm generalizes beyond ESR1 and camizestrant.

### BC-330 · Dormancy, residual disease, and late recurrence

**Carries** that early and late recurrence are different biological problems.

**Threads** Disseminated tumor cells and where they reside. Quiescence programs. Immune-mediated
dormancy and natural killer cell control. Matrix and niche control. Metabolic control including
NRF2. Escape mechanisms including inflammation, NETs, and aging. Minimal residual disease as a
measurable state. The annual hazard of recurrence.

**Anchors** Build the dormancy machinery on the p38-high and ERK-low axis and NR2F1. Patients
with more than 50% NR2F1-high disseminated tumor cells in marrow did not recur, whereas those
with under 1% recurred within a year [@nr2f1_dtc]. Quantify the HR-positive recurrence hazard
extending beyond 20 years. That number is what makes the word cure hard to use, and it answers
the question raised in [[BC-030]].

### BC-345 · Mechanisms of therapeutic resistance

**Carries** the consolidated reference a reader opens at progression. Pathway detail lives in
Part IV and is cited, never repeated.

**Threads** Intrinsic, adaptive, and acquired as a working taxonomy. Endocrine. CDK4/6.
HER2-directed. Conjugate. PARP and platinum including restoration of repair. Immunotherapy.
Cross-resistance between classes. Distinguishing resistance from under-dosing, non-adherence,
and pseudo-progression. What to measure at progression and what it will change.

**Anchors** Conjugate resistance through payload efflux, antigen loss, and repair capacity is
the least settled section and should be written as such. Cross-resistance among
topoisomerase-I-payload conjugates is an unsolved sequencing problem.
