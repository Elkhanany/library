## Thesis

These populations sit largely outside trial eligibility. The discipline of this part is treating
on principle where evidence is thin, and naming the evidence deficit rather than papering over it.

---

### BC-610 · Young adults, fertility, and pregnancy

**Threads** Adolescent and young adult disease. Fertility preservation before treatment.
Pregnancy-associated and postpartum involutional breast cancer, which draws on [[BC-040]].
Treatment during pregnancy and lactation. Pregnancy after breast cancer. Parenting and
long-horizon survivorship.

**Anchors** {{trial:positive}} supports temporary interruption of endocrine therapy to permit
pregnancy without a short-term increase in recurrence. Note the follow-up duration explicitly,
because the reassurance is short-term by design.

**Cross-refs** [[BC-040]] [[BC-490]] [[BC-650]]

### BC-620 · Older adults and complex comorbidity

**Threads** Geriatric assessment in routine practice. Frailty, function, cognition, and
competing mortality. Treatment adaptation. Polypharmacy. Treating outside trial eligibility.

**Anchors** Geriatric-assessment-guided care reduces toxicity without compromising efficacy
[@gap70], which is the anchor that makes assessment a treatment decision rather than a formality.

**Cross-refs** [[BC-250]] [[BC-420]] [[BC-640]]

### BC-630 · Sex, gender, and uncommon disease contexts

**Threads** Male breast cancer and its evidence deficit. Transgender and gender-diverse patients.
Exogenous hormonal exposure. Occult primary. Bilateral and multiple primaries. Rare malignancies.

**Anchors** Male breast cancer management is largely extrapolated from postmenopausal
HR-positive female disease. Tamoxifen, not aromatase inhibitor monotherapy, is the endocrine
standard, and the reason is mechanistic rather than conventional.

**Controversy** How much of the metastatic and adjuvant evidence base legitimately transfers to
male, pregnant, and very old patients, and the ethics of their systematic trial exclusion.
This belongs here and is reinforced in [[BC-740]].
