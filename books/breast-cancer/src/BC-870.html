Part XII · Metastatic systemic therapy by subtype and line · Chapter 69

Endocrine therapy and receptor degraders in metastatic disease

ESR1 mutation defeats oestrogen deprivation and not receptor degradation, which is why the degrader trials split where they do.

1 · Scope, and the sequence this chapter assumes

This chapter covers endocrine therapy for metastatic hormone receptor-positive, HER2-negative disease. It covers the two backbones, the acquired alteration that separates them, the oral receptor degraders, the first proteolysis-targeting chimera result, and the pathway inhibitors that are added to an endocrine partner.

Three things are deliberately left to neighbouring chapters. CDK4/6 inhibition is in CDK4/6 inhibition in metastatic disease, combinations and sequencing, including which inhibitor to choose and whether to continue it. Conjugates and chemotherapy are in Conjugates and chemotherapy in later-line hormone receptor-positive disease. The clinical framing of the four biomarker branches is in Metastatic hormone receptor-positive, HER2-negative disease, which this chapter expands rather than repeats. Receptor biology is in Estrogen receptor signaling and endocrine resistance and agent pharmacology in Endocrine therapy.

The sequence assumed here is the common one. Most patients arrive having had an adjuvant aromatase inhibitor, then a first-line aromatase inhibitor with a CDK4/6 inhibitor. Almost every endocrine decision in this chapter is therefore made in a tumour already selected by oestrogen deprivation. That is not a detail of ordering. It determines which alterations are present and which drugs still have a target.

The tables are generated from the trial registry when the book is built. Five slices appear below: first-line regimens, the whole field of therapy after progression, the oral degraders inside it, the pathway combinations, and the trials still running. A filter is mechanical, so a wide one catches every trial whose fields match and not only the trials the section is about. Where a section makes a specific argument, its table is filtered on the question rather than on the drug class. A new readout enters the registry once and appears in every table whose filter matches it. The prose does not repeat a figure the table carries, except where the argument is about the figure itself.

2 · Aromatase inhibitors and fulvestrant as backbones

Advanced disease · HR+/HER2- · 1L

Choosing the first-line regimen and the moment to leave it

Which endocrine partner should the first-line inhibitor be given with?

Rationale
An aromatase inhibitor lowers the ligand and leaves the receptor standing. A degrader removes the receptor itself, which should matter more as the receptor rather than the ligand becomes the limiting step. The comparison is an old one. It was asked in a backbone that no longer exists in clinic.
Experiment
Fulvestrant was compared with anastrozole as first-line monotherapy in endocrine-naive advanced disease, first in a phase II and then in a phase III. Two further trials added fulvestrant to anastrozole instead of substituting it. A separate randomised comparison of two fulvestrant doses settled the dose, after the addition trials had already been designed. Three phase III trials now put an oral degrader against an aromatase inhibitor with the same CDK4/6 inhibitor in both arms, which is the substitution question asked in the backbone actually used.
Finding
Substituting fulvestrant for the aromatase inhibitor lengthened progression-free survival in endocrine-naive disease in the phase III. The phase II that preceded it did not meet its own clinical benefit endpoint. Adding fulvestrant to anastrozole gave a gain in one trial and nothing in the other. The three degrader trials with an inhibitor in both arms have not reported.
Limitation
Every reported trial in this comparison used an endocrine backbone with no CDK4/6 inhibitor in it. They answer a question about monotherapy and not about the combination now given. The two addition trials disagree. They also differ in how much adjuvant endocrine therapy their populations had already had, which is an explanation for the disagreement that is not the drug. Both used the lower fulvestrant dose, which the dose comparison later showed was the wrong one. The addition question was therefore asked at a dose nobody now prescribes. The phase II's survival analysis was added by protocol amendment after the progression results were known, which makes it something other than a prespecified comparison.
Next question

Two backbones exist and they fail differently. Oestrogen deprivation removes the ligand, using an aromatase inhibitor with ovarian suppression where ovaries are functioning. Receptor-directed therapy leaves the ligand alone and acts on the protein. That distinction is the argument of this whole chapter, so it is worth stating before any trial.

Only two randomised phase III trials address the backbone question directly. FALCON compared fulvestrant with anastrozole in patients who had never received endocrine therapy1. CONFIRM compared two fulvestrant doses and settled the schedule now in use2. FIRST made the same comparison as FALCON at phase II and at the older 250 mg dose, which is why FALCON rather than FIRST is the trial the backbone argument rests on.

First-line regimens. In each of these the endocrine backbone is present and the comparison is about what is added to or substituted into it.

The FALCON population has largely disappeared. Endocrine therapy-naive advanced disease now means de novo metastatic presentation without prior treatment, or relapse many years after tamoxifen alone. Most patients presenting today carry adjuvant aromatase inhibitor exposure.

The trial is still worth reading for its final result, because the progression-free survival advantage did not become a survival advantage40. The cleanest available comparison of the two backbones says that in an unselected, endocrine-naive population the choice does not change how long a patient lives. A signal persisted in the 208 patients with non-visceral disease, where median overall survival was 65.2 months against 47.8 months, with a hazard ratio of 0.85 and a confidence interval from 0.60 to 1.2040. That is a subgroup with an interval crossing one. Read it as a hypothesis.

The fulvestrant dose question is pharmacology rather than biology. The drug is given intramuscularly, reaches steady state slowly, and needs a day-15 loading dose in the first cycle. CONFIRM established that 250 mg was underdosing2. A patient who received the lower dose historically has not had an adequate trial of the drug.

The practical position is that the backbone is now chosen by two things. The first is what the tumour has already seen. The second is whether an ESR1 mutation is detectable.

3 · ESR1 mutation and what it changes

ESR1 mutation is acquired under aromatase inhibitor exposure and is not found in patients who have never received one. The evidence for that, including how prevalence differs between the adjuvant and metastatic settings, is set out in Endocrine therapy and the emergence of ESR1 alterations and is not repeated here.

Three consequences follow and they should be held separately.

The mechanism is ligand independence. A mutant receptor adopts an active conformation without oestrogen, so lowering oestrogen removes nothing that the tumour still needs. The receptor protein remains present and remains transcriptionally active. A drug that acts on the protein therefore still has a target.

The failure is specific. ESR1 mutation predicts failure of oestrogen deprivation, not failure of endocrine therapy as a class. This is the organising fact of the chapter and it is frequently misread. Patients whose disease carries the alteration are often described as endocrine-resistant and moved to chemotherapy, which discards the one branch where the mechanism and the available drugs line up.

The mutation describes a population rather than a tumour. It is subclonal and frequently polyclonal within a single patient. Its prevalence rises across successive endocrine lines Evolution across successive lines and the cost of each switch.

The measurement side is separable from all of that. Detection is a plasma assay, not a tissue assay. Archival primary tissue will be negative because the selection had not yet happened, so a negative result on an old block carries no information. In plasma, sensitivity at low variant allele fractions is the limiting factor. Disease that sheds little DNA can give a false negative. The respective blind spots of tissue and plasma are developed in Liquid biopsy and longitudinal monitoring.

Caution

A detectable ESR1 mutation is not a marker of general endocrine resistance, and it is not a prognostic curiosity either. Its only established use is narrow and specific. It says that continued oestrogen deprivation has lost its target, and that a receptor-directed agent has not. Reading it as a reason to abandon endocrine therapy inverts the result.

Interplay

This is the one place in the book where the evolutionary argument of Part VI converts cleanly into a prescription. Treatment applies a selection, the selection is convergent at the level of a single gene, the gene is detectable in blood, and a drug class exists whose mechanism survives that particular escape. Every step in that chain is required. Where any one is missing, as with cyclin E1 in CDK4/6 inhibition in metastatic disease, combinations and sequencing or with antigen loss in Antibody-drug conjugates and targeted delivery, the same evolutionary reasoning produces knowledge and no action.

4 · Oral selective estrogen receptor degraders and their biomarker-defined benefit

Advanced disease · HR+/HER2- · later

Choosing the next treatment after CDK4/6 inhibition fails

What should the endocrine partner be once an aromatase inhibitor with a CDK4/6 inhibitor has failed?

Rationale
Oestrogen deprivation selects for ESR1 mutations that hold the receptor in an active conformation with no ligand bound. An aromatase inhibitor cannot reach a receptor that no longer needs its ligand. Fulvestrant can, but only at the exposure an intramuscular injection allows. An orally bioavailable degrader was meant to solve both problems at once.
Experiment
Randomised comparisons of an oral degrader against standard endocrine monotherapy, usually fulvestrant, in disease progressing after endocrine therapy. Three prespecified the ESR1-mutant population, called from plasma, alongside all patients. The third of those also randomised the degrader with and without a CDK4/6 inhibitor. One randomised two doses of a degrader against fulvestrant. Two tested a degrader against endocrine monotherapy with no mutation-defined population at all.
Finding
The first degrader to report lengthened progression-free survival against endocrine monotherapy in all patients, and by more in those whose disease carried an ESR1 mutation. The two later agents that prespecified the mutant population gained there and not in the population unselected for it. The agent randomised at two doses beat fulvestrant at both. The two tested with no mutation-defined population missed their primary endpoint. The degrader given with a CDK4/6 inhibitor beat the same degrader alone.
Limitation
Prior CDK4/6 inhibition was an entry requirement in only two of these trials. Elsewhere it was a stratification factor or nothing at all, which means these trials are not all describing the population this position describes. The comparator throughout is endocrine monotherapy in disease that has just progressed on endocrine therapy, where it performs poorly. That establishes superiority over a weak control rather than a rank order among the degraders, none of which has been compared with another. ESR1 status was read from circulating tumour DNA at each assay's own limit of detection. The mutant and wild-type groups are therefore set by a sequencing threshold rather than by a boundary in the biology. A tumour called wild-type may carry a subclone below it. The dose-ranging trial enrolled only postmenopausal patients and reported at a wider confidence convention than the phase III trials beside it. The trial that added a CDK4/6 inhibitor to a degrader admitted patients who had never had one, so that comparison does not settle the continuation question.
Next question

Caution

The control arm in every one of these trials is endocrine monotherapy, usually fulvestrant, given to patients who have already progressed on endocrine therapy. Median progression-free survival in those control arms runs from roughly two to five months. A hazard ratio measured against a nearly inert comparator establishes that a drug is active. It does not establish where the drug belongs relative to a CDK4/6 inhibitor combination, a pathway inhibitor or a conjugate. No oral degrader has yet shown an overall survival benefit in any of these trials.

Two trials moved the decision earlier by acting on a molecular change rather than a scan. PADA-1 monitored patients on a first-line aromatase inhibitor with palbociclib and, on a rising ESR1 mutation without progression, switched the endocrine partner to fulvestrant34. SERENA-6 used the same trigger and switched to camizestrant while continuing the same inhibitor29. The design argument, and its limits, are developed in Acting on evolution, intermittent dosing, adaptive therapy, pre-emptive switching.

The operational cost of that strategy is easy to underestimate. It requires plasma testing every two to three months in patients who are well and responding. It then commits the service to acting on a molecular result while the scan is still reassuring.

5 · Targeted degradation and the first PROTAC result

A proteolysis-targeting chimera is a bifunctional molecule. One end binds the oestrogen receptor. The other recruits an E3 ubiquitin ligase. Bringing the two together marks the receptor for ubiquitination and proteasomal destruction. The molecule is then released and can act again, so the mechanism is catalytic rather than stoichiometric. A conventional degrader binds the receptor and destabilises the molecule it is bound to.

VERITAC-2 tested that chemistry against fulvestrant in a randomised phase 3 trial. Its split is the teaching case of the chapter. Vepdegestrant significantly prolonged progression-free survival in the ESR1-mutant population. In the full randomised population it did not6.

The comparator is what makes this informative. Fulvestrant is itself a receptor degrader. The result is therefore degrader against degrader, so it cannot be explained by saying that degradation beats antagonism. Two readings remain and the trial does not separate them. Mutant disease may be more dependent on the amount of receptor protein present, so more complete degradation matters more. Or fulvestrant may simply perform worse against a mutant receptor, given its incomplete receptor occupancy at the licensed dose. Both predict the observed split.

The wider point is the one to carry away. Three chemically distinct approaches to the receptor, the intramuscular degrader, the oral degraders and now the chimera, produce the same biomarker-defined pattern. What selects the responding patient is the resistance mechanism, not the chemistry used against it. A new molecule in this class should be expected to reproduce the split until a trial shows otherwise.

Two limits apply as they do to the whole class. No survival data exist. No trial has compared this agent with a CDK4/6 inhibitor combination at the same point in the sequence.

6 · Endocrine therapy with PI3K, AKT and mTOR pathway inhibition

Advanced disease · HR+/HER2- · 1L

Choosing the first-line regimen and the moment to leave it

Should the first-line regimen be chosen by the tumour's pathway alterations rather than given as one combination to everyone?

Rationale
PIK3CA, AKT1 and PTEN alterations mark disease that escapes endocrine control early. A germline BRCA mutation marks a repair defect that a different class exploits. Both have been treated as later-line problems, addressed once the standard first line has failed. If the alteration is already there at presentation, waiting for it to cause progression spends the best line of treatment on a regimen the tumour is equipped to escape.
Experiment
One trial took patients whose disease relapsed on adjuvant endocrine therapy or soon after finishing it and whose tumour carried a PIK3CA mutation. It added a PI3K-alpha inhibitor to palbociclib with fulvestrant against the same doublet with placebo. Trials of AKT inhibition added to an inhibitor with fulvestrant, and of a PARP1-selective inhibitor with a degrader replacing the inhibitor in germline BRCA-mutated disease, are running. The pathway drugs themselves were established after progression, each added to fulvestrant and read out in a marker-defined population.
Finding
Adding the PI3K-alpha inhibitor to the first-line doublet lengthened both progression-free and overall survival in that selected population. The later-line trials of the same pathway established a progression benefit in marker-defined disease without establishing a survival benefit. Selection by anything other than a PIK3CA mutation is so far a design rather than a result.
Limitation
The first-line triplet population was selected twice, by the mutation and by relapse on adjuvant endocrine therapy or shortly after it. It is a narrow and unusually resistant slice of the first line. The result does not transfer to a mutation-positive patient with a long treatment-free interval. No trial has tested it in one. The comparison is a triplet against a doublet, which puts added toxicity into what is being traded. No arm isolates the pathway inhibitor from the cell cycle drug. No arm held the pathway inhibitor back to progression either. First-line intensification has therefore never been compared with the same drug given later, which is the question the deferral trial asked of the CDK4/6 inhibitor.
Next question
Does first-line intensification by biology survive the test that was applied to the CDK4/6 inhibitor, an endpoint that runs across both lines rather than within the first?

Advanced disease · HR+/HER2- · later

Choosing the next treatment after CDK4/6 inhibition fails

Does blocking the PI3K, AKT and mTOR axis restore endocrine sensitivity?

Rationale
This pathway is the best-documented route by which a hormone receptor-positive tumour keeps proliferating while its receptor is blocked. Activating PIK3CA mutations are common at diagnosis and are enriched under treatment. Loss of PTEN and activating AKT1 mutations reach the same node from other directions. Blocking the node should return the cell to depending on oestrogen.
Experiment
Randomised additions of an inhibitor to fulvestrant or exemestane at each level of the pathway: mTOR, PI3K-alpha, AKT, and PI3K with mTOR together. They differ in how the pathway alteration entered the design. One required it for entry. One prespecified the altered patients as a co-primary population inside an unselected trial. One reported a cohort defined by the absence of the alteration. Two ran a CDK4/6 inhibitor alongside, one of them in both arms and one in a third arm. A single-arm cohort took the alpha inhibitor into disease that had already progressed on a CDK4/6 inhibitor. A head-to-head comparison of two alpha inhibitors is running and has not reported.
Finding
The mTOR inhibitor, the alpha-selective inhibitor and the AKT inhibitor each lengthened progression-free survival over the endocrine partner alone. For the alpha-selective and the AKT inhibitors the gain was larger in tumours carrying a pathway alteration than in those without one. The dual PI3K and mTOR inhibitor produced the largest effect of any trial here. It produced it in a cohort selected for the absence of a PIK3CA mutation. Adding the alpha inhibitor to a CDK4/6 inhibitor and fulvestrant in the first line lengthened both progression-free and overall survival against those same two drugs. All of it is bought with hyperglycaemia, rash, stomatitis and diarrhoea, which decide how much of it a patient actually receives.
Limitation
Only two of these trials required progression on a CDK4/6 inhibitor. The mTOR and alpha-inhibitor trials were run before that class was routine. The AKT phase II trial enrolled nobody who had received one. The first-line triplet enrolled patients relapsing on adjuvant endocrine therapy, and its control arm carried a CDK4/6 inhibitor rather than endocrine therapy alone. Whether the genotype was an entry criterion, a co-primary population or a separately randomised cohort differs from trial to trial. That difference, rather than the drug, decides how much of each estimate is selection. Alterations were called on tumour tissue in some trials and on circulating tumour DNA in others. The two do not identify the same patients. The dual inhibitor's result is a wild-type cohort read at short follow-up against a fulvestrant control that did almost nothing, and its mutant cohort has not reported. The single-arm cohort that carried the alpha inhibitor into post-CDK4/6 disease has no control arm. Its report also carries a published expression of concern.
Next question
If an alteration in this pathway is what makes a targeted addition worth giving, what should be done with the other alterations a sequencing report returns at progression?

Pathway inhibition added to an endocrine partner. INAVO120 is a first-line trial and so does not appear in the table above.

SOLAR-1 is the design that made PIK3CA mutation a predictive marker rather than a prognostic one, because it enrolled a marker-negative cohort and reported it23. That argument belongs to trial methodology and is worked through in Biomarker-selected, basket, umbrella, and platform trials. What matters clinically is the consequence. A drug licensed on the strength of a marker-negative comparison is on firmer ground than one licensed on an entry criterion.

CAPItello-291 widened the biomarker to include AKT1 and PTEN alterations, and it enrolled without selecting12. It showed benefit in the whole population as well as in the altered group. That is a weaker basis for a predictive claim than SOLAR-1 provides, for a mechanical reason. The overall population contains the altered patients, so an overall effect is expected whenever the altered subgroup benefits. Separating the marker's predictive value requires the marker-negative analysis, which in this trial was exploratory.

INAVO120 selected the most resistant population in the group, requiring relapse during or within 12 months of adjuvant endocrine therapy, and it used a triplet30. It is the only trial in this section with a significant survival benefit41. Whether that reflects the population, the triplet or the drug cannot be separated from a single trial.

BOLERO-2 predates biomarker selection entirely27. Everolimus with exemestane delayed progression in unselected patients and did not extend life42. It remains useful where sequencing is unavailable and the mechanism is otherwise unaddressed.

In practice

Test for PIK3CA, AKT1 and PTEN alterations at progression on first-line therapy, not later. The result determines three of the four options in this section.

Use plasma first and tissue second. A plasma-negative PIK3CA result in low-burden or low-shedding disease should be followed by tissue before the option is discarded Liquid biopsy and longitudinal monitoring.

Read PTEN carefully. It is usually reported as loss of immunohistochemical staining, which is a different kind of claim from a sequenced mutation and is less reproducible between laboratories.

Plan the metabolic monitoring before the first prescription, not after the first fasting glucose. Hyperglycaemia with a PI3K-alpha inhibitor is an on-target effect and is predictable. The discontinuation rate it produced in SOLAR-1 is in PI3K, AKT, and mTOR directed therapy and its toxicity burden and is the number to hold when consenting a patient.

Ask who else is managing the patient. These agents need a plan for glucose, rash, diarrhoea and stomatitis that survives a weekend, and that plan belongs in the notes rather than in the consultation.

Endocrine and pathway trials that have not reported.

Three trials ask the same question and it is the question that would rewrite this chapter. SERENA-4, VERITAC-3 and persevERA each test an oral receptor degrader against an aromatase inhibitor as the first-line backbone, with palbociclib in both arms. All three enrol without selecting for ESR1 mutation, which is the correct design, because the alteration is rare before treatment. A positive result would move the degraders from salvage to backbone. It would also make the pre-emptive switching strategy redundant, since there would be nothing to switch from.

CAPItello-292 and VIKTORIA-1 ask whether pathway inhibition belongs alongside continued CDK4/6 blockade rather than after it. VIKTORIA-1 randomises separately by PIK3CA status, so it can say something about the marker as well as the drug.

INAVO121 compares two PI3K-alpha inhibitors directly. It is the only trial in this area that will produce a within-class comparison. Within-class comparisons are what the field consistently lacks.

EvoPAR-Breast01 removes first-line CDK4/6 inhibition altogether in germline or somatic BRCA1, BRCA2 or PALB2 disease, replacing it with a PARP1-selective inhibitor and an oral degrader.

Three questions have no trial attached to them and should be named. Nothing compares an oral degrader with a conjugate at the same decision point. Nothing randomises the order in which endocrine-based and conjugate therapy are given, which is the decision made in Conjugates and chemotherapy in later-line hormone receptor-positive disease. And no oral degrader trial is powered for overall survival, so the class may reach routine use without a survival result at all.
