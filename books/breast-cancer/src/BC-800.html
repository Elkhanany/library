Part X · Adjuvant and neoadjuvant therapy by subtype · Chapter 55

Adjuvant CDK4/6 inhibition

Four trials, one class, two answers. The differences are enumerable and not one of them was varied alone.

1 · Scope, and why four trials of one drug class gave different answers

Four randomised trials added a CDK4/6 inhibitor to adjuvant endocrine therapy in hormone receptor-positive, HER2-negative early breast cancer. Two were positive and two were negative. This chapter is about why. It treats that question as answerable in part rather than as a standing mystery.

The unhelpful version of the question is whether the class works. Reading the four as a single class experiment is the most consequential error available here, and Adjuvant CDK4/6 inhibition, patient selection and open questions states the practice consequence in brief. This chapter does the comparison in full, axis by axis.

Three neighbouring chapters carry what is absent from this one. The pharmacology of abemaciclib, ribociclib and palbociclib is in Targeted therapy. Metastatic use, combination and sequencing are in CDK4/6 inhibition in metastatic disease, combinations and sequencing. The endocrine backbone on which every one of these trials was built is in Adjuvant endocrine therapy in hormone receptor-positive disease. It is not a detail, and one of the four trials differs from the others mostly there.

2 · monarchE, its population and the durability question

monarchE enrolled the narrowest population of the four and defined it entirely on clinicopathological features. Four or more positive nodes qualified on their own. One to three nodes qualified with a tumour of 5 cm or more, grade 3 histology, or a central Ki-67 of 20% or above4.

Two structural consequences follow from defining the population that way. Ki-67 entered as an eligibility route rather than as a randomisation stratifier, so the trial cannot say whether Ki-67 predicts benefit from abemaciclib. And the population is defined by anatomy and grade rather than by a multigene assay, so a low recurrence score does not exclude a patient who meets the criteria Clinical and genomic risk assessment.

The durability question is the one that bears on whether two years is the right number. Abemaciclib was given for two years. The natural expectation for a cytostatic agent is that its effect fades once the drug stops. That is not what the follow-up has shown. The hazard ratio for invasive disease-free survival was 0.75 at the preplanned interim analysis and 0.734 at a median follow-up of 76.2 months4,6. The curves have gone on separating after treatment ended.

That pattern is the strongest available argument that two years of cell cycle inhibition does something durable to residual disease rather than holding it still while present. A competing explanation exists and it is not obviously the weaker of the two. The highest-risk patients recur early and are removed from both arms, which flattens the later hazard in a way that mimics carry-over. The trial cannot separate the two. Neither can any other trial in this chapter, because none of them randomised the duration of treatment.

Overall survival is now mature and appears in the table in Cross-trial comparison and what actually differs between the populations. The absolute survival difference is small and the recurrence difference is not, which is the arithmetic worked through in Absolute and relative benefit, and why the difference matters.

3 · NATALEE, a broader population at a lower dose

NATALEE differs from monarchE on three axes at once. Each difference was deliberate and each was defended at the time.

The population is broader at both ends of the risk range. Anatomical stage IIA with nodal involvement qualified, as did node-negative stage IIA with additional risk factors, along with stages IIB and III1. That admits patients monarchE would have excluded, and with them a substantially lower baseline event rate.

The dose is lower than the one licensed for the same drug in advanced disease. Ribociclib was given at 400 mg daily rather than at 600 mg, on a three weeks on and one week off schedule. This is dose optimisation applied before registration rather than after it, which is the argument in Dose optimization and the move away from maximum tolerated dose. A well patient taking a drug for three years has a different tolerability requirement from a patient taking it until progression.

The duration is longer, at three years of ribociclib alongside five years of an aromatase inhibitor.

Exposure is the fact most often left out of the comparison. At the final preplanned invasive disease-free survival analysis, ribociclib had been stopped in 78.3% of patients and only 42.8% had completed the planned three years1. The reported hazard ratio is an intention-to-treat estimate in a trial where fewer than half of the experimental arm finished the experimental treatment.

Overall survival remains immature at the most recent reported follow-up7. The trial has now reported three data cuts with different follow-up. Why absolute rates cannot be compared across those cuts is explained in Adjuvant CDK4/6 inhibition, patient selection and open questions.

4 · PALLAS and PENELOPE-B, and why they were negative

The two negative trials probably failed for different reasons.

PALLAS is the closest thing to a direct contradiction of monarchE. It enrolled stage II to III disease, gave two years of palbociclib alongside physician's-choice endocrine therapy, and found nothing2. Its final analysis came at a median follow-up of 31 months, so its four-year estimates rest on relatively little follow-up beyond the treatment period itself.

PENELOPE-B is not a contradiction at all, because it studied a different disease state. Every patient had residual invasive disease after taxane-containing neoadjuvant chemotherapy, with a clinical-pathological staging score of 3 or more, or 2 with node-positive disease3. That is chemotherapy-refractory disease identifiable in the surgical specimen, a state described in Minimal residual disease as a measurable state. It is not the same population as a woman rendered disease-free by surgery and offered treatment against an invisible residue.

Its endocrine backbone was also the weakest of the four. Only 50.1% received an aromatase inhibitor and only a third of premenopausal women received an LHRH analogue3. A CDK4/6 inhibitor works with endocrine blockade rather than instead of it. A trial in which half the patients took tamoxifen alone is testing a weaker combination than one in which every patient received an aromatase inhibitor. Its duration was also the shortest of the four, at one year.

Caution

A negative trial in a population that differs on four axes cannot tell you which axis mattered. PENELOPE-B is routinely cited as evidence that CDK4/6 inhibition does not work in post-neoadjuvant residual disease. What it shows is that one year of palbociclib on a mixed endocrine backbone did not work in that population.

A woman with residual node-positive disease after neoadjuvant chemotherapy who meets the monarchE nodal criteria sits inside monarchE's enrolled population, because prior neoadjuvant chemotherapy was permitted there4. PENELOPE-B does not remove her from consideration for abemaciclib.

Saying that the trials differed is not an analysis. What follows is what differed, axis by axis, with the trials named on each.

Eligibility risk: monarchE required node-positive disease with an additional high-risk feature. NATALEE admitted stage IIA including selected node-negative tumours. PALLAS took stage II to III with no high-risk qualifier. PENELOPE-B took residual disease after neoadjuvant chemotherapy. The baseline event rate therefore differs substantially across the set, and so does the absolute gain that any given hazard ratio can produce.

Agent: abemaciclib in the trial with mature survival data, ribociclib in the other positive trial, palbociclib in both negative trials. The three differ in relative CDK4 and CDK6 selectivity and in which toxicity limits the dose.

Dose relative to the advanced-disease dose: abemaciclib and palbociclib were given at their full advanced-disease doses. Ribociclib was given at two thirds of its advanced-disease dose.

Schedule: abemaciclib is dosed continuously and twice daily. Ribociclib ran three weeks on and one week off. Palbociclib ran 21 days of every 28 in both of the trials that used it. Continuous dosing means uninterrupted target engagement. The cell cycle resumes during a break, and no trial has tested whether that matters in this setting.

Duration: one year in PENELOPE-B, two in monarchE and PALLAS, three in NATALEE.

Endocrine backbone: NATALEE mandated a nonsteroidal aromatase inhibitor, with goserelin for premenopausal women and for men. PENELOPE-B allowed tamoxifen alone and half the patients took it. monarchE and PALLAS both allowed physician's choice of endocrine agent.

Exposure when the events accrued: this is the axis nobody tabulates. NATALEE's final analysis found 78.3% of patients already off ribociclib and 42.8% having completed three years. PALLAS reported at 31 months of median follow-up against two years of planned treatment.

Disease state: PENELOPE-B's population had been selected by chemotherapy for survival through cytotoxic pressure. Whether that also selects against dependence on cell cycle machinery is unknown. It is biologically plausible and it has never been looked for.

Now the outcomes with their horizons, because a horizon is what a patient is actually choosing between. monarchE delivered 6.5 percentage points of invasive disease-free survival at seven years and 1.8 points of overall survival at seven years, in the highest-risk population enrolled anywhere in this set6. NATALEE delivered 3.1 points of invasive disease-free survival at three years in a broader population, with survival still immature1. Within NATALEE's five-year analysis the absolute difference grew from 2.7 points at three years to 4.5 points at five7. PALLAS delivered minus 0.3 points at four years2.

Those four figures are not comparable with each other, and that is the point rather than a caveat. They are measured at different horizons in populations with different event rates. A hazard ratio near 0.75 in a population with a high seven-year event rate and the same ratio in a lower-risk population describe experiences that differ entirely for the woman taking the drug. Absolute and relative benefit, and why the difference matters gives the general form of that translation.

Caution

No trial varied one axis while holding the others fixed. Nothing in this evidence identifies which variable explains the split, so any confident attribution is an assertion rather than an inference.

The specific assertion to resist is that palbociclib is pharmacologically inferior. That may be true. It is equally compatible with the data that two years of an intermittently dosed agent, on a physician's-choice backbone, analysed at 31 months, was simply a weaker experiment than two years of a continuously dosed agent analysed at 76 months. Those two hypotheses have not been separated. No trial now running would separate them either.

6 · Patient selection in practice

The evidence supports a single rule, applied below to the patients who actually turn up.

In practice

Use the agent in the population in which it was tested, for the duration for which it was tested.

Node-positive high-risk disease meeting the monarchE criteria: two years of abemaciclib, with a mature overall survival benefit6. This is the only adjuvant CDK4/6 indication with a survival result behind it.

Stage IIA to III including node-negative disease with risk factors, not meeting monarchE criteria: three years of ribociclib at 400 mg, with a recurrence benefit and immature survival data1. Say that the survival question is open rather than implying it is answered.

A patient meeting both sets of criteria: there is no randomised comparison. Choose on tolerability, on duration and on what she will actually take, and tell her that this is the basis.

Residual node-positive disease after neoadjuvant chemotherapy meeting monarchE's nodal criteria: abemaciclib is supported, because that patient was eligible for monarchE. PENELOPE-B tested a different drug for a different duration.

Low genomic risk with qualifying anatomy: neither positive trial selected on a multigene assay, so a low recurrence score does not disqualify. Those assays estimate prognosis under endocrine therapy, not benefit from cell cycle inhibition Prognostic and predictive assays and somatic profiling.

A germline BRCA carrier eligible for both olaparib and a CDK4/6 inhibitor: unresolved, and the reasoning is set out in Adjuvant PARP inhibition for germline carriers.

Do not substitute palbociclib on grounds of access or familiarity. In the adjuvant setting it has been tested twice and has failed twice.

Two points belong to the conversation rather than to the algorithm. Adding a CDK4/6 inhibitor does not shorten the endocrine course, which continued for at least five years in both positive trials, so the patient is agreeing to an addition and not a substitution. And the decision is made in a woman who is well, has no measurable disease, and will experience only the toxicity with certainty. Communication and shared decision-making sets the register that conversation needs and the framing that makes it honest.

7 · Toxicity, discontinuation, and the cost of two or three years

The toxicity profiles differ by agent, and in the adjuvant setting that difference is decisive rather than incidental. The agent-level detail belongs to Targeted therapy and is not repeated here.

What is specific to this setting is the arithmetic of discontinuation. NATALEE documented it more precisely than the other three trials did. Only 42.8% of patients allocated to ribociclib completed the planned three years1. That is the adherence under which the reported hazard ratio was generated. It is not a defect in the trial or a reason to discount it. It is the trial telling you what three years of a drug costs a patient who feels well.

Two consequences follow for what you say to a patient. The benefit you quote already incorporates real discontinuation, so it should not be presented as the benefit of completing the course. And a patient who stops at eighteen months has not forfeited the trial's result, because a large fraction of the trial population did something similar.

Dose reduction is designed into one of these regimens from the start. NATALEE's 400 mg is already below the advanced-disease dose, chosen because the tolerability requirement changes when the intent is cure and the horizon is three years Dose optimization and the move away from maximum tolerated dose. Abemaciclib's diarrhoea is early in the course and responds to dose reduction. Dose reduction rather than discontinuation is the intended response to it.

Caution

Monitoring burden is part of the cost and is usually left out of the consent conversation. Ribociclib carries scheduled electrocardiographic and hepatic monitoring. Abemaciclib requires counselling about diarrhoea before the first dose rather than after the first episode.

A well woman is being asked to accept a certain toxicity and a fixed monitoring schedule for a probabilistic benefit. Quote both in the same units and over the same horizon, as Absolute and relative benefit, and why the difference matters requires.

Six questions are open at the time of writing. Only one of them currently has a trial running that could answer it.

Overall survival in NATALEE remains immature7. A null result would be harder to excuse here than in metastatic disease, because the adjuvant setting has no crossover to dilute it Neoadjuvant, adjuvant, and metastatic design compared. That makes the readout informative in both directions, which is unusual.

Substitution rather than addition is the question WSG ADAPTcycle asks. It randomises ribociclib with an aromatase inhibitor against chemotherapy followed by endocrine therapy, in intermediate-risk disease selected by a 21-gene recurrence score and by endocrine response measured as post-endocrine Ki-675. A positive result would move the class from an add-on to a chemotherapy substitute. That would change the conversation in exactly the group where the genomic assays are least decisive Genomic assays and the decision to omit chemotherapy.

Duration has no trial anywhere in the current portfolio. Nothing compares two years against three years of the same agent, and the gap between monarchE and NATALEE confounds duration with agent, dose, schedule and population at the same time.

Agent has no trial either, and a head-to-head comparison of the three drugs is unlikely ever to be run.

Selection by molecular residual disease is untested. Nobody has randomised adjuvant CDK4/6 inhibition on a Molecular residual disease signal. The arguments for and against doing so are in Liquid biopsy and longitudinal monitoring.

Re-treatment is the question a relapsing patient raises first. A woman who recurs after adjuvant abemaciclib has no randomised evidence for first-line CDK4/6 inhibition in metastatic disease, and CDK4/6 inhibition in metastatic disease, combinations and sequencing sets out what is and is not known.

Interplay

Two or three years of cell cycle inhibition applied to undetectable disease is a selection experiment with no readout. The RB1 loss and CCNE1 amplification that define CDK4/6 resistance in metastatic disease CDK4/6 inhibition and the evolution of cell cycle escape are being selected for during the adjuvant years, in a population that cannot be sampled. The durability seen in monarchE after treatment stops is the one observation that argues against pure suppression, because a merely suppressed population should regrow. Whether these years instead change which resistance route is available at relapse is the question CDK4/6 inhibition in metastatic disease, combinations and sequencing will eventually have to answer. No trial in this chapter is collecting the tissue that would answer it.
