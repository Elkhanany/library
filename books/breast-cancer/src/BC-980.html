The only randomised adjuvant PARP inhibitor trial in early breast cancer. Its population spans two subtypes with different entry criteria.

One row, and the population cell is the part to read twice. OlympiA enrolled both hormone receptor-positive and triple-negative disease, with risk criteria that differed between the two1. A hormone receptor-positive carrier entered on stage and nodal count. A triple-negative carrier usually entered on residual disease after neoadjuvant chemotherapy. The trial was not designed to separate them, so the transferable claim is about germline carriers at high risk rather than about either subtype on its own. The triple-negative half of the same argument is in Post-neoadjuvant systemic therapy. Invasive disease-free survival separated first and overall survival followed2.

Two observations matter for the consent conversation and neither is in the table. The separation was in distant disease-free survival as well as in invasive disease-free survival, at 87.5% against 80.4% at three years1. The effect is therefore not carried by second primaries and non-breast events. And olaparib was added after chemotherapy rather than in place of it, so a carrier who declines chemotherapy sits outside this evidence1.

A single row also means a single set of choices that nobody varied. Duration was one year in everyone. The comparator was placebo, not another agent. Eligibility required a germline BRCA1 or BRCA2 variant, so carriers of PALB2 and of other homologous recombination genes are outside the evidence entirely. The mechanism is developed in DNA repair, replication stress, and genomic instability and the agents in Targeted therapy. Germline testing should precede the adjuvant decision rather than follow it, and the testing strategy is in Inherited susceptibility and familial breast cancer.

Caution

Sequencing olaparib with adjuvant CDK4/6 inhibition in a dually eligible patient is unresolved and should be presented that way. OlympiA did not permit a CDK4/6 inhibitor and monarchE did not require germline testing, so neither trial observed the combination or the sequence. Choosing one, choosing the other, or giving them in series are three strategies with no comparative evidence behind any of them. A patient who is offered a definite order should be told that the order is a judgement.

PARP inhibition in advanced disease. Two of these set a single agent against physician's choice chemotherapy. One adds a PARP inhibitor to platinum and a taxane rather than replacing them, and one is the expansion study that tested genes other than BRCA1 and BRCA2.

Two of these four rows ask one question with two drugs. Each set a single oral PARP inhibitor against the physician's choice of single-agent chemotherapy in germline BRCA1 or BRCA2 carriers, with progression-free survival by blinded central review as the primary endpoint. Both favoured the inhibitor and both raised the response rate. Both then failed to separate on overall survival at their final analyses7,8. The agreement runs through the negative half as well as the positive half, which is what makes it a class finding rather than two results.

The third row is a different question wearing the same label. BROCADE3 did not replace chemotherapy with a PARP inhibitor. It added veliparib to carboplatin and paclitaxel and compared that with the same chemotherapy plus placebo3. Its control arm is platinum-based combination treatment rather than a single agent, and its survival result is not on the record. Reading it alongside the other two as though all three tested one strategy is the commonest error made with this table.

TBCRC 048 is single-arm and its subject is the gene list rather than the drug. Confirmed responses appeared in two groups only, germline PALB2 carriers and patients with somatic BRCA1 or BRCA2 mutations. No response occurred with ATM or CHEK2 alterations alone4. Homologous recombination related is therefore not an eligibility category. A panel that reports those genes under one heading invites a decision the data do not support. Repair biology is in DNA repair, replication stress, and genomic instability and resistance in PARP inhibitor and platinum resistance, including restoration of repair.

What no row tests is the comparison the biology most invites. Neither physician's-choice menu included platinum. Both were built from capecitabine, eribulin and vinorelbine, with gemcitabine added in one. A PARP inhibitor has therefore never been randomised against the other drug class that exploits the same repair defect. Nor has one been randomised against endocrine-based therapy, so these trials establish activity in hormone receptor-positive disease without establishing where in the sequence it belongs. The populations were defined by a germline mutation rather than by receptor status, which leaves the hormone receptor-positive patients inside them a subgroup nobody powered.
