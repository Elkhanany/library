Part XII · HER2-positive disease · Chapter 69

Adjuvant and post-neoadjuvant therapy

Why the duration, the second antibody and the conjugate for residual disease are three separate decisions resting on three separate randomisations.

1 · Scope, and the two distinct decisions this chapter serves

This chapter serves two decisions that share a subtype and almost nothing else.

The first belongs to a patient taken straight to surgery. The specimen is out and the pathology is final. No tumour in this patient was ever exposed to a drug under observation. Everything that follows is inferred from stage, nodal status, hormone receptor status, age and comorbidity. Sections The trastuzumab adjuvant trials and the duration question, Dual blockade in the adjuvant setting, De-escalated adjuvant therapy for small node-negative tumours and Extended adjuvant therapy after trastuzumab serve that decision.

The second belongs to a patient who received neoadjuvant therapy and has residual invasive disease at surgery. That tumour has already met HER2-directed therapy and survived it. The selection variable here is observed rather than predicted. Post-neoadjuvant intensification for residual disease carries that decision, and Unresolved questions and what reads out next sets out what is still open in it.

Merging the two decisions is the commonest error in this subtype, and it is usually made in the favourable direction. The adjuvant trials randomised patients after definitive surgery, so their populations contain nobody whose response was known. The post-neoadjuvant trials enrolled only patients whose response was known and inadequate. A hazard ratio from one population does not transfer to the other.

Neighbouring chapters carry the rest, beginning with the choice between surgery first and treatment first in Early disease and Stage and risk-adapted treatment selection. Neoadjuvant regimens are in Neoadjuvant therapy, with hormone receptor-positive HER2-positive disease taking its own algorithm in Early triple-positive disease. Conjugate design, resistance and toxicity are in Antibody-drug conjugates and targeted delivery and are cited here rather than repeated.

Read the tables this way, since each is generated from the trial registry at build time. Each table answers the question of the section that carries it, so a trial recurs where it bears on more than one. The result and overall survival cells are taken from the publication named in the reference column, and a row and its citation therefore always agree. Longer follow-up, subgroup structure and cross-trial comparison are argued in the prose.

Two of the [Chapter Stories](stories.html) run through this chapter, one for each decision. Each section that carries a clinical question opens with it, written as what was being asked, what came back and what the design could not settle. The stories carry no figures at all, so a story and a table can never disagree about a number.

2 · The trastuzumab adjuvant trials and the duration question

Early disease · HER2+ · adjuvant

Matching adjuvant HER2 treatment to baseline risk

Does adding a year of trastuzumab to adjuvant chemotherapy lower recurrence?

Rationale
HER2 amplification marked the fastest-relapsing early breast cancer of the pre-antibody era. The antibody already worked against established metastatic disease. Micrometastatic disease left behind after surgery should be the easier target, being smaller and less heterogeneous than a measurable metastasis.
Experiment
Adjuvant programmes randomised trastuzumab against no trastuzumab on a chemotherapy backbone. They differ in whether the antibody ran concurrently with the taxane or began only after all chemotherapy. They differ again in whether the backbone contained an anthracycline, and in how long the antibody was continued.
Finding
Recurrence fell in every comparison. The separation was still open at the longest follow-up any of them reported. The anthracycline-free backbone gave disease control close to that of the anthracycline backbone, with less cardiac dysfunction.
Limitation
A year of antibody was a protocol convention rather than a tested dose, so these trials establish that the antibody works without establishing how much of it is needed. The trial that compared the antibody against observation allowed its control group to cross over once the first results were public, so its long-term survival comparison is diluted by design. The two trastuzumab backbones in the anthracycline comparison were never set against each other under a non-inferiority design. The absence of a significant difference between them is therefore an absence of evidence and not a demonstration of equivalence. The short-course signal that prompted everything downstream came from a small HER2-positive substudy nested inside a randomisation of two different chemotherapies.

Can adjuvant trastuzumab be shortened below a year without losing disease control?

Rationale
The year was inherited from the pivotal protocols rather than derived from them. It carries cardiac risk, a long course of infusions, and a cost that decides access in much of the world. The nested short course suggested the antibody might do most of its work early.
Experiment
Two trials randomised six months of the antibody against the standard year. Two more randomised a nine-week course against it. Each set its own non-inferiority margin, and not all on the same scale. The pivotal observation trial had already tested the question from the other end by randomising a second year against the first.
Finding
A second year bought nothing over the first. Of the shortened comparisons, only one was declared non-inferior. Both nine-week comparisons pointed against the short course, one of them with a point estimate a long way from equivalence. Cardiac events were fewer on the shorter arms wherever they were counted.
Limitation
These results do not stack. Estimates that look alike did not answer alike. The two six-month trials returned closely similar hazard ratios and reached opposite verdicts. One set its margin as a difference in percentage points of disease-free survival at a fixed time point, and the other set its margin on the hazard ratio scale, so the verdict turns on the scale as much as on the estimate. The chemotherapy partners differ across these trials, as does the point at which the antibody started relative to them. None randomised duration inside a defined risk stratum, so failing non-inferiority in an unselected population says nothing about whether a small node-negative tumour needs the full year.
Next question
If the duration cannot be cut for everyone, can the chemotherapy be cut for the patients whose baseline risk is lowest?

The trials that established adjuvant trastuzumab. Each fixed its duration by protocol rather than by comparison.

Four trials established the class and none of them tested how much of it to give. They agree on the finding and differ on nearly everything used to produce it. Recurrence fell in every comparison, and where follow-up ran longest the curves had not reconverged.

The differences between them are design rather than drug. The antibody ran with the taxane in the American joint analysis and in BCIRG-006. It began only after all chemotherapy had finished in HERA. The backbone carried an anthracycline in the joint analysis and in FinHer, and in two of the three BCIRG-006 arms. HERA asked only that chemotherapy had been given, so its backbone was not one regimen. The antibody ran for a year in three and for nine weeks in the fourth. Each of those choices was written into a protocol rather than randomised, so the table records four conventions alongside its four comparisons.

Three of the four report an overall survival result and they do not carry equal weight. NSABP B-31 and N9831 were analysed jointly against a prespecified number of deaths, on randomisations that stayed intact throughout2. HERA also reported a lower hazard of death against observation, but half of its observation group crossed over to trastuzumab once the first results were public1. Crossover on that scale dilutes a survival comparison by design, and no amount of further follow-up repairs it. HERA is the trial that answers the duration question. The joint analysis is the one whose survival comparison was never diluted.

BCIRG-006 was asked a second question, and its answer is weaker than the way it is usually quoted. Both trastuzumab regimens beat chemotherapy alone. Neither was set against the other under a non-inferiority design, and the trial reports no significant difference between the two3. That is an absence of evidence rather than a demonstration of equivalence. The cardiac case for dropping the anthracycline is stronger than the efficacy case, and it is argued in Cardiac safety across the adjuvant trials.

The short-course exception is worth naming, because everything downstream came out of it. FinHer gave nine weeks of trastuzumab rather than a year, and it found the same kind of recurrence benefit4. That result came from a 232-patient HER2-positive substudy nested inside a randomisation of two different chemotherapies. A signal from a nested substudy is a reason to run a duration trial. It is not a duration result.

Longer is now closed. HERA is the only trial that randomised a second year, and the second year bought nothing on disease-free survival. It cost cardiac function, and that trade is in Cardiac safety across the adjuvant trials.

Shorter is not closed. Four trials tested it and the conclusion in each turns on the margin rather than on the estimate.

The four trials of less than a year. Read the endpoint column before the result column, because the margin is where these trials differ most.

Designing de-escalation trials and the non-inferiority margin problem names four things to establish before accepting any non-inferiority claim. The margin and its justification. Whether a per-protocol analysis accompanied the intention-to-treat one. The observed control event rate against the rate the power calculation assumed. And whether the margin was set on an absolute or a relative scale. Three of the four decide this section.

Start with the two six-month trials, because they are the pair that makes the point. PHARE observed a hazard ratio of 1.08 and did not declare non-inferiority. PERSEPHONE observed 1.07 and did. Two trials of one comparison, in broadly similar populations, reached opposite verdicts on estimates that differ by one hundredth.

The margin separated them, and so did the scale the margin was set on. PHARE set a hazard margin of 1.15. PERSEPHONE set three percentage points of four-year disease-free survival. A fixed allowance in percentage points becomes more permissive the fewer events the control arm has, and events in this population are few. Converted to the scale PHARE used, the allowance PERSEPHONE worked to was the wider of the two by a long way.

Put the two allowances side by side and the verdicts stop contradicting each other. PERSEPHONE established that six months is not worse by more than its own generous allowance. PHARE established that six months has not been shown to be less than 15% worse on the hazard scale. Both statements are true of closely similar data. The conclusion that reached practice was written by whoever set the wider margin.

That is the lesson of Designing de-escalation trials and the non-inferiority margin problem arriving inside a decision made weekly. A margin expressed in percentage points behaves differently from one expressed as a ratio, and the gap between them widens as the control event rate falls. Neither trial did anything improper, and neither result should be described as a mistake. Only one of them published an allowance that a patient would have been asked to accept in advance.

The four shorter trials also differ structurally, and the differences sit upstream of any margin. PHARE randomised patients who were already receiving adjuvant trastuzumab, between the third and sixth month, so its population had already tolerated the first part of the course. PERSEPHONE randomised at the outset, with the antibody given alongside or after chemotherapy. Short-HER used sequential anthracycline and taxane. SOLD gave docetaxel with trastuzumab up front and then fluorouracil, epirubicin and cyclophosphamide. SOLD also reported that patients given the lower docetaxel dose had inferior disease-free survival7. Part of what reads as a duration effect in that trial may be a chemotherapy dose effect, and its design cannot separate the two.

The two nine-week trials are not a matched pair either, and they should not be read as one finding. Short-HER's interval crossed its own margin and also crossed equality, so it is inconclusive in both directions. SOLD's interval excluded equality at a lower bound of 1.12, so its estimate points to harm rather than to absent proof. Failing to demonstrate non-inferiority and demonstrating inferiority are different findings. Only SOLD produced the second, and that distinction should reach the patient being offered the short course.

The third of the four checks is the one these trials make hardest to apply. A margin is justified against an assumed control event rate, so it becomes more or less demanding as the observed rate departs from that assumption. Recurrence in this subtype has fallen steadily as the rest of treatment improved, which makes every margin set a decade ago more permissive than it looked when it was written.

Two things the table cannot show constrain all of this. None of the four randomised duration inside a defined risk stratum. A failed non-inferiority claim in an unselected population therefore says nothing about the small node-negative tumour, which is the commonest candidate for a short course. And none of them gave dual blockade, so no shortened course has ever been tested under two antibodies.

Four trials therefore left three states of knowledge rather than one answer. Six months is acceptable under a generous margin and unproven under a strict one. Nine weeks is unproven in one trial and probably worse in the other.

In practice

One year remains the default. It was fixed by protocol convention rather than chosen by comparison, and four attempts to shorten it have not displaced it.

Six months is defensible where cardiac risk, frailty, distance or cost make twelve unattractive. Say what the supporting trial allowed. Its margin was an absolute difference in disease-free survival at a fixed time point, which in a population with few events is a wide allowance5.

Nine weeks is for a patient who has already had a cardiac event on treatment, or whose baseline risk is very low. One trial of it was inconclusive and the other pointed towards harm8,7.

None of the four duration trials used dual blockade and most predate routine pertuzumab. A duration result obtained under trastuzumab alone does not license a shortened course under two antibodies.

3 · Dual blockade in the adjuvant setting

Early disease · HER2+ · adjuvant

Matching adjuvant HER2 treatment to baseline risk

Does escalating beyond chemotherapy and a year of trastuzumab lower recurrence, and if so for whom?

Rationale
A second antibody binds a different epitope and blocks the dimerisation that trastuzumab leaves intact. Neoadjuvant trials had already shown that the pair raises pathological complete response above the single antibody. A kinase inhibitor offers the same second block from inside the cell, and a conjugate offers to carry the cytotoxic to the target rather than through the patient. The adjuvant question is whether any of that converts into fewer recurrences years later.
Experiment
One trial randomised pertuzumab against placebo on top of chemotherapy and a year of trastuzumab, in node-positive and high-risk node-negative disease. A second tested a different route to a dual block, randomising a kinase inhibitor given with trastuzumab, or sequenced after it, against trastuzumab alone. A third kept pertuzumab running through both arms and replaced the taxane and trastuzumab portion of the regimen with the conjugate.
Finding
The second antibody lowered invasive recurrence, with the gain sitting in the node-positive stratum and nothing to show in node-negative disease. The kinase inhibitor given concurrently fell short of its significance threshold. Sequencing it after trastuzumab added nothing at all. The conjugate substitution did not improve on the taxane arm on either of its co-primary populations.
Limitation
The nodal split is a subgroup gradient inside one randomisation rather than a randomised comparison of two populations. Overall survival in that trial has not separated. The kinase inhibitor's concurrent arm was judged against a threshold tightened after a third arm closed for futility, so the same estimate reads as a narrow miss or as a small real benefit depending on which threshold is applied to it. The conjugate's null result is a failure to improve on an active control and not evidence of an inactive drug. Far fewer patients completed treatment on the conjugate arm, so it compares strategies as delivered rather than drugs as prescribed.

Three ways of adding a second HER2-directed agent after surgery. One worked in one stratum and two did not work at all.

Three trials asked whether more HER2 blockade after surgery lowers recurrence, and each was set against a control arm of chemotherapy with a year of HER2 antibody. Only one moved its endpoint. Reading them as three verdicts on dual blockade misreads them, because they differ in what they added, in what they replaced and in the threshold each was judged against.

APHINITY is the one that worked, and its benefit should be described honestly. The gain sits in the node-positive stratum, and the node-negative stratum shows nothing10,12. That split is the whole clinical content of the trial.

Nodal status is not a subgroup here in the usual weak sense. It was a stratification factor rather than a post-hoc slice, so the strata are balanced on everything randomisation balances. Balance is not the issue. A difference in effect between two strata is not itself a randomised comparison, so it carries less weight than the overall result does.

ALTTO is the instructive failure, and what makes it instructive is arithmetic rather than biology. Its lapatinib-alone arm closed for futility during the trial, and the protocol was then amended to require a p value at or below 0.025 for the two remaining comparisons. The combination against trastuzumab returned 0.04811. The same estimate reads as a narrow miss or as a small real effect depending on which threshold is applied to it. The sequential arm is the cleaner result and it is rarely quoted. Giving lapatinib after trastuzumab rather than with it added nothing, which separates the question of a second block from the question of which second block.

ALTTO carries a second lesson that belongs to the whole subtype. Its drug pair had raised pathological complete response substantially before surgery, and the adjuvant trial then failed in more than eight thousand patients. That sequence is the subject of Dual blockade and what the pathological complete response gains bought and its methodology is in Endpoints, surrogates, and the overall survival question. A response gain measured at one operation predicted nothing about recurrence years later.

KAITLIN tested substitution rather than addition, which is a different question with a different failure mode. Pertuzumab ran in both arms, so KAITLIN is not a test of dual blockade at all. It replaced the taxane and trastuzumab portion of the regimen with a conjugate, and invasive disease-free survival was unchanged in both coprimary populations. Completion of the planned 18 cycles fell from 88.4% to 65.0%, largely through discontinuation for laboratory abnormality9. That is a comparison of strategies as delivered rather than of drugs as prescribed. A regimen that a large share of patients never finish is a different regimen from the one that was designed.

The decision rests on APHINITY alone, because it is the only one of the three that randomised an addition against what the clinic would otherwise give. What the table cannot show is the rest of that decision. Nobody randomised the duration of pertuzumab, which was fixed at a year by the same convention that fixed trastuzumab. Nobody powered a trial on node-negative disease alone, so the absence of benefit there rests on a stratum rather than on a trial. And overall survival has not separated across three interim analyses13.

In practice

Add pertuzumab for node-positive disease. Quote the absolute difference rather than the hazard ratio, because the relative effect flatters it Absolute and relative benefit, and why the difference matters.

Do not add pertuzumab for node-negative disease. The randomised evidence there shows no benefit at all12.

Do not substitute trastuzumab emtansine for the taxane after an anthracycline. It did not improve outcome and far fewer patients completed it9.

Do not read ALTTO as a verdict on pertuzumab. It tested a different second agent against a threshold that moved mid-trial11.

Tell a node-positive patient consenting today that the survival endpoint has not separated. What remains open is set out in Unresolved questions and what reads out next.

4 · De-escalated adjuvant therapy for small node-negative tumours

Early disease · HER2+ · adjuvant

Matching adjuvant HER2 treatment to baseline risk

For a small node-negative HER2-positive tumour, how little chemotherapy is enough?

Rationale
The pivotal trials were built on node-positive and high-risk disease and prescribed an anthracycline followed by a taxane. Small node-negative tumours were largely excluded from them. Those tumours still recur, so they are still treated. They are asked to carry a toxicity that was justified against a much higher baseline risk.
Experiment
One trial gave a single-group adjuvant course of a weekly taxane with trastuzumab in node-negative disease below a size cut-off. A second randomised the antibody-drug conjugate against that same taxane-and-antibody pair, with a protocol-defined composite of clinically relevant toxicity as a co-primary endpoint alongside invasive disease-free survival. From the neoadjuvant side, a third tested whether patients responding early to dual blockade could be spared the taxane altogether.
Finding
Almost everyone given the short taxane-and-antibody course was free of invasive disease at the primary analysis, and the great majority still were at the long-term report. The conjugate arm reached a very low rate of invasive disease of its own. It did not lower the composite toxicity endpoint, which was the comparison that trial was built to make. Dropping the taxane from dual blockade lost pathological response, including among the early responders it was designed to protect.
Limitation
The single-group trial has no control arm, so its outcome measures the population selected into it as much as the regimen given to it. The substitution trial was powered for toxicity rather than for a disease-free survival comparison, and it allocated most of its patients to the conjugate while keeping only a small taxane comparator. Its outcome arms therefore cannot establish equivalence. Its null toxicity result is also a statement about a composite. The totals matched, which is not the same as the two regimens costing a patient the same things. The taxane-omission question was settled on pathological response rather than on recurrence, in hormone receptor-negative disease only, with very few patients in each response group.

Patients with small node-negative tumours were largely excluded from the registration trials16. The regimen used for them was therefore built rather than inherited, and the way it was built constrains what can be claimed for it. Both rows agree that outcomes in this population are excellent. Neither row is a comparison against a full regimen, and the reason differs between them.

APT has no comparator at all, which fixes in advance the ceiling on what it can claim. A single-group trial in a population with a very low event rate can show only that outcomes are excellent. It cannot show that a shorter regimen is equivalent to a longer one, because the longer one is absent from the dataset. No margin was stated and none could have been tested. Ten-year follow-up has since been reported and it does not change that structure17.

ATEMPT was randomised, and only one of its two co-primary endpoints was a comparison. Clinically relevant toxicity was compared between the arms. Invasive disease-free survival was evaluated on the conjugate arm alone, so it was never set against the taxane under a margin. The 3:1 allocation left 114 patients in the comparator arm. An efficacy non-inferiority claim from that arm is unavailable at any margin a clinician would accept Designing de-escalation trials and the non-inferiority margin problem.

Its null toxicity result also needs reading as a statement about a composite. The totals matched. That is not the same as the two regimens costing a patient the same things. Neuropathy and alopecia sit on one side of the trade and hepatic and haematological abnormality on the other14.

De-escalation has been tried once from the other end, in the neoadjuvant setting rather than here. WSG-ADAPT HER2+/HR− asked whether patients responding early to dual blockade could be spared the taxane altogether, and dropping it lost pathological response, including among the early responders it was meant to protect. That result was read on response rather than on recurrence, in hormone receptor-negative disease, with very few patients in each response group. It narrows the question without settling it.

What a real de-escalation trial here would need is worth stating, because it explains why none exists. A margin narrow enough to matter, against a control event rate of a few per cent, demands a sample size that no sponsor will fund for a population this favourable. The gap is therefore structural rather than an oversight.

The honest position is narrow. Twelve weeks of paclitaxel with trastuzumab produces excellent outcomes in node-negative tumours of 3 cm or less, and that is the whole of what has been shown. Trastuzumab emtansine produces excellent outcomes in stage I disease with a different toxicity profile rather than a smaller one. Neither statement is a demonstration of non-inferiority against a full regimen.

Caution

An impressive event-free rate in a low-risk population is not evidence of equivalence. It is mostly evidence that the population was low risk. The rate would also have been high on a longer regimen, and nothing in either trial measures the gap between the two. Treat the phrase "non-inferior" as unavailable here, because no margin was ever set.

In practice

For a node-negative tumour of 3 cm or less, weekly paclitaxel with trastuzumab is the default15. Describe the evidence as a single-arm trial with an excellent rate.

Choose trastuzumab emtansine where neuropathy or alopecia dominates the conversation rather than where total toxicity does, since clinically relevant toxicity was not reduced14.

Neither trial carried an untreated arm, so nothing here says whether a T1a or T1b node-negative tumour needs HER2-directed treatment at all. That decision is extrapolation, and the consultation should say so.

5 · Post-neoadjuvant intensification for residual disease

Early disease · HER2+ · post-neoadjuvant

Escalating treatment on residual disease after HER2 blockade

For residual invasive disease at surgery, does switching to an antibody-drug conjugate lower recurrence more than completing the planned trastuzumab?

Rationale
Residual disease is observed resistance rather than inferred risk. The tumour met trastuzumab and a taxane and was still present at operation. Completing the same antibody asks a drug to finish work it has already been shown not to do in that patient. A conjugate keeps the target and changes the mechanism, by delivering a cytotoxic to a cell the antibody can still find. The selection rule itself was not new when it reached this subtype. Escalating chemotherapy on residual disease had already lowered recurrence in HER2-negative disease.
Experiment
KATHERINE randomised patients with residual invasive disease in the breast or axilla after neoadjuvant taxane-based chemotherapy with trastuzumab to trastuzumab emtansine or to completing trastuzumab, with invasive disease-free survival as the primary endpoint. CREATE-X had tested the same selection rule in HER2-negative residual disease, with capecitabine as the escalation. Beside both sits KAITLIN, which gave the same conjugate with pertuzumab after upfront surgery in place of the taxane. Its patients were selected by nodal status and tumour size rather than by residual disease. It is the same drug asked to work without the selection rule.
Finding
In selected residual disease the conjugate lowered invasive recurrence against continuing the antibody. Follow-up ran long enough for that recurrence benefit to become an overall survival benefit, which is uncommon in this setting. The selection rule had already held outside the subtype, where post-neoadjuvant capecitabine lowered recurrence and lengthened survival in HER2-negative residual disease. Where the rule was absent the conjugate did nothing. In the adjuvant population it matched the taxane with dual antibody on invasive disease-free survival. Fewer patients completed the planned course.
Limitation

An anthracycline was optional. Most patients were treated before pertuzumab was routine. Residual disease in the trial therefore followed a weaker regimen than residual disease in clinic today. Rationale
The first conjugate carries a payload that stays inside the cell it enters. It therefore kills only cells that bind and internalise enough antibody. Residual disease after HER2 blockade is the compartment most likely to carry reduced or patchy antigen. A single-arm neoadjuvant study had already reported no responses at all to the non-bystander conjugate where HER2 was spatially heterogeneous.
Experiment
DESTINY-Breast05 randomised trastuzumab deruxtecan against trastuzumab emtansine in residual invasive disease, with entry restricted to node-positive disease at surgery or inoperable disease at diagnosis. The comparator was the established conjugate rather than the antibody. This is the first trial in this position whose control arm was already known to work. Finding
The newer conjugate lowered invasive recurrence against an active comparator. Drug-related interstitial lung disease was far more frequent than on the older conjugate. Recurrence was the endpoint that reported. Overall survival was immature at the primary analysis. A pulmonary toxicity that killed patients is therefore being weighed against a recurrence endpoint whose survival counterpart has not yet read out. The enrolled population was also narrower than that of the trial it displaced, because residual disease with negative nodes at surgery was not admitted. Read the entry criteria against each other rather than the results. KATHERINE admitted any residual invasive disease in the breast or axilla. Early disease · HER2+ · adjuvant

Matching adjuvant HER2 treatment to baseline risk

Does extending HER2-directed treatment beyond the first year add anything?

Rationale
Recurrences keep arriving after the antibody stops, and in hormone receptor-positive disease they arrive late. Whether more time on treatment helps at all had already been put to the antibody itself. An irreversible pan-HER kinase inhibitor acts inside the cell and covers signalling the antibody leaves intact, including the crosstalk with the oestrogen receptor that is one proposed route of escape.
Experiment
The pivotal observation trial had randomised a second year of the antibody against one. The extension trial randomised a further year of the kinase inhibitor against placebo in patients who had completed trastuzumab-based adjuvant therapy, stratified by hormone receptor status, with invasive disease-free survival read at a fixed early landmark as the primary endpoint.
Finding
A second year of the antibody itself added nothing over the first. The kinase inhibitor lowered invasive recurrence inside that early window. Severe diarrhoea was far more common than on placebo. By the final analysis the two arms had the same overall survival.
Limitation
An early landmark is a short window for the very group the drug is now aimed at, whose recurrences come late, so the primary result was read before the population it selects for had time to relapse. Hormone receptor status was a stratification factor and not a randomised comparison of the strata. The trial enrolled before adjuvant pertuzumab and before conjugate treatment for residual disease, so almost nobody in it had received the treatment a candidate would receive today. No antidiarrhoeal strategy was part of the randomisation, so the tolerability on record is the tolerability of the drug given without one.

7 · Cardiac safety across the adjuvant trials

The two cardiac toxicities in this chapter behave differently and the mechanisms are set out in Cardiac surveillance and cumulative toxicity. Surveillance schedules and the management of asymptomatic decline are in Treatment toxicity and supportive care and Cardio-oncology, surveillance and prevention. This section adds only what the adjuvant trials themselves settle.

The duration trials are the cleanest cardiac dataset in breast oncology, because they randomised exposure time rather than observing it. HERA randomised a second year, and secondary cardiac endpoints occurred in 7.3% of the two-year group, 4.4% of the one-year group and 0.9% of the observation group1. PERSEPHONE randomised half the exposure and early discontinuation for cardiotoxicity fell from 8% to 3%5. Short-HER randomised nine weeks and reported a cardiac event risk ratio of 0.33 for the short course8. SOLD reported better maintenance of ejection fraction with nine weeks7.

Read those four results together. Trastuzumab cardiotoxicity is usually described as idiosyncratic and reversible, in contrast to the dose-dependent anthracycline injury. The duration trials show that exposure time behaves as a dose. That is a randomised dose-response relationship, and it is the reason a shortened course is a genuine cardiac intervention rather than a concession.

It also changes how the margin argument in The trastuzumab adjuvant trials and the duration question should be used, and it is the one place in this chapter where a de-escalation can be defended on positive evidence. A patient with real cardiac risk is not choosing between equal options under an uncertain margin. They are trading an unproven loss of disease control against a randomised reduction in cardiac events. The two sides of that trade are known to different standards, and the consultation should say which is which.

The anthracycline is the other lever and it was tested directly. In BCIRG-006, congestive heart failure and cardiac dysfunction were significantly more frequent with the anthracycline and trastuzumab regimen than with docetaxel, carboplatin and trastuzumab. Seven of the eight reported leukaemias arose in anthracycline-containing groups3.

In practice

Treat planned trastuzumab duration as a modifiable cardiac exposure in a patient with cardiac risk factors. The evidence that shorter means safer is randomised in four trials.

Treat anthracycline omission as the larger cardiac decision. BCIRG-006 found no significant difference in disease-free survival between its two trastuzumab regimens, and clearly less cardiac toxicity without the anthracycline3. The efficacy side of that trade is an absence of difference rather than a demonstration of equivalence.

Measure ejection fraction before the anthracycline and again at the end of chemotherapy, and concentrate surveillance in the first year Cardiac surveillance and cumulative toxicity.

A fall on HER2-directed therapy warrants holding the drug and reassessing. A fall after an anthracycline is treated rather than observed.

Early disease · HER2+ · post-neoadjuvant

Escalating treatment on residual disease after HER2 blockade

Does adding a second agent to the established post-neoadjuvant conjugate lower recurrence further?

Rationale
Once systemic control improves in this subtype, a large share of first relapses arrive in the brain. A conjugate is a poor drug for that site. The obvious partner is therefore the small molecule that has intracranial activity in metastatic disease. Residual disease has also survived in an intact immune context, which is the separate argument for adding a checkpoint antibody.
Experiment
Two randomised trials add an agent to the established conjugate in residual disease. CompassHER2 RD adds tucatinib against placebo, with entry restricted to oestrogen receptor-negative or node-positive disease. ASTEFANIA adds atezolizumab against placebo in high-risk residual disease. The case for each partner was made elsewhere. HER2CLIMB tested tucatinib in metastatic disease already treated with the antibodies and the conjugate. It enrolled patients with brain metastases. IMpassion050 tested a checkpoint antibody added to preoperative chemotherapy and dual blockade.
Finding
Neither addition has reported. The only publication on the record for the immunotherapy trial is its design protocol. The enrolment figure attached to that record is a plan rather than a count of patients randomised. The borrowed evidence points in two directions. Tucatinib lengthened progression-free and overall survival in previously treated metastatic disease. It also delayed progression in the patients with brain metastases. The checkpoint antibody did not raise pathological complete response before surgery in this subtype. It did not do so in the tumours selected for PD-L1 expression either.
Limitation
Both trials were designed when the established conjugate was the only post-neoadjuvant option. A positive result will therefore show that an addition improves on a backbone that a newer conjugate has already beaten in node-positive residual disease. No trial compares those two strategies against each other. The tucatinib evidence also comes from disease that had already progressed on the conjugate in the metastatic setting, which is not the same biology as disease left behind at surgery. The checkpoint evidence comes from before surgery and reports a response endpoint. It constrains the immunotherapy question without settling it.

Does a stronger preoperative regimen remove the population this whole strategy was built for?

Rationale
Every question in this position presupposes residual disease at surgery. The preoperative regimen decides how many patients arrive with it and which ones they are. It also decides which drugs remain unused, because a conjugate spent before surgery is not available after it.
Experiment
Three trials move that boundary in different directions. DESTINY-Breast11 randomised a conjugate given alone, or followed by the taxane with dual antibody, against dose-dense anthracycline chemotherapy followed by the same triplet. CompassHER2 pCR gave a chemotherapy-light preoperative regimen in a single arm and omitted further chemotherapy after a pathological complete response. KRISTINE substituted the older conjugate with pertuzumab for chemotherapy altogether.
Finding
The conjugate sequence raised pathological complete response against the anthracycline standard, which shrinks the residual-disease population. The chemotherapy-light regimen left most patients with residual disease. Those patients were predominantly oestrogen receptor-positive. Substituting the conjugate for chemotherapy lowered pathological complete response and enlarged the residual-disease population. Acute toxicity on that arm was much lower. Recurrence on follow-up was nonetheless more frequent than on chemotherapy. The substitution cost more than it saved.
Limitation
All three moved a surrogate first. Pathological complete response is the endpoint that reports early. Event-free survival in DESTINY-Breast11 was immature at the primary report. Enrolment in its conjugate-alone arm also closed early. What survives from it is a comparison of two sequences rather than of a conjugate against chemotherapy. The response-adapted trial has not reported its recurrence endpoint at all. Nothing has randomised what to do about residual disease that persisted through a preoperative conjugate. Every post-neoadjuvant trial in this position enrolled tumours that survived an antibody and a taxane. A tumour that survives trastuzumab deruxtecan before surgery is a different population with no evidence behind it.

Four questions have no trial addressing them at all.

Whether the DESTINY-Breast05 benefit extends to node-negative residual disease. The trial excluded that group and there is no plan to study it separately.

Whether trastuzumab deruxtecan, or trastuzumab emtansine with tucatinib, is the better choice for a patient at risk of brain metastasis. No randomised trial exists or is planned to make that comparison.

Whether a shortened trastuzumab course is acceptable under dual blockade. Every duration trial predates routine pertuzumab, and the cardiac argument for shortening is strongest in exactly the patients who now receive two antibodies.

Whether molecular residual disease can select post-neoadjuvant intensification better than pathology can. The assay question is in Molecular residual disease, tumor-informed and tumor-naive approaches and the lead-time problem in Lead time and the unresolved question of whether acting early helps.

One older question remains open on its own terms. Overall survival in APHINITY has not crossed its boundary12,13. The trial continues, and a node-positive patient consenting to pertuzumab today is consenting on a recurrence benefit rather than a survival one.

Interplay

Both decisions in this chapter are attempts to act on a tumour that is no longer in the body. The adjuvant decision infers risk from a resected specimen. The post-neoadjuvant decision infers it from what a specimen looked like after treatment. Part VI argues that a single specimen misrepresents a heterogeneous tumour, and residual disease is where that argument bites hardest. The surviving compartment after HER2 blockade need not carry the antigen level the diagnostic biopsy reported HER2 loss as an acquired resistance mechanism. That is one reason a bystander-capable conjugate outperforms a non-bystander one in exactly this population, and the mechanism is in Bystander killing and why it matters in heterogeneous tumors rather than here.
