Part XI · Metastatic disease: principles and cross-cutting management · Chapter 66

Organ-specific metastases and complications

Which organ-specific decisions rest on a randomised comparison, and which rest on custom.

1 · Brain metastases, screening, systemic activity, and local therapy

The central nervous system is the one compartment this book gives a chapter of its own. Central nervous system metastatic disease carries the local therapy, the systemic therapy, the delivery argument underneath both, and the endpoints these trials report. This section says only why the compartment is separated out, and what a reader of an organ-specific chapter has to carry away.

Three things make it behave unlike bone, liver or lung.

Most of the randomised evidence came from radiation oncology, in trials of brain metastases from solid tumours generally. A breast-specific estimate out of those trials is a subgroup at best.

The registration trials of the modern systemic agents excluded untreated or progressing brain metastases by protocol. The claim that a large molecule cannot reach the compartment was therefore assumed rather than tested. Nobody enrolled the patients who would have tested it.

The endpoints are different objects. An intracranial response rate, a central nervous system progression-free rate at a fixed timepoint and a response by neuro-oncology criteria are routinely lined up in one sentence. They are not comparable, and what separates them is mostly who was admitted to each study Reading an intracranial response rate, and what the cohort contributes to it.

Two practical points belong here rather than there. Routine imaging of the asymptomatic brain is not supported by randomised evidence in any subtype, and that should be said rather than implied. And the lesion in the brain may not carry the target the primary carried, because acquired ERBB2 amplification and intrinsic subtype switching are both described in resected brain metastases1.

Why the brain is colonised at all belongs to Metastatic dissemination and organ tropism. The subtype distribution is in2 and the colonisation biology in3,4.

2 · Leptomeningeal disease

Leptomeningeal disease is a second compartment inside the first, and it is not the same problem as parenchymal disease. The diagnosis, the drugs and the evidence behind them are in Leptomeningeal disease and whether delivery has to be intrathecal.

Two points belong in an organ-specific chapter. The first is that cytology is highly specific and insensitive. A negative result in a patient with characteristic symptoms and characteristic imaging does not exclude the diagnosis, and should not be allowed to.

The second is the compartment point, and it is the one to carry away. Cerebrospinal fluid is separated from blood, which means that plasma circulating tumour DNA does not report what is in it. A patient with undetectable plasma can have detectable disease in the cerebrospinal fluid, so the two assays answer different questions rather than the same question with different sensitivity Liquid biopsy and longitudinal monitoring. Cerebrospinal fluid circulating tumour DNA improves on cytology where it is available, and the technical account is in Cerebrospinal fluid analysis in leptomeningeal disease5.

For most patients here the goal is neurological function and symptom control rather than length of life. Saying so at the outset makes every later decision easier Palliative and end-of-life care.

3 · Bone metastases and skeletal-related events

Bone is the commonest first site of distant relapse in hormone receptor-positive disease2. The colonisation programme behind that is described in Bone, liver, lung, and brain microenvironments compared and in6.

The endpoint these trials share deserves inspection before any of their results are quoted. A skeletal-related event is a composite of pathological fracture, radiation to bone, surgery to bone and spinal cord compression. Two of those four are interventions rather than events. The composite therefore measures clinical behaviour as well as disease behaviour. That does not invalidate the endpoint. It does mean the absolute rates travel badly between health systems with different referral thresholds.

Bone-modifying agents in established bone metastasis. One trial compares the two drugs and two compare schedules of the same drug. The adjuvant bone trials ask a different question and sit elsewhere.

Every trial in that table randomised one active bone-modifying schedule against another. That shared design is the finding about the class. Where the two agents met head to head, neither survival nor disease progression separated them, and the two interval trials varied the spacing of one drug rather than testing it against nothing. Nothing here says that a bone-modifying agent alters the course of the disease outside the skeleton. These are supportive drugs with a supportive-care justification, however oncological they look on a prescription.

Where the three diverge is endpoint and population rather than drug. Denosumab versus zoledronic acid measured time to a first skeletal event and tested it for superiority9. The two interval trials measured the proportion of patients reaching at least one event inside a fixed window, and tested that against a non-inferiority margin their investigators chose in advance. A delay in a first event and a proportion with any event by two years are different objects, and the second carries a judgement about what size of difference would matter. Neither design was capable of producing the other's answer.

The agent question rests on the head-to-head, the only randomised comparison of the two drugs against each other. It makes the choice between them a choice between toxicity profiles rather than between disease outcomes. Renal adverse events and acute-phase reactions were more frequent with zoledronic acid. Hypocalcaemia was more frequent with denosumab, which is why calcium is checked after starting it.

The interval question was asked twice, at different moments in a patient's course. CALGB 70604 (Alliance) randomised the interval from the start of treatment and for the whole two years, in three cancers enrolled together7. OPTIMIZE-2 randomised it in breast cancer alone, and only after nine or more monthly doses had already been given8. Choosing a twelve weekly interval at the outset and stepping down to it after a year are different decisions. Only the first is addressed by CALGB 70604, which makes that trial the one the common practice rests on, and only the second by OPTIMIZE-2. The schedules did not separate within the breast cancer stratum of CALGB 70604, which is reassuring rather than powered.

Bone turnover was less completely suppressed on the longer interval in both. That is an assay result, and the same trials counted events, which did not follow it. A pharmacodynamic marker that fails to track the clinical endpoint in its own trial is not a reason to shorten the interval.

Three things the table cannot show. None of these trials was built around a comparison with no bone-modifying agent at all, so none of them establishes that the class beats nothing in a patient who already has bone metastases. Every interval result is a zoledronic acid result. No randomised trial has tested a longer denosumab interval, so the twelve weekly habit does not transfer across the class. And the composite gives a radiotherapy referral the same weight as a fracture.

The adjuvant literature makes a different claim and is regularly quoted as though it made the same one. Adjuvant bisphosphonates reduce bone recurrence and breast cancer mortality in postmenopausal women10. That is prevention of bone metastasis in patients who do not have it. It says nothing about the treatment of established bone disease Treatment toxicity and supportive care, and the two bodies of evidence should not be merged.

Bone is also the hardest compartment in which to call a response. Sclerotic change in a healing lytic lesion reads as a new finding on computed tomography, and tracer uptake on scintigraphy reflects osteoblastic activity rather than tumour cell number Response assessment and patterns of progression.

Caution

None of these trials randomised when to stop. Entry required established bone metastasis and the randomised comparisons ran for one or two years. The evidence therefore covers the beginning of treatment and says nothing about a patient five years into a bone-modifying agent.

The interval result has the same boundary. Non-inferiority measured over two years is not a statement about year six, and the osteonecrosis risk that argues for stopping accumulates with exposure rather than resetting each year.

In practice

Arrange dental assessment before starting a bone-modifying agent, and give calcium and vitamin D alongside it.

Monitor calcium after starting denosumab. Hypocalcaemia is the toxicity that differs between the two agents9.

A twelve weekly schedule is a reasonable default for zoledronic acid. It reduces visits without a measured cost in skeletal events over two years7,8. Do not carry the schedule across to denosumab, which has never been randomised at a longer interval.

Treat a painful bone deposit with radiation, and refer an impending fracture in a weight-bearing bone for stabilisation before it breaks rather than after.

Record why the agent is still being given at each annual review. No trial covers the decision to continue indefinitely, so it should at least be a decision.

4 · Liver and lung disease

Liver disease is what most often converts a chronic illness into an acute one. The variable that matters is function rather than volume. A patient with extensive parenchymal replacement and normal synthetic function has a different set of options from one with similar imaging and a rising bilirubin. Drug clearance changes with the second and not with the first.

Visceral crisis is defined clinically rather than radiologically, as rapidly progressive visceral involvement with organ dysfunction that requires a rapid response. It is the one situation in hormone receptor-positive disease where chemotherapy has customarily displaced endocrine and targeted therapy.

That custom has been tested. The question those trials were built to answer is argued in Rapid progression and visceral crisis, and the trials themselves are tabulated in CDK4/6 inhibition in metastatic disease, combinations and sequencing, where the first-line record belongs.

RIGHT Choice is the one that reaches this section, and its result needs stating carefully. It enrolled pre- and perimenopausal women with clinically aggressive disease. Of its 222 patients, 106 were recorded as being in visceral crisis by their own investigator rather than by an adjudicated protocol definition11. That is a measurement problem before it is a biological one, because investigator judgement is exactly the thing a clinic is trying to calibrate. What the result establishes is that the reflex to chemotherapy is wrong more often than it was assumed to be. It does not establish that it is never right, and it does not mark the threshold at which it becomes right.

The practical form of that is a question about time rather than about drug class. Median time to response in RIGHT Choice was 3.2 months with combination chemotherapy and 4.9 months with ribociclib and endocrine therapy. Chemotherapy is the faster of the two. Neither is fast. Where organ function is failing over days, the ordering of those two medians is the whole decision, and where it is declining over months it is not.

Two limits sit on that trial and neither is a criticism of its conduct. It is a randomised phase 2 with an open-label investigator's choice control, and overall survival has not been reported, so the trade has been measured on progression-free survival alone. And the patient in true visceral crisis with failing organs is excluded from trials of this kind by performance status and organ function criteria. That decision will not acquire a randomised answer, which is the reason for naming it as a judgement rather than dressing it as evidence.

Lung parenchymal metastases are usually asymptomatic until they are extensive, with a tempo slower than liver disease of comparable burden. The decision is therefore rarely forced by the lung itself.

Lymphangitic carcinomatosis is a separate problem with a separate tempo, and it is the one to recognise early. Breathlessness out of proportion to the parenchymal findings, with septal thickening on imaging, is attributed first to infection and second to treatment-related pneumonitis. In a patient receiving a deruxtecan-based conjugate that second possibility is urgent, because drug-related interstitial lung disease in this class carries real mortality12. The distinction changes what happens in the next 24 hours and cannot be made on a chest radiograph.

Why particular organs are colonised is developed in Metastatic dissemination and organ tropism, with the lung programme described in13 and the vesicle-based account of preferential seeding in14.

5 · Malignant effusions and chest wall disease

A malignant pleural effusion has three management options, and the choice between them turns on prognosis and on preference rather than on efficacy in relieving the symptom.

The two randomised comparisons of an indwelling pleural catheter against talc pleurodesis. They chose different primary endpoints, which is why they read as disagreeing and do not.

Both trials randomised the same two strategies against each other, and they agree on more than they are usually credited with. Breathlessness improved on both arms in both trials. Both found that a catheter reduces the need for a further pleural procedure. Both found more adverse events with the catheter. None of that is in dispute.

They read as disagreeing because they set different primary endpoints. TIME2 set a daily breathlessness score over the weeks immediately after the procedure16. AMPLE set total days in hospital out to death or twelve months15. A short-horizon symptom score and a year-long count of hospital days cannot contradict each other. The divergence is the design, not the device.

That also decides which trial answers which question. TIME2 is the one that settles relief of breathlessness, and it settles it as equivalence. AMPLE is the one the choice actually rests on. Time out of hospital is the only axis on which the two strategies separate, and AMPLE measured it over a horizon long enough for the difference to accumulate.

TIME2's late breathlessness difference in favour of the catheter is the single result that looks like a device effect, and it should be read with care. It was a secondary comparison made months after randomisation, in the patients still alive to complete a score rather than in the population that was randomised.

This is therefore a preference-sensitive decision rather than an efficacy one, and it should be offered as such. A patient with months to live and a strong wish for time out of hospital chooses differently from one expecting a longer course.

Three things the table cannot supply. Neither trial enrolled breast cancer alone, so no breast-specific estimate exists for either endpoint. Neither randomised a catheter against repeated therapeutic aspiration, which is what a frail patient often actually receives. And neither reports what managing a drain at home costs the person who does the draining, which is the burden the catheter moves out of the hospital and onto the household.

Pericardial effusion is dangerous for physiological reasons rather than volumetric ones. Tamponade depends on the rate of accumulation and on pericardial compliance. A small rapidly accumulating effusion is more dangerous than a large slow one. Echocardiography answers the question that a computed tomography report describing a moderate effusion does not.

Malignant ascites is managed by paracentesis, and by an indwelling catheter where drainage is repeated, on the same logic as the pleural space. That logic is an analogy rather than a result. Neither space has been randomised, and the table above holds nothing outside the pleura.

Chest wall disease produces a symptom burden disproportionate to its extent and disproportionate to the attention it receives. Ulceration, exudate, odour, bleeding and pain occur together and each needs specific management. Radiation and systemic therapy address the tumour. The wound needs dressing selection, odour control and bleeding management, and that is a specialist skill. Referring it to tissue viability or wound care services is not an admission that oncology has failed. Treating it as one leaves a manageable problem unmanaged.

6 · Obstructive complications and oncologic emergencies

Metastatic spinal cord compression is the emergency in this disease where hours change the outcome. The variable predicting function afterwards is function beforehand.

The two randomised trials of what to do about a compressed cord. Both enrolled metastatic cancer of any primary site, and breast cancer was a minority of each.

The two trials there are not two answers to one question. One asked whether to add decompressive surgery to radiotherapy. The other asked how many fractions of radiotherapy to give. They share a population, metastatic cancer of any primary site, and they share an endpoint expressed as walking. They constrain each other in nothing else, and reading them as agreeing or disagreeing is a category error.

Patchell decompressive surgery trial is why a surgical opinion is sought before radiotherapy alone is accepted18. It is load-bearing because it randomised the addition against the thing every such patient was otherwise going to get. Two limits belong with it, and both are about how far the result travels. The trial enrolled metastatic cancer generally rather than breast cancer. It also stopped early at a prespecified interim analysis, which tends to inflate the estimated effect.

SCORAD asked the narrower question, and the way it failed is the instructive part. A single fraction missed its non-inferiority margin for walking at week eight, while meeting that margin at weeks one, four and twelve17. A result that holds before a timepoint and again after it is more likely to be a property of the timepoint than of the dose. The explanation is attrition. A large part of the randomised population had died before the week eight assessment, so the primary endpoint was measured in the survivors. Its survival column points the same way. The two schedules did not separate on survival, which is what makes the choice of schedule a logistical decision rather than an oncological one.

Three things neither trial can tell a breast oncologist. Neither enrolled breast cancer alone, so the estimate a reader of this chapter wants does not exist. The patient with hormone receptor-positive or HER2-positive disease and years of expected survival is under-represented in both. Nobody randomised the modern operation, because separation surgery followed by stereotactic radiotherapy has not been compared with either arm of either trial. And nobody randomised corticosteroid dose or the interval from symptom to scan, which are the two variables a physician actually controls in the first hour.

Ambulatory status at presentation carries the same warning. It is the strongest predictor of ambulatory status afterwards, and it is a baseline variable rather than a randomised one. No treatment choice can improve it. Only a shorter delay can preserve it.

Superior vena cava obstruction in breast cancer is usually subacute rather than immediately life-threatening. Endovascular stenting relieves it faster than radiation does, and the two are commonly combined. The immediate priorities are airway and cerebral oedema rather than the caval anatomy itself.

Hypercalcaemia of malignancy is treated with rehydration and a bone-modifying agent. Its appearance is also information, because it frequently marks a change in disease behaviour, so it should prompt reassessment rather than only correction.

Biliary and ureteric obstruction present the same structural question. The decision to stent is a decision about what follows the stent. A stent placed without a subsequent systemic plan buys a laboratory value rather than time.

In practice

New back pain with any neurological sign in a patient with known metastatic disease is imaged the same day, with magnetic resonance of the whole spine rather than the symptomatic level alone.

Start corticosteroids while arranging the imaging, not after it.

Obtain a surgical opinion for cord compression before settling on radiotherapy alone, particularly where the patient is still walking or the spine is unstable18.

Choose the radiotherapy schedule from the expected survival. A single fraction is reasonable where that is short, and the eight-week evidence for it is weakest exactly there17.

Record ambulatory status at presentation. It is the strongest predictor of ambulatory status afterwards and it is the number most often missing from the notes.

7 · Local intervention in the metastatic setting

Local treatment in metastatic disease is given for three different reasons, and they should never be argued as one.

Symptom control. A painful bone deposit, a bleeding chest wall, an obstructing lesion. The justification is the symptom, and no survival claim is required or implied.

Structural protection. An impending fracture, a threatened cord, a compromised airway. The justification is the event being prevented Obstructive complications and oncologic emergencies.

Disease control intent. The oligometastatic and oligoprogressive arguments, where local treatment is given in the hope of changing the course of the disease.

Only the third rationale needs a randomised test, and only the third has had one.

The randomised tests of local treatment given with disease-control intent. One treated the primary tumour and one treated the metastases. Only the first was conducted in breast cancer.

Both trials asked the same question in form. Does local treatment given for disease control change overall survival, against continuing systemic therapy alone? They differ in what was treated, in who was enrolled, and above all in the standard of proof each set itself. That last difference is the one that matters, and it is a matter of design rather than a disagreement about whether ablating a deposit helps anybody.

EA2108 is the clearer of the two, and it contradicts the stronger intuition. Women presenting with metastatic disease and an intact primary received systemic therapy for four to eight months. Those without progression were then randomised to locoregional treatment of the primary or to continuing systemic therapy alone19. Removing the primary improved local control while improving neither survival nor quality of life. Local control and survival are separate outcomes, and improving the first delivered neither of the others.

That design is what makes it load-bearing. It is the only one of the two conducted in breast cancer. It randomised the comparison a clinic is actually tempted by. And the run-in placed the intuition in its strongest possible position. Randomising only the patients whose disease had not progressed selects for systemic control, which is the setting in which clearing a residual primary should pay if it ever does. It did not pay there either.

SABR-COMET points the other way and is much the weaker design. It was a randomised phase 2 screening study with its significance threshold set at 0.20, against the conventional standard a phase 3 trial holds itself to20. A threshold of that kind is built for deciding what to test next rather than for deciding what to do on Monday. Breast cancer was one of four common primaries in it and a minority of the enrolment, so the breast-specific evidence inside it is thinner than the headline suggests.

There is a measurement problem underneath it as well. Entry required a countable number of lesions, all of them treatable by ablation. That count is a property of the scanner and of the reporting convention as much as of the disease. Randomisation balances indolent biology between the arms and it does nothing to make the result transferable to a patient whose deposits were merely counted differently on a more sensitive study.

Three comparisons nobody has randomised follow from that table. Ablation of metastases has never been tested in breast cancer alone. The patients who progressed during that run-in were never randomised, so the situation in which a primary is left behind in advancing disease is untested. And nobody has randomised local treatment of an oligoprogressive site at all.

Oligoprogression is the one situation where a disease-control rationale rests on a coherent biological argument. A single progressing deposit while everything else responds suggests a resistant subclone in one place, which is the divergence picture in Convergent and divergent evolution and what each implies for sequencing. Treating that site and continuing the systemic regimen follows from the inference. It has still not been shown to extend survival Oligometastatic and oligoprogressive disease.

Interplay

Organ-specific management is where heterogeneity stops being a property of a tumour and becomes a property of a patient. Different deposits sit in different microenvironments. They are exposed to different drug concentrations and have taken different evolutionary routes Convergent and divergent evolution and what each implies for sequencing. A bone deposit is held in a signalling programme the liver does not supply Bone, liver, lung, and brain microenvironments compared. A brain metastasis can carry an acquired ERBB2 amplification that the primary did not1, and the cerebrospinal fluid compartment is invisible to the assay used to monitor everything else5. Mixed response is therefore an expected finding rather than an anomaly. A treatment decision made on the behaviour of the largest lesion is a decision about that lesion. This chapter is the clinical form of the argument that a patient with metastatic breast cancer has several related diseases in several places.
