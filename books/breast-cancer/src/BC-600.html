Organ-specific metastases and complications

Where the disease stops being one problem in several places and becomes several problems in one patient.

1 · Brain metastases, screening, systemic activity, and local therapy

Screening asymptomatic patients is not supported by randomised evidence, and that should be stated rather than implied. The argument for it is that smaller lesions are more amenable to radiosurgery and less likely to require whole brain treatment. The argument against is lead time without benefit, together with the cascade of treating lesions that would never have caused symptoms. Neither argument has been tested against survival in this disease. The subtype distribution and the colonisation biology behind it belong to Metastatic dissemination and organ tropism and are not restated1,2,3.

Systemic activity against intracranial disease is established and it changed practice. In HER2CLIMB, 291 patients had brain metastases at baseline, 198 in the tucatinib arm and 93 in the control arm. The hazard ratio for intracranial progression or death was 0.32, with a 95% confidence interval of 0.22 to 0.48, and median central nervous system progression-free survival was 9.9 months against 4.2 months. The hazard ratio for death was 0.58, with an interval of 0.40 to 0.85, and median overall survival was 18.1 months against 12.0 months. The confirmed intracranial response rate was 47.3% against 20.0%4,5.

Central nervous system disease · HER2+ · any

Reaching the central nervous system and reading what comes back

HER2-positive disease sends more patients to the brain than any other subtype, in part because the drugs that hold the body have been better than the ones that reach the head. For years the registration trials excluded brain metastases that were untreated or progressing. So the belief that a large molecule cannot cross was assumed rather than tested, and the questions in this cell run on two tracks at once: whether the compartment can be reached, and whether the endpoints used to report it mean what they appear to mean.

Does adding a brain-penetrant HER2 tyrosine kinase inhibitor to systemic treatment control brain metastases that are still active?

Rationale
The compartment was treated as radiation's problem, on the argument that a full-length antibody does not cross an intact blood-brain barrier. The argument was never tested, because trials excluded patients whose brain disease was untreated or progressing. An earlier single-arm study had already paired a tyrosine kinase inhibitor with capecitabine in brain metastases that had never been irradiated, and reported volumetric intracranial responses. That made a randomised version of the question worth asking.
Experiment
Tucatinib was randomised against placebo on a trastuzumab and capecitabine backbone in pretreated disease, and entry was opened to patients whose brain metastases were untreated or progressing after local therapy. Intracranial progression and intracranial response in that group were specified in advance rather than found afterwards. The same drug was then randomised on a conjugate backbone and as first-line maintenance, in populations that admitted brain metastases without requiring them.
Finding
Adding tucatinib lengthened the time to intracranial progression as well as the time to systemic progression. It improved overall survival in the trial as a whole. The direction of effect in the brain metastasis group followed the direction in the whole trial rather than diverging from it.
Limitation
This is a group within a systemic trial, not a trial whose entry required brain disease. The randomisation protects the drug comparison and not the choice of a central nervous system strategy. The control arm carried capecitabine, which has intracranial activity of its own, so the contrast is against an active backbone rather than against local therapy or against nothing. Entry followed trastuzumab, pertuzumab and a conjugate, so the result is silent about the same question asked earlier in the disease. The two later tucatinib trials admitted brain metastases without requiring them, so neither carries a central nervous system population that stands on its own.

Can an antibody-drug conjugate produce intracranial responses when its antibody is too large to cross an intact barrier?

Rationale
The size argument applies to an intact barrier. A metastasis large enough to measure on imaging has vasculature that is already disrupted, so the question is not whether the antibody crosses healthy brain but whether it reaches tumour. A conjugate also has only to deliver payload, and a membrane-permeable payload can act on cells the antibody never bound.
Experiment
Single-arm studies of conjugates in patients with brain metastases, entered by the state of the brain disease rather than by line of treatment. Trastuzumab deruxtecan was given in small cohorts defined as stable after local therapy, untreated or progressing, and then in a large brain metastasis cohort studied alongside a cohort without brain disease. Trastuzumab emtansine was reported from the brain metastasis subgroup of a single-arm safety study, and was also given with neratinib in successive cohorts.
Finding
Intracranial responses with the deruxtecan conjugate are routine rather than anecdotal, in active disease as well as in disease already treated and stable. In the largest cohort the time to intracranial progression was close to the time to systemic progression in the patients who had no brain metastases at all. The emtansine conjugate produced intracranial responses too, at a lower rate than the deruxtecan conjugate in cohorts that are not otherwise comparable.
Limitation
None of these studies has a control arm, so each is measured against what its cohort was expected to do. The central nervous system endpoints are not the same object: a progression-free rate at a fixed timepoint, an intracranial response by neuro-oncology criteria and an intracranial response by solid tumour criteria cannot be read off against one another, and the fixed timepoints are not the same timepoint either. The largest of these studies performed no formal hypothesis testing. The emtansine result is a post hoc subgroup of a safety study rather than a prespecified endpoint. The first-line conjugate module has only a design on the record, so nothing here speaks to untreated disease.

Does an intracranial response rate mean the same thing in a radiotherapy-naive cohort as in one that has progressed after radiotherapy?

Rationale
A response rate is read as a property of the drug. It is at least as much a property of who was admitted and of how a lesion was measured. A previously irradiated lesion has been selected for resistance to treatment, and it is also harder to measure, because treatment effect, necrosis and tumour resemble each other on imaging.
Experiment
Studies that split their population by central nervous system state on purpose and reported the strata separately. One regimen was given to a radiotherapy-naive cohort and to a post-radiotherapy cohort within the same trial. Another split its cohorts by whether disease was stable after local therapy, untreated or progressing. A third required progression after radiotherapy for entry, and the earliest of them required that no brain radiotherapy had been given at all.
Finding
Within a single regimen, intracranial response is higher in the radiotherapy-naive stratum than in the stratum that progressed after radiotherapy. Across trials, the ranking of intracranial response rates tracks how permissive the entry criteria were more closely than it tracks which drug was given.
Limitation
Prior radiotherapy is not a clean variable. It marks lesions selected for radioresistance, and it marks lesions that are harder to measure, and no design here separates the two. No trial randomised the order of radiotherapy and systemic treatment, so what a radiotherapy-naive strategy costs the patient, if anything, is unmeasured. Reading these strata as a ranking of drugs is the error that this grouping exists to prevent.

Does giving a HER2-directed drug concurrently with brain radiotherapy improve intracranial control over radiotherapy alone?

Rationale
The compartment is being irradiated in any case. A HER2 inhibitor that reaches it could act as a radiosensitiser. That would buy local control without adding a modality and without waiting for the drug to work on its own.
Experiment
Whole brain radiotherapy or radiosurgery was randomised with or without concurrent lapatinib in HER2-positive brain metastases, with intracranial complete response at an early fixed timepoint as the endpoint. A dose-finding study is now testing the same idea with a more brain-penetrant inhibitor alongside radiosurgery.
Finding
Concurrent lapatinib did not improve intracranial complete response over radiotherapy alone. Objective response at the same timepoint was not improved either. Complete responses were seen only in the arm that received radiotherapy alone.
Limitation
A complete response rate at an early fixed timepoint is a radiographic surrogate chosen because it reads out quickly. It is not a measure of how long the compartment stays controlled, and it is the endpoint most sensitive to post-radiotherapy imaging change. Lapatinib is also the weakest brain-penetrant member of its class, and the same class given without radiotherapy did change intracranial outcomes, so this is evidence against one drug on one schedule rather than against concurrency. The study that would test the concept with a better drug is a dose-finding protocol with no outcomes on the record, so it cannot settle efficacy whatever it eventually reports.

Is intracranial failure a failure of drug delivery, and does engineering for penetration fix it?

Rationale
The standing explanation for central nervous system relapse is that drug does not reach the compartment. That explanation makes a prediction. Raising exposure, building a carrier that crosses, or choosing a molecule that crosses by design should convert patients who do not respond into patients who do.
Experiment
Four treatment studies came at that prediction from different directions, and one measurement study tested its premise directly. Trastuzumab was given at high dose with pertuzumab in brain metastases progressing after radiotherapy, and again with a checkpoint inhibitor added, on the argument that exposure was the limiting variable. Paclitaxel was conjugated to a peptide that uses a receptor-mediated transport route across the barrier, and everolimus, which crosses, was added to trastuzumab and vinorelbine in progressive brain metastases under a staged design built to separate a useless intracranial response rate from a useful one. Separately, a window study measured a conjugate and its payload in resected brain metastasis tissue.
Finding
Drug is measurable in resected brain metastasis tissue, at the concentration the study had set out to demonstrate. None of the four treatment studies met its preset intracranial response rule, and two closed early on that basis. Reaching the compartment is necessary, and on this evidence it is not sufficient.
Limitation
The window study measured concentration and not benefit. Its lesions were operable and therefore large, it enrolled a glioblastoma cohort alongside the breast one, and no design here connects tissue concentration to response. The peptide conjugate produced an investigator-read and an independently-read intracranial response rate that differ, and they have to be read separately rather than averaged. The everolimus study reported response on the patients evaluable for efficacy rather than on everyone enrolled, and its correlative claim that pathway mutations did not predict response rests on very few responders. The high-dose antibody studies stopped at a preset threshold, which is a judgement about that dose and that schedule rather than about the hypothesis.

In leptomeningeal HER2-positive disease, does the drug have to be put into the compartment?

Rationale
The leptomeninges are not the parenchyma. There is no bulky lesion with disrupted vasculature to exploit. Disease is read from cerebrospinal fluid cytology and from neurological examination rather than from target lesions. Survival is short enough that the choice of endpoint constrains the choice of design before any drug is chosen.
Experiment
Two routes, tested separately and never against each other. Systemically, tucatinib with trastuzumab and capecitabine, and trastuzumab deruxtecan, were each given as a single arm with overall survival against a prespecified historical control as the endpoint. By the compartment-directed route, dose-finding and phase II studies of intrathecal trastuzumab established a tolerated dose and reported neurological progression-free survival, and the only randomised test of the principle added intrathecal liposomal cytarabine to systemic therapy in breast cancer of any subtype.
Finding
Both routes report survival longer than the historical expectation each was written against. Intrathecal trastuzumab is tolerable and produces cerebrospinal fluid and neurological responses. The randomised intrathecal comparison lengthened leptomeningeal progression-free survival without a matching gain in overall survival.
Limitation
A prespecified historical control is a fixed number taken from an era before brain-penetrant HER2 therapy, so a cohort enrolled now beats it partly by being enrolled now, and the HER2-positive leptomeningeal cohorts here are very small. The intrathecal studies do not agree on a dose or a schedule, one of them admitted HER2-positive tumours of any origin rather than breast alone, and their endpoints are different objects: a maximum tolerated dose, a cerebrospinal fluid response and a neurological progression-free survival cannot be pooled into a response rate. The randomised evidence for the intrathecal route comes from a drug that is not HER2-directed in a population not selected by subtype, so it supports the principle of compartment-directed treatment and not the use of intrathecal trastuzumab. The peptide conjugate's leptomeningeal group is a subset of a parenchymal study compared against historical survival, so it sits beside these single arms rather than inside them.

DESTINY-Breast12 reports intracranial activity for trastuzumab deruxtecan in 263 patients with stable or active brain metastases, of whom 58.9% were free of central nervous system progression at 12 months6. That trial is single-arm, and the reason a rate and a between-arm difference are not comparable is set out in Central nervous system active strategies rather than repeated here.

Local therapy is where the trade-off is explicit. In a randomised trial of 213 patients with one to three brain metastases, cognitive deterioration at three months occurred in 63.5% after radiosurgery alone against 91.7% after radiosurgery with whole brain radiotherapy7. Time to intracranial failure was shorter with radiosurgery alone, with a hazard ratio of 3.6 and an interval of 2.2 to 5.9. Median overall survival was 10.4 months against 7.4 months, with a hazard ratio of 1.02 and an interval of 0.75 to 1.38.

Read that as a statement about what is being offered rather than about which treatment is better. Adding whole brain radiotherapy buys intracranial control and costs cognition, with no survival difference. Which side of the trade a patient wants depends on how long they are likely to live and on what they use their cognition for.

In practice

Image the brain when there are symptoms, and say explicitly that routine screening is not evidence-based rather than letting the patient assume it is.

For one to three lesions amenable to radiosurgery, treat with radiosurgery and follow with imaging rather than adding whole brain radiotherapy by default7.

Biopsy a solitary brain lesion when the diagnosis is not certain. Acquired ERBB2 amplification and intrinsic subtype switching are both described in brain metastases8, so the target in the brain may differ from the target in the primary.

Do not abandon an effective systemic regimen for an isolated intracranial progression that local treatment can address Oligometastatic and oligoprogressive disease.

2 · Leptomeningeal disease

Leptomeningeal disease is under-diagnosed because the test that confirms it is insensitive. Cerebrospinal fluid cytology is highly specific and misses a substantial proportion of cases on a single sample. Repeated large-volume sampling raises the yield. A negative cytology in a patient with characteristic symptoms and characteristic imaging does not exclude the diagnosis and should not be allowed to.

Imaging is magnetic resonance of brain and whole spine with contrast, performed before lumbar puncture where possible, because the puncture itself produces meningeal enhancement.

Central nervous system disease · all · any

The central nervous system as a compartment

The brain is the one site where the question is not only which drug to give but whether it arrives, and where the treatment that controls the disease can cost the patient the faculties she was being treated to keep. The population here is defined by the compartment rather than by the tumour, so most of the randomised evidence was generated by radiation oncology in brain metastases of any primary cancer, while the breast-specific work is almost all single-arm. Two arguments run through the position. One is what local treatment does to cognition, and the other is whether anything given into the blood or into the cerebrospinal fluid changes the course of disease behind a barrier built to exclude it.

When a limited number of brain metastases has already been treated focally, does adding whole-brain radiotherapy earn what it costs?

Rationale
Deposits exist beyond what MRI resolves, so irradiating the whole brain should postpone the appearance of new lesions and the neurological death that follows them. The hippocampus and the rest of the normal brain take the same dose as the deposits, so whatever is bought in intracranial control is paid for in cognition. That trade has to be measured rather than assumed.
Experiment
Randomised comparisons that held the focal treatment constant and varied the whole-brain component. Whole-brain radiotherapy against observation after complete resection or radiosurgery, radiosurgery alone against radiosurgery plus whole-brain treatment with a cognitive primary endpoint, cavity radiosurgery against whole-brain treatment after resection, cavity radiosurgery against observation after resection, and whole-brain treatment with or without a radiosurgical boost. Alongside these, a prospective cohort asked whether the number of lesions treated with radiosurgery alone changes survival.
Finding
Whole-brain radiotherapy lowers relapse both at the treated site and at new sites, and it lengthens neither survival nor the time to loss of performance status. It worsens cognition against focal treatment alone, whether it follows resection or radiosurgery. Leaving the resection cavity untreated is a separate question and costs local control, so the case is against irradiating the whole brain rather than against irradiating anything.
Limitation
Every one of these trials enrolled brain metastases from solid tumours generally and was powered on that mixed population, so a breast-specific estimate is a subgroup at best. Cognitive deterioration is a scoring convention rather than a clinical event. One trial called it a decline beyond a set margin on any single test in a battery and another used a reliability index, and both scored only the patients still well enough to be tested, which is an increasingly selected group as the disease progresses. The survival advantage for a radiosurgical boost lives in a single-lesion subgroup on univariate analysis of a trial that was negative overall. The lesion-count comparison used to justify radiosurgery for several metastases comes from a prospective observational cohort compared against itself, not from a randomisation.
Next question

If whole-brain radiotherapy has to be given, can its cognitive cost be engineered down?

Rationale
The memory decline that follows whole-brain treatment is attributed to the dose delivered to the hippocampal stem cell niche and to a vascular and glutamatergic injury that a receptor antagonist might blunt. Neither mechanism requires the dose to the metastases to change, so both are modifiable without giving up intracranial control.
Experiment
One randomised trial gave memantine or placebo through and after whole-brain radiotherapy, with delayed recall at a fixed timepoint as the primary measure. A later trial held memantine constant in both arms and varied the plan instead, conformally avoiding the hippocampi against conventional whole-brain treatment.
Finding
Avoiding the hippocampi lowers the risk of cognitive failure against conventional whole-brain treatment given on the same memantine backbone, with survival and intracranial progression unchanged. The memantine trial favoured the drug on its own primary measure without crossing its threshold for significance, and a secondary measure of time to cognitive decline did favour it.
Limitation
The memantine result is an attrition failure and not a demonstration of no effect, since only a minority of those randomised were analysable at the endpoint and the report states its own power as far below what the design called for. That matters for how the later trial reads, because its control arm already contained memantine on evidence that never met its own threshold, so the avoidance question was asked on a backbone the field had adopted rather than proved. Both trials scored cognition by a change index on a test battery, which is a measurement convention, and neither enrolled breast cancer specifically.

Does adding a systemic drug to brain radiotherapy improve intracranial control?

Rationale
Radiation and a drug act on the same lesion at the same time, and a drug that reaches the brain at all should reach it best while the barrier is disrupted by the tumour and by the radiation itself. Radiosensitisation would also let the delivered dose do more without raising it.
Experiment
Randomised trials that held brain radiotherapy constant and added a drug. Concurrent lapatinib in HER2-positive disease, concurrent temozolomide, and induction bevacizumab with etoposide and cisplatin given before whole-brain treatment rather than with it. A single-arm study paired radiosurgery with a checkpoint antibody to ask whether the combination was safe rather than whether it worked.
Finding
No randomised addition improved intracranial response. Concurrent lapatinib produced no complete responses at the radiological timepoint and no gain in objective response. Temozolomide did not raise response above whole-brain treatment alone. The induction arm separated from radiotherapy alone on brain-specific progression only against a significance level the trial had set unusually permissive, and its early brain response was no better than the control arm's.
Limitation
The induction trial declared its result against a prespecified alpha far looser than convention, and its interval for brain progression or death included no difference, so it is a signal-seeking result and not a positive trial. The response endpoints were read on MRI at a fixed early timepoint, where radiation change and residual tumour are hard to separate, which is a measurement problem rather than a biological one. The radiosurgery and checkpoint study was tiny, single-arm, sized for safety and reported only as a long-term follow-up, so its local control has no comparator. The tucatinib and radiosurgery study on the record is a protocol with no outcomes, and it asserts nothing about efficacy.

Is exclusion by the blood-brain barrier the reason systemic drugs fail in the brain?

Rationale
The standard explanation for intracranial progression during effective systemic treatment is exposure, in that the drug holds disease everywhere it reaches and the brain is the place it does not. If exposure is the whole story, a molecule engineered to cross, or a drug chosen for its penetration, should convert into intracranial response.
Experiment
Three different ways of testing that claim. A window study gave an antibody-drug conjugate before craniotomy and measured the payload recovered from the resected metastasis, making delivery itself the endpoint. A peptide conjugate built to cross by receptor-mediated transcytosis was given to patients with recurrent brain metastases. Two further studies took drugs already chosen for brain penetration, a CDK4/6 inhibitor in hormone receptor-positive disease and an mTOR inhibitor added to HER2 blockade with chemotherapy, and asked for intracranial response.
Finding
Delivery and activity came apart. The window study met its concentration endpoint in resected brain metastases. The transcytosis conjugate, the CDK4/6 inhibitor and the mTOR combination each missed the intracranial response rule they had set themselves in advance.
Limitation
A drug concentration in resected tissue is a pharmacological surrogate with no clinical outcome attached, and the tissue came only from patients selected for being operable. The transcytosis conjugate was read at two different response rates by investigators and by independent review, and those should be read separately rather than merged, with its preset rule assessed on the independent read. The mTOR study's response rests on the smaller group evaluable for efficacy rather than on everyone enrolled, and its correlative work found no pathway alteration that marked a responder, so no biomarker-defined subgroup survives in which the rationale still holds.

Can systemic therapy alone change the course of leptomeningeal disease?

Rationale
Leptomeningeal involvement has been treated as a terminal event of the compartment, and the trials that established the modern systemic agents excluded it by protocol. If a drug reaches the meningeal deposits at all, survival counted in weeks might become survival counted in months without any intrathecal or radiation treatment at all.
Experiment
Two single-arm studies enrolled leptomeningeal disease specifically and set overall survival as the primary endpoint against a prespecified historical median. One gave tucatinib with trastuzumab and capecitabine in HER2-positive disease. The other gave trastuzumab deruxtecan in a cohort that admitted HER2-positive and HER2-low disease. A leptomeningeal group within the brain-penetrant conjugate study was reported alongside its parenchymal population.
Finding
Both prespecified survival endpoints were met against their historical comparators, and the leptomeningeal group within the conjugate study was reported as surviving longer than its own historical expectation while the parenchymal primary endpoint of that same study was negative.
Limitation
A historical median is the weakest comparator available here, and the medians on offer were assembled before the systemic drugs these patients had already received existed, so the comparison contains an era effect as well as a treatment effect. Both cohorts are very small, entry required fitness for a trial, and diagnosis rested on cytology or on MRI depending on the protocol, which is a case definition rather than a biological boundary. The conjugate's leptomeningeal group is a subset of an enrolled population rather than a separately powered cohort, so its survival claim is a subgroup measured against history.

Does treating the leptomeningeal compartment directly add to treating the patient systemically?

Rationale
Cerebrospinal fluid is a space that can be dosed on its own, either by placing a drug in it or by irradiating the whole neuraxis. Both approaches take the exposure problem at face value. Both accept a toxicity confined to the compartment in exchange for an exposure that systemic dosing cannot reach.
Experiment
One randomised trial added intrathecal liposomal cytarabine to whatever systemic therapy the patient was already receiving and left that systemic choice to the treating clinician. Three single-arm studies gave intrathecal trastuzumab in HER2-positive leptomeningeal disease, two as dose-finding and one with a neurological progression endpoint. A randomised study compared proton craniospinal irradiation against photon radiotherapy to the involved field, with a phase III successor now open.
Finding
Adding intrathecal cytarabine lengthened leptomeningeal progression-free survival against systemic therapy alone. Intrathecal trastuzumab proved deliverable, and most patients treated at the recommended dose were free of neurological progression at the early timepoint, with responses in a minority. Craniospinal proton irradiation lengthened central nervous system progression-free survival against involved-field photon treatment.
Limitation
The randomised intrathecal trial measured progression in the compartment it was treating and allowed systemic therapy to vary from patient to patient, so the endpoint favours the intervention by construction and the background treatment is uncontrolled. The two intrathecal trastuzumab programmes arrived at different recommended doses and different schedules, which is a property of their escalation rules and their definitions of dose-limiting toxicity rather than of the drug, and one of them admitted HER2-positive tumours of any origin rather than breast cancer alone. The proton trial randomised breast and lung cancer together, stopped early at a planned interim analysis, and compared treatment of the whole neuraxis against a field that by definition did not cover disease outside it, so part of the separation is the field size and not the particle. Its confirmatory trial has no result on the record and asserts nothing yet.

Cerebrospinal fluid circulating tumour DNA improves on cytology. It increases diagnostic accuracy and permits therapeutic monitoring in breast cancer leptomeningeal metastasis9, and the technical account is in Cerebrospinal fluid analysis in leptomeningeal disease.

The compartment point is the one to carry away. Cerebrospinal fluid is separated from blood, and plasma circulating tumour DNA does not report what is in it. A patient with undetectable plasma can have detectable disease in the cerebrospinal fluid, so the two assays answer different questions rather than the same question with different sensitivity Liquid biopsy and longitudinal monitoring.

Treatment options are intrathecal chemotherapy, focal radiation to symptomatic or bulky sites, and systemic agents that reach the compartment. Randomised evidence is scarce and one recent trial is worth knowing. Proton craniospinal irradiation was compared with photon involved-field radiotherapy in patients with leptomeningeal metastasis from non-small-cell lung cancer and breast cancer, with 42 assigned to craniospinal irradiation and 21 to involved-field treatment10. Median central nervous system progression-free survival was 7.5 months against 2.3 months. Median overall survival was 9.9 months against 6.0 months, with P equal to 0.029. Rates of grade 3 and 4 treatment-related adverse events did not differ significantly.

Two limits belong with that result. The trial enrolled two diseases and randomised 63 patients in total, and proton craniospinal irradiation is available in few centres.

3 · Bone metastases and skeletal-related events

Bone is the commonest first site of distant relapse in hormone receptor-positive disease1, and the colonisation programme is described in Bone, liver, lung, and brain microenvironments compared and11.

The endpoint measured by the trials in this area deserves inspection before their results are quoted. A skeletal-related event is a composite of pathological fracture, radiation to bone, surgery to bone and spinal cord compression. Two of those four are interventions rather than events, so the composite partly measures clinical behaviour rather than disease behaviour. That does not invalidate it and it does affect how the numbers transfer between health systems.

Denosumab was compared with zoledronic acid in a double-blind trial of patients with breast cancer and bone metastases, with 1026 assigned to denosumab and 1020 to zoledronic acid12. Denosumab delayed the time to a first skeletal-related event, with a hazard ratio of 0.82 and a 95% confidence interval of 0.71 to 0.95. For first and subsequent events the rate ratio was 0.77, with an interval of 0.66 to 0.89. Overall survival and disease progression did not differ. Renal adverse events and acute-phase reactions were more frequent with zoledronic acid, and hypocalcaemia with denosumab. Osteonecrosis of the jaw occurred in 2.0% and 1.4% respectively, a difference that was not significant.

Dosing interval was tested separately. Zoledronic acid every 12 weeks was non-inferior to every 4 weeks for the proportion of patients experiencing at least one skeletal-related event within two years, at 28.6% against 29.5%. That trial randomised 1822 patients with breast cancer, prostate cancer or multiple myeloma, of whom 855 had breast cancer13. Suppression of bone turnover was less complete on the longer interval, which is a pharmacodynamic difference without a clinical one over two years.

The adjuvant literature makes a different claim and is regularly quoted as though it made the same one. Adjuvant bisphosphonates reduce bone recurrence and breast cancer mortality in postmenopausal women14. That is prevention of bone metastasis in patients who do not have it. It says nothing about the treatment of established bone disease, and the two bodies of evidence should not be merged.

In practice

Arrange dental assessment before starting a bone-modifying agent, and give calcium and vitamin D alongside it.

Treat painful bone metastases with radiation, and refer an impending fracture in a weight-bearing bone for stabilisation before it breaks rather than after.

Monitor calcium after starting denosumab, because hypocalcaemia is the toxicity that differs between the two agents12.

A twelve-weekly schedule is a reasonable default for zoledronic acid. It reduces visits without a measured cost in events over two years13.

4 · Liver and lung disease

Liver disease is what most often converts a chronic illness into an acute one, and the variable that matters is function rather than volume. A patient with extensive parenchymal replacement and normal synthetic function has a different set of options from one with similar imaging and a rising bilirubin. Drug clearance changes with the second and not with the first.

Visceral crisis is defined clinically rather than radiologically. It means rapidly progressive visceral involvement with organ dysfunction requiring a rapid response, and it is the one situation in hormone receptor-positive disease where chemotherapy has customarily displaced endocrine and targeted therapy.

That custom has been tested and the result needs stating carefully. RIGHT Choice randomised premenopausal women with clinically aggressive hormone receptor-positive, HER2-negative advanced disease to ribociclib with endocrine therapy or to combination chemotherapy, and median progression-free survival was 21.8 months against 12.8 months15. Clinically aggressive disease as enrolled in that trial is not the same population as visceral crisis with organ failure. What the result establishes is that the reflex to chemotherapy is wrong more often than it was assumed to be. It does not establish that it is never right.

Lung parenchymal metastases are usually asymptomatic until extensive, and the tempo is slower than liver disease of comparable burden.

Lymphangitic carcinomatosis is a separate problem with a separate tempo, and it is the one to recognise early. Breathlessness out of proportion to the parenchymal findings, with septal thickening on imaging, is frequently attributed first to infection and second to treatment-related pneumonitis. In a patient receiving a deruxtecan-based conjugate that second possibility is urgent, because drug-related interstitial lung disease in this class carries real mortality16. The distinction changes what happens in the next 24 hours and it cannot be made on a chest radiograph.

Why particular organs are colonised is developed in Metastatic dissemination and organ tropism, with the lung programme described in17 and the vesicle-based account of preferential seeding in18.

5 · Malignant effusions and chest wall disease

A malignant pleural effusion has three management options, and the choice between them turns on prognosis and on preference rather than on efficacy.

In a randomised trial of 106 patients with malignant pleural effusion and no previous pleurodesis, an indwelling pleural catheter was compared with chest tube drainage and talc slurry pleurodesis19. Breathlessness improved in both groups with no significant difference over the first 42 days. At six months the catheter group reported less breathlessness, with a mean difference of 14.0 mm on a 100 mm visual analogue scale. Median length of initial hospitalisation was 0 days against 4 days. Further pleural procedures were needed in 3 of 52 patients against 12 of 54. Adverse events occurred in 21 of 52 patients against 7 of 54.

The trade is legible in those numbers. The catheter buys hospital days back and reduces repeat procedures, and it costs a higher rate of adverse events and the burden of managing a drain at home. A patient with months to live and a strong preference for time out of hospital chooses differently from one expecting a longer course.

Pericardial effusion is dangerous for physiological reasons rather than volumetric ones. Tamponade depends on the rate of accumulation and on pericardial compliance, so a small rapidly accumulating effusion is more dangerous than a large slow one. Echocardiography answers the question that a computed tomography report describing a moderate effusion does not.

Malignant ascites is managed by paracentesis, and by an indwelling catheter where drainage is repeated, on the same logic as the pleural space.

Chest wall disease produces a symptom burden disproportionate to its extent and disproportionate to the attention it receives. Ulceration, exudate, odour, bleeding and pain occur together and each needs specific management. Radiation and systemic therapy address the tumour. The wound needs dressing selection, odour control and bleeding management, and that is a specialist skill. Referring it to tissue viability or wound care services is not an admission that oncology has failed, and treating it as one leaves a manageable problem unmanaged.

6 · Obstructive complications and oncologic emergencies

Metastatic spinal cord compression is the emergency in this disease where hours change the outcome, and the variable predicting function afterwards is function beforehand.

Direct decompressive surgery followed by radiotherapy was compared with radiotherapy alone in 101 patients with spinal cord compression from metastatic cancer20. After treatment 42 of 50 patients in the surgical group could walk, against 29 of 51 in the radiotherapy group, an odds ratio of 6.2 with a 95% confidence interval of 2.0 to 19.8. Median time with retained ambulation was 122 days against 13 days. Among 32 patients who entered unable to walk, 10 of 16 regained ambulation with surgery against 3 of 16 with radiotherapy alone.

Two limits belong with that result. The trial enrolled metastatic cancer generally rather than breast cancer specifically. And it stopped early at a prespecified interim analysis, which tends to inflate the estimated effect.

Superior vena cava obstruction in breast cancer is usually subacute rather than immediately life-threatening. Endovascular stenting relieves it faster than radiation and the two are commonly combined. The immediate priorities are airway and cerebral oedema rather than the caval anatomy itself.

Hypercalcaemia of malignancy is treated with rehydration and a bone-modifying agent, and it is also information. Its appearance frequently marks a change in disease behaviour, so it should prompt reassessment rather than only correction.

Biliary and ureteric obstruction present the same structural question. The decision to stent is a decision about what follows the stent, and a stent placed without a subsequent systemic plan buys a laboratory value rather than time.

In practice

New back pain with any neurological sign in a patient with known metastatic disease is imaged the same day, with magnetic resonance of the whole spine rather than the symptomatic level alone.

Start corticosteroids while arranging the imaging, not after it.

Obtain a surgical opinion for cord compression before settling on radiotherapy alone, particularly where the patient is still walking or the spine is unstable20.

Record ambulatory status at presentation. It is the strongest predictor of ambulatory status afterwards and it is the number most often missing from the notes.

7 · Local intervention in the metastatic setting

Local treatment in metastatic disease is given for three different reasons, and they should never be argued as one.

Symptom control. A painful bone deposit, a bleeding chest wall, an obstructing lesion. The justification is the symptom, and no survival claim is required or implied.

Structural protection. An impending fracture, a threatened cord, a compromised airway. The justification is the event being prevented.

Disease control intent. The oligometastatic and oligoprogressive arguments, where local treatment is given in the hope of changing the course of the disease. The evidence there is thin and is set out in Oligometastatic and oligoprogressive disease.

The clearest randomised test of the third rationale addressed the primary tumour rather than a metastasis. Women presenting with metastatic disease and an intact primary received systemic therapy for four to eight months. Those without progression were then randomised to locoregional treatment of the primary or to continuing systemic therapy alone21. Among 256 randomised patients, three-year overall survival was 68.4% with early locoregional therapy against 67.9% without, with a hazard ratio of 1.11. Median overall survival was 54.9 months against 53.1 months. Locoregional progression was less frequent with local treatment, at a three-year rate of 16.3% against 39.8%. Quality of life was largely similar between the arms.

That result is worth stating in full, because the intuition it contradicts is strong. Removing the primary improved local control, did not improve survival, and did not improve quality of life. Local control and survival are separate outcomes, and improving the first did not deliver either of the others.

Oligoprogression is the one situation where a disease-control rationale rests on a coherent biological argument. A single progressing deposit while everything else responds suggests a resistant subclone in one place, which is the divergence picture in Convergent and divergent evolution and what each implies for sequencing. Treating that site and continuing the systemic regimen follows from the inference. It has still not been shown to extend survival.

Interplay

Organ-specific management is where heterogeneity stops being a property of a tumour and becomes a property of a patient. Different deposits sit in different microenvironments, are exposed to different drug concentrations, and have taken different evolutionary routes Convergent and divergent evolution and what each implies for sequencing. A brain metastasis can carry an acquired ERBB2 amplification that the primary did not8, and the cerebrospinal fluid compartment is invisible to the assay used to monitor everything else9. Mixed response is therefore an expected finding rather than an anomaly, and a treatment decision made on the behaviour of the largest lesion is a decision about that lesion. This chapter is the clinical form of the argument that a patient with metastatic breast cancer has several related diseases in several places.
