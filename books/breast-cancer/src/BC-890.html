Part XII · Metastatic systemic therapy by subtype and line · Chapter 70

Conjugates and chemotherapy in later-line hormone receptor-positive disease

Three conjugates, two targets, one payload class, and no randomised evidence on the order in which to give them.

1 · Scope, and the point at which endocrine therapy is exhausted

This chapter covers what happens after endocrine-based therapy stops working in metastatic hormone receptor-positive, HER2-negative disease. It covers the three antibody-drug conjugates with randomised evidence in this population, the sequencing problem they create, and the place single agent chemotherapy still holds.

Endocrine therapy itself is in Endocrine therapy and receptor degraders in metastatic disease and CDK4/6 inhibition in CDK4/6 inhibition in metastatic disease, combinations and sequencing. Conjugate design, payload chemistry and resistance mechanism are in Antibody-drug conjugates and targeted delivery and are cited here rather than restated. Chemotherapy agent detail is in Cytotoxic chemotherapy. The visceral crisis decision is in Rapid progression and visceral crisis.

The phrase endocrine exhausted is a clinical judgment and not a test result. It is worth being explicit about what it usually means in practice. A patient has progressed on an aromatase inhibitor with a CDK4/6 inhibitor, then on at least one further endocrine-based line chosen by biomarker, and no remaining endocrine option matches a detectable alteration. Or the disease is moving fast enough that waiting to find out is not safe.

Both halves of that definition are soft. The first depends on which alterations were tested for and when. The second depends on a judgment about tempo that no scan reports.

Two features of that table set up the rest of the chapter. The populations are defined by how much prior chemotherapy the patient had, not by biology. And the four randomised trials were each conducted against chemotherapy, never against each other.

2 · Sacituzumab govitecan in hormone receptor-positive disease

TROPiCS-02 enrolled the most heavily pretreated population of any trial in this chapter. Every patient had received endocrine therapy, a taxane and a CDK4/6 inhibitor, together with two to four prior chemotherapy regimens for metastatic disease7.

The result to hold is that this produced a survival benefit and not only a delay in progression5. Overall survival gains are rare in a fourth-line or later population, and they are the reason the trial changed practice despite absolute medians measured in months. A reader should set expectations from those absolute numbers rather than from the hazard ratio.

Trop-2 expression did not select the responders. The survival benefit was consistent across expression subgroups measured by immunohistochemistry5. That sits awkwardly beside the biomarker analysis of the same drug in triple-negative disease, where progression-free survival tracked a Trop-2 expression gradient8. The two findings are usually reported as if they conflict.

They may not. The triple-negative analysis graded progression-free survival across three expression bands in a subset of assessable patients, and it was exploratory8. The hormone receptor-positive analysis reported overall survival by subgroup5. Different endpoint, different population, different analysis plan. The safest reading is that Trop-2 staining has never been shown to identify a group that should not receive the drug, which is the only question a clinician needs answered.

The toxicity is predictable and is managed rather than avoided. Neutropenia and diarrhoea dominate, and both are payload effects rather than target effects Toxicity mechanisms, interstitial lung disease, ocular, and mucosal. UGT1A1 genotype affects SN-38 clearance and is worth knowing before the first dose in a patient with a suggestive history.

3 · Trastuzumab deruxtecan in HER2-low and HER2-ultralow disease

Two randomised trials moved this drug progressively earlier in the same population, and the second also moved the eligibility boundary.

DESTINY-Breast04 enrolled patients with HER2-low disease after one or two prior chemotherapy lines, and the large majority had hormone receptor-positive disease6. DESTINY-Breast06 enrolled after endocrine-based therapy and before any chemotherapy for metastatic disease, and it added a HER2-ultralow cohort3.

The eligibility boundary is an assay boundary rather than a biological one, and that argument is made in full in HER2-low and ultralow reframed as a heterogeneity problem and Antigen thresholds as eligibility criteria, the general lesson. Its practical form here is uncomfortable. HER2-ultralow means immunohistochemistry 0 with faint incomplete membrane staining in some cells, which is a category defined at the least reproducible end of the assay. Pathologist concordance is poorest exactly there, as Where heterogeneity sets a ceiling on precision medicine sets out. A patient's eligibility can therefore depend on who read the slide.

The response data argue that the boundary is arbitrary in the other direction too. In DAISY, confirmed responses occurred in patients with HER2 immunohistochemistry 0 disease at a rate well above zero4. Activity does not stop where the scoring category does.

Caution

DESTINY-Breast06 is often read as showing that a conjugate should replace endocrine therapy once resistance appears. It shows something narrower. Its comparator was chemotherapy, and every patient enrolled had already been judged ready for chemotherapy. The trial therefore establishes that if the next line is cytotoxic, the conjugate is the better cytotoxic choice. It does not establish when to leave endocrine-based therapy, which is the decision that actually precedes it Endocrine therapy and receptor degraders in metastatic disease.

Interstitial lung disease is the toxicity that should shape the decision. Adjudicated interstitial lung disease or pneumonitis occurred in 11.3% of patients receiving the conjugate in DESTINY-Breast06, including three grade 5 events3. In a patient with pre-existing lung disease, a low baseline oxygen saturation or prior thoracic radiotherapy, that risk is a reason to think carefully about ordering. Surveillance and management are in Toxicity mechanisms, interstitial lung disease, ocular, and mucosal and the difference between a grade 1 and a grade 5 event is mostly how early it was found.

4 · Datopotamab deruxtecan and the TROP2 question

TROPION-Breast01 carried two primary endpoints, progression-free survival and overall survival. It met the first and missed the second. The final survival analysis gave a hazard ratio of 1.01, with a confidence interval from 0.83 to 1.229.

That combination is worth separating from the more common pattern of an unpowered secondary endpoint failing to confirm a progression-free survival result. Here survival was a stated primary endpoint that the trial was designed and powered to test. The trial answered its own question and the answer was no.

Three explanations have been offered and none has been excluded. Subsequent conjugate use was imbalanced, at 12.3% in the datopotamab arm against 24.0% in the chemotherapy arm, which would favour the control arm after progression9. Progression-free survival was assessed by blinded central review in an open-label trial, where the timing of scans and the decision to stop treatment are not blinded. Or the drug may delay progression in this setting without extending life.

The target itself does not resolve the question. Trop-2 is an address rather than a driver, chosen because it is differentially expressed on epithelium and not because tumours depend on it Antibody, linker, and payload design. Normal tissue expression is what limits the dose, which is why the dominant toxicities are mucosal and ocular rather than marrow-based. Two conjugates against the same antigen carrying the same payload class still differ in dominant toxicity, and the reason is linker chemistry rather than target Toxicity mechanisms, interstitial lung disease, ocular, and mucosal.

The practical consequence is a ranking argument rather than a prohibition. Where more than one conjugate is available and the disease is hormone receptor-positive, an agent that met a survival endpoint has a stronger claim than one that missed it. That is an inference across trials with different populations, and it should be labelled as such.

5 · Sequencing conjugates and the cross-resistance problem

Three conjugates are now used in this population. They engage two antigens and they all carry a topoisomerase I inhibitor payload. That is the whole problem in one sentence.

The mechanistic case for expecting cross-resistance, and the preclinical work separating target loss from payload resistance and from transporter change, is set out in Payload class, cross-resistance, and sequencing between conjugates and Mechanisms of conjugate resistance. Three findings from there govern this section and are not repeated. Resistance can arise at any of six steps. Only the first of those steps is measured by any assay in clinical use. And payload class, rather than the conjugate as a unit, is what preclinical models show carrying the cross-resistance.

The clinical evidence is a retrospective series of 85 patients who received two conjugates in sequence, described in Payload class, cross-resistance, and sequencing between conjugates10. It is consistent with partial rather than complete cross-resistance. It is also 85 patients treated off protocol, which is the best evidence available for a decision made many times a week.

What follows for practice is a rationale and not a result, and the difference should be stated to patients. HER2-low or ultralow status determines whether trastuzumab deruxtecan is an option at all, so it is worth establishing before the conjugate decision rather than during it. Trials enrolled these agents at different depths of prior treatment, so the trial populations imply an ordering even though no trial compared orders. And a patient who progressed quickly on the first conjugate has, on the retrospective data, a poor prospect from the second.

Interplay

This is the clearest clinical instance of the ceiling argued in Where heterogeneity sets a ceiling on precision medicine. The decision is made on an antigen measurement taken from one block at one time, and that measurement answers only whether the antibody can bind. It says nothing about internalisation, lysosomal transport, efflux or repair capacity. A proportion of what is recorded here as conjugate failure is therefore allocation failure, and no improvement in the payload addresses it. Raising the ceiling requires a different kind of measurement rather than a better drug.

Two observations from that table carry most of the weight.

EMBRACE showed that a single cytotoxic agent can extend survival in a population with two to five prior chemotherapy regimens17. That was not the expectation at the time, and it remains the reason eribulin holds its position.

Study 301 then compared eribulin with capecitabine directly and found neither superior on either co-primary endpoint16. Within the class, in other words, there is no evidence of a best agent. Choice is therefore driven by prior exposure, residual neuropathy, marrow reserve, renal and hepatic function, route and schedule. Those are patient variables, not trial variables.

One older principle survives all of this. Combination chemotherapy raises response rate and toxicity together, while sequential single agents preserve function and allow more lines. RIGHT Choice tested that principle in aggressive but compensated disease and found that endocrine therapy with a CDK4/6 inhibitor outperformed combination chemotherapy11. Its limits are discussed in Rapid progression and visceral crisis.

In practice

Say what the line is for before starting it. Control of a symptom, protection of a threatened organ, and extension of time are different aims with different evidence behind them.

Choose the agent by what the patient can tolerate rather than by expected efficacy, because the comparative trials show no efficacy difference within the class.

Re-check performance status before each new line rather than at progression. A patient who has had three conjugates and two cytotoxics is a different physiological proposition from the trial populations above.

Ask about access explicitly. Conjugate availability is the binding constraint in most of the world, and single-agent chemotherapy is the realistic comparator there rather than the fallback Global breast cancer care.

Name the alternative of no further systemic therapy as an option, not as a failure. It is the right answer more often than the number of remaining licensed agents suggests Long-term disease control and the cost of staying on treatment.

Conjugate trials in this population that have not reported.

ASCENT-07 tests sacituzumab govitecan against chemotherapy in endocrine-resistant disease with no prior chemotherapy for metastatic disease. That is the same slot DESTINY-Breast06 now occupies, so a positive result would create a genuine choice between two conjugates at the same decision point, with no trial comparing them.

MK-2870-010 brings a third Trop-2 conjugate into that slot. Its payload is also a topoisomerase I inhibitor, so it extends the problem this chapter describes rather than solving it.

TRADE-DXd is the trial to watch, because it is the first prospective study designed around conjugate sequencing. It treats patients whose disease has progressed on a previous conjugate, in cohorts defined by which conjugate came first. Its endpoint is response rate and its cohorts are non-comparative, so it will not rank sequences. It will produce the first prospective estimate of how much activity remains after a conjugate fails, which is currently guessed at from a retrospective series.

Three gaps will remain after all three report.

No trial randomises the choice between leaving endocrine-based therapy and continuing it, which is the decision that precedes everything in this chapter.

No assay in clinical use predicts which patient retains sensitivity after a topoisomerase I payload, and none of the running trials will produce one.

And no conjugate in this population carries a payload from a different class. Until one does, sequencing decisions will continue to rest on target expression, which answers one question out of six Mechanisms of conjugate resistance.
