Conjugates and chemotherapy in later lines

Why six conjugate trials against physician's choice chemotherapy still leave the order of giving them unmeasured.

1 · Scope, and the point at which endocrine therapy is exhausted

This chapter covers what happens after endocrine-based therapy stops working in metastatic hormone receptor-positive, HER2-negative disease. It covers the five antibody-drug conjugates with randomised evidence in this population, the sequencing problem they create, and the place single agent chemotherapy still holds.

Endocrine therapy itself is in Endocrine therapy and receptor degraders after progression and CDK4/6 inhibition in CDK4/6 inhibition, combinations and sequencing. Conjugate design, payload chemistry and resistance mechanism are in Antibody-drug conjugates and targeted delivery and are cited here rather than restated. Chemotherapy agent detail is in Cytotoxic chemotherapy. The visceral crisis decision is in Rapid progression and visceral crisis.

The phrase endocrine exhausted is a clinical judgment and not a test result. It is worth being explicit about what it usually means in practice. A patient has progressed on an aromatase inhibitor with a CDK4/6 inhibitor, then on at least one further endocrine-based line chosen by biomarker, and no remaining endocrine option matches a detectable alteration. Or the disease is moving fast enough that waiting to find out is not safe.

Both halves of that definition are soft. The first depends on which alterations were tested for and when. The second depends on a judgment about tempo that no scan reports.

Advanced disease · HR+/HER2- · later

Choosing the next treatment after CDK4/6 inhibition fails

When should endocrine-directed treatment give way to chemotherapy or an antibody-drug conjugate?

Rationale
The convention is to hold cytotoxic treatment back while any endocrine option remains, because endocrine treatment is easier to live with. The convention assumes the remaining endocrine option still works. After a CDK4/6 inhibitor that is exactly what is in doubt. A conjugate was meant to move the threshold by delivering a cytotoxic payload only to cells carrying a surface antigen.
Experiment
Two trials randomised a CDK4/6 inhibitor with endocrine therapy against capecitabine in endocrine-resistant disease. Two more randomised a TROP2-directed conjugate against chemotherapy of the physician's choice, at different depths of prior chemotherapy. One asked whether adding a checkpoint inhibitor to such a conjugate helped. A single-arm study took a HER3-directed conjugate into disease that had had a CDK4/6 inhibitor and a line of chemotherapy. One ongoing trial moves the TROP2 conjugate ahead of the first chemotherapy line.
Finding
The two capecitabine trials disagree on progression-free survival. The disagreement follows their populations rather than their drugs. Neither of them produced an overall survival difference, which is the one thing they agree on. Both TROP2-directed conjugates lengthened progression-free survival against physician's choice chemotherapy. Only one of the two also lengthened overall survival. Adding a checkpoint inhibitor to a conjugate did not lengthen progression-free survival. The HER3-directed conjugate produced responses in a single arm.
Limitation
Neither capecitabine trial belongs to this position as it stands, because both gave a CDK4/6 inhibitor to patients who had never had one. They differ from each other in menopausal status and in how much of the population had had any treatment for metastatic disease, which is enough to explain the disagreement without invoking the drugs. Physician's choice names a different set of agents in each conjugate trial and in each country. The comparator is therefore not a fixed quantity across them. The two conjugate trials also sit at different depths of prior chemotherapy, which is the other reason their results are not interchangeable. A progression-free survival gain not followed by a gain in overall survival is a difference measured on imaging. The conjugate whose survival curves lay on top of each other is the warning this class has to answer. The checkpoint result that looks positive is a secondary analysis confined to the patients with evaluable tissue scoring PD-L1-positive. It is not significant.

Six of those rows are the same experiment. A conjugate was randomised against the physician's choice of single-agent chemotherapy in disease out of endocrine options, and each lengthened the time to progression. That is the only claim the table supports as a class. Everything else in it varies. None of the variation is between one conjugate and another.

The first difference is where the patient was standing when she entered. Eligibility is written in units of prior chemotherapy rather than in units of biology. TROPiCS-02 required two to four previous chemotherapy regimens for metastatic disease. TROPION-Breast01, DESTINY-Breast04 and PANKU-Breast01 required one or two2. DESTINY-Breast06 required none, and excluded anyone who had had chemotherapy for metastatic disease at all. HORIZON-Breast06 admitted none or one. Its primary endpoint was read in the patients with none and again in everyone1. Read down the population column and the six trials line up as a ladder. That ordering is an artefact of protocol writing rather than a tested sequence, and stacking the medians would compare populations rather than drugs. A hazard ratio from the shallowest of them describes a decision the deepest never tested.

The second difference is one of measurement. Three of the six required an assay result for the antibody's own target, and two required nothing of the kind. Trop-2 expression was never an entry criterion, so those trials enrolled whoever qualified on treatment history alone. The published eligibility of PANKU-Breast01 names no EGFR or HER3 measurement either2. HER2 expression entered that trial only as a stratification factor. The HER2-low trials enrolled on a staining category, which is a threshold on a continuous stain rather than a biological boundary HER2-low and ultralow reframed as a heterogeneity problem. A patient's eligibility moves with the assay in three of the trials and not in the other three.

The third difference is the endpoint. Only one of the six named overall survival a primary endpoint. That is the trial that failed it. Two of the others reported survival separately and three have not. Two of those three were short of follow-up when the primary analysis was done. The report of HORIZON-Breast06 gives no survival result at all1. A blank survival cell and a tested negative survival result are different objects. The column cannot tell them apart.

The six remaining rows answer other questions and should not be read as further conjugate comparisons. SACI-IO HR+ randomised the addition of a checkpoint inhibitor to a conjugate rather than a conjugate against chemotherapy, and belongs to Unresolved questions and what reads out next. DAISY and ICARUS-BREAST01 are single-arm. One maps activity across the HER2 staining range, and the other carries the same payload class to a third antigen. IMMU-132-01, DS8201-A-J101 and U31402-A-J101 are the early single-arm studies of sacituzumab govitecan, trastuzumab deruxtecan and patritumab deruxtecan in breast cancer12,11,9. Each enrolled heavily pretreated patients in more than one subtype. The first two are the activity signals that randomised rows in the table were later built to test. The HER3-directed drug is followed in the registry only by the single-arm ICARUS-BREAST01.

Two comparisons are missing. Neither can be supplied by longer follow-up. No row sets one conjugate against another. And no trial here randomised the decision that precedes everything in the table, which is whether to leave endocrine-based therapy at all. Every patient in every row had already been judged ready for a cytotoxic by the clinician who enrolled her.

The order within the class is therefore set by eligibility and habit rather than by evidence, and Sequencing conjugates and the cross-resistance problem is where that problem is worked through. Two trials that set a CDK4/6 inhibitor against capecitabine are sometimes quoted at this point and do not belong to it. Both gave the inhibitor to patients who had never had one, which is not the patient this chapter is about. They are argued in Single-agent chemotherapy and when it remains the right choice.

2 · Sacituzumab govitecan in hormone receptor-positive disease

TROPiCS-02 is where this drug's position rests. The reason is the depth of what it randomised. Every patient had received endocrine therapy, a taxane and a CDK4/6 inhibitor, together with two to four prior chemotherapy regimens for metastatic disease13. The control arm was the menu of single agents such a patient would otherwise have been given.

The result to hold is that survival moved and not only the time to progression8. Overall survival gains are rare this far into the disease, which is why the trial changed practice despite absolute medians measured in months. Set a patient's expectations from those medians rather than from the hazard ratio.

Trop-2 expression did not select the responders. The survival benefit was consistent across expression subgroups measured by immunohistochemistry8. That sits awkwardly beside the biomarker analysis of the same drug in triple-negative disease, where progression-free survival tracked a Trop-2 expression gradient14. The two findings are usually reported as if they conflict.

They may not. The triple-negative analysis graded progression-free survival across three expression bands in a subset of assessable patients, and it was exploratory14. The hormone receptor-positive analysis reported overall survival by subgroup8. Different endpoint, different population, different analysis plan. The safest reading is that Trop-2 staining has never been shown to identify a group that should not receive the drug, which is the only question a clinician needs answered.

The toxicity is predictable and is managed rather than avoided. Neutropenia and diarrhoea dominate, and both are payload effects rather than target effects Toxicity mechanisms, interstitial lung disease, ocular, and mucosal. UGT1A1 genotype affects SN-38 clearance and is worth knowing before the first dose in a patient with a suggestive history.

What the trial cannot support is the use most clinics now want. Its population had exhausted several cytotoxic lines, so nothing in it establishes the drug ahead of the first of them. That question is being asked prospectively and is taken up in Unresolved questions and what reads out next.

3 · Trastuzumab deruxtecan in HER2-low and HER2-ultralow disease

Two randomised trials moved this drug progressively earlier in the same population, and the second also moved the eligibility boundary. DESTINY-Breast04 enrolled patients with HER2-low disease after one or two prior chemotherapy lines, and the large majority had hormone receptor-positive disease10. DESTINY-Breast06 enrolled after endocrine-based therapy and before any chemotherapy for metastatic disease, and it added a HER2-ultralow cohort6.

They agree on the thing that matters. Against the physician's choice of chemotherapy, at two different depths of prior treatment, the conjugate delayed progression. Where they part is on survival. That is a difference of maturity rather than of effect. The earlier trial has reported survival with long follow-up15. The later one had too few events at its primary analysis to say anything, which is what a trial reads out when it is moved forward into a population that lives longer.

HORIZON-Breast06 put a second HER2-directed conjugate, trastuzumab rezetecan, through the same comparison in China1. Its population straddles the two deruxtecan trials, because it admitted patients with no more than one chemotherapy line for metastatic disease. Progression-free survival favoured the conjugate both in the patients with none and in everyone. It also enrolled hormone receptor-negative disease, so only part of it belongs to this chapter.

The eligibility boundary is an assay boundary rather than a biological one. That argument is made in full in HER2-low and ultralow reframed as a heterogeneity problem and Antigen thresholds as eligibility criteria, the general lesson. Its practical form here is uncomfortable. HER2-ultralow means immunohistochemistry 0 with faint incomplete membrane staining in some cells, which is a category defined at the least reproducible end of the assay. Pathologist concordance is poorest exactly there, as Where heterogeneity sets a ceiling on precision medicine sets out. A patient's eligibility can therefore depend on who read the slide.

DAISY argues that the boundary is arbitrary from the other direction. It gave the same conjugate across three staining cohorts and confirmed responses occurred well above zero in immunohistochemistry 0 disease7. Within that cohort, response did not track the underlying ERBB2 transcript level either. Activity does not stop where the scoring category does. The study is single-arm, so it cannot say whether those patients would have done as well on chemotherapy, and the randomised ultralow evidence remains an exploratory cohort of one trial.

Caution

DESTINY-Breast06 is often read as showing that a conjugate should replace endocrine therapy once resistance appears. It shows something narrower. Its comparator was chemotherapy, and every patient enrolled had already been judged ready for chemotherapy. The trial therefore establishes that if the next line is cytotoxic, the conjugate is the better cytotoxic choice. It does not establish when to leave endocrine-based therapy, which is the decision that actually precedes it Endocrine therapy and receptor degraders after progression.

Interstitial lung disease is the toxicity that should shape the decision, because it is the one that can be fatal. Adjudicated interstitial lung disease or pneumonitis occurred in 11.3% of patients given the conjugate in DESTINY-Breast06 against 0.2% of those given chemotherapy6. In a patient with pre-existing lung disease, a low baseline oxygen saturation or prior thoracic radiotherapy, that asymmetry is a reason to think carefully about ordering. Surveillance and management are in Toxicity mechanisms, interstitial lung disease, ocular, and mucosal, and the difference between a grade 1 and a grade 5 event is mostly how early it was found.

4 · Datopotamab deruxtecan and the TROP2 question

TROPION-Breast01 carried two primary endpoints, progression-free survival and overall survival. It met the first and missed the second. The final survival analysis gave a hazard ratio for death of 1.01, with a confidence interval from 0.83 to 1.2216.

That combination is worth separating from the more common pattern of an unpowered secondary endpoint failing to confirm a progression-free survival result. Here survival was a stated primary endpoint that the trial was designed and powered to test. The trial answered its own question and the answer was no.

Three explanations have been offered and none has been excluded. Subsequent conjugate use was imbalanced, at 12.3% in the datopotamab arm against 24.0% in the chemotherapy arm, which would favour the control arm after progression16. Progression-free survival was assessed by blinded central review in an open-label trial, where the timing of scans and the decision to stop treatment are not blinded. Or the drug may delay progression in this setting without extending life.

The target itself does not resolve the question. Trop-2 is an address rather than a driver, chosen because it is differentially expressed on epithelium and not because tumours depend on it Antibody, linker, and payload design. Normal tissue expression is what limits the dose, which is why the dominant toxicities are mucosal and ocular rather than marrow-based. Two conjugates against the same antigen carrying the same payload class still differ in dominant toxicity. Linker chemistry and normal tissue handling separate them rather than the target Toxicity mechanisms, interstitial lung disease, ocular, and mucosal.

The practical consequence is a ranking argument rather than a prohibition. Where more than one conjugate is available and the disease is hormone receptor-positive, an agent that met a survival endpoint has a stronger claim than one that missed it. That inference crosses two trials with different entry criteria and different comparator menus. It should be labelled as such. Nobody has randomised the two drugs against each other, and no trial has selected patients for either one by Trop-2 staining.

5 · Sequencing conjugates and the cross-resistance problem

Advanced disease · HR+/HER2- · later

Antibody-drug conjugates after endocrine therapy

Does a second antibody-drug conjugate work after a first one has failed?

Rationale
The conjugates used here share a payload mechanism while differing in the antibody. If resistance is to the payload, changing the antibody buys nothing. If it is to the target, to its internalisation or to trafficking inside the cell, a different address might still work. Patients now arrive in later lines having already had a conjugate. The question has stopped being theoretical.
Experiment
A HER3-directed conjugate was given as a single arm after progression on CDK4/6 inhibition and a line of chemotherapy. Paired baseline and on-treatment biopsies were taken, to see how far the drug distributed inside the tumour and what the tumour did in response. A prospective platform then gave one of four new conjugates to patients who had already had one. Each was aimed at a different target, with a topoisomerase I inhibitor payload in all four. A second prospective study is giving two deruxtecan conjugates one after the other, in separate cohorts defined by which came first.
Finding
Redirecting the payload to a third antigen met its response endpoint in a population already treated with CDK4/6 inhibition and chemotherapy. The on-treatment tissue work associated efficacy with how far the conjugate distributed within the tumour and with an interferon response. At baseline the associations ran to where the receptor sat in the tissue and to the absence of an ESR1 mutation, with receptor level itself tracking only the progression endpoint. After a previous conjugate, changing the target while the payload class stayed the same produced few confirmed responses. Two of the four cohorts had none. The responses that did occur followed a previous conjugate whose payload was of a different class.
Limitation
The HER3 study is single-arm. Its response rate has nothing to be read against and cannot rank HER3 delivery against another conjugate or against chemotherapy. That response is reported with a narrower confidence interval than the randomised trials beside it use, which is what a single-arm design of this kind specifies and not a sign of greater precision. The on-treatment biopsies come only from patients who stayed on the drug long enough to be rebiopsied. That is selection on the very outcome the biomarkers are being asked to explain. The authors state that every association is pending validation. The platform's cohorts were small, non-comparative and enrolled in China. Its result has been presented at a congress rather than published. That result suggests that a change of target bought little in that population, without estimating how little. The deruxtecan sequencing study is still running. Its cohorts are non-comparative by design. It will report activity after a conjugate rather than the value of a sequence.

Three conjugates are now used in this population. They engage two antigens and they all carry a topoisomerase I inhibitor payload. That is the whole problem in one sentence.

Two more antibodies make the point sharper rather than softer. ICARUS-BREAST01 took a HER3-directed conjugate into patients who had progressed on a CDK4/6 inhibitor and one line of chemotherapy, and met its response endpoint4. Its target is a third antigen. Its payload is the same deruxtecan class as two of the three agents already in use. PANKU-Breast01 set a conjugate whose bispecific antibody binds EGFR and HER3 against chemotherapy2. Its payload is again a topoisomerase I inhibitor. Adding antigens does not add payload classes. Payload class is what the preclinical work in Mechanisms of conjugate resistance shows carrying cross-resistance.

That response rate has nothing to be read against. The study is single-arm, so it cannot rank HER3 delivery against another conjugate or against the chemotherapy those patients would otherwise have had. Its interval is reported at 90% rather than 95%, which is what a single-arm design of this kind specifies and not a sign of greater precision. Comparing it with the intervals in the randomised rows above is comparing two different constructions.

Its correlative work is the more interesting part. It also carries a selection problem worth naming. Baseline associations ran to where the receptor sat in the tissue and to the absence of an ESR1 mutation, while receptor level itself tracked only the progression endpoint4. The on-treatment associations ran to how far the conjugate distributed inside the tumour and to an interferon response. Those came from biopsies taken only in patients who stayed on the drug long enough to be rebiopsied. That is selection on the very outcome the biomarkers are being asked to explain. The authors state that all of it awaits validation.

The mechanistic case for expecting cross-resistance, and the preclinical work separating target loss from payload resistance and from transporter change, is set out in Payload class, cross-resistance, and sequencing between conjugates and Mechanisms of conjugate resistance. Three findings from there govern this section and are not repeated. Resistance can arise at any of six steps. Only the first of those steps is measured by any assay in clinical use. And payload class, rather than the conjugate as a unit, is what preclinical models show carrying the cross-resistance.

The retrospective evidence for a second conjugate is a series of 85 patients who received two in sequence, described in Payload class, cross-resistance, and sequencing between conjugates17. It is consistent with partial rather than complete cross-resistance. It is also a series of patients treated off protocol.

SWITCH is the first prospective sequencing study in the registry to report18. After a previous conjugate, it gave patients one of four conjugates aimed at different targets, every one of them carrying a topoisomerase I inhibitor payload. Confirmed responses were absent or rare in every cohort. The few recorded came after a previous conjugate carrying MMAE, a payload of another class. Its cohorts were small, non-comparative and enrolled in China. The result suggests that changing the target buys little when the payload class stays the same, without measuring how little.

TRADE-DXd tests a specific sequence instead, trastuzumab deruxtecan and datopotamab deruxtecan given one after the other. It has not reported. Its cohorts are defined by which conjugate came first and are non-comparative by design, so it will estimate how much activity survives a conjugate rather than rank one order against another.

What no trial in this chapter did is randomise the order of the same drugs. The design that answers a sequencing question randomises the position and then measures progression on the line after next, which is how SONIA settled the first-line CDK4/6 argument19. No conjugate trial and no chemotherapy trial in this disease has been built that way. Until one is, moving a drug earlier is demonstrated for the first segment of the sequence and assumed for the rest.

What follows for practice is a rationale and not a result, and the difference should be stated to patients. HER2-low or ultralow status determines whether trastuzumab deruxtecan is an option at all, so it is worth establishing before the conjugate decision rather than during it. Trials enrolled these agents at different depths of prior treatment, so the trial populations imply an ordering even though no trial compared orders. And a patient who progressed quickly on the first conjugate has, on the retrospective data, a poor prospect from the second.

Interplay

This is the clearest clinical instance of the ceiling argued in Where heterogeneity sets a ceiling on precision medicine. The decision is made on an antigen measurement taken from one block at one time, and that measurement answers only whether the antibody can bind. It says nothing about internalisation, lysosomal transport, efflux or repair capacity. A proportion of what is recorded here as conjugate failure is therefore allocation failure, and no improvement in the payload addresses it. Raising the ceiling requires a different kind of measurement rather than a better drug.

6 · Single-agent chemotherapy and when it remains the right choice

Antibody-drug conjugates after endocrine therapy, continued

Once chemotherapy is reached, does the choice of agent matter?

Rationale
Two newer taxane formulations and an epothilone were developed to improve on weekly paclitaxel. The argument was that a different delivery vehicle, or a different tubulin-binding site, would raise response or lower toxicity. Each had promising single-arm data before the comparison was run. Two other arguments ran alongside. One held that a second cytotoxic might do more than one. The other proposed that a checkpoint inhibitor could sustain a response that chemotherapy had produced.
Experiment
Three-arm randomisation of weekly nab-paclitaxel and weekly ixabepilone against weekly paclitaxel, each with bevacizumab, in chemotherapy-naive advanced disease. Ixabepilone was also added to capecitabine after an anthracycline and a taxane, with overall survival as the primary endpoint. A third trial asked whether chemotherapy that had controlled the disease could give way to a checkpoint inhibitor alone as maintenance.
Finding
Neither experimental agent improved progression-free survival and one was clearly worse. The oldest and cheapest arm was the best of the three. Adding ixabepilone to capecitabine delayed progression and raised response without lengthening survival on the primary analysis. Replacing maintenance chemotherapy with the checkpoint inhibitor did worse on progression. In hormone receptor-positive disease its authors found continued chemotherapy the more effective arm.
Limitation
The comparison was made in chemotherapy-naive disease with a bevacizumab backbone, and the chapter that carries it is about later lines where the alternatives are conjugates rather than single agents. A negative result against paclitaxel does not establish how any of these agents performs after a conjugate has failed. The capecitabine combination trial was not restricted by receptor status and predates conjugates and CDK4/6 inhibitors. Its survival gain appeared only in a secondary analysis adjusted for a performance status imbalance. The maintenance trial is phase II. Its receptor-positive result is a subgroup reading.

Antibody-drug conjugates after endocrine therapy, continued

Once endocrine therapy is exhausted, does an antibody-drug conjugate beat the single-agent chemotherapy a clinic would otherwise give?

Rationale
The payload inside these conjugates is an ordinary cytotoxic. What the antibody changes is where that payload is delivered, not what it does to a dividing cell. The question was therefore whether a better address buys enough tumour exposure to beat giving the same class of drug systemically and accepting the toxicity that follows.
Experiment
Four randomised phase III trials gave a conjugate against physician's choice of single-agent chemotherapy in disease that was hormone receptor-positive and out of endocrine options. Two address TROP2 and one HER2. The fourth binds EGFR and HER3 with a single bispecific antibody. They also set different requirements for how much chemotherapy a patient had to have had before entry. The control arm in each was drawn from the same short menu of single agents. That menu had been assembled by earlier trials that set those agents against physician's choice and against one another.
Finding
Each conjugate lengthened the time to progression against physician's choice chemotherapy. Overall survival did not follow in every case. In the trial that made progression-free survival and overall survival both primary, progression-free survival moved against chemotherapy. Overall survival did not. In the bispecific trial, survival data are still immature.
Limitation
Physician's choice is a composite control rather than a drug. The trials that assembled that menu did not find its members interchangeable in the way the phrase implies. One of them showed a single agent lengthening overall survival against physician's choice in heavily pretreated disease. Another set one menu agent against another and separated them on neither co-primary endpoint. So the comparator is an average of agents whose relative merit was never settled, rather than the drug a particular clinician would have chosen for a particular patient. The four conjugate trials also demanded different amounts of prior chemotherapy before entry. They cannot be stacked into a sequence or read as a ranking. The bispecific trial enrolled only in China. Its authors place the result in that population and call for evaluation in global populations. The dual-primary trial is the standing warning here, because it shows that a progression-free survival gain against chemotherapy in this setting does not convert into a survival gain on its own.

Should a conjugate replace the first chemotherapy rather than follow it?

Rationale
Every trial that established these drugs enrolled after chemotherapy had already been given. What they measured is the value of a conjugate at that point in the course. Moving it forward means giving a drug with its own distinctive toxicity to people who might have done well for a long time on one gentle cytotoxic. It also means giving it to people who might not have needed cytotoxic treatment at all yet.
Experiment
One phase III trial moved the HER2-directed conjugate ahead of any chemotherapy for metastatic disease in endocrine-pretreated patients, against physician's choice chemotherapy. Two TROP2 trials are doing the same in the first chemotherapy slot and have not reported. A second HER2-directed conjugate was set against physician's choice chemotherapy after no more than one chemotherapy line, with the chemotherapy-naive patients analysed as a primary population of their own. From the opposite direction, a randomised trial in aggressive disease compared endocrine therapy with a CDK4/6 inhibitor against combination chemotherapy, asking whether cytotoxic treatment was needed at that point at all.
Finding
The conjugate given before any chemotherapy lengthened time to progression against physician's choice chemotherapy, with overall survival immature at that analysis. The second HER2-directed conjugate lengthened it too, in the chemotherapy-naive patients and in its whole population. Endocrine therapy with a CDK4/6 inhibitor outlasted combination chemotherapy in aggressive disease that would once have gone straight to cytotoxic treatment. The conjugate results and the endocrine result push conventional single-agent chemotherapy out of the slot it used to hold, from opposite directions.
Limitation
The control arm in the earlier-line conjugate trial is the same physician's-choice menu. Its overall survival was immature at the primary analysis. The second HER2-directed trial reports no survival result, enrolled only in China and admitted hormone receptor-negative disease alongside. A longer time to first progression is the whole of the case for moving either drug forward. The aggressive-disease trial is phase II and premenopausal. It enrolled on a clinical judgement of aggressiveness rather than on a stated threshold. It therefore does not define who can safely be kept off chemotherapy. Most of all, no trial here randomised the order of the same drugs. The design that would answer that randomises the position and then measures progression on the line after next. That is how the first-line CDK4/6 question was settled. It is not how any conjugate in this position has been studied.

On the first question the answer is consistently negative. That is the finding to carry. Study 301 compared eribulin with capecitabine directly and separated them on neither co-primary endpoint28. CALGB 40502 / NCCTG N063H set nab-paclitaxel and ixabepilone against weekly paclitaxel as first chemotherapy for advanced disease, and neither experimental arm beat it27. Choice is therefore driven by prior exposure, residual neuropathy, marrow reserve, renal and hepatic function, route and schedule. Those are patient variables, not trial variables. Capecitabine, eribulin, vinorelbine, gemcitabine and a weekly taxane are the working set, and agent detail is in Cytotoxic chemotherapy.

EMBRACE looks like an exception and is not one. It showed that a single cytotoxic agent can extend survival after several previous chemotherapy regimens29. That was not the expectation at the time. EMBRACE set eribulin against a composite of whatever the physician would otherwise have chosen, with overall survival as the sole primary endpoint, in a deeply pretreated population. In practice

Say what the line is for before starting it. Control of a symptom, protection of a threatened organ, and extension of time are different aims with different evidence behind them.

Choose the agent by what the patient can tolerate rather than by expected efficacy, because the comparative trials show no efficacy difference within the class.

Test for a germline BRCA1 or BRCA2 mutation before choosing the cytotoxic rather than after, since the result moves the patient into a different table.

Re-check performance status before each new line rather than at progression. A patient who has had three conjugates and two cytotoxics is a different physiological proposition from the trial populations above.

Ask about access explicitly. Conjugate availability is the binding constraint in most of the world, and single-agent chemotherapy is the realistic comparator there rather than the fallback Global breast cancer care.

Name the alternative of no further systemic therapy as an option, not as a failure. It is the right answer more often than the number of remaining licensed agents suggests Long-term disease control and the cost of staying on treatment.

Antibody-drug conjugates after endocrine therapy, continued

Does adding checkpoint blockade to a TROP2 conjugate improve on the conjugate alone?

Rationale
A topoisomerase payload kills in a way that releases tumour antigen and can draw immune cells into the tumour, which is the standard argument for putting a conjugate next to a checkpoint inhibitor. Hormone receptor-positive disease is the least immunogenic of the common subtypes. If a conjugate is what makes a tumour visible to the immune system, this is where an added effect should be easiest to attribute to the conjugate rather than to the tumour's own starting immunogenicity.
Experiment
A randomised phase II trial gave a TROP2 conjugate with or without a checkpoint inhibitor, in disease pretreated with endocrine therapy and at most one chemotherapy regimen. Progression-free survival was the primary endpoint. The PD-L1-positive population was a key secondary one. A phase III trial is now testing the same addition with a different TROP2 conjugate, ahead of any chemotherapy, against physician's choice chemotherapy. Elsewhere in this subtype, a placebo-controlled phase III trial added the same checkpoint inhibitor to the first chemotherapy, in disease selected on PD-L1. The other studies named here gave checkpoint blockade either in single-arm combinations, where the partner drug cannot be separated from the checkpoint inhibitor, or in populations selected for a high mutational burden.
Finding
Adding the checkpoint inhibitor did not improve progression-free survival over the conjugate alone in the population unselected for PD-L1. The PD-L1-positive subgroup pointed in the favourable direction without reaching significance. Added to chemotherapy in disease selected on PD-L1, the checkpoint inhibitor lengthened progression-free survival. Overall survival did not follow. Among the single-arm studies, checkpoint blockade has produced responses only where the population was selected for a mutational phenotype.
Limitation
The PD-L1 result is a key secondary analysis, confined to the patients who had evaluable tissue and scored positive on it. It did not reach significance. Its own authors describe it as a trend for larger randomised trials to test rather than as a result. Eligibility also allowed entry with no chemotherapy at all for metastatic disease, and much of the enrolled population came in on that basis. The trial therefore sits earlier in the course than the line at which this conjugate is registered in this subtype. A single line label is covering a mixed population. The single-arm combination that sits beside it cannot establish what its checkpoint inhibitor added, as its authors say themselves, so it supplies no counterweight in either direction. The mutational-burden study selected on a threshold that its own correlative subgroup was too small to fix. It says that selection matters without saying where the line falls. The conjugate comparison here is phase II and was not sized to exclude the size of effect a phase III would chase. The phase III with chemotherapy as the partner was cut in size when PD-L1 positivity proved less common than planned. Its partner is not a conjugate, so it cannot stand in for the conjugate question. The phase III now putting the same addition ahead of chemotherapy is the trial that will settle it.

ASCENT-07 tests sacituzumab govitecan against chemotherapy in endocrine-resistant disease with no prior chemotherapy for metastatic disease. That is the slot DESTINY-Breast06 now occupies, so a positive result would create a genuine choice between two conjugates at one decision point, with no trial comparing them.

MK-2870-010 does two things at once. It brings a third Trop-2 conjugate into that same slot, carrying a topoisomerase I inhibitor payload again, so it extends this chapter's problem rather than solving it. It also carries a checkpoint-inhibitor arm, which makes it the phase 3 test of the addition that SACI-IO HR+ could not exclude at phase 2.

TRADE-DXd is the sequencing trial among the three. Sequencing conjugates and the cross-resistance problem sets out what it can add to the one prospective sequencing study that has already reported.

Three gaps will remain after all three report.

No trial randomises the choice between leaving endocrine-based therapy and continuing it, which is the decision that precedes everything in this chapter.

No assay in clinical use predicts which patient retains sensitivity after a topoisomerase I payload, and none of the running trials will produce one.

And no conjugate with randomised evidence in this population carries a payload from a different class. Until one does, sequencing decisions will continue to rest on target expression, which answers one question out of six Mechanisms of conjugate resistance.
