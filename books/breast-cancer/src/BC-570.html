Metastatic HER2-positive disease

A conjugate now holds the first line, which leaves every later result measured in a population nobody treats any more.

1 · First-line therapy and maintenance

The first line in this disease was assembled in layers, and the table below is those layers in order. Three of its trials tested adding an antibody to chemotherapy. Two tested replacing the chemotherapy with a conjugate. Two tested adding an oral inhibitor to the antibodies. One has no comparator at all. First-line trials in HER2-positive metastatic disease, oldest layer last. The line-by-line trial record, including the trials that added a kinase inhibitor to the antibody pair, is argued in BC-900.

Every randomised addition here worked in the same direction. H0648g and M77001 added trastuzumab to chemotherapy and each lengthened both time to progression and survival8,7. CLEOPATRA then added pertuzumab to trastuzumab and a taxane and did the same5. That is the whole of the mature survival evidence in this section, and it is why the taxane triplet held the position for a decade. The 2015 confirmatory analysis quoted in HER2-directed antibodies is an earlier data cut of CLEOPATRA and not a competing estimate9.

The substitution question has been asked twice and answered in opposite directions. MARIANNE replaced the taxane with trastuzumab emtansine and reached only non-inferiority against trastuzumab plus a taxane, including in the arm that added pertuzumab to the conjugate6. DESTINY-Breast09 replaced the taxane with trastuzumab deruxtecan and beat the full triplet1. The easy reading is that trial conduct improved over the intervening decade. It is the wrong one. MARIANNE ran against the weaker of the two control arms, because its comparator carried no pertuzumab. A conjugate that cannot beat the weaker comparator, set beside one that beats the stronger, separates the two drugs rather than the two designs. The chemistry behind that separation is in Antibody-drug conjugates and targeted delivery.

PHILA and HER2CLIMB-05 both added an oral HER2 inhibitor, and they are not the same experiment. PHILA added pyrotinib to trastuzumab and docetaxel in Chinese patients, against a control arm with no pertuzumab in it3,10. HER2CLIMB-05 added tucatinib to trastuzumab and pertuzumab maintenance after induction, against the regimen a clinic actually gives2. Part of the PHILA separation is the contribution pertuzumab was never allowed to make in that trial. The difference between the two results is the control arm before it is the drug, and the toxicity that decides each is different too. Pyrotinib produced grade 3 or higher treatment-related events in 90% of patients against 76% on placebo3. Tucatinib produced diarrhoea in 72.7% of a maintenance population who were otherwise well2.

The decision rests on DESTINY-Breast09, because it is the only trial in the table randomised against the thing the clinic would otherwise do. Two features belong with its result. Overall survival was not the primary endpoint and was not mature at the reported interim analysis. Adjudicated drug-related interstitial lung disease or pneumonitis occurred in 12.1% of patients on the conjugate combination against 1.0% on the taxane triplet, with two deaths1.

Four things the table cannot show. The third group in DESTINY-Breast09 received the conjugate without pertuzumab and is still blinded, so what pertuzumab contributes to the new first line is unknown. No trial randomises the conjugate strategy against the tucatinib maintenance strategy, and a patient cannot be given both. PERUSE carries a long median overall survival in a single arm, which describes who enrolled rather than what the regimen did4. And none of these trials enrolled patients progressing on or shortly after adjuvant conjugate therapy, which is now a growing group with no first-line evidence of its own. HER2 eligibility was also assayed locally in the oldest of these trials and centrally in the newest, so the populations differ by measurement as well as by era.

Caution

A chemotherapy-free first line is the obvious headline and it is not quite what the trial tested. The conjugate carries a cytotoxic payload, so what has changed is delivery rather than the presence of chemotherapy. What is genuinely lost is the finite induction model. The taxane triplet gives chemotherapy for a defined period and then stops, leaving antibody maintenance. The conjugate continues until progression. For a patient facing years of treatment, the gain in median progression-free survival and a continuous 12.1% pneumonitis risk are being traded against a regimen with a known exit5,1.

This chapter is about how to approach the patient in front of you. Its subjects are the shape of the first regimen, what each choice costs at the next progression, when the target is worth remeasuring and when to stop. HER2-directed therapy in metastatic disease from first line onwards owns the line-by-line trial record and is where each comparison is argued in full. Central nervous system disease has its own chapter in Central nervous system metastatic disease, and hormone receptor-positive HER2-positive disease is developed in Metastatic triple-positive disease. The tables here are generated from the trial registry and filtered on the decision each section argues, so a figure stays in the prose only where the sentence is about that figure.

2 · Sequencing HER2-directed agents after first progression

Second-line treatment is the position where the evidence is strongest. It is also the position the first-line change has damaged most.

DESTINY-Breast03 compared trastuzumab deruxtecan with trastuzumab emtansine after trastuzumab and a taxane. The effect on progression is one of the largest in the disease, and survival followed it11,12. The figures are in the table in Antibody-drug conjugates and the reordering of later lines and the comparison is argued in HER2-directed therapy in metastatic disease from first line onwards.

That result was measured in a population in which everyone was conjugate-naive. If trastuzumab deruxtecan becomes the first-line standard on the strength of DESTINY-Breast09, no randomised trial describes what should follow it.

The practical consequence is that second line now has two answers, and which one applies is set by what came first. After a taxane triplet, the answer is trastuzumab deruxtecan on direct randomised evidence. After first-line trastuzumab deruxtecan, the answer is inferred. The candidates are a tyrosine kinase inhibitor combination Tyrosine kinase inhibitor selection, trastuzumab emtansine, or a non-cross-resistant conjugate. None has been tested in that position, and the inference rests on the class argument in Resistance after conjugate exposure rather than on a trial.

Caution

Trial evidence attaches to a population, not to a line number. EMILIA put trastuzumab emtansine into the second line against a lapatinib doublet that no clinic would now offer, and DESTINY-Breast03 then displaced it in a population that had never seen a conjugate. Reporting either as establishing a line number was accurate only while the line above it stood still. Whenever a regimen moves earlier, every downstream result loses the population it was measured in.

3 · Antibody-drug conjugates and the reordering of later lines

Three conjugates now act in this disease and they are not interchangeable. Trastuzumab emtansine carries a non-cleavable linker and a microtubule payload with no bystander effect. Trastuzumab deruxtecan carries a cleavable linker and a topoisomerase-I payload that crosses membranes. The chemistry and its consequences are developed in Antibody-drug conjugates and targeted delivery.

Randomised conjugate trials at first progression. Read the population column first, because the prior-therapy requirement is what places each result in a sequence rather than the line number on the label.

Five randomised comparisons sit in that cell and they answer two different questions. Three ask which conjugate to give, and against what. Two hold trastuzumab emtansine constant in both arms and ask what to add to it. Effect sizes from the two groups do not belong on one axis.

Where the first three agree is narrow and firm. Wherever a conjugate was randomised against the accepted treatment of its own day, progression was delayed by a wide margin. That held against a lapatinib doublet in EMILIA, against a capecitabine doublet after emtansine failure in DESTINY-Breast02, and against emtansine itself in DESTINY-Breast0316,14,11. The spread between those three results is therefore partly a spread in how weak each comparator was, and partly a spread in what patients had already received. That second variable is what the population column records, and it is the one to read first.

The two add-on trials are the only pair in the table with an identical control arm. HER2CLIMB-02 and KATE2 both gave trastuzumab emtansine to everyone, in patients previously treated with trastuzumab and a taxane. Adding a selective HER2 kinase inhibitor delayed progression. Adding a checkpoint inhibitor did not, and the study was unmasked early for futility13,15. With design, backbone and population matched, that difference is mechanism rather than trial construction. It is the one place in this chapter where two trials can fairly be compared with each other.

The second-line decision rests on DESTINY-Breast03. It randomised against the drug that was the standard in exactly its own population, and the survival result followed the progression result17. Nothing else in the table carries that combination.

Four limits travel with the rest of it. DESTINY-Breast02 has a direction for overall survival and no magnitude on the record, so it cannot be set against the survival results either side of it14. EMILIA's tabulated survival comes from the second interim analysis, while the 2017 final analysis gave medians of 29.9 against 25.9 months with a hazard ratio of 0.7518. That is one trial reported twice rather than two estimates in conflict. KATE2 is a phase 2 study with no survival data at all and belongs in the table as a negative result rather than as a comparator. And both add-on trials were built on a backbone that DESTINY-Breast03 has since displaced, so their question has not been asked again on a deruxtecan backbone.

The reordering that followed DESTINY-Breast03 was not simply that one drug beat another. It was that a conjugate whose payload kills neighbouring cells outperformed one whose payload does not, in a disease where antigen expression is not uniform. That is the heterogeneity argument arriving as a treatment result, and the evidence for it across the expression range comes from DAISY19.

Trastuzumab emtansine has not disappeared. It retains a defined role in residual disease after neoadjuvant therapy, which is an early-disease question and belongs to Early HER2-positive disease. In the metastatic setting it is now a later option rather than a second-line standard.

What no trial answers is what a second conjugate is worth after the first has failed. The available evidence is retrospective. Among 85 patients who received both trastuzumab deruxtecan and sacituzumab govitecan in either order, progression-free survival on the second conjugate was shorter than on the first in 75.2%. Fourteen of the 85 were still on treatment at the data cutoff20. Activity was retained and the expected magnitude was smaller. The reasoning is developed in Cross-resistance between classes and what it means for sequencing.

4 · Tyrosine kinase inhibitor selection

Three oral inhibitors are in use and they differ in selectivity, which determines both their toxicity and where they are useful.

Tucatinib is highly selective for HER2 and spares EGFR. That is why its dose-limiting toxicity is not the rash and diarrhoea that constrain the less selective agents. HER2CLIMB added it to trastuzumab and capecitabine after trastuzumab, pertuzumab and trastuzumab emtansine, lengthening both progression-free and overall survival21. The trial record for the class is in HER2-directed therapy in metastatic disease from first line onwards.

Neratinib is an irreversible pan-HER inhibitor. Its EGFR activity produces diarrhoea severe enough to require prophylaxis, and it is also the reason it has activity in HER2-mutant disease HER2-mutant disease as a separate therapeutic problem.

Lapatinib is reversible and dual, and is now largely displaced.

The selection logic is short. Central nervous system disease favours tucatinib, on the evidence assembled in Central nervous system metastatic disease. HER2-mutant disease favours neratinib. Otherwise the choice is driven by toxicity tolerance and by what the patient can take orally.

One point from resistance biology belongs here. Antibodies and kinase inhibitors are escaped by different routes, with distinct contributions from oestrogen receptor signalling and from receptor reactivation22. Failure of an antibody is therefore weak evidence about a kinase inhibitor, and the converse holds. Class detail is in Targeted therapy and mechanism in HER2-directed therapy resistance across antibodies and kinase inhibitors.

5 · Central nervous system active strategies

Brain metastases occur in a large minority of patients with HER2-positive metastatic disease, and for many years the systemic options were assumed to be inactive there. That assumption was wrong. Both a selective kinase inhibitor and a bystander-capable conjugate have intracranial activity, with the trials that establish it collected in Central nervous system metastatic disease.

Only the decisions that change a systemic plan belong in this chapter. There are two of them.

The first is that a demonstrated intracranial response does not remove the need for local therapy. A symptomatic or structurally threatening lesion still needs radiotherapy or surgery. What systemic activity has changed is how often whole-brain radiotherapy can be deferred.

The second is that the two agents most often placed side by side are not supported by the same kind of evidence. One has a randomised between-arm comparison. The other has a single-arm rate. That distinction, and what an intracranial response rate can carry, is worked through in Reading an intracranial response rate, and what the cohort contributes to it. Leptomeningeal disease and the rest of organ-specific management are in Central nervous system metastatic disease and Organ-specific metastases and complications.

6 · Hormone receptor-positive HER2-positive disease and endocrine integration

Roughly half of HER2-positive disease is also hormone receptor positive, and it has been treated for years as though the second receptor were incidental. It is not.

The biological reason is reciprocal. Oestrogen receptor signalling is upregulated as an adaptive survival mechanism in HER2-positive tumours under anti-HER2 therapy23. Blocking one pathway increases dependence on the other. That predicts that blocking both should work better than blocking either, and it predicts that endocrine maintenance should not be optional.

PATINA tested the strongest version of that idea by adding palbociclib to HER2-targeted and endocrine maintenance after induction chemotherapy. Median progression-free survival was 44.3 months with palbociclib against 29.1 months without it24. The trial and its comparators are argued in Metastatic triple-positive disease.

What belongs here is the price. Grade 3 adverse events occurred in 79.7% of patients on palbociclib against 30.6% without it, predominantly neutropenia24. Those events are being incurred in a maintenance setting, in patients who are well and who have already finished their chemotherapy. Overall survival has not been reported, so the trade is currently a progression endpoint against a toxicity rate.

Real-world data support endocrine maintenance itself rather than the addition of palbociclib25. Heterogeneity in both receptors, and why a report describing both as positive has averaged twice, is in HER2 heterogeneity and hormone receptor signaling in triple-positive disease. Pathway cross-talk is in HER family signaling.

7 · Heterogeneous or changing HER2 expression

HER2-positive is a binary category applied to a continuous and spatially variable quantity. Two consequences follow for metastatic management.

The first is heterogeneity at diagnosis. A tumour can contain amplified and unamplified compartments, and the arrangement matters as much as the proportion. In a neoadjuvant study of trastuzumab emtansine with pertuzumab, no patient with HER2 heterogeneity achieved a pathologic complete response against 55% of those without it26. The full argument is in HER2 heterogeneity.

The second is change over time. A biopsy at progression that reports HER2-negative disease is frequently an accurate measurement of what treatment has selected, not a laboratory error HER2 loss as an acquired resistance mechanism. Preclinical models with mixed high and low expressing populations reproduce this during the evolution of resistance27.

Layering HER2 blockade and choosing what follows it

Can the next drug be chosen by measuring the target rather than by counting previous lines?

Rationale
A patient's HER2 status is usually assigned once, on the primary tumour, by an immunohistochemistry cut-off chosen to select for trastuzumab. Metastatic deposits are not obliged to match the primary, and they are not obliged to match each other. If the antigen can be measured in the disease actually being treated, the choice of the next drug could rest on the target rather than on the treatment history.
Experiment
Three ways of measuring rather than counting. Imaging the antigen with a labelled antibody before giving a conjugate, read alongside an early metabolic scan. An early metabolic response read embedded in the chemotherapy-free dual blockade study. A conjugate given to prespecified cohorts defined by immunohistochemistry score, from overexpressing through low down to no detectable staining. Set against those, the registration study that gave a newer conjugate to disease that had already failed an older conjugate aimed at the same antigen.
Finding
Antigen imaging found patients whose lesions took up no tracer, and found uneven uptake within single patients. Its predictive values against radiological response improved when the early metabolic scan was read with it. In the chemotherapy-free study, absence of an early metabolic response predicted failure to respond by imaging criteria, and did so in both cohorts. The conjugate produced responses in every staining cohort, most often in the overexpressing one and not rarely in the cohort scored as non-expressing. Failure of one conjugate aimed at HER2 did not predict failure of the next.
Limitation
These are measurement studies rather than allocation trials. A predictive value is computed in one cohort all treated the same way, against a radiological definition of response. It describes how a scan tracks an outcome rather than showing that acting on the scan changes one. Nobody has randomised patients to be allocated by imaging. DAISY's cohorts were cut from a central immunohistochemistry score, so the boundary between them is an assay convention rather than a biological class. Each cohort was single-arm, so activity in the non-expressing cohort may be reporting the floor of that assay rather than activity without the target. The metabolic read in TBCRC 003 sat inside a single-arm study, which gives its predictive claim the same shape. DESTINY-Breast01 had no control arm, so it cannot say whether the second conjugate worked because the antigen persisted or because its payload differs.

Three studies tried to replace the count of previous lines with a measurement of the target, and they agree on what they found. The antigen is measurable, it is unevenly distributed within a single patient, and measuring it does not yet allocate treatment. ZEPHIR imaged HER2 with a labelled antibody before a conjugate. It found patients whose lesions took up no tracer, and it found uneven uptake within individual patients28. TBCRC 003 read an early metabolic response inside a chemotherapy-free dual blockade study, where absence of that response predicted failure to respond by imaging criteria in both cohorts29. DAISY gave a conjugate to cohorts cut by central immunohistochemistry score and produced responses in every cohort, including the one scored as non-expressing19.

They diverge in what they measured rather than in what they showed. None of them is an allocation trial. A predictive value is computed in one cohort all treated the same way, against a radiological definition of response. It describes how a scan tracks an outcome. It does not establish that acting on the scan changes one, and nobody has randomised patients to be allocated by imaging.

DESTINY-Breast01 carries the conclusion that actually changed practice, and it measured nothing. It gave a deruxtecan conjugate to disease that had already failed a conjugate aimed at the same antigen, and objective response occurred in 112 of 184 patients30. Failure of one HER2 conjugate does not predict failure of the next. Because there was no control arm, the trial cannot say whether that is persistence of the antigen or a difference in payload.

What none of this supports is a threshold. DAISY's cohorts were cut from a central immunohistochemistry score, so the boundary between them is an assay convention rather than a biological class. Each cohort was single-arm, so activity in the non-expressing cohort may be reporting the floor of that assay as much as activity without the target. The reproducibility of the boundary itself is poor31,32. The generalised version of this argument is Expression and alteration-defined treatment across subtypes.

The practical rule is to obtain recent tissue before a decision that depends on expression level, and to treat the result as a statement about the biopsied lesion at that time. A falling HER2 result does not mean that HER2-directed treatment is finished. Receptor discordance and how to interpret it is in Receptor discordance and conversion.

Interplay

HER2-positive disease is where the heterogeneity argument of Part VI is most directly operational. The mean expression level determines eligibility. The variance determines what survives. A conjugate with a membrane-permeable payload converts part of that variance problem into a delivery solution, which is why bystander-capable agents outperformed non-bystander agents in a disease whose antigen is unevenly distributed12,19. Mean and variance are separate axes, and current reporting gives only the mean.

8 · HER2-mutant disease as a separate therapeutic problem

Activating HER2 mutations occur in tumours that are not HER2-amplified, and they were identified as oncogenic drivers in gene-amplification-negative breast cancer33. This is a different disease from HER2-positive breast cancer and it is regularly filed under the same heading.

Three distinctions matter. The alteration is a point mutation, not an amplification. The tumours are usually hormone receptor positive and HER2-negative by standard testing, so they belong clinically to Metastatic hormone receptor-positive, HER2-negative disease. And the mutation is enriched in lobular histology, which makes sequencing worthwhile in that setting Invasive lobular disease in the metastatic setting.

The drug that works is an irreversible pan-HER inhibitor rather than an antibody. In a basket study, neratinib produced responses across HER2-mutant and HER3-mutant cancers, with response depending on the specific allele34. The breast-specific result comes from the same programme. Seventy-one patients had hormone receptor positive, HER2-mutant metastatic breast cancer and had progressed on a CDK4/6 inhibitor. The 57 of them who received neratinib with fulvestrant and trastuzumab had an objective response rate of 39%, with a 95% confidence interval of 26% to 52%. Both that figure and the median progression-free survival are computed on those 57 and not on the 71. No responses occurred with fulvestrant alone or with fulvestrant and trastuzumab35. The last sentence is what establishes that the kinase inhibitor is doing the work.

Two limits belong with that result. The study was single-arm within a basket, and 57 patients is a small denominator for a response rate. And longitudinal circulating tumour DNA showed acquisition of further HER2 alterations and of PIK3CA mutations at progression35, which places this branch alongside the others rather than outside them. The wider question of whether blocking a mutant receptor restores endocrine sensitivity is developed in Endocrine therapy and receptor degraders in metastatic disease.

9 · Resistance after conjugate exposure

Progression on a conjugate is not one event. A conjugate has to bind, internalise, traffic, release payload, retain it and kill with it. Antigen expression answers the first step only, and no routine assay measures the others. The mechanistic account is in Conjugate resistance, antigen loss, payload efflux, and repair capacity.

Two experimental results define the shape of the problem. Cell line models selected for resistance to trastuzumab emtansine failed by unrelated routes, one through reduced HER2 with MDR1 upregulation and the other through loss of the lysosomal transporter SLC46A3 with PTEN deficiency. The rescues were not interchangeable36. In patients, PTEN loss was associated with de novo resistance to trastuzumab deruxtecan. The hazard ratio was 3.20, with a 95% confidence interval of 1.47 to 6.97. For sacituzumab govitecan the hazard ratio was 1.18, with an interval crossing one20. The same payload class, and a genomic lesion that affected only one of the two agents.

The practical position is uncomfortable and should be stated. A patient progressing on trastuzumab deruxtecan may have lost antigen, may have lost payload handling, or may have lost a downstream dependency. Only the first is measurable in clinic. Measuring HER2 and finding it retained does not establish that a different HER2-directed conjugate will work.

What follows in practice is an antibody-independent or payload-independent option. A tyrosine kinase inhibitor combination changes both the delivery mechanism and the mode of killing Tyrosine kinase inhibitor selection. That is a stronger argument than switching to a second conjugate with the same payload class.

10 · Treatment beyond progression and when to stop HER2 blockade

The convention in this disease is that HER2 blockade continues across lines while the backbone changes. It is a well-founded convention and it is worth knowing what founds it.

The argument is that HER2 amplification is a truncal event in most of these tumours, so the dependency usually persists even when a particular agent stops working. That is a claim about the genome and it is generally right. The claim it does not support is that continued blockade is therefore always beneficial, because dependence is a separate question from presence HER2-directed therapy resistance across antibodies and kinase inhibitors. A stable HER2 result at progression establishes that the antigen is still there.

Three situations argue for stopping.

The first is documented loss of the target on recent tissue, in a patient whose disease is now behaving like HER2-negative disease. That is selection rather than error HER2 loss as an acquired resistance mechanism, and it moves the patient to a different chapter.

The second is cardiac. Cumulative anthracycline exposure and years of antibody therapy produce declines in left ventricular function that are sometimes not recoverable. Cardiac surveillance and its management are in Treatment toxicity and supportive care.

The third is that continuing blockade is being used as a substitute for a conversation. Antibody therapy is easy to continue and hard to stop, because stopping is legible to the patient as a decision in a way that continuing is not.

In practice

Reassess HER2 on recent tissue before assuming the target is still present, particularly after conjugate exposure.

Do not treat a retained HER2 result as evidence that a conjugate will work again. It answers one of six questions Conjugate resistance, antigen loss, payload efflux, and repair capacity.

Check left ventricular function on schedule rather than on symptoms, and treat a fall as a reason to reconsider the whole regimen rather than to pause one drug.

When a patient has progressed through conjugates, kinase inhibitors and antibodies, say so plainly. Continuing an antibody alongside best supportive care is a reasonable choice made explicitly. It is a poor substitute for the conversation in Palliative and end-of-life care.
