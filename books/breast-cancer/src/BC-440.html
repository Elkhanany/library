Part VIII · Therapeutic foundations · Chapter 45

Cytotoxic chemotherapy

The lesion each drug makes, and why it predicts both the resistance and the toxicity.

1 · Major drug classes and mechanisms

Cytotoxic drugs are usefully sorted by the lesion they make rather than by the era they arrived in. The lesion predicts the resistance mechanism and it predicts the dose-limiting toxicity, so one fact does two jobs.

Anthracyclines poison topoisomerase II, intercalate into DNA and generate reactive oxygen species. The first of those is the antitumour mechanism. The third is the cardiac one, which is why cardiac risk tracks cumulative dose rather than dose intensity1.

Taxanes stabilise microtubules and arrest cells in mitosis. Neuropathy follows from the same mechanism acting on axonal transport in long sensory neurons, which is why it is cumulative and frequently irreversible2.

Non-taxane microtubule agents destabilise rather than stabilise. Eribulin inhibits microtubule dynamics through a distinct mode, and it retains activity after taxane exposure3.

Alkylating agents and platinum salts create adducts and interstrand crosslinks that require homologous recombination for repair. That is why germline BRCA status predicts platinum benefit and why a genomic scar score does not4.

Antimetabolites act in S phase. Capecitabine is a prodrug, so its exposure depends on host enzyme activity rather than on tumour biology. Dihydropyrimidine dehydrogenase deficiency is a germline property of the patient, and genotype-guided dosing reduces the toxicity that follows from it5.

The organising claim is that each class has a matched pair of consequences. A drug that damages DNA is escaped by repair. A drug that acts on the spindle is escaped by efflux or by not dividing. Neither is escaped the way a targeted agent is, which is why no assay predicts cytotoxic sensitivity in this disease.

2 · Regimen selection and scheduling

The adjuvant meta-analyses settle more than the individual trials do, and one result inside them is almost never quoted. The earliest overviews established that polychemotherapy reduces recurrence and mortality before any regimen comparison could6,7.

Adding four cycles of a taxane to a fixed anthracycline-based regimen reduced breast cancer mortality. The rate ratio was 0.86 with a standard error of 0.04. Some trials counterbalanced those extra taxane cycles in the control arm with extra cycles of other cytotoxic drugs, roughly doubling the non-taxane dose. In those trials there was no significant difference, with a rate ratio of 0.94 and a standard error of 0.068. Part of what looks like a taxane class effect in the first comparison is a total-dose effect.

The same analysis separates regimens by cumulative dose rather than by name. Standard four-cycle anthracycline and cyclophosphamide was equivalent to standard CMF, with a rate ratio of 0.98. The trials behind that row agree. Four cycles of doxorubicin and cyclophosphamide matched six of CMF in NSABP B-159, and the two did not differ in premenopausal node-positive women in another trial10. Intensifying cyclophosphamide within the doxorubicin combination improved nothing11. Higher-dose epirubicin with cyclophosphamide beat classical CMF, and the lower epirubicin dose did not, which is a dose-response inside one trial12. Anthracycline regimens with substantially higher cumulative dose were superior to standard CMF, with a rate ratio of 0.78 and a standard error of 0.068. Regimen names encode dose, and comparisons between names are often comparisons between doses.

Schedule is a separate axis from dose. Weekly paclitaxel after standard adjuvant doxorubicin and cyclophosphamide improved disease-free and overall survival compared with paclitaxel every three weeks, with an odds ratio for survival of 1.32. Grade 2, 3 or 4 neuropathy was more common on the weekly schedule, at 27% against 20%13. More frequent administration of a spindle agent buys efficacy and costs nerve. With longer follow-up the advantage persisted in smaller form, and an exploratory analysis suggested a substantial benefit in triple-negative disease14.

Other additions have been tested and mostly failed. Docetaxel added to doxorubicin and cyclophosphamide improved five-year disease-free survival from 68% to 75% against fluorouracil in the same combination15. Capecitabine added to a docetaxel, epirubicin and cyclophosphamide regimen did not significantly improve recurrence-free survival16. How much any of this matters depends on receptor status. In node-positive disease, the gains from successive generations of chemotherapy were concentrated in ER-negative tumours17.

Platinum is a selection question rather than a scheduling question in advanced triple-negative disease, and the selection that works is germline BRCA status4.

3 · Dose intensity, dose density, and dose modification

Three quantities are routinely conflated and they come apart in practice. Dose intensity is dose per unit time. Dose density is the same total dose delivered at shorter intervals. Cumulative dose is the total received over the course. A regimen can raise one and lower another.

The randomised demonstration compared dose-dense and conventionally scheduled adjuvant therapy in node-positive disease. Dose-dense treatment improved disease-free survival, with a risk ratio of 0.74, and overall survival, with a risk ratio of 0.69. Four-year disease-free survival was 82% against 75%. There was no difference between concurrent and sequential administration and no interaction between density and sequence. Severe neutropenia was less frequent on the dose-dense regimens than on the conventional ones18. The last of those is counterintuitive and it is the reason the strategy was adoptable.

The patient-level meta-analysis puts a size on it. Data were available for 37,298 of 40,070 women randomised in 26 trials. Ten-year recurrence risk was 28.0% with dose-intense treatment against 31.4% with standard scheduling, giving a rate ratio of 0.86 and a 95% confidence interval of 0.82 to 0.89. Ten-year breast cancer mortality was 18.9% against 21.3%, and all-cause mortality was 22.1% against 24.8%. In the seven trials comparing two-weekly with three-weekly delivery of the same chemotherapy, ten-year recurrence was 24.0% against 28.3% among 10,004 women19.

The objection this is usually met with is that intensification kills people. The meta-analysis answers it directly. Ten-year risk of death without recurrence was 4.1% with dose-intense treatment and 4.6% with standard scheduling, with a rate ratio of 0.88 and an interval of 0.78 to 0.9919. Mortality from causes other than breast cancer was not higher.

Dose modification is where planned intensity is lost silently. A reduction made during a toxicity episode commonly persists after the toxicity has resolved. The reasoning for treating delivered dose as a standing question rather than an assumption is in Distinguishing resistance from under-dosing, non-adherence, and pseudo-progression.

4 · Neoadjuvant and adjuvant applications

The drugs are the same and the information is not. Preoperative treatment converts a course of chemotherapy into an experiment with a tissue readout, and that readout, residual cancer burden, is what later escalation is built on.

Three trials instantiate the same structure with three different classes. In CREATE-X, capecitabine after residual invasive disease improved five-year disease-free survival, with a hazard ratio for recurrence, second cancer or death of 0.70 and a 95% confidence interval of 0.53 to 0.92. Five-year overall survival was 89.2% against 83.6%, with a hazard ratio for death of 0.59. In the triple-negative subgroup, disease-free survival was 69.8% against 56.1% and overall survival was 78.8% against 70.3%20. In KATHERINE, a conjugate replaced trastuzumab after residual HER2-positive disease21 Antibody-drug conjugates and targeted delivery. In KEYNOTE-522, checkpoint blockade was added before surgery rather than after22,23 Immunotherapy.

The common element is worth naming, because later chapters instantiate it repeatedly. Observed response to treatment is a stronger selection variable in this disease than any baseline biomarker currently available. The de-escalation and escalation framework that follows from that is set out in Principles of multidisciplinary treatment.

Two cautions belong with it. Pathologic complete response is defined by a sampling protocol, so how much of the bed was examined affects the answer. And a trial that escalates only non-responders tells you nothing about whether the escalation would have helped the responders.

5 · Metastatic use and cumulative exposure

The goal changes and so does the arithmetic. Sequential single agents are preferred over combinations unless a rapid response is needed for organ function, because combinations raise response rate and toxicity without reliably extending survival.

The benchmark for late-line single-agent chemotherapy is lower than most conversations assume. One randomised trial compared sacituzumab govitecan with physician's choice of eribulin, vinorelbine, capecitabine or gemcitabine in relapsed metastatic triple-negative disease. Median progression-free survival on the chemotherapy arm was 1.7 months in 233 patients, with a 95% confidence interval of 1.5 to 2.624. That figure is the comparator against which newer classes should be read.

Eribulin is the one agent with a survival result in heavily pretreated disease. Among 762 women randomised 2:1, median overall survival was 13.1 months with eribulin against 10.6 months with physician's choice. The hazard ratio for death was 0.81, with a 95% confidence interval of 0.66 to 0.99. Peripheral neuropathy was the commonest reason for discontinuing eribulin, in 24 of 503 patients treated3.

Cumulative exposure constrains the sequence in three ways. Anthracycline cardiac risk tracks lifetime dose1. Taxane neuropathy accumulates and often does not resolve2. And conjugates now occupy lines that single-agent chemotherapy used to occupy, so cumulative payload exposure has become a version of the same constraint in a new class Payload class, cross-resistance, and sequencing between conjugates.

Body surface area is a poor proxy for the volume in which a drug distributes. Sarcopenia was a determinant of chemotherapy toxicity and of time to tumour progression in metastatic breast cancer25, which means two patients with the same calculated dose can receive quite different exposures.

6 · Resistance mechanisms

Cytotoxic resistance is less often a single lesion than targeted-agent resistance, which is the main reason no assay predicts it. Four mechanisms account for most of it and they are not mutually exclusive.

Efflux moves drug out of the cell. The transporters that do it, including MDR1 and ABCC1, are the same ones that defeat conjugate payloads26,27, which is why conjugate and chemotherapy resistance are not independent problems.

Target alteration changes what the drug acts on. Tubulin isotype composition alters taxane binding, and topoisomerase II level alters anthracycline effect. Neither is measured routinely.

Repair capacity determines what a DNA lesion costs. Restoration of homologous recombination removes platinum sensitivity and PARP inhibitor sensitivity together28, and the detailed argument is in PARP inhibitor and platinum resistance, including restoration of repair.

Drug-tolerant states account for the rest and they are not genetic. A small subpopulation survives through a reversible chromatin-mediated state29. Cells in that state acquire a different dependency, which is itself targetable30. Rare cell variability precedes and predicts which cells will reprogram31. Persisters cycle rather than simply resting32. Metabolic state contributes, with MYC and MCL1 cooperating to sustain chemotherapy-resistant populations through mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation33.

One immune-adjacent mechanism belongs here rather than in Immunotherapy. CD73 promotes anthracycline resistance and poor prognosis in triple-negative disease34, which means a single molecule links cytotoxic escape and adenosine-mediated immune suppression.

The taxonomy that puts these in clinical order, and distinguishes a population that was never sensitive from one that was made resistant, is in A working taxonomy, intrinsic, adaptive, and acquired resistance.

7 · Toxicity mitigation and supportive standards

Four areas have evidence worth acting on, and they differ in how much they change.

Myelosuppression. Growth factor support makes shorter intervals deliverable, and the randomised evidence is that severe neutropenia was less frequent on supported dose-dense regimens than on conventional ones18. The supportive drug changed which schedules were possible rather than making an existing schedule safer.

Alopecia. Scalp cooling has two prospective datasets. In a randomised trial stopped early for efficacy, hair was preserved in 48 of 95 women who received cooling, which is 50.5%, and in 0 of 47 who did not35. In a prospective cohort, hair loss of 50% or less occurred in 67 of 101 evaluable women with cooling and 0 of 16 controls. No participant in the cooling group in that study received an anthracycline36. That exclusion is the reading caution, because it is the anthracycline-containing regimens where patients most want the intervention.

Neuropathy. There is no established preventive agent, so dose modification and schedule choice are the intervention2. Telling a patient that nothing prevents it, early, is more useful than adding a supplement.

Host genotype. Fluoropyrimidine toxicity is partly predictable before the first dose, and genotype-guided dosing based on DPYD reduces it5. This is one of the few places in this chapter where a pre-treatment test changes a decision.

In practice

Three questions are worth asking before each cycle, and none of them is on a standard toxicity form.

What proportion of the planned dose has actually been delivered so far, counting reductions and delays. Planned intensity and received intensity diverge quietly Distinguishing resistance from under-dosing, non-adherence, and pseudo-progression.

Is the current reduction still needed. Reductions made for a resolved episode tend to persist for the rest of the course.

Which toxicity is cumulative and which is not. Neuropathy and cardiac injury accumulate, so a tolerable cycle four is not evidence about cycle eight. Myelosuppression does not accumulate the same way, so it can be managed cycle by cycle.

Cardiac surveillance belongs in the first year after anthracycline exposure. In a cohort of 2625 patients, 98% of cardiotoxicity events occurred within that year. Median onset was 3.5 months after the end of chemotherapy. Recovery was more likely when heart failure therapy was started early1.

Interplay

Cytotoxic therapy is the least selective agent in the book and it still selects. It kills dividing cells, so it enriches for the compartment that is not dividing, and the drug-tolerant states above are exactly that compartment. A heterogeneous tumour therefore survives chemotherapy through a different subpopulation than it survives targeted therapy through, which is why sequence of class changes what is left behind.
