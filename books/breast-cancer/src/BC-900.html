Metastatic therapy from first line onwards

Why the sequence a clinic uses was assembled from trials that never tested it.

1 · Scope, and the line structure this chapter assumes

This chapter enumerates the systemic evidence for HER2-positive metastatic disease, line by line. The decision architecture, meaning how to weigh a patient's disease burden, symptoms and comorbidity against these options, is in When the target changes, and when to stop blocking it. Conjugate mechanism and toxicity are in Antibody-drug conjugates and targeted delivery. Disease that is both hormone receptor-positive and HER2-positive is treated in Metastatic triple-positive disease, because its sequencing question is different. Brain and leptomeningeal disease has its own chapter in Central nervous system metastatic disease.

Two features of this subtype shape everything below. Survival is long enough that a patient may pass through many lines of treatment, so the order matters more here than in any other subtype. And the lines were established sequentially against the standard of their own day. The trial that defined second line enrolled patients who had never received what is now first line.

That second point is the one to hold onto. The evidence base is a stack of comparisons, each of them valid against its own control. The stack does not tell you what to do after a modern first line. Every table below therefore carries the population column, because the population is what dates a comparison and the result column does not.

The word line is doing less work in these trials than a reader expects. Most later-line trials set a minimum prior exposure and capped nothing above it, so a second-line label is a floor rather than a description of who was enrolled. Trials that admit a wide range of prior treatment tend to report a median number of prior regimens well above their stated minimum. Read that median, not the label.

The endpoint column dates a comparison as thoroughly as the population column does. The earliest trials here measured time to progression or response rate. The later ones measure progression-free survival by independent review. Those are not the same object, and a table sorted by year lines them up as though they were.

Read the tables as a record of what was asked rather than as a ranking of the drugs in them. Two rows may sit a generation apart and share no backbone at all. Nothing in the registry adjusts for that and nothing could. The ordering a clinic uses is a reading across the stack, and no trial inside it tested that ordering.

Each section below opens with the question its trials were built to answer, taken from the [Chapter Stories](stories.html) map, and closes with a table filtered on that same question. The story says what was being looked for and what the design cannot settle. The table says what else is in the cell. The prose argues the decision.

2 · First-line dual blockade with a taxane

Advanced disease · HER2+ · any

Layering HER2 blockade and choosing what follows it

Does blocking HER2 at more than one site do more than blocking it at one?

Rationale
HER2 has no ligand of its own. It signals by pairing. Its most active partner is HER3, which is ligand-driven. Trastuzumab binds the membrane-proximal domain and leaves the dimerisation arm exposed. Ligand-driven pairing therefore continues underneath it. A second antibody aimed at that arm, or a small molecule that shuts the kinase down from inside the cell, should close an escape the first antibody never covered.
Experiment
A sequence of first-line randomised trials, each adding one more layer to the layer below. Trastuzumab added to chemotherapy, in a large trial and again in a small one. Then pertuzumab added to trastuzumab and a taxane. Then a HER2 kinase inhibitor added to trastuzumab and a taxane in one trial, and added to trastuzumab with pertuzumab as maintenance in another. A large single-arm study ran alongside them, giving the same antibody pair with whichever taxane the investigator chose.
Finding
Adding the first antibody to chemotherapy delayed progression and lengthened overall survival, against the same chemotherapy given alone. Adding the second antibody did both again, against a single antibody given with the same taxane. Adding a kinase inhibitor delayed progression in both trials that tried it, each against the regimen it was added to. The kinase inhibitor added to the taxane doublet has since reported an overall survival advantage over that doublet. The kinase inhibitor added to the antibody pair as maintenance has not reported overall survival at all. The endpoint is not constant along the chain. The earliest trials measured time to progression or response rate rather than progression-free survival, so a longer interval is not the same measurement at both ends of it. The single-arm study reported tolerability for the antibody pair with each of the three taxanes, and had no comparator to rank them.
Limitation
These trials were run against the standard of their own year, so the additions are not stacked on one backbone. The first layer rests on one large trial with a time-to-progression endpoint and one small trial whose primary endpoint was response rate, so the two are not the same grade of evidence. CLEOPATRA's control arm had none of the drugs that now follow first-line progression. The survival difference it measured therefore belongs to a sequence rather than to pertuzumab alone. PHILA's control arm was trastuzumab and docetaxel without pertuzumab, so it says what a kinase inhibitor adds to a doublet rather than what it adds to the triplet a clinic now starts with. It also enrolled in one country. HER2CLIMB-05 randomised only patients who had not progressed during induction, so its population is selected for disease the backbone already controls. PERUSE had safety as its primary endpoint and no comparator at all, so its efficacy figures describe a cohort rather than test anything.

Does the cytotoxic have to be given as free chemotherapy, or can it travel on the antibody?

Rationale
A conjugate uses the antibody as a delivery vehicle. The cytotoxic should concentrate where the antigen is displayed and spare tissue that does not display it. If that holds at the front of the disease course, the free taxane is doing nothing the conjugate does not do better. The stronger version of the idea drops the cytotoxic altogether, on the argument that a HER2-dependent tumour can be held by blockade alone.
Experiment
Two first-line randomised trials substituted a conjugate for the taxane. One compared trastuzumab emtansine, alone and with pertuzumab, against trastuzumab plus a taxane. The other compared trastuzumab deruxtecan plus pertuzumab against the taxane, trastuzumab and pertuzumab triplet, and carried a third arm of the conjugate alone. An early-phase programme is giving the newer conjugate alone and in combinations in the same line. The chemotherapy-free version of the question was asked twice and separately. A single-arm study gave lapatinib with trastuzumab across two cohorts defined by prior treatment. A randomised trial gave lapatinib with or without trastuzumab in disease progressing on trastuzumab.
Finding
The first-generation conjugate was noninferior to the taxane backbone and was not superior to it, with or without the second antibody. The newer conjugate with the second antibody delayed progression against the triplet. Chemotherapy-free dual blockade produced responses in a cohort untreated for metastatic disease and in a pretreated cohort, more often in the untreated one. Where blockade without chemotherapy was randomised against less blockade without chemotherapy, the pair delayed progression and lengthened overall survival against the single agent.
Limitation
Noninferiority is a claim about a prespecified margin rather than a demonstration that two regimens are the same. MARIANNE's primary publication reported no overall survival at all. DESTINY-Breast09's third arm, the conjugate without pertuzumab, was still blinded at the interim analysis, so nothing yet separates what the conjugate contributes from what the second antibody contributes. Its overall survival is not reported either. DESTINY-Breast07 has no publication on the record, so it names a programme rather than a result. TBCRC 003 is single-arm and its two cohorts were defined by prior treatment, so the gap between their response rates is a difference between populations rather than a comparison. A chemotherapy-free response rate from that era cannot stand as a comparator for a modern chemotherapy-containing regimen. EGF104900 did drop the cytotoxic, but it set its pair against a single kinase inhibitor rather than against any chemotherapy-containing regimen. It therefore says nothing about how chemotherapy-free blockade stands against the triplet a clinic starts with.

Pertuzumab, trastuzumab and a taxane held first line for a decade. The reason is durability rather than response rate. Dual blockade covers the juxtamembrane domain and the dimerisation arm at once, which is the mechanism set out in HER family signaling.

Most randomised rows in that table add one more layer of HER2 blockade to the layer beneath it. Each of those delayed progression against the regimen its extra layer was added to. None of them failed. That is the class finding, and it is the only thing those rows say with one voice. MARIANNE and DESTINY-Breast09 are not layering trials. Each substitutes a conjugate for the free taxane, which is a different question.

What varies down the column is the measurement and the comparator rather than the drug. H0648g reported time to disease progression, the convention when trastuzumab was registered8. M77001 is a phase 2 trial whose primary endpoint was response rate7. The 2013 confirmatory and 2015 final prespecified survival analyses of CLEOPATRA quoted in HER2-directed antibodies are earlier data cuts of that trial rather than competing estimates9,10. Its primary report established the progression-free survival advantage11. From CLEOPATRA onwards the endpoint is progression-free survival, though not by the same reader. CLEOPATRA, MARIANNE and DESTINY-Breast09 used independent central review. PHILA and HER2CLIMB-05 used the investigator. An interval at the head of this table is therefore not the same object as an interval at its foot. The two cannot be read as one scale.

The control arms diverge further than the endpoints do. CLEOPATRA was run against a regimen carrying none of the drugs that now follow first-line progression, which places its survival difference in a whole sequence rather than in pertuzumab alone5. PHILA added a kinase inhibitor to a trastuzumab and docetaxel doublet rather than to the triplet a clinic now starts with3,12. It also enrolled in a single country. Part of its separation is therefore the contribution pertuzumab was never allowed to make in it, and the overall survival it has since reported inherits the same control arm. HER2CLIMB-05 randomised only patients whose disease had not progressed during induction2. Its population is selected for disease the backbone already controls. It describes what a third layer adds to a responder rather than what a three-drug start does for everyone.

The toxicity that decides between those two is different in kind as well as in size. Pyrotinib produced grade 3 or higher treatment-related events in 90% of patients against 76% on placebo3. Tucatinib produced diarrhoea in 72.7% of a maintenance population who were otherwise well2. A rate measured in a maintenance phase is paid by a patient with no symptoms to weigh it against.

DESTINY-Breast09 is the one comparison whose control arm is the taxane triplet a clinic starts with today, which is what makes it load-bearing rather than its effect size1. Its rationale is not that a conjugate is more active, which the later lines had already shown. It is that giving the agent before resistance is selected may convert responders into durable responders. That is the problem set out in Mechanisms of therapeutic resistance. Two features belong with its result. Overall survival was not the primary endpoint and was not mature at the reported interim analysis. And adjudicated drug-related interstitial lung disease or pneumonitis occurred in 12.1% of patients on the conjugate combination against 1.0% on the taxane triplet1.

MARIANNE is the reason to be careful with substitution arguments in general. The first-generation conjugate was noninferior to the taxane backbone and was not superior to it, with or without pertuzumab6. The easy reading of the two substitution trials is that trial conduct improved across the intervening decade, and it is the wrong one. MARIANNE ran against the weaker of the two available control arms, because its comparator carried no pertuzumab. A conjugate that could not beat the weaker comparator, set beside one that beats the stronger, separates the two drugs rather than the two designs. The chemistry behind that separation is in Antibody-drug conjugates and targeted delivery. Noninferiority is a claim about a prespecified margin rather than a demonstration that two regimens are the same Designing de-escalation trials and the non-inferiority margin problem. A failed superiority claim for one conjugate is not evidence about another. The stronger version of the substitution argument drops the cytotoxic rather than carrying it on the antibody, and that version is argued under Maintenance, de-escalation and treatment interruption.

Four things this table cannot show. It carries no overall survival for DESTINY-Breast09, whose third arm, the conjugate without pertuzumab, was still blinded at the interim analysis. Until that arm reads out, nothing separates what the conjugate contributes from what the second antibody contributes. DESTINY-Breast07 is the programme giving the newer conjugate alone and in combinations at this position, and it has no publication on the record. PERUSE gave the antibody pair with whichever taxane the investigator chose, with safety as its primary endpoint and no comparator4. Its preliminary report had already found paclitaxel a valid alternative backbone to docetaxel13. Its figures describe a cohort and cannot rank three taxanes against one another. And no trial here enrolled a patient progressing after a first-line deruxtecan conjugate, which is the population first-line practice is now creating. Central confirmation of HER2 was required in MARIANNE and HER2CLIMB-05. The trials of the first decade record no such step, so these populations differ by measurement as well as by era.

Other partners for the antibody pair were tested in single-arm studies, and none has a comparator. Weekly paclitaxel with trastuzumab and pertuzumab gave a six-month progression-free survival of 86% in 69 patients, with no febrile neutropenia14. Vinorelbine was tested in the two VELVET cohorts. In the first, 106 patients had a response rate of 74.2% and a median progression-free survival of 14.3 months15. In the second, where the two antibodies shared one infusion bag, the figures were 63.7% and 11.5 months16.

The taxane triplet has not become wrong. It remains the regimen with the longest follow-up, the widest availability and the best characterised late toxicity. Pertuzumab did not increase cardiac adverse events in CLEOPATRA. Cardiac adverse events occurred in 14.5% of the pertuzumab arm against 16.4% on placebo, and left ventricular systolic dysfunction in 4.4% against 8.3%17. Nor did it cost quality of life, with no difference in time to a 5-point decline in the FACT-B trial outcome index, at a hazard ratio of 0.9718. Where a deruxtecan conjugate is unavailable, or where baseline lung function makes cumulative pneumonitis risk hard to accept, the older triplet is a defensible first line rather than a compromise.

Caution

Interstitial lung disease is cumulative and does not respect line of therapy. Moving a deruxtecan conjugate earlier lengthens the exposure of patients who will live for years. The toxicity data in Antibody-drug conjugates and targeted delivery were generated in later-line populations with shorter exposure.

A chemotherapy-free first line is the obvious headline and it is not quite what the trial tested. The conjugate carries a cytotoxic payload, so what has changed is delivery rather than the presence of chemotherapy. What is genuinely lost is the finite induction model. The taxane triplet gives chemotherapy for a defined period and then stops, leaving antibody maintenance. The conjugate continues until progression. For a patient facing years of treatment, a gain in median progression-free survival and a continuous pneumonitis risk are being traded against a regimen with a known exit5,1.

3 · Second-line conjugate therapy

Advanced disease · HER2+ · any

Layering HER2 blockade and choosing what follows it

Is HER2 still worth blocking once the disease has progressed on HER2 blockade?

Rationale
Progression on trastuzumab admits two readings. Either the tumour has stopped depending on HER2, in which case the target should be abandoned. Or it has escaped one way of blocking a receptor it still needs, in which case the target should be attacked differently. The two readings imply opposite prescriptions. Only a trial that holds the chemotherapy fixed and varies the blockade can tell them apart.
Experiment
Randomised trials that held the chemotherapy fixed and varied only the HER2 blockade after progression on trastuzumab. Capecitabine with continued trastuzumab against capecitabine alone. Capecitabine with lapatinib against capecitabine alone. Lapatinib with or without trastuzumab in disease already progressing on trastuzumab, with no chemotherapy in either arm. Further down the course, and holding nothing fixed, a conjugate against whatever the physician would otherwise have chosen.
Finding
Both capecitabine trials delayed progression against capecitabine given alone, one by continuing the antibody through progression and one by adding a kinase inhibitor. Blocking the receptor at two sites delayed progression and lengthened overall survival against blocking it at one, in disease already progressing on one of them. Later in the course the conjugate delayed progression against whatever else would have been given. The target survives progression on the drugs aimed at it.
Limitation
These are separate trials with separate control arms. Two of those arms are weak by design. EGF104900 compared lapatinib plus trastuzumab against lapatinib alone, which is not a regimen a clinic would choose for anyone. TH3RESA's comparator was physician's choice in heavily pretreated disease, which is a floor rather than a standard, and it varied the chemotherapy as well as the blockade. GBG 26 randomised a small population against a time-to-progression endpoint. Its overall survival difference was not significant, so it shows that continuing the antibody delays progression rather than that it extends life. EGF100151 stopped at an interim analysis for superiority in time to progression, and its final overall survival did not separate. None of these trials required a repeat biopsy at progression, so the receptor's persistence is inferred from the fact that drugs against it still work rather than measured.

Second-line trials. The two capecitabine trials at the foot of the table are where the principle of blocking HER2 through progression was established.

The principle underneath this line is older than every drug in the table. GBG 26 / BIG 03-05 and EGF100151 each held capecitabine fixed in both arms and varied only the HER2 blockade after progression on trastuzumab25,26. One continued the antibody through progression. The other added a kinase inhibitor. Both delayed progression against capecitabine given alone. That is the evidence that the target survives progression on the drugs aimed at it. It is why the answer to HER2 failure is usually a different way of attacking the same receptor rather than a different target.

Neither of those trials lengthened life. The reason is a property of their designs rather than of the principle. Both used time to progression as the endpoint, which was the era's convention. GBG 26 / BIG 03-05 randomised a small population, and its survival difference was not significant. EGF100151 stopped at an interim analysis for superiority in time to progression, and its final survival analysis did not separate27. A trial stopped early on a progression endpoint is not built to answer a survival question.

The two conjugates in that table are not interchangeable and the difference is chemical. Trastuzumab emtansine carries a non-cleavable linker and a microtubule payload with no bystander effect. Trastuzumab deruxtecan carries a cleavable linker and a topoisomerase-I payload that crosses membranes, so it reaches cells that do not display the antigen. The consequences are developed in Antibody-drug conjugates and targeted delivery. That is the heterogeneity argument arriving as a treatment result, and the evidence for it across the expression range comes from DAISY28.

EMILIA and DESTINY-Breast03 are the same experiment run in two different eras. Each set a conjugate against the standard its own era had established, and each separated on progression and on survival24,21,29. DESTINY-Breast03 is the load-bearing row. Its comparator was the agent EMILIA itself had put in second line, and its population was conjugate-naive30. That lineage is what makes the ranking interpretable, because each step was tested against the thing it replaced.

Two rows are answering a different question from the rest of the table. PHOEBE set one kinase inhibitor against another on a shared capecitabine backbone, in China, so it ranks members of a class rather than asking whether the class belongs at this position22. Its result is a prespecified interim analysis, and the arm it beat has since been displaced from this line by the conjugates above it. A class ranking made against a comparator nobody now offers does not transfer. KATE2 appears here because its backbone is a conjugate. Its question is immunological, and it is argued under Third line and beyond with the other trials that added a mechanism from outside HER2.

HER2CLIMB-02 and KATE2 are the only pair in this chapter with an identical control arm. Both gave trastuzumab emtansine to everyone, in patients previously treated with trastuzumab and a taxane. Adding a selective HER2 kinase inhibitor delayed progression. Adding a checkpoint inhibitor did not, and that trial was unmasked early for futility19,23. With design, backbone and population matched, the difference between them is mechanism rather than trial construction. Both were built on a backbone DESTINY-Breast03 has since displaced, so the question has not been asked again on a deruxtecan backbone.

HER2CLIMB-02 is the row that should slow a reader down. It improved progression-free survival against the conjugate alone, while its interim overall survival point estimate lay on the wrong side of one19. A progression endpoint moving one way and an interim survival estimate moving the other is not a shape on which to build a sequencing decision. Longer follow-up may resolve the discrepancy. Until it does, adding a kinase inhibitor at this position needs a reason beyond the curve it was licensed on.

What the table cannot show is the population second line is about to contain. DESTINY-Breast02 carries a second-line label that is a floor rather than a description, because entry required progression on trastuzumab emtansine and capped nothing before that20. Its overall survival favoured the conjugate, without a published median or hazard ratio, so the size of that advantage is not on the record. EMILIA carries the opposite problem. Its tabulated survival comes from the second interim analysis, while the 2017 final analysis gave medians of 29.9 against 25.9 months with a hazard ratio of 0.7531. That is one trial reported twice rather than two estimates in conflict. No trial above enrolled a patient who had already received a deruxtecan conjugate. If DESTINY-Breast09 moves that agent into first line, this section describes a population that is leaving the clinic.

The practical consequence is that second line now has two answers, and which one applies is set by what came first. After a taxane triplet, the answer is trastuzumab deruxtecan on direct randomised evidence. After first-line trastuzumab deruxtecan, the answer is inferred. The candidates are a tyrosine kinase inhibitor combination Third line and beyond, trastuzumab emtansine, or a non-cross-resistant conjugate. None has been tested in that position, and the inference rests on a class argument rather than on a trial.

None of these trials required a repeat biopsy at progression. The persistence of the receptor is inferred from the fact that drugs against it still work. It is nowhere measured.

What no trial answers is what a second conjugate is worth after the first has failed. The available evidence is retrospective. In a series of patients given both trastuzumab deruxtecan and sacituzumab govitecan in either order, progression-free survival on the second conjugate was usually shorter than on the first32. Activity was retained and the expected magnitude was smaller. The reasoning is developed in Cross-resistance between classes and what it means for sequencing.

Trastuzumab emtansine has not disappeared with all this. It retains a defined role in residual disease after neoadjuvant therapy, which is an early-disease question and belongs to Post-neoadjuvant intensification for residual disease. In the metastatic setting it is now a later option rather than a second-line standard.

In practice

When a patient progresses after a conjugate, the question is not which conjugate next but whether the resistance was to the target, the linker-payload, or the delivery. Antibody-drug conjugates and targeted delivery separates the three. Only the first is addressed by switching target.

Caution

Layering HER2 blockade and choosing what follows it

In what order should the later-line HER2 drugs be given?

Rationale
After the first-line triplet the choices are a conjugate, a kinase inhibitor with an oral cytotoxic, or another antibody. Each was developed against whatever was standard when it entered trial. So the sequence a clinic uses was assembled from a chain of comparisons rather than chosen in any one of them.
Experiment
A conjugate against a kinase inhibitor with capecitabine, after trastuzumab and a taxane. A newer conjugate against that conjugate in the same line. The newer conjugate against physician's choice in disease that had already progressed on the older one. A kinase inhibitor added to the older conjugate. One kinase inhibitor against another with the same oral partner, early in the sequence and again late. A kinase inhibitor added to trastuzumab and capecitabine in heavily pretreated disease.
Finding
Every one of these comparisons favoured the newer agent, or the more heavily blocked arm, on its own progression endpoint. Overall survival did not follow everywhere. It separated for the conjugate against the kinase inhibitor doublet, for the newer conjugate against the older one, and for the kinase inhibitor added to trastuzumab and capecitabine. It did not separate for one kinase inhibitor against another with the same oral partner in the latest line. It ran the wrong way at the interim analysis for the kinase inhibitor added to the older conjugate. The single head-to-head of the two conjugates was decisive enough to move the newer one into second line. The order a clinic now uses is a reading across that chain rather than the result of any trial in it.
Limitation
No trial randomised the order. The controls are era-specific, and EMILIA's kinase inhibitor doublet is no longer what a clinic would put in a control arm. Physician's choice inside a conjugate trial is not the set of options a clinic would now reach for, so the separation measured against it overstates what the drug adds today. DESTINY-Breast02's second-line label is a floor rather than a description, because entry required only progression on trastuzumab emtansine and capped nothing before that. PHOEBE's result is a prespecified interim analysis and its comparator has since been displaced. Can a mechanism other than HER2 blockade be added to extend what HER2 blockade can do?

Rationale
Part of trastuzumab's effect is immune. It engages Fc receptors on effector cells. The response to it tracks with the lymphocytic infiltrate in the tumour. Two routes follow from that. Re-engineer the Fc so it binds the low-affinity receptor allele better, or take the brake off the effector cells with a checkpoint antibody. A third route has nothing to do with the immune system. HER2 amplification is associated with replication stress, which raised the possibility that a PARP inhibitor would work here without a germline BRCA variant.
Experiment
An Fc-engineered anti-HER2 antibody against trastuzumab, each with chemotherapy, randomised in pretreated disease. Pembrolizumab added to trastuzumab in a single-arm study, with PD-L1 scored centrally on a metastatic biopsy and with parallel PD-L1-positive and PD-L1-negative cohorts. The engineered antibody delayed progression against trastuzumab given with the same chemotherapy. Its final overall survival did not favour it. That second table is short because the field has tried this less often than the rationale deserves. Part of trastuzumab's effect is immune. It engages Fc receptors on effector cells, and the response to it tracks with the lymphocytic infiltrate in the tumour.

KATE2 asked the randomised version of the question on a conjugate backbone, and it did not meet its primary endpoint23. It was unmasked early for futility, so follow-up is short on both arms. Neither reported trial closes the immune question. Both enrolled after HER2 blockade had already failed, which is where a lymphocytic infiltrate is least likely to be intact. The design that would settle it puts the checkpoint antibody at the front of the course. Nobody has run that trial in this subtype.

What all of this leaves for the clinic is a ranking assembled from unlike comparisons. An agent with a survival result against an active comparator has a stronger claim than one with a progression result against a floor. Brain disease, residual neuropathy, diarrhoea tolerance and hand-foot syndrome then decide between candidates of similar rank. Those are patient variables rather than trial variables, and no trial in either table helps weigh them.

The compartment now has its own chapter. Central nervous system metastatic disease carries the argument about whether the barrier explains what it is taken to explain. It also sets out how an intracranial response rate depends on who was admitted to the cohort, and what to do about leptomeningeal disease.

Two facts from that chapter bear on the choice of a systemic agent here, and only two. HER2CLIMB admitted patients whose brain metastases were untreated or progressing. Its central nervous system analyses were prespecified rather than found afterwards35. Neratinib with capecitabine had shown intracranial activity in the phase II TBCRC 022 trial, with a composite central nervous system response rate of 49% in its main cohort52. DESTINY-Breast12 gave a full-length antibody conjugate in a single arm, to a cohort with stable or active brain metastases and to a cohort without53. It reported central nervous system progression-free survival in the first of those cohorts. There is no comparator, so it establishes activity in the compartment rather than a rank.

6 · Maintenance, de-escalation and treatment interruption

A patient in prolonged response faces a question the registration trials did not ask. The taxane is usually stopped for cumulative neuropathy while antibody therapy continues. That practice is near-universal and rests on very little randomised evidence. None of the first-line trials randomised the stopping point.

The chemotherapy-free version of the question has been asked twice. Neither answer transfers cleanly. TBCRC 003 gave dual blockade without chemotherapy in two cohorts split by prior treatment. The gap between those cohorts is a difference between populations rather than a comparison54. EGF104900 did randomise chemotherapy-free blockade, against a single kinase inhibitor rather than against any chemotherapy-containing regimen39. Neither of them speaks to removing the taxane from a modern triplet in a patient who is responding to it.

HER2CLIMB-05 is the closest thing this subtype has to a maintenance trial, because it randomised after induction rather than at diagnosis2. What it adds is a drug rather than permission to remove one. Nothing in the record tests subtraction directly.

Whether HER2-directed therapy itself can ever stop is unresolved. It is the same question Unresolved questions and future directions raises about defining cure in metastatic disease. No assay distinguishes eradicated disease from controlled disease, so the decision is made without the information that would settle it. Endocrine maintenance after induction does have randomised support in the hormone receptor-positive half of this subtype, and that evidence is in Metastatic triple-positive disease.

Interplay

Every de-escalation decision in this section is taken on a HER2 measurement made once, usually on the primary tumour, against a threshold chosen to select for trastuzumab. Continuing blockade through progression works because the antigen persists in most patients. The ones in whom it does not are not identified by anything in routine use, so they are treated as though they were the others. That is the ceiling argued in Where heterogeneity sets a ceiling on precision medicine appearing as a treatment-duration problem rather than a diagnostic one.

Does an activating HER2 mutation found at progression change what to give?

Rationale

Experiment

Responses occurred in each of these cohorts, in a population defined by the mutation rather than by amplification. No overall survival is reported anywhere in this table, and none of these designs could produce one.

Acquired HER2 mutation is also a described resistance mechanism to endocrine therapy, which connects this section to the evolution argument in Temporal heterogeneity and clonal evolution. The practical consequence is a testing decision rather than a treatment one. A patient with hormone receptor-positive disease progressing through successive endocrine lines is a patient in whom sequencing may find a target the original pathology report was never designed to look for. The mutation is also enriched in lobular histology, which makes sequencing worth requesting there on the histology alone Invasive lobular disease in the metastatic setting.

8 · Unresolved questions and what reads out next

Four questions matter most and none of them has an answer.

What follows first-line trastuzumab deruxtecan. If DESTINY-Breast09 moves that agent forward, every line below it rests on trials that enrolled patients for whom it was still available. Nobody has randomised anything in the post-conjugate setting. DESTINY-Breast07 does not fill that gap. It gives the conjugate alone and in combinations in first line, and it has no publication on the record.

Whether conjugates carrying the same payload class are cross-resistant. Practice already sequences them and the evidence does not yet support that practice. This is the problem flagged in Antibody-drug conjugates and targeted delivery and generalised in Unresolved questions and future directions.

Whether a mechanism other than HER2 blockade can extend what HER2 blockade does. TBCRC 050 is testing a PARP inhibitor without germline selection, on the argument that amplification brings replication stress. Nothing beyond conference material has been published. The immune route has been tried only in late lines, which is the least favourable place in the disease course to test it.

Whether HER2-directed therapy can be stopped in prolonged responders. No trial has asked the question in a randomised design. That absence reflects how hard such a trial is to run rather than how important the question is to the patients who would be in it. A patient in year six of continuous antibody therapy is being treated on an extrapolation from year one.
