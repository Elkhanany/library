Part XIII · Triple-negative disease · Chapter 79

The biomarker triage, and what is left when it finds nothing

Three positive markers now organise a category defined by absence, and most of the work of this chapter is with the patients in whom none of the three is found.

1 · Biomarker-driven treatment selection

This chapter is about how to approach the patient. Risk, disease burden, symptom load, biomarker triage and what the line of treatment is for. The trial record itself, arranged by line, is in Metastatic therapy from first line onwards. Where a decision here rests on a result, the evidence for it is tabulated there and is pointed to rather than restated.

The division matters because the two questions come apart in clinic. Which regimen has the better hazard ratio is answered by Metastatic therapy from first line onwards. Whether this patient should start a regimen at all, and what to measure to find out if it worked, is answered here.

Triple-negative is defined by what is not there. Three receptor assays report below threshold and the tumour is assigned to a group on that basis. The category is therefore an artefact of an assay panel, and the biological diversity inside it is real1. The classification argument is developed in Triple-negative and other biologically diverse phenotypes.

What has changed is that management no longer runs on the absence. Three positive markers now determine the first decision, and they are checked in a fixed order.

PD-L1 determines whether first-line treatment includes a checkpoint inhibitor First-line chemoimmunotherapy and the PD-L1 threshold. It is an assay output with an antibody clone, a scoring algorithm and a cut-point attached, not a property of the tumour.

Germline BRCA1 and BRCA2 status, extended to PALB2 and to somatic BRCA alterations, determines eligibility for PARP inhibition PARP inhibition for germline carriers. It also changes family counselling, which is a second reason to test early.

HER2 expression level determines whether trastuzumab deruxtecan is available, because a proportion of tumours called triple-negative score immunohistochemistry 1+ or 2+ without amplification. The eligibility argument is in Expression and alteration-defined treatment across subtypes.

Trop-2 expression is measured in trials and is not a selection criterion. In the biomarker analysis of ASCENT, sacituzumab govitecan outperformed chemotherapy across high, medium and low Trop-2 expression, and the low-expression group was too small for a firm conclusion2. Tumour mutational burden is high in only a small minority of breast cancers and is not a routine selection tool here3.

In practice

Order all three at the first metastatic presentation, not sequentially as options run out.

PD-L1 on the most recent available tissue, with the assay and cut-point named in the report.

Germline BRCA1 and BRCA2 if never tested, with PALB2 included.

HER2 immunohistochemistry on recent tissue, read for the 0 against 1+ boundary specifically.

The order matters because the first-line decision depends on PD-L1 and cannot wait for the others. The others determine the second and third decisions and there is time to get them right.

2 · Androgen receptor and other subtype-directed targets

Advanced disease · TNBC · any

Finding something to aim at in a disease defined by absence

Is the androgen receptor a target in a subtype defined by the absence of two other receptors?

Rationale
A minority of triple-negative tumours express the androgen receptor and carry a luminal transcriptional programme. If that receptor drives growth in the way the oestrogen receptor does elsewhere, an antagonist already licensed in another disease would be available immediately.
Experiment
A single-arm study of enzalutamide in advanced triple-negative disease with any nuclear androgen receptor staining. Clinical benefit was reported at a fixed time point, in all enrolled patients and in a subgroup defined by a higher staining threshold.
Finding
A minority of patients had clinical benefit, and the proportion was higher in the subgroup defined by the higher threshold.
Limitation
There is no control arm, so nothing separates drug effect from the natural history of a slower-growing luminal subset. The threshold that defined the better-performing subgroup was applied to the same data that suggested it, which is the general problem Expression and alteration-defined treatment across subtypes sets out.
Next question
Does the androgen receptor mark a targetable dependency, or simply identify the patients in this category whose disease was never going to behave like the rest of it?

Subtype-directed targets tested inside triple-negative disease. Every row is exploratory and none supports an indication.

Three of those four rows tested androgen receptor blockade, and they agree on the shape of the answer. A minority of selected patients avoid progression for a few months. The measure is a clinical benefit rate at a fixed timepoint. No trial carried a chemotherapy control arm. Activity is real and it is not a treatment, and no androgen receptor directed agent is approved in this setting.

What separates the three is the assay rather than the drug. TBCRC 011 required more than 10% nuclear staining on central immunohistochemistry7. MDV3100-11 admitted any nuclear staining above zero, then reported an evaluable subgroup defined at 10% or more6. TBCRC 032 set its threshold at 10% and randomised between enzalutamide and enzalutamide with taselisib5. The clocks differ as well. Clinical benefit was scored at six months in the first trial and at sixteen weeks in the other two. Three studies of the same target with three eligibility definitions and two scoring windows cannot be ranked against each other.

The measurement problem sits underneath all of it. In MDV3100-11 benefit was higher in the receptor-defined subgroup than in the whole enrolled population, which is what an imperfect selection assay produces. TBCRC 032's investigators concluded that androgen receptor protein expression alone does not identify androgen receptor-dependent tumours, and that the luminal androgen receptor subtype and receptor splice variants may5. A threshold that has not been reproduced between laboratories cannot support an indication however the responses distribute around it.

The assay also discards most patients before it selects any. TBCRC 011 screened 424 patients with oestrogen and progesterone receptor-negative disease, and 12% scored androgen receptor-positive7. A single-arm study inside a selected fraction that small has to show a large effect to earn a randomised test. None of these did. The one randomisation in the table, inside TBCRC 032, compared two experimental arms, closed early, and left no clinical benefit rate on the record for the enzalutamide-alone arm.

SGNTUC-019 is in this table for a different reason, and its population column is the part to read. It enrolled heavily pretreated patients selected by an activating HER2 mutation in disease that local testing called HER2-negative, across receptor status rather than within triple-negative disease4. It is one cohort of a single-arm basket study. Its response rate belongs to a genotype and not to this subtype. The alteration-defined options that do cross this subtype are collected in Antigen-defined and alteration-defined options.

The general lesson recurs across this chapter. A transcriptional subtype is a research classification. Converting it into an eligibility criterion requires an assay that ordinary laboratories can run reproducibly, and the gap between those two things is the same gap described for HER2 scoring in HER2-low and ultralow reframed as a heterogeneity problem. The trial design consequences are set out in Clinical trials and evidence interpretation.

Immune-directed and repair-directed options are covered in First-line chemoimmunotherapy and the PD-L1 threshold and PARP inhibition for germline carriers. Everything else in this subtype remains investigational, and saying so is more useful than listing targets without evidence.

3 · Rapidly progressive and refractory disease

This subtype produces the fastest clinical deterioration in breast cancer, and the interval between a decision and its consequences is short.

Two practical features follow. The first is that a line of therapy must be assessed early. Waiting twelve weeks for a scan in a patient deteriorating over three is a decision to spend the remaining time on a failing regimen. The second is that performance status falls faster than in other subtypes, so eligibility for anything, including trials, is lost quickly.

The judgment that is hardest and most important is when not to treat. The evidence for it is indirect and consistent. Response rates on single-agent chemotherapy in the refractory setting are in single figures, as the control arm of ASCENT shows Conjugates in second line and beyond. A patient with declining performance status has a low probability of benefit and a high probability of toxicity, and the toxicity arrives first.

In practice

Set a reassessment interval that matches the tempo of the disease, not the convention of the department. In rapidly progressive disease that is often six weeks.

Decide in advance what would make you stop, and say it out loud before starting. A named stopping rule is easier to act on than a judgment made in the room.

Involve palliative care in parallel with active treatment rather than after it8. The referral trigger that works is disease status, not exhaustion of options Palliative and end-of-life care.

Performance status is the variable that closes doors. Record it at every visit, because the trial that is open today will not be open at the next decline.

4 · The unmet need after conjugate failure

The population that defines this disease has moved. The unmet need used to sit after chemotherapy. It is now after conjugates, and the evidence base behind that position is retrospective.

The relevant data are the sequential conjugate series, and they were not assembled inside this subtype. Among 85 patients with HER2-low metastatic breast cancer treated with both trastuzumab deruxtecan and sacituzumab govitecan, progression-free survival on the second conjugate was shorter than on the first in 75.2%. Fourteen of the 85 were still on treatment at the data cutoff9. Longer progression-free survival on the first conjugate predicted longer progression-free survival on the second, which is the signature of partial rather than complete cross-resistance. Hormone receptor status was not associated with that second interval, which is what allows the series to be read here at all. A second conjugate is a reasonable option with a smaller expected magnitude.

What makes the position difficult is that three of the payloads now in routine use are topoisomerase I inhibitors. Irinotecan works through the same mechanism, and its active metabolite is the payload of sacituzumab govitecan. A patient who has received sacituzumab govitecan, datopotamab deruxtecan and trastuzumab deruxtecan has had three exposures to the same payload class delivered by three different antibodies. That is not three lines of non-cross-resistant therapy, whatever the line count says.

The mechanisms that are not antigen loss remain unmeasurable in clinic. PTEN loss was associated with de novo resistance to trastuzumab deruxtecan and not to sacituzumab govitecan, despite the shared payload class9. That association is retrospective and it points at handling rather than at target. The full taxonomy is in Conjugate resistance, antigen loss, payload efflux, and repair capacity.

The honest summary is that this is now the largest gap in the management of the disease. Trial enrolment is the most useful thing on offer, and it should be discussed at the start of the post-conjugate line rather than after it fails Clinical trials and early integration of palliative care.
