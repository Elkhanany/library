Part XIII · Triple-negative disease · Chapter 78

Metastatic disease

A category defined by absence, managed on three positive markers and read through the control arm of every conjugate trial.

1 · Biomarker-driven treatment selection

This chapter is about how to approach the patient. Risk, disease burden, symptom load, biomarker triage and what the line of treatment is for. The trial record itself, arranged by line, is in Metastatic therapy from first line onwards. Where a decision here rests on a result, the evidence for it is tabulated there and is pointed to rather than restated.

The division matters because the two questions come apart in clinic. Which regimen has the better hazard ratio is answered by Metastatic therapy from first line onwards. Whether this patient should start a regimen at all, and what to measure to find out if it worked, is answered here.

Triple-negative is defined by what is not there. Three receptor assays report below threshold and the tumour is assigned to a group on that basis. The category is therefore an artefact of an assay panel, and the biological diversity inside it is real1. The classification argument is developed in Triple-negative and other biologically diverse phenotypes.

What has changed is that management no longer runs on the absence. Three positive markers now determine the first decision, and they are checked in a fixed order.

PD-L1 determines whether first-line treatment includes a checkpoint inhibitor Immunotherapy-based strategies and PD-L1 assessment. It is an assay output with an antibody clone, a scoring algorithm and a cut-point attached, not a property of the tumour.

Germline BRCA1 and BRCA2 status, extended to PALB2 and to somatic BRCA alterations, determines eligibility for PARP inhibition DNA repair directed treatment. It also changes family counselling, which is a second reason to test early.

HER2 expression level determines whether trastuzumab deruxtecan is available, because a proportion of tumours called triple-negative score immunohistochemistry 1+ or 2+ without amplification. The eligibility argument is in Expression and alteration-defined treatment across subtypes.

Trop-2 expression is measured in trials and is not a selection criterion. In the biomarker analysis of ASCENT, sacituzumab govitecan outperformed chemotherapy across high, medium and low Trop-2 expression, and the low-expression group was too small for a firm conclusion2. Tumour mutational burden is high in only a small minority of breast cancers and is not a routine selection tool here3.

In practice

Order all three at the first metastatic presentation, not sequentially as options run out.

PD-L1 on the most recent available tissue, with the assay and cut-point named in the report.

Germline BRCA1 and BRCA2 if never tested, with PALB2 included.

HER2 immunohistochemistry on recent tissue, read for the 0 against 1+ boundary specifically.

The order matters because the first-line decision depends on PD-L1 and cannot wait for the others. The others determine the second and third decisions and there is time to get them right.

The first-line chemoimmunotherapy record. Read the assay column before the result column.

Four of those trials added a checkpoint inhibitor to first-line chemotherapy. None of them lengthened overall survival in the unselected population. Every survival claim that exists here belongs to a PD-L1-defined subgroup, and one of the four produced nothing even inside its own. KEYNOTE-355 shows the dependence on the marker as a gradient rather than as a step. Survival separates at a combined positive score of 10 or more. It weakens and loses significance at a score of 1 or more. It is absent across the whole randomised population6. A marker whose effect decays as its threshold is lowered is behaving predictively. That is what makes the cut-point load-bearing rather than administrative.

The apparent disagreement in the table is between the two atezolizumab trials. It is not a disagreement about atezolizumab. IMpassion130 and IMpassion131 used the same antibody and scored PD-L1 on immune cells with the same assay8,7. They differ in the taxane. The explanation usually offered is that paclitaxel requires corticosteroid premedication and nab-paclitaxel does not. Nobody has randomised the two backbones against each other with the checkpoint inhibitor held constant, so that remains a hypothesis about the partner drug rather than a finding about the antibody.

The second design point explains why atezolizumab carries no confirmed survival claim. IMpassion130 improved progression-free survival in both of its co-primary populations. Its testing hierarchy put overall survival in the intention-to-treat population first, and formal testing stopped when that gate did not open8,9. The survival result in the PD-L1-positive subgroup was therefore never tested. That is an alpha-control decision, not a statement about the drug.

TORCHLIGHT is the third checkpoint inhibitor programme here. It improved both progression-free and overall survival in its PD-L1-positive population5. Survival was a secondary endpoint there, read at a prespecified interim analysis. The row does not say which antibody clone or cut-point defined that population, and the trial tested a single chemotherapy backbone. A PD-L1-positive population is not a portable object between trials. The TORCHLIGHT estimate cannot be added to the pembrolizumab estimate or read as a larger version of it.

The decision rests on KEYNOTE-355. It is the only trial here that carried overall survival in a prespecified biomarker population as a primary endpoint and met it, against the chemotherapy the clinic would otherwise have given6. The decision that follows is narrow. Most triple-negative disease does not reach the high cut-point, so this branch covers a minority of patients seen in clinic. For that minority the first line is settled and should not be delayed while other results are chased.

ASCENT-04 then asked whether the chemotherapy partner can be replaced by a conjugate in exactly this PD-L1-positive population, and on a progression-free survival endpoint it can4. The practical consequence is that a positive PD-L1 result no longer determines the whole regimen. It determines only that a checkpoint inhibitor belongs in it.

What the table cannot show is where the boundary belongs. No trial randomised patients with a combined positive score between 1 and 9 as a stratum of their own. The weak result at a score of 1 or more is therefore a diluted superset rather than a test of that band. No trial scored the same specimens with both assays and reported how many patients change side. And no trial randomised how long a checkpoint inhibitor should continue, or whether it should be carried past progression.

Caution

A PD-L1 result is the output of one antibody clone scored by one algorithm at one cut-point. It is not a property of the tumour, and the same specimen scored by a different assay can cross the boundary in either direction. The predictive biomarker problem is developed in Predictive biomarkers and patient selection and the immune escape biology in Immunotherapy resistance, primary and acquired. One further point restrains over-reading. The early disease result was obtained without PD-L1 selection at all10,11. The marker predicts benefit in the metastatic setting and does not in the neoadjuvant one, and nobody has explained why.

Reported PARP inhibitor trials reaching this population.

Three of those five are randomised phase 3 trials in germline BRCA1 or BRCA2 carriers, and they agree. A PARP inhibitor lengthens progression-free survival in germline-mutated HER2-negative advanced disease. Three drugs, three sponsors, one direction.

They do not agree on what that delay is bought against, and the control arm is the reason. OlympiAD and EMBRACA substituted an oral agent for the single-agent chemotherapy a clinic would otherwise prescribe16,15. BROCADE3 added veliparib to carboplatin and paclitaxel, and both arms received that doublet13. BROCADE3 therefore asks about addition to an active platinum-containing regimen rather than about replacement of a weak one. Its control arm is the strongest in the table and its margin is the narrowest. Reading the three result cells as a ranking of PARP inhibitors is the error that column invites.

Neither substitution trial improved overall survival17,18. BROCADE3 has not reported survival at all. The table has no column for what patients received after progression, and that is the column the survival reading depends on. In both substitution trials the control arm could go on to a PARP inhibitor or to platinum. EMBRACA's investigators reported an analysis adjusted for that subsequent treatment, and its interval still included no difference18. What that pattern means for a progression-free survival endpoint generally is worked through in PARP inhibition for germline carriers.

The decision rests on OlympiAD and EMBRACA taken together rather than on either alone. Their comparator is the thing that would otherwise be prescribed, which is what makes them load-bearing. Two drugs tested the same way with the same result is what makes the class claim safe.

The other two rows answer different questions and should not be read alongside the first three. TBCRC 048 widened the eligible set beyond germline BRCA and then narrowed it again. Confirmed responses occurred only with germline PALB2 and with somatic BRCA1 or BRCA2 alterations. No confirmed response followed an ATM or CHEK2 mutation alone14. A repair gene on a panel is not by itself an indication. Expansion cohorts have since been reported separately19. DORA asks whether a patient responding to platinum can be maintained off chemotherapy. It enrolled by response to platinum rather than by germline status, and sustained benefit tracked neither germline BRCA nor PD-L112. Each of its two arms was measured against a historical control of continued platinum rather than against the other, so its result cell cannot say whether durvalumab added anything.

Two comparisons nobody randomised sit behind this whole table. A PARP inhibitor has never been tested against platinum in germline-mutated disease, which is the choice actually faced. Nor has the order of the two been randomised, so cross-resistance is inferred rather than measured.

What the table does not carry is the reason to test early. The eligible population is defined by a germline result, so a patient never tested is a patient never eligible, and the result also changes counselling for relatives. Testing is therefore part of the first consultation rather than part of the second-line decision Disparities across the breast cancer continuum.

Platinum belongs in this section because it exploits the same defect. That shared mechanism also predicts cross-resistance, and the prediction holds. Secondary intragenic mutations that restore the BRCA2 reading frame confer resistance both to platinum and to PARP inhibition20. The full account is in PARP inhibitor and platinum resistance, including restoration of repair and the platinum trial record is in Chemotherapy sequencing when no target is present.

Caution

A homologous recombination deficiency score records past genomic instability. Scars persist after repair function returns, so a tumour can score Homologous recombination deficiency high while repairing normally at the moment of testing. The score describes history. The drug needs the present. Repair biology is in DNA repair, replication stress, and genomic instability and agent detail in Targeted therapy.

Randomised TROP2 conjugate trials in triple-negative disease. The control arm column is the one to read for expectation setting.

All four agree. A TROP2-directed conjugate delayed progression against the control regimen each trial would otherwise have given. That holds in the third line and beyond, in the first line without a checkpoint inhibitor, and in the first line with one. Both conjugates carry a topoisomerase I payload, so this is one delivery mechanism tested in three positions rather than two independent classes.

Where the four diverge is the control arm, and that is the column to read before the result column. ASCENT compared against single-agent chemotherapy in patients who had already had at least two chemotherapy regimens23. What that arm achieved is the baseline every later conversation with a patient should start from. ASCENT-03 and TROPION-Breast02 were set against first-line chemotherapy, which in ASCENT-03 could be a gemcitabine and carboplatin doublet22,21. ASCENT-04 was set against chemotherapy plus pembrolizumab, the strongest comparator in the table4. The conjugate's margin narrows as the comparator strengthens. None of that is a statement about the conjugates.

The second divergence is endpoint maturity, and it governs what can be claimed today. TROPION-Breast02 carried overall survival as a dual primary endpoint and met it. ASCENT-03 and ASCENT-04 report progression-free survival with survival not yet reported. For first-line patients who are not candidates for immunotherapy, which is most of them, TROPION-Breast02 is therefore the load-bearing trial. It is the only first-line conjugate trial in which the delay in progression has so far been shown to be paid out as time alive.

ASCENT remains load-bearing further down the disease course. It is the only trial in this subtype to have improved overall survival against single-agent chemotherapy in a population already treated with two or more regimens. Its final analysis also reported outcomes by HER2 and TROP2 expression24, which is the closest the class has come to a selection assay and is not one.

The practical result is that a conjugate is now the default first cytotoxic for nearly every patient in this chapter. Three things the table cannot show qualify that. No conjugate has been randomised against another conjugate, and ASCENT-03 and TROPION-Breast02 enrolled the same intended population without ever being compared. No trial selected patients by TROP2 expression2. And no trial randomised the order, so whether starting with a conjugate beats reserving it for the second line is unknown.

Caution

The first-line conjugate trials differ in what they have shown. TROPION-Breast02 carried overall survival as a primary endpoint and met it21. ASCENT-03 reports progression-free survival with survival not yet reported, and a published critique argues that crossover-adjusted analysis is needed before first-line sacituzumab govitecan is treated as settled25. The two are frequently presented as equivalent first-line options. They are equivalent in indication and not in the strength of what has been demonstrated.

Interplay

Triple-negative disease is where the heterogeneity argument of Part VI meets a category built from three negative results. Trop-2 is expressed across most of these tumours and its level does not select patients2, while HER2 at immunohistochemistry 0 still supports conjugate responses26. Both findings point the same way. Antigen abundance is not the variable governing conjugate benefit at the low end of the range, and a subtype defined by receptor absence is not thereby a subtype without targets. What varies within these tumours is not only how much antigen is present but how the payload is handled once delivered, and no routine assay reports that.

5 · Chemotherapy sequencing and realistic expectations

Chemotherapy still carries most of the treatment time in this disease. The expectations attached to it should be stated rather than implied, and the honest source for them is the control arm column of the conjugate table in Antibody-drug conjugates.

Read the ASCENT, ASCENT-03 and TROPION-Breast02 control arms together. Single-agent chemotherapy in the refractory setting delays progression by a matter of weeks. In the first line without immunotherapy it delays progression by several months. The difference between them is the effect of line rather than of agent. It is the most useful single thing to have in mind before a fourth-line conversation.

Three principles follow.

Sequential single agents are preferred to combinations outside a situation requiring immediate response. Combinations raise response rate and toxicity without a consistent survival gain.

Platinum has a specific indication in germline BRCA-altered disease and no general superiority otherwise DNA repair directed treatment.

Response duration shortens with each line, and the shortening is steeper here than in hormone receptor-positive disease. A regimen that works in the fourth line is more often working for weeks than for months.

Agent-level detail belongs to Cytotoxic chemotherapy and the randomised comparisons between cytotoxics to Chemotherapy sequencing when no target is present. The point specific to this chapter is that expectation setting is a clinical act, and it is done with absolute medians rather than with hazard ratios.

Subtype-directed targets tested inside triple-negative disease. Every row is exploratory and none supports an indication.

Three of those four rows tested androgen receptor blockade, and they agree on the shape of the answer. A minority of selected patients avoid progression for a few months. The measure is a clinical benefit rate at a fixed timepoint. No trial carried a chemotherapy control arm. Activity is real and it is not a treatment, and no androgen receptor directed agent is approved in this setting.

What separates the three is the assay rather than the drug. TBCRC 011 required more than 10% nuclear staining on central immunohistochemistry30. MDV3100-11 admitted any nuclear staining above zero, then reported an evaluable subgroup defined at 10% or more29. TBCRC 032 set its threshold at 10% and randomised between enzalutamide and enzalutamide with taselisib28. The clocks differ as well. Clinical benefit was scored at six months in the first trial and at sixteen weeks in the other two. Three studies of the same target with three eligibility definitions and two scoring windows cannot be ranked against each other.

The measurement problem sits underneath all of it. In MDV3100-11 benefit was higher in the receptor-defined subgroup than in the whole enrolled population, which is what an imperfect selection assay produces. TBCRC 032's investigators concluded that androgen receptor protein expression alone does not identify androgen receptor-dependent tumours, and that the luminal androgen receptor subtype and receptor splice variants may28. A threshold that has not been reproduced between laboratories cannot support an indication however the responses distribute around it.

The assay also discards most patients before it selects any. TBCRC 011 screened 424 patients with oestrogen and progesterone receptor-negative disease, and 12% scored androgen receptor-positive30. A single-arm study inside a selected fraction that small has to show a large effect to earn a randomised test. None of these did. The one randomisation in the table, inside TBCRC 032, compared two experimental arms, closed early, and left no clinical benefit rate on the record for the enzalutamide-alone arm.

SGNTUC-019 is in this table for a different reason, and its population column is the part to read. It enrolled heavily pretreated patients selected by an activating HER2 mutation in disease that local testing called HER2-negative, across receptor status rather than within triple-negative disease27. It is one cohort of a single-arm basket study. Its response rate belongs to a genotype and not to this subtype. The alteration-defined options that do cross this subtype are collected in Antigen-defined and alteration-defined options.

The general lesson recurs across this chapter. A transcriptional subtype is a research classification. Converting it into an eligibility criterion requires an assay that ordinary laboratories can run reproducibly, and the gap between those two things is the same gap described for HER2 scoring in HER2-low and ultralow reframed as a heterogeneity problem. The trial design consequences are set out in Clinical trials and evidence interpretation.

Immune-directed and repair-directed options are covered in Immunotherapy-based strategies and PD-L1 assessment and DNA repair directed treatment. Everything else in this subtype remains investigational, and saying so is more useful than listing targets without evidence.

7 · Rapidly progressive and refractory disease

This subtype produces the fastest clinical deterioration in breast cancer, and the interval between a decision and its consequences is short.

Two practical features follow. The first is that a line of therapy must be assessed early. Waiting twelve weeks for a scan in a patient deteriorating over three is a decision to spend the remaining time on a failing regimen. The second is that performance status falls faster than in other subtypes, so eligibility for anything, including trials, is lost quickly.

The judgment that is hardest and most important is when not to treat. The evidence for it is indirect and consistent. Response rates on single-agent chemotherapy in the refractory setting are in single figures, as the control arm of ASCENT shows Antibody-drug conjugates. A patient with declining performance status has a low probability of benefit and a high probability of toxicity, and the toxicity arrives first.

In practice

Set a reassessment interval that matches the tempo of the disease, not the convention of the department. In rapidly progressive disease that is often six weeks.

Decide in advance what would make you stop, and say it out loud before starting. A named stopping rule is easier to act on than a judgment made in the room.

Involve palliative care in parallel with active treatment rather than after it31. The referral trigger that works is disease status, not exhaustion of options Palliative and end-of-life care.

Performance status is the variable that closes doors. Record it at every visit, because the trial that is open today will not be open at the next decline.

8 · The unmet need after conjugate failure

The population that defines this disease has moved. The unmet need used to sit after chemotherapy. It is now after conjugates, and the evidence base behind that position is retrospective.

The relevant data are the sequential conjugate series, and they were not assembled inside this subtype. Among 85 patients with HER2-low metastatic breast cancer treated with both trastuzumab deruxtecan and sacituzumab govitecan, progression-free survival on the second conjugate was shorter than on the first in 75.2%. Fourteen of the 85 were still on treatment at the data cutoff32. Longer progression-free survival on the first conjugate predicted longer progression-free survival on the second, which is the signature of partial rather than complete cross-resistance. Hormone receptor status was not associated with that second interval, which is what allows the series to be read here at all. A second conjugate is a reasonable option with a smaller expected magnitude.

What makes the position difficult is that three of the payloads now in routine use are topoisomerase I inhibitors. Irinotecan works through the same mechanism, and its active metabolite is the payload of sacituzumab govitecan. A patient who has received sacituzumab govitecan, datopotamab deruxtecan and trastuzumab deruxtecan has had three exposures to the same payload class delivered by three different antibodies. That is not three lines of non-cross-resistant therapy, whatever the line count says.

The mechanisms that are not antigen loss remain unmeasurable in clinic. PTEN loss was associated with de novo resistance to trastuzumab deruxtecan and not to sacituzumab govitecan, despite the shared payload class32. That association is retrospective and it points at handling rather than at target. The full taxonomy is in Conjugate resistance, antigen loss, payload efflux, and repair capacity.

The only prospective study addressing this position.

TRADE-DXd will give the first prospective estimate of how much activity remains after a conjugate fails. It enters patients on the state of their disease after a previous conjugate, which is the population every retrospective series has had to assemble after the fact. It enrols by HER2 result rather than by subtype, with separate hormone receptor-positive and negative cohorts, so only part of it will speak to this chapter. Its cohorts are non-comparative, so it will not rank sequences. What it will and will not settle is set out in Unresolved questions and what reads out next.

The honest summary is that this is now the largest gap in the management of the disease. Trial enrolment is the most useful thing on offer, and it should be discussed at the start of the post-conjugate line rather than after it fails Clinical trials and early integration of palliative care.
