Part XIII · Triple-negative disease · Chapter 77

Adjuvant and post-neoadjuvant therapy

Why residual disease rather than stage decides what is given after surgery in this subtype.

1 · Scope, and the residual disease decision that organises it

This chapter covers what is given after surgery to a patient with triple-negative disease. What is given before surgery is in Neoadjuvant therapy and checkpoint blockade. The argument that frames both is in Selection by stage, and the price of treating everyone, which these two chapters extend with trial-level detail. Radiotherapy, bone-modifying agents, ovarian protection and surveillance are out of scope. Molecular residual disease detection is in Liquid biopsy and longitudinal monitoring.

One fact organises everything in the sections that follow. Response to neoadjuvant therapy is the strongest prognostic variable available in this subtype, with its trial-level limits set out in Endpoints, surrogates, and the overall survival question. It is generated by treatment rather than measured at baseline. A patient taken to surgery first cannot be stratified by it. The adjuvant options open to that patient are therefore narrower, which is a reason to settle the neoadjuvant question at the first consultation rather than after the operation.

Ten of those rows did not require this subtype to enter. CALGB 9344, NSABP B-28, ECOG E1199, US Oncology 9735, ABC trials, WSG-PlanB, CALGB 9741, GIM2, CALGB 49907 and MINDACT enrolled early breast cancer selected by nodal status, by other risk features or by age. None of them required a triple-negative result to enter. They include the largest trials in the table and have the least specific populations. Everything they say about triple-negative disease is a subgroup or an interaction test. Adjuvant chemotherapy regimens and dose density argues the chemotherapy comparisons among them. MINDACT, which asked whether a genomic assay could spare chemotherapy, is argued in Adjuvant chemotherapy and the genomic assay decision.

Five rows selected triple-negative disease without reference to response. CIBOMA/GEICAM, SYSUCC-001, PATTERN, CITRINE and ALEXANDRA/IMpassion030 enrolled on pathological stage or on other risk features. Response to neoadjuvant treatment was an entry criterion in none of them. Five more required residual invasive disease after neoadjuvant chemotherapy. CREATE-X, EA1131, ASCENT-05/OptimICE-RD, TROPION-Breast03 and SASCIA / GBG 102, the last of them also admitting hormone receptor-positive disease, enrolled a population that had already declared itself resistant to the chemotherapy it was given. CREATE-X did not require this subtype to enter. It enrolled HER2-negative residual disease of either hormone receptor status. Its triple-negative result is a subgroup.

Those two groups look alike in the table and are not alike in clinic. A stage-selected population contains a large fraction of patients who would have been cured by what they had already received. A residual disease population contains very few. The absolute event rate differs accordingly, so a hazard ratio measured in one does not carry the same absolute consequence in the other. Most of the apparent disagreement in this literature is disagreement about which population a number came from.

The remaining four rows are defined by response in one direction or the other. OlympiA and A-BRAVE admitted patients by residual disease or by stage, in strata they were not powered to separate. In A-BRAVE, however, the residual disease stratum carried its own co-primary disease-free survival endpoint. PEARLY randomised patients treated in either setting. OptimICE-pCR enrolls only patients with a pathological complete response. A stratified trial is not two trials. What it reports is an average over populations whose prognosis differs.

Residual disease marks resistance to the regimen that was actually given. It says nothing about a drug the tumour never met. Every trial in the sections below makes that assumption, and none of them tests it.

Two things the map cannot show matter more than anything in it. There is no column for what the patient received before surgery, so two rows that both read residual invasive disease may describe disease that survived two drugs or disease that survived four. And no reported post-surgical trial here enrolled a patient whose neoadjuvant treatment was the regimen now given. Every benchmark in this chapter was set before carboplatin and pembrolizumab entered that position.

Geography is worth one sentence. CITRINE, PATTERN and SYSUCC-001 were run in China and PEARLY in Korea. None of the cytotoxic strategies they support has been reproduced in a trial run outside those countries.

Most sections below open with the question their trials were built to answer, taken from the [Chapter Stories](stories.html) map, and close with a table filtered on that same question. The story says what was being looked for and what the design cannot settle. The table says what else is in the cell. The prose argues the decision.

2 · Adjuvant chemotherapy regimens and dose density

The backbone is not in dispute anywhere in this literature. None of the trials that fixed it set out to study this subtype. Sequential anthracycline and taxane remains the standard against which everything else in this chapter is measured, on comparisons that are subtype-wide23.

Those comparisons agree on the taxane and disagree on what it buys. CALGB 9344 and NSABP B-28 both added four cycles of paclitaxel after doxorubicin and cyclophosphamide in node-positive disease, and both lowered recurrence21,20. Five-year overall survival in B-28 was 85 percent in both arms. In CALGB 9344 it was 80% with paclitaxel against 77% without. The two trials tested the same addition and differed in what their survival analyses could resolve. That is a difference in events and follow-up rather than a disagreement about the drug. CALGB 9344 also settled a second question in passing. Escalating the doxorubicin dose changed nothing, which is the first evidence in this chapter that schedule and composition matter more than intensity.

Anthracycline omission looks like three trials disagreeing and is three different comparisons. US Oncology 9735 found docetaxel and cyclophosphamide superior to doxorubicin and cyclophosphamide on disease-free and overall survival at seven years, against a control arm that contained no taxane18. ABC trials put six cycles of the same docetaxel and cyclophosphamide doublet against anthracycline plus taxane and could not declare non-inferiority. Its hazard ratio for an invasive disease-free survival event was 1.202, against a predefined inferiority boundary of 1.1815,24. WSG-PlanB compared epirubicin and cyclophosphamide followed by docetaxel with six cycles of docetaxel and cyclophosphamide, and found them within a 4.4 point margin25,14. Beating an anthracycline doublet is not the same claim as matching a regimen containing a taxane and an anthracycline. The control arm is where these trials differ, not the experimental one.

ECOG E1199 is the only one of them that held composition constant and varied schedule inside a single randomisation. Weekly paclitaxel improved disease-free and overall survival against paclitaxel every three weeks. Weekly docetaxel improved neither19. The schedule effect belongs to the taxane rather than to taxanes, which is why the regimen used in this subtype is weekly paclitaxel and not weekly anything.

Dose density is a scheduling change rather than a dose increase. Total cytotoxic exposure is broadly the same in both arms of these trials, the difference being the interval between cycles. The label is routinely misread as more chemotherapy, which drives the wrong conversation about tolerability.

The two randomised trials that anchor the dose-density meta-analysis.

The two trials in that table agree in direction and neither enrolled this subtype on purpose. Both took node-positive disease of any receptor status, and both compared a two-weekly schedule with a three-weekly one inside a factorial design testing something else as well22,17. Neither is large enough on its own to carry the decision, which is why the case for dose density is meta-analytic. The patient-level meta-analysis pooled 26 randomised trials and 37,298 women26. Ten-year recurrence and ten-year breast cancer mortality were both lower with dose-intensified scheduling. The proportional reduction was similar across subtypes rather than concentrated in this one. Absolute gain therefore tracks absolute risk, which is why this schedule matters most in node-positive triple-negative disease.

Three things none of these trials provides should be said plainly. None randomised adjuvant chemotherapy against no chemotherapy in stage I triple-negative disease, so treatment below stage II is extrapolation from prognosis on the reasoning in Risk assessment and treatment selection. None stratified by receptor subtype, so every subtype-specific estimate quoted from them is an interaction test rather than a randomised allocation. And none contained a platinum or a checkpoint inhibitor, so the backbone they validated is now the part of the regimen that is least often given alone.

In practice

Give a taxane and an anthracycline sequentially, at two-weekly intervals with growth factor support, unless something specific argues against it.

Do not substitute single-agent capecitabine for that regimen. It was tested as a substitute for cyclophosphamide, methotrexate and fluorouracil, or for doxorubicin and cyclophosphamide, in patients aged 65 and over. Neither regimen contains a taxane. Capecitabine was inferior, most clearly in hormone receptor-negative disease CALGB 4990713.

3 · Platinum in the adjuvant and post-neoadjuvant setting

Early disease · TNBC · post-neoadjuvant

Allocating further treatment on what surgery finds

Is a platinum a better post-neoadjuvant cytotoxic than capecitabine for residual disease?

Rationale
Triple-negative tumours carry defects in homologous recombination often enough to give a DNA-crosslinking agent a mechanistic claim on them. Adding carboplatin before surgery raises the pathological response rate over the same chemotherapy without it. If it works on the tumour while it is still in the breast, it ought to work on what is left of it.
Experiment
Residual disease above a size threshold was randomised to a platinum or to capecitabine, as a non-inferiority design. Subtype was assigned by a multigene classifier run on the residual tissue. The primary comparison was prespecified in the basal group.
Finding
Platinum did not prove non-inferior to capecitabine. The estimate ran against it. Severe toxicity was more common with platinum than with capecitabine. The monitoring committee closed the trial well short of its planned enrolment. Outcome was poor in both arms.
Limitation
A non-inferiority trial closed before it has its events can show neither non-inferiority nor inferiority. This one read a fraction of the information it was designed for. An estimate that had looked similar between the arms would not have settled the question either. The classifier was run on tissue that had already been through chemotherapy. The subtype defining the analysis population is therefore a measurement made after selection rather than a property of the tumour that was enrolled. The size threshold narrows the population further. Residual disease below that threshold is not represented. The case for platinum came from trials in chemotherapy-naive tumours, which is precisely the extrapolation at issue.

Platinum given after surgery, either added to the backbone or replacing part of it.

The first question is whether carboplatin added to a full backbone improves outcome. CITRINE and PEARLY both added it without removing anything, and both met their primary endpoint. Both also cost a large increase in grade 3 or worse toxicity, which is the price the table has no column for and the clinic pays first.

They are not replicates of each other, and the difference is design rather than drug. CITRINE randomised after surgery and measured disease-free survival3. PEARLY randomised patients treated in either setting and measured event-free survival, which in its neoadjuvant patients counts events occurring before the operation1. Their survival columns also differ in what they were able to establish, and follow-up does not explain it, because the trial with the shorter median follow-up is the one whose survival difference reached significance. The reasons to hold that survival result loosely are specific. CITRINE was conducted at a single centre. Its proportional hazards assumption was formally rejected3. A single hazard ratio therefore does not describe its curves, which is a statement about the model rather than about the drug. Whether carboplatin prevented recurrence or deferred it cannot be told apart at this length of follow-up.

The second question is whether platinum can replace the anthracycline. PATTERN replaced an anthracycline regimen with a platinum and taxane doublet, changing both drugs at once12. A comparison in which the experimental arm differs from the control in two components cannot attribute its result to either one. Its subgroups with BRCA1/2 and homologous recombination repair variants are the most quoted part of it and were not randomised allocations.

The third question is whether platinum is the right drug for residual disease. EA1131 tested platinum against capecitabine in that population as a non-inferiority design, and its monitoring committee closed it after roughly three fifths of the planned information had accrued7. A non-inferiority trial closed that early can demonstrate neither non-inferiority nor inferiority. Two features of it are nevertheless load-bearing. The analysis population was defined by a PAM50 call made on residual tissue, which is a measurement taken after the selection event rather than a property of the tumour that was enrolled. And the design assumed a four-year invasive disease-free survival of 67% with capecitabine, while the observed three-year estimates in basal residual disease fell far below that in both arms. Residual disease after neoadjuvant chemotherapy is a worse prognostic state than the trials that study it have assumed.

The decision for a patient going to surgery first therefore rests on CITRINE, because it is the only trial here that randomised the addition of carboplatin entirely after surgery. That is a single-centre trial with non-proportional hazards, which is a thinner foundation than the strength of its primary result suggests.

Two comparisons nobody has randomised bound all of this. No trial has added carboplatin after surgery to a patient who already received carboplatin before it. And no trial in this setting has allocated platinum prospectively by homologous recombination status, so the mechanistic rationale for the class has never been tested as an eligibility criterion.

Caution

All of these trials were run without checkpoint blockade. In stage II to III disease treated with the KEYNOTE-522 regimen, carboplatin has already been given to every patient before surgery27. The adjuvant platinum question now applies mainly to patients treated with surgery first, and to patients whose neoadjuvant regimen contained no platinum. Applying these results to a patient who has already had four cycles of carboplatin is an extrapolation with no data behind it.

4 · Capecitabine for residual disease

Early disease · TNBC · post-neoadjuvant

Allocating further treatment on what surgery finds

Does a further course of chemotherapy after surgery lower recurrence in residual triple-negative disease?

Rationale
A tumour still present after a full anthracycline and taxane course has demonstrated resistance to both classes in the patient it is growing in. The interval before relapse is short. Anything that will work has to be given now. A fluoropyrimidine is not cross-resistant with either class. It is tolerable enough to start soon after surgery.
Experiment
Residual HER2-negative disease after neoadjuvant anthracycline, taxane or both was randomised to capecitabine or to no further chemotherapy, with disease-free survival as the endpoint. The same drug was tested separately in populations selected by stage rather than by response. One of those trials gave a conventional course after standard adjuvant chemotherapy. The other gave a metronomic year.
Finding
Capecitabine for residual disease lowered recurrence and death against no further chemotherapy. The effect was larger in the triple-negative subgroup than in the hormone receptor-positive one. In the populations selected by stage rather than by response the same drug missed its endpoint in one trial and met it in the other. Those two differed in dose, schedule and duration.
Limitation
The residual disease trial stopped early at an interim analysis that had met its endpoint, which is the circumstance in which an effect is most likely to be overstated. Its triple-negative result is a subgroup of a HER2-negative trial rather than a trial in this subtype. It was run where neither carboplatin nor a checkpoint inhibitor formed part of neoadjuvant treatment. Its control arm is therefore a patient nobody now treats. The basal and non-basal gradient reported in the trial that selected by stage is an interaction test on a centrally scored phenotype, not a randomised allocation by that phenotype.

Capecitabine as an addition, as a substitute, and as the comparator for platinum.

CREATE-X is the trial the decision rests on, because it is the only randomised comparison against what the clinic would otherwise do, which was nothing further16. Three qualifications travel with it. Its population was HER2-negative rather than triple-negative, so the result that changed practice is a subgroup of the trial rather than the trial. It stopped early at an interim analysis that had met its endpoint, which is the circumstance in which an effect is most likely to be overstated. And its control arm received neither carboplatin nor pembrolizumab at any point, so the residual disease it selected was selected by a weaker regimen than the one used today. The incremental value of capecitabine after a platinum-containing chemoimmunotherapy backbone has never been measured.

The two trials that gave capecitabine to unselected early triple-negative disease reached opposite conclusions. They differ in dose, in schedule and in duration rather than in drug. CIBOMA/GEICAM gave eight conventional cycles11. SYSUCC-001 gave a year of continuous low-dose treatment10. Their confidence intervals for disease-free survival overlap across most of their range, so what separates the two conclusions is which side of a threshold each interval fell on. Neither trial is evidence that metronomic dosing works where conventional dosing does not. That comparison has never been randomised.

CALGB 49907 is in this table for the opposite reason. It substituted capecitabine for standard adjuvant chemotherapy in patients aged 65 and over, and standard chemotherapy won13. Its receptor-negative result is a subgroup of an age-selected, subtype-blind trial. It nevertheless does the most useful work in the table, because it fixes the direction of the whole class. Capecitabine is worth giving after an anthracycline and a taxane when residual invasive disease is found at surgery. It is not worth giving instead of standard chemotherapy. In CALGB 49907 that meant cyclophosphamide, methotrexate and fluorouracil, or doxorubicin and cyclophosphamide, neither of which contains a taxane.

EA1131 appears here as the comparator rather than as an experimental arm. Its presence records the point at which capecitabine stopped being the new idea and became the thing to beat, which is also the role it plays in the conjugate trials in Conjugates after residual disease.

The preplanned subgroup signals in this table point in opposite directions, and the reason is measurement rather than biology. CIBOMA/GEICAM reported its advantage in non-basal disease, on a phenotype scored centrally by immunohistochemistry. That interaction reached significance for overall survival and not for disease-free survival11. CREATE-X reported its largest effect in the triple-negative subgroup, defined by receptor status16. Those two partitions do not divide the same patients along the same line. Neither was a randomised allocation, and an interaction test on a scoring convention is not a claim about repair biology.

In practice

Offer capecitabine for residual invasive disease after neoadjuvant chemotherapy in a patient without a germline BRCA variant. Six to eight cycles, with dose reduction expected.

Warn about hand-foot syndrome before the first cycle rather than after it. It occurred in 73.4% of treated patients in CREATE-X16.

Do not use adjuvant capecitabine outside the residual disease indication on the strength of SYSUCC-001 alone. The trial that used a conventional schedule in the same setting was negative.

5 · Adjuvant PARP inhibition for germline carriers

Early disease · TNBC · post-neoadjuvant

Allocating further treatment on what surgery finds

Does a PARP inhibitor lower recurrence in germline BRCA carriers left with residual disease?

Rationale
A germline BRCA1 or BRCA2 loss is a repair defect the tumour cannot work around. PARP inhibition is lethal in that background. Carriers are over-represented in triple-negative disease. The carriers at highest risk are the ones whose tumours survived neoadjuvant chemotherapy.
Experiment
High-risk HER2-negative germline carriers were randomised after local treatment and chemotherapy to a year of olaparib or to placebo, with invasive disease-free survival as the endpoint. High risk was met either by residual disease after neoadjuvant chemotherapy or by stage in patients who had surgery first.
Finding
Olaparib lowered invasive and distant recurrence against placebo. The advantage held on longer follow-up. It became an overall survival difference as well.
Limitation
The two routes into the trial are not one population. The trial was not designed to separate them. What the drug is worth specifically in residual triple-negative disease is therefore an inference from a stratified trial rather than a result of it. Selection was germline, which leaves outside the result the larger group of triple-negative tumours with a BRCA-like phenotype and no inherited variant. Attempts to identify that group by a repair-deficiency score have not predicted response to a platinum. In carriers themselves a platinum did not outperform an anthracycline regimen before surgery. The genotype does not simply mean platinum sensitivity. Enrolment also largely preceded the checkpoint inhibitor. Nothing here says how olaparib sits alongside adjuvant pembrolizumab or alongside capecitabine.

Three things that trial does not establish should be said out loud.

It never compared olaparib with capecitabine, which is the alternative in this population. The preference rests on a larger population, a longer follow-up and a placebo control. Those are reasons to prefer the evidence rather than evidence of superiority.

It did not test olaparib alongside pembrolizumab in any arm. A patient with residual disease after KEYNOTE-522 who carries a germline variant is now routinely offered both, on the strength of two trials that each excluded the other drug.

It enrolled on germline status alone, with no somatic or functional assay. Somatic BRCA alterations, PALB2 and the wider homologous recombination gene set were not eligibility criteria. The distinction between a germline mutation, a somatic one and a genomic scar is developed in Germline and somatic DNA repair alterations and DNA repair, replication stress, and genomic instability.

Caution

Germline testing has to be resulted before the post-neoadjuvant decision, not after it. The window between surgery and the start of adjuvant treatment is short. A test sent at the time of the residual disease result will often return too late to change the first cycle, and the pathway in Inherited susceptibility and familial breast cancer is built around sending it at diagnosis.

6 · Adjuvant immunotherapy and the question stratified by response

Early disease · TNBC · post-neoadjuvant

Allocating further treatment on what surgery finds

Does checkpoint blockade have to continue after surgery?

Rationale
The registration trial gave pembrolizumab before and after surgery inside a single randomisation. It cannot say which half of the schedule produced the benefit. There is reason to think the halves are not equivalent. Before surgery the antigen and the infiltrating lymphocytes are still in the breast. Afterwards they are not. The adjuvant phase also adds exposure to a class of toxicity that does not resolve when the drug is stopped.
Experiment
One trial added an antibody to adjuvant chemotherapy in patients operated first. Another gave a year of a different antibody after surgery in high-risk disease, with a residual disease stratum as a co-primary population. A third continued its antibody across surgery on a carboplatin-containing neoadjuvant backbone. A fourth randomises omission of the adjuvant phase in patients who achieved a complete response.
Finding
Checkpoint blockade begun after surgery has not lowered recurrence. The trial in patients operated first was halted for futility with its estimate on the wrong side of no difference. The trial with the residual disease stratum missed both co-primary endpoints. The trial that continued its antibody across surgery missed its primary endpoint as well. The omission trial has not reported.
Limitation
None of the reported trials asked what the clinic asks, which is whether the adjuvant phase can be dropped in a patient who has already had the neoadjuvant phase. The unreported omission trial asks it only in patients with a complete response. Residual disease is left where it started. The reported trials use different antibodies on different chemotherapy backbones. They are not replicates of one another. A negative result in one does not transfer to another. The survival comparison favouring avelumab was descriptive and sat outside the testing hierarchy. It is not a positive result recovered from a negative trial. The trial that continued its antibody across surgery had carboplatin in both arms. Nothing in it separates the antibody from its backbone.
Next question
If the adjuvant checkpoint inhibitor now sits inside the control arm of every residual disease trial, can a new drug class beat that control?

Checkpoint blockade begun after surgery has not lowered recurrence in any trial that tested it. That is the class finding, and the failures behind it are not the same kind of failure. ALEXANDRA/IMpassion030 enrolled patients treated by surgery first, added atezolizumab to adjuvant chemotherapy, and was halted for futility with its estimate on the wrong side of no difference4. A-BRAVE gave a year of avelumab after all treatment was finished, to a population that was mostly residual disease, and missed both co-primary endpoints2. Outside this table, NSABP B-59/GeparDouze started atezolizumab before surgery, with carboplatin-containing neoadjuvant chemotherapy, and continued it afterwards. It missed its primary endpoint too. As a neoadjuvant trial, it does not test blockade begun after surgery.

Those are two antibodies on three backbones in three populations. They are not replicates of one another. A negative result with one PD-L1 antibody on one chemotherapy schedule does not transfer to another. What does generalise is the timing. Before surgery the antigen and the infiltrating lymphocytes are still in the breast. Afterwards they are not, and Why response rates in breast cancer sit where they do develops that mechanism.

ALEXANDRA/IMpassion030 is the load-bearing trial for exactly that reason. It is the only randomised test of a checkpoint inhibitor added to adjuvant chemotherapy in patients with no neoadjuvant exposure at all. Nothing in its design confounds the drug with a pre-surgical phase, which is why its futility result is the cleanest available statement that the timing matters.

A-BRAVE is the harder of the two to read. Its descriptive overall survival hazard ratio is more favourable than its disease-free survival hazard ratio, and it is widely quoted as though the trial were positive2. A trial whose disease-free survival curves do not separate convincingly should not show a larger separation in overall survival. That analysis sat outside the testing hierarchy and the trial was not powered for it. It is a hypothesis rather than a result recovered from a negative trial. The trial should also be read as post-neoadjuvant rather than adjuvant, because the great majority of its patients entered through the residual disease stratum.

The question in the heading is the one none of them asked. KEYNOTE-522 gave pembrolizumab before and after surgery, to every patient, inside a single randomisation27. It cannot apportion its effect between the two phases. GeparNuevo gave no checkpoint blockade after surgery at all. It missed its pathological complete response endpoint. Its later reports carry an overall survival difference in favour of durvalumab30,31. That is an indirect argument that the adjuvant phase may be contributing little. It is also a secondary survival endpoint from a randomised phase II trial, which is the grade of evidence this section has just declined to accept from A-BRAVE.

OptimICE-pCR tests omission after a pathological complete response, which is the half of the question with the least at stake. ASCENT-05/OptimICE-RD and TROPION-Breast03 will not settle the other half. ASCENT-05/OptimICE-RD gives pembrolizumab in its experimental arm and in its control arm. TROPION-Breast03 pairs its conjugate with durvalumab and leaves pembrolizumab inside the choice from which its control arm is drawn. Both ask what to add to the post-surgical phase rather than whether a checkpoint inhibitor belongs in it. No randomised study is asking whether pembrolizumab should be continued in residual disease after a pembrolizumab-containing neoadjuvant regimen, which is where the recurrence risk actually sits.

Caution

A regimen tested as a whole becomes unfalsifiable in parts. The decision to continue pembrolizumab after surgery was made once, at trial design, and every patient since has inherited it. The adjuvant cycles carry a real risk of permanent endocrinopathy in people who are already cured Immune-related toxicity in a curative-intent population. No trial in this table carries that toxicity as an endpoint, so the evidential status of those cycles and their cost are not weighed against each other anywhere.

7 · Conjugates after residual disease

Early disease · TNBC · post-neoadjuvant

Allocating further treatment on what surgery finds

Can an antibody-drug conjugate displace capecitabine as the treatment for residual disease?

Rationale
The capecitabine benchmark was set in patients who had received neither carboplatin nor a checkpoint inhibitor. The group left with residual disease today has survived more treatment than the group in which that benchmark was measured. Conjugates deliver a topoisomerase-I payload to antigens expressed on most triple-negative tumours. They work in pretreated metastatic disease. Residual disease is the earliest point at which that activity can be aimed at a population still curable.
Experiment
One randomised trial gives a conjugate with a checkpoint inhibitor against a physician's choice arm of the checkpoint inhibitor with or without capecitabine. Another gives a different conjugate, alone or with a checkpoint inhibitor, against investigator's choice of capecitabine, a checkpoint inhibitor or both. A third compares a conjugate against physician's choice in residual HER2-negative disease, in separate hormone receptor-positive and triple-negative cohorts. A single-arm neoadjuvant study had already shown that a conjugate alone produces pathological responses in untreated disease.
Finding
None of the randomised trials has reported. What is on the record is design, rationale and a single-arm response signal in untreated disease. It should be read as that and nothing more.
Limitation
A design paper is not an efficacy result and must never be cited as one. The comparator in each randomised trial is a choice rather than a fixed regimen. The control is therefore a mixture that will drift as practice drifts. A difference against it is harder to attribute than a difference against a named drug. The trial spanning hormone receptor-positive and triple-negative residual disease has to be read by cohort, because the two differ in biology and in what their choice arm is likely to contain. The single-arm study left subsequent systemic treatment to the treating physician. Its later outcomes describe a whole pathway rather than the conjugate.
Next question
If a conjugate, a PARP inhibitor and a checkpoint inhibitor each prove themselves against a control arm that contained neither of the other two, on what evidence does a clinic give one carrier with residual disease all three?

None of these three has reported, so what is on the record is design and rationale. They agree on most of it. All three randomise residual invasive disease after neoadjuvant chemotherapy. All three test a conjugate directed at Trop-2 and carrying a topoisomerase-I payload. All three set a choice of treatment as the comparator rather than a fixed regimen.

Where they differ is the choice they offer the control arm, and that is the difference that will decide how each result reads. ASCENT-05/OptimICE-RD offers pembrolizumab with or without capecitabine5. TROPION-Breast03 offers capecitabine, pembrolizumab or both6. SASCIA / GBG 102 spans hormone receptor positive and triple-negative cohorts, which have to be read separately, because the two differ in biology and in what a physician's choice is likely to contain. The gap between pembrolizumab alone and pembrolizumab with capecitabine is not small. A conjugate that beats the weaker of those options has not beaten current practice everywhere, and the comparator will drift as practice drifts.

TROPION-Breast03 is the one to watch for a reason unconnected to its drug. It randomises the conjugate alone as well as the conjugate with durvalumab, so it is the only trial of the three that can separate the conjugate from the antibody it is given with6. Without that arm a positive result belongs to a combination rather than to a class.

NeoSTAR sits outside this table and is the reason the class was brought here at all. A single-arm neoadjuvant study showing that a conjugate alone produces pathological responses in untreated disease is a proof of concept, not a comparison. Its patients had received nothing, and their subsequent systemic treatment was left to the treating physician, so its later outcomes describe a pathway rather than a drug. A design paper and a single-arm response rate are the two things in this field most often cited as though they were efficacy results.

Transfer from the metastatic setting is not automatic either. The populations in which these drugs established themselves had measurable, pretreated, progressing disease The biomarker triage, and what is left when it finds nothing. Residual disease is chemotherapy-selected, micrometastatic and invisible to imaging. None of these three trials requires Trop-2 expression for entry, and the metastatic trials did not require it either, so nothing is known about whether antigen abundance in a residual disease specimen predicts anything.

Toxicity carries a different weight in a population treated with curative intent. Neutropenia and diarrhoea with sacituzumab govitecan and interstitial lung disease with datopotamab deruxtecan are manageable risks in advanced disease Antibody-drug conjugates and targeted delivery. A treatment-related death in a patient who was going to be cured is a different event.

Interplay

The conjugates are being brought into this setting because they tolerate heterogeneity, which is the property developed in Bystander-capable modalities as a heterogeneity-tolerant strategy. A payload that kills neighbouring cells does not require every cell to carry the antigen. The complication specific to residual disease is that the population being treated has already been filtered. Cells surviving a platinum, an anthracycline, a taxane and a checkpoint inhibitor are not a random sample of the untreated tumour. Their antigen expression is not the antigen expression measured in a pretreatment core. A conjugate selected on metastatic biology is being asked to work in a compartment defined by what chemotherapy could not kill.

Post-surgical trials in triple-negative disease that have not reported.

OptimICE-pCR asks whether the adjuvant pembrolizumab phase can be dropped after a pathological complete response. A result showing no loss from omission would take the adjuvant phase and its permanent endocrine risk away from the majority of treated patients. An inferiority result would establish that the adjuvant phase carries effect, which no trial has yet shown.

ASCENT-05/OptimICE-RD and TROPION-Breast03 ask whether a Trop-2 directed conjugate beats the current post-neoadjuvant standard5,6. SASCIA / GBG 102 asks the same question across both HER2-negative subtypes, which makes it the only one of the three whose triple-negative result will arrive alongside a hormone receptor positive comparison in the same protocol. A positive result in any of them would displace capecitabine from the post-neoadjuvant standard. None would tell you what to do for a germline carrier, because olaparib is not the comparator in any arm.

Four questions have no trial running and should be named as such.

Whether capecitabine adds anything after a platinum-containing chemoimmunotherapy backbone is unmeasured, because the regimen it was tested against no longer exists.

Whether olaparib is better than capecitabine in a germline carrier has never been asked directly.

Whether pembrolizumab should be continued in residual disease after a pembrolizumab-containing neoadjuvant regimen is the highest-risk group's open question. It is currently answered by convention rather than by evidence.

Whether any systemic treatment helps stage I triple-negative disease remains an extrapolation Risk assessment and treatment selection.

Molecular residual disease detection would in principle identify who needs escalation here. Whether acting on a positive result changes outcome is a separate question, with the trials that address it collected in Liquid biopsy and longitudinal monitoring.
