Part X · Adjuvant and neoadjuvant therapy by subtype · Chapter 62

Adjuvant and post-neoadjuvant therapy in triple-negative disease

Why the systemic plan in this subtype is finished after surgery rather than before it.

1 · Scope, and the residual disease decision that organises it

This chapter covers what is given after surgery to a patient with triple-negative disease. What is given before surgery is in Neoadjuvant therapy in triple-negative disease including checkpoint blockade. The argument that frames both is in Early triple-negative disease, which these two chapters extend with trial-level detail.

One fact organises everything in the sections that follow. Response to neoadjuvant therapy is the strongest prognostic variable available in this subtype, with its trial-level limits set out in Endpoints, surrogates, and the overall survival question. It is generated by treatment rather than measured at baseline. A patient taken to surgery first cannot be stratified by it. The adjuvant options open to that patient are therefore narrower, which is a reason to settle the neoadjuvant question at the first consultation rather than after the operation.

The tables divide by the population a trial enrolled rather than by drug class. A trial that selected patients by residual disease belongs to a different evidence class from one that selected by stage after primary surgery. Results do not transfer from one of those populations to the other. Most of the apparent disagreement in this literature is disagreement about which population a number came from.

2 · Adjuvant chemotherapy regimens and dose density

The backbone itself is not in dispute anywhere in this literature. Sequential anthracycline and taxane remains the standard against which everything in this chapter is measured, on comparisons that are subtype-wide rather than specific to this disease16.

Dose density is a scheduling change rather than a dose increase. Total cytotoxic exposure is broadly the same in both arms of these trials, the difference being the interval between cycles. The label is routinely misread as more chemotherapy, which drives the wrong conversation about tolerability.

The evidence for it is meta-analytic rather than from any single trial. Across 26 trials and 37,298 women, ten-year recurrence was 28.0% with dose-intensified schedules against 31.4%, with a rate ratio of 0.86 and a 95% confidence interval of 0.82 to 0.89. Ten-year breast cancer mortality was 18.9% against 21.3%17. The proportional reduction was similar across subtypes rather than concentrated in this one. Absolute gain therefore tracks absolute risk, which is why this schedule matters most in node-positive triple-negative disease.

The two randomised trials that anchor the dose-density meta-analysis.

What no trial provides is a randomised comparison of adjuvant chemotherapy against no chemotherapy in stage I triple-negative disease. Treatment below stage II is extrapolation from prognosis, on the reasoning set out in Risk assessment and treatment selection.

In practice

Give a taxane and an anthracycline sequentially, at two-weekly intervals with growth factor support, unless something specific argues against it.

Do not substitute single-agent capecitabine for that regimen. It was tested as a substitute and was inferior, most clearly in hormone receptor negative disease CALGB 4990712.

Reserve the discussion about omitting the anthracycline for patients being treated before surgery, where it is an open randomised question The other immunotherapy trials and why they read out differently.

Platinum given after surgery, either added to the backbone or replacing part of it.

The first is whether carboplatin added to a standard backbone improves outcome. Two randomised trials say yes on their primary endpoints in unselected triple-negative populations. The hazard in CITRINE is not proportional, which is the reason to read its piecewise estimates. The hazard ratio was 0.31 in the first year, 0.65 between one and three years, and 1.98 thereafter3. A curve that separates early and then converges is consistent with deferred rather than prevented recurrence. Longer follow-up is the only thing that will settle which of those it is.

The second is whether platinum can replace the anthracycline. PATTERN replaced an anthracycline regimen with a platinum and taxane doublet, changing both drugs at once11. A comparison in which the experimental arm differs from the control in two components cannot attribute its result to either one.

The third is whether platinum is the right drug for residual disease. EA1131 tested platinum against capecitabine in that population and stopped early on the recommendation of its monitoring committee7. The more useful finding in that trial is not the comparison it was designed to make. The design assumed a four-year invasive disease-free survival of 67% with capecitabine. Observed three-year estimates in basal residual disease were far below that in both arms. Residual disease after neoadjuvant chemotherapy is a worse prognostic state than the trials that study it have assumed.

Caution

All of these trials were run without checkpoint blockade. In stage II to III disease treated with the KEYNOTE-522 regimen, carboplatin has already been given to every patient before surgery18. The adjuvant platinum question now applies mainly to patients treated with surgery first, and to patients whose neoadjuvant regimen contained no platinum. Applying these results to a patient who has already had four cycles of carboplatin is an extrapolation with no data behind it.

4 · Capecitabine for residual disease

The population in CREATE-X was HER2-negative, not triple-negative13. Its triple-negative patients were a subgroup of that trial rather than the trial itself. The effect in that subgroup was larger than the effect in the whole population, which is why the subgroup result changed practice. It remains a subgroup result from a trial that stopped early at a positive interim analysis.

Two features of that trial constrain how far the result travels. Its control arm received neither carboplatin nor pembrolizumab at any point. The residual disease it selected was therefore selected by a weaker regimen than the one used today. The incremental value of capecitabine after a platinum-containing chemoimmunotherapy backbone has never been measured.

Capecitabine as an addition, as a substitute, and as the comparator for platinum.

The two trials that gave capecitabine to unselected early triple-negative disease disagree, and the disagreement is instructive. CIBOMA/GEICAM found a disease-free survival hazard ratio of 0.82 with a confidence interval of 0.63 to 1.0610. SYSUCC-001 found 0.64 with an interval of 0.42 to 0.959. The drug in the two trials is the same. The dose, the schedule, the duration and the enrolled population are not. One gave eight conventional cycles and the other gave a year of continuous low-dose treatment.

Their preplanned subgroup signals also point in opposite directions from one another. The preplanned analysis in CIBOMA/GEICAM suggested benefit confined to non-basal disease, with an overall survival interaction p value of 0.005210. CREATE-X found its largest effect in the triple-negative subgroup13. Both subgroup claims cannot be describing the same underlying biology.

In practice

Offer capecitabine for residual invasive disease after neoadjuvant chemotherapy in a patient without a germline BRCA variant. Six to eight cycles, with dose reduction expected.

Warn about hand-foot syndrome before the first cycle rather than after it. It occurred in 73.4% of treated patients in CREATE-X13.

Do not use adjuvant capecitabine outside the residual disease indication on the strength of SYSUCC-001 alone. The trial that used a conventional schedule in the same setting was negative.

5 · Adjuvant PARP inhibition for germline carriers

OlympiA enrolled germline BRCA1 and BRCA2 carriers with high-risk HER2-negative disease, so its population includes hormone receptor positive patients8. Entry to that trial ran through two different routes. Patients treated with surgery first qualified on pathological stage. Patients treated before surgery qualified on residual disease. For a triple-negative patient the second route is usually the relevant one, which makes germline status and response status jointly determine the post-neoadjuvant plan.

The only randomised adjuvant PARP inhibitor trial in early breast cancer.

Follow-up is now long enough for the survival result to stand on its own19. That is unusual in this space and it is the reason olaparib rather than capecitabine is offered to a carrier.

Three things that trial does not establish should be said out loud.

It never compared olaparib with capecitabine, which is the alternative in this population. The preference rests on a larger population, a longer follow-up and a placebo control, which are reasons to prefer the evidence rather than evidence of superiority.

It did not test olaparib alongside pembrolizumab in any arm. A patient with residual disease after KEYNOTE-522 who carries a germline variant is now routinely offered both, on the strength of two trials that each excluded the other drug.

It enrolled on germline status alone, with no somatic or functional assay. Somatic BRCA alterations, PALB2 and the wider homologous recombination gene set were not eligibility criteria. The distinction between a germline mutation, a somatic one and a genomic scar is developed in Germline and somatic DNA repair alterations and DNA repair, replication stress, and genomic instability.

Caution

Germline testing has to be resulted before the post-neoadjuvant decision, not after it. The window between surgery and the start of adjuvant treatment is short. A test sent at the time of the residual disease result will often return too late to change the first cycle, and the pathway in Inherited susceptibility and familial breast cancer is built around sending it at diagnosis.

Checkpoint blockade started or continued after surgery. Neither reported trial met its primary endpoint.

ALEXANDRA/IMpassion030 stopped for futility with a hazard ratio above one4. That is the cleanest available statement that the timing matters, with the mechanistic reading in Why response rates in breast cancer sit where they do.

A-BRAVE is the harder of the two to interpret. It missed both co-primary disease-free survival endpoints, while reporting a descriptive overall survival hazard ratio of 0.66 against a disease-free survival hazard ratio of 0.812. A trial whose disease-free survival curves do not separate convincingly should not show a larger separation in overall survival. The honest reading is that the overall survival analysis was descriptive, the trial was not powered for it, and the result is hypothesis-generating. It should also be read as a post-neoadjuvant trial rather than an adjuvant one, because more than four fifths of its patients entered with residual disease.

The question in the heading is the unresolved one. KEYNOTE-522 gave pembrolizumab before and after surgery, to every patient, including those with a pathological complete response18. The trial cannot apportion its effect between the two phases. GeparNuevo gave no checkpoint blockade after surgery at all and still produced a survival benefit20,21. That is the strongest indirect argument that the adjuvant phase may be contributing little.

OptimICE-pCR tests omission after a pathological complete response, which is the half of the question with the least at stake. The other half of the question has no trial at all. No randomised study is asking whether pembrolizumab should be continued in residual disease after a pembrolizumab-containing neoadjuvant regimen, which is where the recurrence risk actually sits.

Caution

A regimen tested as a whole becomes unfalsifiable in parts. The decision to continue pembrolizumab after surgery was made once, at trial design, and every patient since has inherited it. Nine adjuvant cycles carry a real risk of permanent endocrinopathy in people who are already cured Immune-related toxicity in a curative-intent population. The evidential status of those cycles and their toxicity are not symmetrical.

7 · Conjugates after residual disease

Residual disease is the enrichment that makes a post-surgical trial of an expensive drug feasible. The event rate is high enough to power an invasive disease-free survival endpoint in a few thousand patients. That is the design logic established in HER2-positive disease by KATHERINE and developed in Post-neoadjuvant intensification for residual disease.

Trop-2 directed conjugates in residual triple-negative disease. Both trials are accruing.

Two features of these designs will determine how the results are read.

The control arm in both trials is a physician's choice of treatment. That makes the comparator depend on local practice. The gap between pembrolizumab alone and pembrolizumab with capecitabine is not small. A conjugate that beats the weaker of those two options has not beaten current practice everywhere.

Transfer from the metastatic setting to this one is not automatic. The populations in which these drugs established themselves had measurable, pretreated, progressing disease Metastatic triple-negative disease. Residual disease is chemotherapy-selected, micrometastatic and invisible to imaging. The metastatic trials did not require target expression for entry, so nothing is known about whether Trop-2 abundance in a residual disease specimen predicts anything.

Toxicity carries a different weight in a population treated with curative intent. Neutropenia and diarrhoea with sacituzumab govitecan and interstitial lung disease with datopotamab deruxtecan are manageable risks in advanced disease Antibody-drug conjugates and targeted delivery. A treatment-related death in a patient who was going to be cured is a different event.

Interplay

The conjugates are being brought into this setting because they tolerate heterogeneity, which is the property developed in Bystander-capable modalities as a heterogeneity-tolerant strategy. A payload that kills neighbouring cells does not require every cell to carry the antigen. The complication specific to residual disease is that the population being treated has already been filtered. Cells surviving a platinum, an anthracycline, a taxane and a checkpoint inhibitor are not a random sample of the untreated tumour. Their antigen expression is not the antigen expression measured in a pretreatment core. A conjugate selected on metastatic biology is being asked to work in a compartment defined by what chemotherapy could not kill.

Post-surgical trials in triple-negative disease that have not reported.

OptimICE-pCR asks whether the adjuvant pembrolizumab phase can be dropped after a pathological complete response. A non-inferiority result would remove nine cycles and their permanent endocrine risk from the majority of treated patients. An inferiority result would establish that the adjuvant phase carries effect, which no trial has yet shown.

ASCENT-05/OptimICE-RD and TROPION-Breast03 ask whether a Trop-2 directed conjugate beats the current post-neoadjuvant standard5,6. Either positive result would displace capecitabine from the post-neoadjuvant standard. Neither would tell you what to do for a germline carrier, since olaparib is not the comparator.

Four questions have no trial running and should be named as such.

Whether capecitabine adds anything after a platinum-containing chemoimmunotherapy backbone is unmeasured, because the regimen it was tested against no longer exists.

Whether olaparib is better than capecitabine in a germline carrier has never been asked directly.

Whether pembrolizumab should be continued in residual disease after a pembrolizumab-containing neoadjuvant regimen is the highest-risk group's open question. It is currently answered by convention rather than by evidence.

Whether any systemic treatment helps stage I triple-negative disease remains an extrapolation Risk assessment and treatment selection.

Molecular residual disease detection would in principle identify who needs escalation here. Whether acting on a positive result changes outcome is a separate question, with the trials that address it collected in Liquid biopsy and longitudinal monitoring.
