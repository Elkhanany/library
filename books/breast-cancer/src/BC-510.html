Part XIII · Triple-negative disease · Chapter 75

Early disease

Where the survival gain outran the response gain, and what that did to the endpoint.

1 · Risk assessment and treatment selection

Selection here is driven by stage more than by biology, because no biological variable in routine use changes the regimen.

Stage II and stage III disease is treated with neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy, which is the population KEYNOTE-522 enrolled1. The evidence in Neoadjuvant chemotherapy with immunotherapy is confined to that population and does not extend below it.

Below stage II the evidence thins fast. The smallest node-negative tumours were excluded from the trials that define current practice, so treatment there is extrapolation from prognosis rather than from a randomised result. Node-negative triple-negative tumours with dense lymphocytic infiltration have a substantially better prognosis without chemotherapy than those without it2,3. That observation is prognostic. It has not been used prospectively to withhold treatment, and the scoring convention that makes it reproducible is in4.

What a surgery-first patient actually receives comes from a different generation of trials. It is worth seeing what they enrolled.

The randomised trials that built the adjuvant chemotherapy backbone. Most enrolled all comers before subtype was routinely recorded, which is the population column's point.

Only two of those rows restricted entry by receptor status. Both did so by HER2 rather than by triple-negative status. The taxane, the schedule and the anthracycline question were all settled in populations defined by nodal status and tumour size. They were applied to this subtype afterwards.

The rows agree on more than they are usually credited with. Adding a taxane after doxorubicin and cyclophosphamide lowered recurrence in both trials that tested it. Shortening the interval between cycles lowered it again in both trials that tested that. Escalating the doxorubicin dose did nothing, in the same trial that established the taxane. The backbone was therefore built by adding agents and by compressing intervals. It was never built by giving more of the same drug.

The rows that look like a disagreement are the ones about removing the anthracycline, and the disagreement is design rather than drug. US Oncology 9735 set docetaxel and cyclophosphamide against doxorubicin and cyclophosphamide, a control arm with no taxane in it8. ABC trials and WSG-PlanB set a docetaxel and cyclophosphamide regimen against a control containing both a taxane and an anthracycline6,5. Those are two different questions sharing one experimental arm. The two later trials then disagree with each other, and their noninferiority margins are not the same object either. ABC tested a hazard ratio against a fixed boundary. PlanB tested a difference in five-year disease-free survival counted in percentage points. A regimen can fail one convention and pass the other on data of the same shape.

ABC is the row the decision rests on. It enrolled HER2-negative disease specifically, it prespecified a joint analysis across three trials, and it reported early for futility with the hazard ratio above the boundary it had to clear. Noninferiority was not declared. That is why an anthracycline is still given where risk is high, and why leaving one out is a judgement rather than a result13. Dose density is where the transfer into this subtype is best supported, and Adjuvant chemotherapy regimens and dose density holds the two trials that anchor it.

What the table cannot show is a triple-negative population. Not one of these trials enrolled the subtype on its own, and the oldest of them ran before subtype was routinely recorded. Nothing here was randomised in the presence of carboplatin or of a checkpoint inhibitor, so the regimens the table settled are now the backbone underneath drugs they never met. PlanB's genomic omission rule applied to hormone receptor-positive tumours only, so the table holds no test of withholding chemotherapy on an assay in this disease.

Two things should happen at diagnosis in every patient regardless of stage.

Germline testing should be arranged before the systemic plan is fixed. A BRCA1 or BRCA2 pathogenic variant changes the post-neoadjuvant option Post-neoadjuvant systemic therapy, the surgical conversation and the family's testing, and the strategy is set out in Inherited susceptibility and familial breast cancer.

And the neoadjuvant decision should be taken deliberately rather than by default. If the tumour is large enough or node-positive enough that residual disease would trigger a different adjuvant regimen, treating first is what makes that regimen available.

This chapter is the decision architecture and the argument. The trial record it rests on is split between two neighbours. What is given before surgery is in Neoadjuvant therapy and checkpoint blockade. What is given after it is in Adjuvant and post-neoadjuvant therapy. Where the two touch, this chapter points forward rather than restating.

2 · Neoadjuvant chemotherapy with immunotherapy

Early disease · TNBC · neoadjuvant

Choosing what to add to preoperative chemotherapy, and what to take out

Does adding a checkpoint inhibitor to preoperative chemotherapy lower recurrence, and does it have to be given before surgery?

Rationale
Triple-negative tumours carry more tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes and more neoantigen than the other subtypes. Chemotherapy releases antigen as it kills. A checkpoint inhibitor given while the primary tumour and its draining nodes are still in place should prime a wider response than one given to a patient who has neither left.
Experiment
Several randomised additions of a checkpoint inhibitor to preoperative chemotherapy. They differ in the drug, in whether the backbone carried a platinum, and in whether the checkpoint inhibitor continued after surgery. Set against them, two trials gave a checkpoint inhibitor only after surgery, with no preoperative phase at all. A further trial is randomising the omission of the post-surgical half in patients whose tumours cleared.
Finding
Adding a checkpoint inhibitor raised pathological complete response over the same chemotherapy with placebo in most of these trials. In the trial that also continued it after surgery, the event-free survival advantage held. A large trial of a different checkpoint inhibitor, given across both halves of the pathway, missed its primary endpoint. Both trials that waited until after surgery were negative on their primary endpoints.
Limitation
The practice-defining trial varies more than the checkpoint inhibitor. Its backbone carries a platinum. Its treatment continues after surgery. Neither of those was randomised separately. The trial cannot say which part is load-bearing. The trial that ran no post-surgical phase reported a survival separation its own response endpoint did not support, on few events. Checkpoint ligand expression did not select here the way it does in metastatic disease. The co-primary analysis in the marker-positive population did not cross its significance boundary. The assay and its cut-point differ between these trials in any case. The adjuvant-only trials differ in drug and in population as well as in timing. The case for the preoperative window is therefore drawn between trials rather than inside one. Whether the post-surgical half is needed at all is only now being randomised. That trial has not reported.

Checkpoint blockade in early triple-negative disease, including the two trials that gave it only after surgery. Read the arms column for whether treatment continued past the operation.

Ten rows and four designs. Six randomised a checkpoint inhibitor added to preoperative chemotherapy. Two gave one only after surgery. One is a single-arm regimen study. One is still recruiting.

The agreement is narrower than the class language suggests, and it sits on the time-to-event side. Among the preoperative trials that have followed patients past surgery, every point estimate for event-free or overall survival favours the antibody. The response endpoint did not track it. KEYNOTE-522 and IMpassion031 raised response over placebo. GeparNuevo missed its response endpoint and its survival curves separated anyway. That dissociation is the finding, and Pathologic response and residual disease adaptation is where it is argued.

Where the rows diverge, the variable is rarely the drug. Atezolizumab appears four times in this table and gives four answers. IMpassion031 raised response before surgery. NeoTRIP did not. NSABP B-59/GeparDouze moved event-free survival without clearing its significance boundary. ALEXANDRA/IMpassion030 was stopped for futility. What differs between them is the chemotherapy underneath and whether the primary tumour was still in place. IMpassion031 ran nab-paclitaxel and then an anthracycline with no platinum19. NeoTRIP ran carboplatin with nab-paclitaxel and deferred the anthracycline until after surgery18. B-59 ran all three agents and continued treatment past the operation14. ALEXANDRA gave chemotherapy after surgery to patients who had none before it16. Reading any of these as a verdict on atezolizumab means ignoring the columns that differ.

The preoperative trials also differ in what they were powered to detect. GeparNuevo randomised 174 patients on a response endpoint. B-59 randomised 1550 on event-free survival. A response difference that misses in the first and a hazard ratio that misses in the second are not the same kind of negative. I-SPY2 pembrolizumab arm is a third kind again. It estimated response against a shared adaptively randomised control, with Bayesian graduation, in a HER2-negative population of which a minority had triple-negative disease20. That is a screening result, and what it did was send the drug to phase 3.

KEYNOTE-522 is the trial the decision rests on, and the reason is structural rather than numerical. It carries a response endpoint and a time-to-event endpoint inside one randomisation, it reported overall survival, and its control arm is the chemotherapy the clinic would otherwise give1,22,23. No other row has all three properties.

GeparNuevo is the row that separates the two halves of the pathway. Durvalumab stopped at surgery, so whatever it did was done before the operation21,24,25. That separation is a secondary endpoint of a phase 2 trial that missed its primary response endpoint, and it rests on few events. KEYNOTE-522 cannot make the attribution at all, because every patient in its experimental arm received the antibody on both sides of the operation.

Both trials that waited until after surgery were negative on their primary endpoints. They are not replicates of each other. ALEXANDRA gave atezolizumab with adjuvant chemotherapy to patients treated by surgery first. A-BRAVE gave avelumab alone to a population that was mostly residual disease after neoadjuvant chemotherapy15. Different drug, different population, different relationship to the chemotherapy. The case that the preoperative window is where the drug works is therefore drawn between trials and never inside one. Nobody has randomised the same antibody before and after surgery.

The rest of the table carries limits of its own. A-BRAVE's overall survival hazard ratio is descriptive and its own disease-free survival co-primaries were not met, so it is a survival signal without the endpoint that would support it. ALEXANDRA stopped at a futility analysis with overall survival not reported, so it cannot exclude a late difference either way. NeoPACT is single-arm and appears here only because its regimen contains pembrolizumab, so its response rate has no concurrent control17. SCARLET has not reported.

The table also has no column for checkpoint ligand expression, and one would not help. The assays and their cut-points differ between these trials. IMpassion031's marker-positive co-primary did not cross its own significance boundary19. The missing predictive biomarker is where that is argued, the priming argument is in Why response rates in breast cancer sit where they do, and the trial-by-trial reading is in The other immunotherapy trials and why they read out differently.

Caution

Three trials of the same class in the same disease gave a survival benefit, a missed primary endpoint and a futility stop. The differences include the agent, the chemotherapy backbone, whether the tumour was in place, and whether treatment continued after surgery. None of those variables was isolated. Reading any one of these results as a statement about checkpoint blockade in triple-negative breast cancer, rather than about the regimen actually tested, overstates what the evidence supports.

Platinum before surgery. Two of these rows randomised the drug and two selected the patient by a repair assay.

Three of these rows added carboplatin to an anthracycline and taxane course before surgery, on three different backbones, and all three raised pathological complete response. That is the most reproducible response finding in this subtype.

They then diverge on outcome, and the divergence is sample size and trial structure rather than drug. CALGB 40603 found no long-term gain from carboplatin at a median follow-up of 7.9 years32. It randomised 443 patients on a response endpoint, with a second randomisation to bevacizumab running through the same factorial30. BrighTNess reported a 4.5-year event-free survival hazard ratio of 0.57 for carboplatin with paclitaxel against paclitaxel alone33. GeparSixto reported a disease-free survival hazard ratio of 0.56 for carboplatin in its triple-negative stratum34. Neither of those two was powered for the endpoint it reported. Three trials reading out on an endpoint none of them was designed to test is not a disagreement about carboplatin.

PEARLY is the row the decision now rests on. It randomised carboplatin against no carboplatin on a doxorubicin, cyclophosphamide and taxane backbone, in 868 patients, with event-free survival as the primary endpoint26. Two features make it load-bearing rather than merely positive. The arms differ in the drug and in nothing else. And it enrolled patients treated before and after surgery, so the gain it reports does not have to run through pathological response. Overall survival favoured carboplatin without reaching significance.

The other two rows selected patients rather than randomising the drug against a common control. INFORM randomised germline carriers to cisplatin or to doxorubicin with cyclophosphamide, and cisplatin did not win27. That is a comparison of platinum against an alkylator rather than against nothing, and an alkylator is active in a repair-deficient tumour. TBCRC 030 randomised cisplatin against paclitaxel in wild-type or untested disease and asked whether a homologous recombination deficiency score, positive at 33 or above, predicted response28. It predicted response to neither drug and showed no interaction with either. Both trials read out at surgery and neither reports a survival endpoint.

The mechanistic case for germline selection comes from the metastatic setting. TNT compared carboplatin with docetaxel in unselected advanced triple-negative disease and found no difference in response. Among patients with germline BRCA1 or BRCA2 variants, carboplatin produced double the response rate of docetaxel, at 68% against 33%, with a biomarker-by-treatment interaction p value of 0.0135. Benefit was not seen in patients selected by BRCA1 methylation, by low BRCA1 messenger RNA, or by a high score on a commercial homologous recombination deficiency assay35. A germline mutation predicted platinum sensitivity there. The assays built to capture the same phenotype did not. Before surgery only the second half of that repeats. INFORM's carriers did no better on cisplatin than on an alkylator, and TBCRC 030's score predicted response to neither drug.

What the table cannot show is the modern backbone. No row randomised carboplatin on top of a pembrolizumab-containing regimen. Carboplatin sits inside the KEYNOTE-522 backbone and was given to both of its arms, so that trial cannot estimate the platinum contribution either1. PEARLY's control carried no checkpoint inhibitor. The value of carboplatin in the regimen actually used today is therefore held by transfer rather than by a trial. BrighTNess stratified its randomisation on germline BRCA status and the table has no column for that stratum. BrighTNess also holds the one randomised PARP question asked before surgery here, and adding veliparib to carboplatin added nothing to response29.

The biology of homologous recombination deficiency and the difference between a germline mutation, a functional deficiency and a genomic scar are developed in DNA repair, replication stress, and genomic instability, and the assay landscape in DNA repair and homologous recombination deficiency assays. The practical position is that germline testing changes management and somatic repair assays do not.

4 · Pathologic response and residual disease adaptation

Early disease · TNBC · post-neoadjuvant

Allocating further treatment on what surgery finds

Does what surgery finds after neoadjuvant treatment identify who needs more treatment?

Rationale
Giving the same chemotherapy before surgery rather than after it does not change survival. The reason to give it first is that surgery then reports whether the drugs worked. In triple-negative disease that report separates the population more sharply than anything measurable before treatment. A complete response is common in this subtype. Residual invasive disease relapses within a few years of surgery rather than over the decades that hormone receptor-positive disease can take.
Experiment
The foundational trials randomised the timing of chemotherapy, and then the addition of a taxane to the preoperative course, with survival as the endpoint and pathological response recorded alongside. A platform trial read response against outcome across its molecular subtypes on a shared chemotherapy control. Randomised trials that added carboplatin to preoperative chemotherapy reported the response rate first and their survival endpoints years later. The registration trial for chemo-immunotherapy carried both endpoints inside one randomisation.
Finding
Response sorts the population steeply. Patients who clear the breast and the axilla relapse rarely. Residual disease accounts for most of the events. That sorting is prognostic within an arm. It has repeatedly failed to behave as a surrogate between arms. Carboplatin raised the response rate over the same chemotherapy without it, and the later outcome analyses of those same trials found no gain. Where a trial moved both endpoints together, that is agreement rather than evidence that the first predicts the second.
Limitation
The timing trials enrolled all comers before subtype was routinely recorded. The response gradient they established is being read into a subtype they did not define. What counts as a complete response is a convention rather than a fact. The trials differ on whether residual in-situ disease disqualifies it. Residual cancer burden grades what that dichotomy discards. What the pathologist reports also depends on how the specimen and the post-chemotherapy axilla were sampled. Residual disease marks resistance to the regimen that was actually given. It says nothing about drugs the tumour never met.
Next question
If residual disease marks the population carrying the events, does giving those patients a further course of a different chemotherapy lower recurrence?

Pathological complete response in this subtype is the strongest patient-level prognostic marker in breast oncology, and it is not a valid trial-level surrogate. Both statements come from the same analysis and both are true.

One pooled analysis covered 12 trials and 11,955 patients. Among those with triple-negative disease, eradication of tumour from breast and nodes carried an event-free survival hazard ratio of 0.24, with a 95% confidence interval of 0.18 to 0.33. The overall survival hazard ratio was 0.16, with an interval of 0.11 to 0.2536. The trial-level analysis of the same dataset ran the other way. The increase in pathological complete response frequency between treatment groups explained almost none of the variation in event-free survival, with a coefficient of determination of 0.03. For overall survival that coefficient was 0.2436.

The gradient itself was established by NSABP B-18 and NSABP B-27, which randomised when chemotherapy was given and then what was added to the preoperative course. Both carried survival as the endpoint and recorded response alongside it. Both enrolled all comers, before subtype was routinely recorded. The sorting this subtype is now managed by was measured in a population that did not distinguish it.

Two trials in this chapter raised response and did not move outcome. Carboplatin raised pathological complete response in CALGB 40603 and produced no long-term gain32. Nab-paclitaxel with carboplatin raised it over nab-paclitaxel with gemcitabine in WSG-ADAPT-TN, and the invasive disease-free survival hazard ratio for the arm that produced the higher response rate was 1.0437. In that same trial, five-year invasive disease-free survival was 90.6% in patients who cleared the breast and the axilla and 62.8% in those who did not. One dataset, two directions. The gradient within an arm is steep and the shift between arms carries almost nothing.

I-SPY 2 is the design that reads response against outcome across molecular subtypes while holding one chemotherapy control common to all of them. KEYNOTE-522 moved both endpoints inside a single randomisation. That is agreement between two measurements rather than evidence that the first predicts the second.

What counts as a complete response is a convention, and the trials in this chapter do not share one. GeparSixto scored it as no residual invasive or in-situ carcinoma in breast and nodes. WSG-ADAPT-TN and INFORM allowed residual in-situ disease. CALGB 40603 reported the breast alone and the breast with the axilla as two separate rates, which are two separate numbers. A response rate is therefore not comparable across trials that define it differently, and eradication from the breast alone carries a weaker association with outcome than eradication from both36. What the pathologist can report also depends on how the specimen was sampled and on how the post-chemotherapy axilla was handled.

GeparNuevo is what the surrogacy failure looks like inside a single trial rather than across a pooled dataset. Its response endpoint was not met and its survival curves separated regardless. The authors' own analysis puts that separation outside the response effect24. Whatever moved the curves reached patients whose tumours were not eradicated, which is precisely the population a response-mediated mechanism cannot reach.

Residual disease is not one prognosis. It is a spectrum, which is why Residual cancer burden and the continuous measures in Residual cancer burden and continuous response measures carry more information than a binary result. Immune context adds a further axis to that spectrum. Stromal lymphocyte density is prognostic in this subtype and is scored to a published convention38,4. It is measurable on the residual disease specimen as well as on the diagnostic core, and the long-term GeparNuevo report reads it there25. Those subgroups are small, so the direction is the finding rather than the size.

Residual disease marks resistance to the regimen that was actually given. It says nothing about a drug the tumour never met. Every post-neoadjuvant trial in Post-neoadjuvant systemic therapy makes that assumption, and none of them tests it.

In practice

Report response as breast and nodes together. Eradication from the breast alone carries a weaker association with outcome than eradication from both36.

Use response to assign the next treatment, not to evaluate the last one. Assigning on observed response is what Post-neoadjuvant systemic therapy rests on, and it is a patient-level use that survives the surrogacy failure.

When a regimen raises pathological complete response and has no mature survival data, say that the survival question is open. The trial-level correlation between the two is close to nothing36.

Ask for stromal lymphocyte scoring on the residual disease specimen where it is available. It is prognostic and it costs a slide25,4.

Three of the seven rows in the first table have reported and one of them changed practice. The other two are negative about different things, which is the distinction the table cannot draw for the reader.

CREATE-X is load-bearing because of its control arm. Patients with residual disease were randomised against no additional chemotherapy, which is what the clinic would otherwise do. Its triple-negative subgroup carried a larger effect on both endpoints than the trial as a whole42. That subgroup is a subgroup of a trial that enrolled both. The control arm received neither carboplatin nor pembrolizumab, so its residual disease was selected by a weaker regimen than the one used today. What capecitabine adds after a platinum-containing chemoimmunotherapy backbone has not been measured anywhere.

EA1131 is negative about drug choice rather than about treating at all. It set a platinum against capecitabine in patients who were all going to receive something, and it stopped early at 61% of the information it was designed to accumulate41. Both arms did worse than the design had assumed. A noninferiority trial that closes on a control arm performing below its own planning assumption cannot rule the experimental arm in or out Capecitabine for residual disease.

A-BRAVE appears in this table because most of its population entered on residual disease. It gave avelumab after surgery with no preoperative phase, and neither co-primary disease-free survival endpoint was met15. Its overall survival hazard ratio is descriptive. The section's other timing evidence is in Neoadjuvant chemotherapy with immunotherapy, and the same caution applies here. A survival separation that the trial's own disease-free endpoint does not support is a reason to run another trial.

OlympiA is the second established option and it is restricted to germline carriers43,44. Its two entry routes were residual disease after neoadjuvant chemotherapy and stage after primary surgery. The trial was not designed to separate them. What olaparib is worth specifically in residual triple-negative disease is therefore an inference from a stratified trial rather than a result of it. Its comparator was placebo, which makes it the cleanest randomisation in this section and also the least like any comparison a clinic now faces.

Caution

Olaparib and capecitabine have never been compared. The preference for olaparib in a germline carrier rests on a larger randomised population, a longer follow-up, a placebo control and a contemporary chemotherapy background. Those are reasons to prefer the evidence, not evidence of superiority. A patient told that olaparib is better should be told the comparison does not exist.

The four rows that have not reported are where this section will change, and three of them are asking one class question with three different control arms. ASCENT-05/OptimICE-RD randomises sacituzumab govitecan with pembrolizumab against pembrolizumab with or without capecitabine39. TROPION-Breast03 randomises datopotamab deruxtecan with durvalumab, and datopotamab deruxtecan alone, against investigator's choice of capecitabine, pembrolizumab or both40. SASCIA / GBG 102 randomises sacituzumab govitecan against physician's choice in a population that includes hormone receptor-positive disease. When they report they will not be comparable with one another, and the control arms are the reason.

The fourth unreported row is the adjuvant checkpoint phase. KEYNOTE-522 gave up to nine adjuvant cycles to every patient in its experimental arm, including those with a pathological complete response. It cannot separate the neoadjuvant from the adjuvant contribution1. GeparNuevo gave no adjuvant checkpoint blockade at all. Its survival curves separated regardless, as a secondary endpoint of a phase 2 trial that missed its primary24,25. OptimICE-pCR is randomising omission after a complete response directly, and De-escalation questions in the immunotherapy era is where that design is read.

Neither table carries a column for the neoadjuvant regimen, and that regimen is the variable that selected the population in every row. Residual disease after anthracycline and taxane alone is not the same state as residual disease after carboplatin and pembrolizumab. No reported row tested its experimental arm on top of the adjuvant pembrolizumab most of these patients now also receive.

Molecular residual disease detection would in principle identify who needs post-neoadjuvant escalation. Whether acting on a positive result improves outcome is unresolved, and the evidence including the ctDNA-guided intervention trials is in Liquid biopsy and longitudinal monitoring.

6 · Immune-related toxicity in a curative-intent population

The calculation that governs immune toxicity here is not the one used in metastatic disease.

About half the patients in the control arm of KEYNOTE-522 reached a pathological complete response without pembrolizumab1. A permanent endocrinopathy in a patient who would have been cured by chemotherapy alone is a different event from the same endocrinopathy in someone with incurable disease. The trial cannot identify which patients those were, which is the whole difficulty.

What the antibody adds does not show up on the cytotoxic scale. In NSABP B-59/GeparDouze, grade 3 or higher treatment-emergent adverse events were 75.3% with atezolizumab and 73.4% with placebo14. A toxicity table built for cytotoxic drugs therefore reports the two arms as nearly the same. The events that separate them are a different class with a different natural history. In GeparNuevo, thyroid dysfunction of any grade occurred in 47% of patients21.

Three structural features of these toxicities, developed in Immune-related toxicity and its management, matter most in this setting. Onset is often delayed and is not dose-related. Reversibility differs by organ, and endocrinopathy frequently does not reverse. And the same numerical grade carries different urgency in different organs, which is why the management algorithms branch by organ45.

In practice

Hold the immunotherapy and continue the chemotherapy when an immune-related event is suspected. Those are separable decisions and treating them as one costs the patient the cytotoxic backbone that most of the curative effect came from.

Check thyroid function before every cycle. It is the commonest event and the cheapest to find21.

Steroids for a confirmed grade 2 or higher event, by the organ-specific algorithm, rather than by grade alone45.

Say before the first dose that a minority of patients acquire a permanent endocrine deficiency. Say also that about half the control arm of the defining trial cleared the tumour on chemotherapy alone1. That is the honest frame for consent in a curative setting.

Surveillance does not stop at the last dose. Events occur months after treatment ends, so the handover to primary care has to carry that instruction explicitly Rehabilitation and survivorship.

De-escalation in this disease is harder to prove than to propose. 8 · The missing predictive biomarker

Everyone with stage II or III triple-negative breast cancer receives checkpoint blockade because no test identifies who will benefit. That is a statement about measurement, not about biology.

PD-L1 does not select in this setting. KEYNOTE-522 enrolled without a PD-L1 criterion and established benefit in the unselected population1,23. In metastatic disease the same class works only above a combined positive score of 10, and loosens to nothing below it48. The assays themselves are not interchangeable, because the combined positive score and the immune cell score count different cells over different denominators, and that argument is in Predictive biomarkers and patient selection. The metastatic trials that do select on the assay are tabulated in Biomarkers for immunotherapy benefit, and why PD-L1 fails here.

The two early trials that did look at the ligand show the gap between an association and a randomised prediction. IMpassion031 carried a marker-positive co-primary endpoint and it did not cross its significance boundary19. NeoTRIP found checkpoint ligand expression the strongest factor for pathological complete response in its multivariable analysis, in a trial whose randomised comparison of atezolizumab was null18. A marker associated with response in both arms is prognostic. Only an interaction with treatment makes it predictive, and neither trial produced one.

Tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes are prognostic and reproducibly scored, and they are not established as predictive of checkpoint benefit38,3,4. Prognostic and predictive are different properties, and the difference is defined in Analytical validity, clinical validity, and clinical utility defined.

Tumour mutational burden carries tumour-agnostic evidence from trials that included no breast cohort49, and a high burden is uncommon in this disease50. The eligible population is small before the question of whether the inference transfers is reached.

One exploratory signal from the negative trial is worth naming without overreading. In NSABP B-59/GeparDouze, the subgroup analyses pointed to benefit in patients presenting with clinical lymph node involvement14. That is a subgroup of a trial that missed its primary endpoint, and nodal status is a stage variable rather than an assay. It cannot serve as the predictive marker this section is looking for.

Interplay

The reason no single marker predicts here is the reason Part VI gives for most biomarker failure in this disease. Immune response is a property of an organisation rather than of a molecule, and the spatial phenotypes in Inflamed, excluded, and desert phenotypes separate patients that any percentage computed on bulk tissue cannot. PD-L1 scored on a block reports abundance and discards arrangement, exactly as a HER2 percentage does in Spatial patterns, clustered, mosaic, and scattered. Immune pressure also selects among a mixed population in the way drug pressure does, so the compartment that presents antigen is removed first and the residual disease is enriched for the compartment that does not. That is why a marker measured before treatment cannot describe the tumour that treatment leaves behind.

See Integrative biological interplay

The honest close is narrow. Checkpoint blockade added to neoadjuvant chemotherapy improves survival in stage II and III disease23. The benefit is not uniform, since a large trial of the same class missed its endpoint14. No assay in clinical use identifies who is in which group. Until one exists, the field will keep treating everyone, and the permanent toxicities in Immune-related toxicity in a curative-intent population will keep falling on patients who were going to be cured anyway.
