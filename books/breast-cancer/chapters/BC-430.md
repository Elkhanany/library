## BS-3800 Ovarian suppression and ablation

In a premenopausal woman the ovary is the dominant source of oestrogen, and it is under
hypothalamic feedback control. That single fact explains the whole section. An aromatase inhibitor
given alone to a premenopausal woman lowers circulating oestrogen, raises gonadotropin drive, and
provokes the ovary to compensate. Aromatase inhibition therefore requires ovarian suppression to
work at all, while tamoxifen does not.

Suppression can be achieved with a gonadotropin-releasing hormone agonist, by oophorectomy, or by
ovarian irradiation. The three differ in reversibility and in speed rather than in mechanism.

Adding suppression to tamoxifen improves outcome, and the benefit is concentrated in women at
higher risk. In {{trial:soft-text}}, eight-year disease-free survival was 78.9% with tamoxifen
alone, 83.2% with tamoxifen and ovarian suppression, and 85.9% with exemestane and ovarian
suppression [@francis2018]. Eight-year overall survival was 91.5%, 93.3% and 92.1%. Among women who
remained premenopausal after chemotherapy the corresponding survival figures were 85.1%, 89.4% and
87.2%. Grade 3 or higher adverse events occurred in 24.6%, 31.0% and 32.3%.

The combined analysis of the two trials covered 4690 patients [@pagani2014]. Five-year disease-free
survival was 91.1% with exemestane and suppression against 87.3% with tamoxifen and suppression. The
hazard ratio was 0.72, with an interval of 0.60 to 0.85. Overall survival did not differ.

A meta-analysis pooled 7030 women from four trials of an aromatase inhibitor against tamoxifen under
ovarian suppression [@ebctcg2022ofs]. The recurrence rate ratio was 0.79, and the benefit was
confined to the years when the treatments differed. Five-year recurrence was 6.9% against 10.1%, an
absolute reduction of 3.2 percentage points. Breast cancer mortality did not differ, with a rate
ratio of 1.01 and an interval of 0.82 to 1.24. Fractures were more common with the aromatase
inhibitor, at 6.4% against 5.1%.

```caution
The aromatase inhibitor advantage under ovarian suppression is a recurrence advantage that has not
become a mortality difference at current follow-up [@ebctcg2022ofs]. Both statements are true and
they support different conversations.

The related measurement trap is menopausal status itself. Chemotherapy-induced amenorrhoea is not
proof of ovarian failure, and ovarian function recovers in some women who appear postmenopausal by
history. Prescribing an aromatase inhibitor on an assumed menopausal status rather than a measured
one risks giving an ineffective drug. The clinical selection rules are in [[BS-6770]] and the
fertility consequences in [[BC-610]].
```

## BS-3810 Aromatase inhibition and estrogen synthesis blockade

After menopause the residual oestrogen comes from peripheral aromatisation of adrenal androgens,
much of it in adipose tissue and some of it within the breast itself. Aromatase inhibition removes
the substrate rather than blocking the receptor. Anastrozole and letrozole inhibit the enzyme
reversibly. Exemestane is a steroidal substrate analogue that inactivates it irreversibly.

The efficacy comparison against tamoxifen rests on individual patient data from 31,920
postmenopausal women with oestrogen receptor positive disease [@ebctcg2015ai]. Five years of an
aromatase inhibitor against five years of tamoxifen reduced 10-year breast cancer mortality from
14.2% to 12.1%, a rate ratio of 0.85. Switching to an aromatase inhibitor after two or three years
of tamoxifen reduced 10-year breast cancer mortality from 10.1% to 8.7%. Endometrial cancer was less
frequent with aromatase inhibition, at 0.4% against 1.2% at 10 years, and fractures more frequent,
at 8.2% against 5.5% at five years.

The structure of that benefit matters more than its size. Recurrence rate ratios favoured the
aromatase inhibitor during the years when the treatments differed, at 0.70, and not afterwards, at
0.93 [@ebctcg2015ai]. The drug works while it is being taken. It does not eradicate a population
that then stays eradicated, which is the same argument that governs duration in [[BS-3840]].

Adipose tissue is a site of aromatase expression, and inflamed breast adipose tissue in women with
obesity expresses more of it [@morris2011]. That connects body composition to the substrate supply
this class is trying to remove, and the host biology is developed in [[BC-250]].

The measurement point in this section is the eligibility criterion rather than the drug.
Postmenopausal status is defined by a combination of age, history and hormone assays, and each of
those can be wrong in an individual.

## BS-3820 Selective receptor modulation and degradation

Tamoxifen is a competitive antagonist in breast tissue and a partial agonist elsewhere, which is
where both its endometrial risk and its bone benefit come from. The efficacy data come from
individual patient data on 21,457 women in 20 trials [@ebctcg2011tam]. In oestrogen receptor
positive disease, about five years of tamoxifen reduced recurrence with a rate ratio of 0.53 during
years 0 to 4. The ratio was 0.68 during years 5 to 9 and 0.97 during years 10 to 14. Breast cancer
mortality was reduced by about a third throughout the first 15 years.

Two findings from that analysis have outsized consequences. Oestrogen receptor status was the only
recorded factor importantly predictive of the proportional benefit, which is why the receptor assay
carries so much weight. And benefit was substantial even in marginally positive disease, with a
recurrence rate ratio of 0.67 in tumours expressing 10 to 19 fmol per mg of cytosol protein
[@ebctcg2011tam]. The modern version of that boundary problem is the 1% threshold and the ER-low
category discussed in [[BS-0910]] and [[BS-1170]].

Genotype-guided tamoxifen dosing has not been established. Analysis of CYP2D6 and UGT2B7 genotype in
tamoxifen-treated patients did not support genotype as a determinant of recurrence risk [@rae2012].

Fulvestrant degrades the receptor rather than modulating it, and its limitation is delivery. It
requires intramuscular injection and achieves incomplete receptor occupancy at tolerable doses.
Dose matters, which is itself evidence that occupancy was the constraint. In CONFIRM, 500 mg
outperformed 250 mg with a hazard ratio for progression-free survival of 0.80 and an interval of
0.68 to 0.94 [@dileo2010]. In {{trial:falcon}}, 462 endocrine-naive patients with advanced disease
had median progression-free survival of 16.6 months with fulvestrant and 13.8 months with
anastrozole, a hazard ratio of 0.797 [@robertson2016].

The practical reading is that degradation is a better mechanism than modulation against a receptor
that has stopped needing its ligand, and that fulvestrant delivers that mechanism imperfectly. That
is the entire rationale for the agents in [[BS-3830]].

## BS-3830 Oral degraders and next-generation agents

The problem these agents were built for is specific. Mutations in the ligand-binding domain of ESR1
confer ligand-independent receptor activity, so removing oestrogen stops working while the receptor
remains present and still targetable. The biology is developed in [[BC-140]] and the evidence that
the mutation is selected by aromatase inhibitor exposure rather than present at diagnosis is in
[[BS-2490]].

{{trial:emerald}} randomised 477 patients with previous CDK4/6 inhibitor exposure to elacestrant or
standard endocrine monotherapy [@bidard2022emerald]. The hazard ratio for progression-free survival
was 0.70 in all patients and 0.55 in patients with a detectable ESR1 mutation, who made up 47.8% of
the trial. Grade 3 or 4 treatment-related adverse events occurred in 7.2% against 3.1%, and nausea
of any grade in 35.0% against 18.8%.

{{trial:ember3}} randomised 874 patients three ways and produced the more informative comparison
[@jhaveri2025ember3]. Among 256 patients with ESR1 mutations, median progression-free survival was
5.5 months with imlunestrant and 3.8 months with standard endocrine therapy. In the overall
population the medians were 5.6 and 5.5 months, with a hazard ratio of 0.87 and a p value of 0.12.
Adding abemaciclib to imlunestrant gave 9.4 months against 5.5 months, with a hazard ratio of 0.57,
and did so regardless of ESR1 status. Grade 3 or higher adverse events occurred in 17.1%, 20.7% and
48.6% of the three groups.

Read plainly, the largest effect in that trial did not come from the degrader. It came from adding
cell cycle inhibition, and it cost roughly three times the rate of grade 3 or higher toxicity.

A phase 2 trial compared camizestrant with fulvestrant in 240 postmenopausal patients
[@oliveira2024]. Median progression-free survival was 7.2 months at the 75 mg dose against 3.7
months, with a hazard ratio of 0.59.

The other use of these agents is pre-emptive switching on a molecular signal. {{trial:pada1}}
monitored 1017 patients on a first-line aromatase inhibitor and palbociclib for a rising ESR1
mutation in blood, and randomised 172 of them to switch to fulvestrant or to continue
[@bidard2022pada]. Progression-free survival from randomisation was 11.9 months with the switch and
5.7 months without it, a hazard ratio of 0.61. {{trial:serena6}} applied the same design with
camizestrant, switching at ESR1 emergence before radiographic progression [@turner2026serena6].

```caution
Both switching trials randomised only the patients whose blood test converted. In {{trial:pada1}},
279 of 1017 patients developed a rising ESR1 mutation and 172 were randomised [@bidard2022pada].
The progression-free survival gain is therefore a gain in about a sixth of the population that was
monitored.

A strategy result is not a drug result. The question the trial answers is what happens to those who
convert, and the question a clinic faces is whether to monitor everyone. Those two have different
denominators, and the general form of this problem is set out in [[BS-3490]].
```

Targeted receptor degradation by heterobifunctional molecules is the next mechanism in development,
and it is covered in [[BC-760]]. The metastatic sequencing that follows from all of these agents
belongs to [[BS-7380]] and is not repeated here.

## BS-3840 Treatment duration and extended therapy

Hormone receptor positive breast cancer recurs at a steady rate for at least two decades, so the
duration question never closes. One meta-analysis followed 62,923 women who were disease-free after five years of endocrine therapy
[@pan2017]. The risk of distant recurrence between years 5 and 20 ranged from 13% in T1N0 disease to
41% in T2 disease with four to nine positive nodes. Within T1N0 disease the 5 to 20 year
distant recurrence risk was 10% for low grade, 13% for moderate grade and 17% for high grade.
Recurrences occurred at a steady rate throughout. The biology of that long tail is in [[BC-330]] and
[[BS-2880]].

Extending tamoxifen to 10 years reduces recurrence and mortality, and the effect is delayed. {{trial:atlas}} randomised 12,894 women after they completed five years, and 6846 of them had
oestrogen receptor positive disease [@davies2013atlas]. In that group the cumulative recurrence risk
during years 5 to 14 was 21.4% with continuation and 25.1% with stopping. Breast cancer mortality
during years
5 to 14 was 12.2% and 15.0%, an absolute reduction of 2.8 percentage points. The recurrence rate
ratio was 0.90 during years 5 to 9 and 0.75 thereafter. Endometrial cancer incidence during years 5
to 14 was 3.1% against 1.6%, with mortality of 0.4% against 0.2%, and the rate ratio for pulmonary
embolus was 1.87.

Extending an aromatase inhibitor produces a smaller and more ambiguous result. In {{trial:ma17r}},
1918 women received five further years of letrozole or placebo after prior aromatase inhibitor
therapy [@goss2016]. Five-year disease-free survival was 95% against 91%, a hazard ratio of 0.66.
Five-year overall survival was 93% against 94%, a hazard ratio of 0.97. The annual incidence of
contralateral breast cancer was 0.21% against 0.49%. Bone pain, fractures and new osteoporosis were
all more frequent with letrozole.

```caution
Much of the {{trial:ma17r}} disease-free survival difference came from contralateral breast cancers
[@goss2016]. A contralateral cancer is a new primary rather than a recurrence of the treated
disease, and preventing one is a different achievement from preventing relapse.

Overall survival was unchanged. "Extended aromatase inhibition improves disease-free survival" and
"extended aromatase inhibition did not improve survival in this trial" are both accurate. A patient
weighing five more years of arthralgia and bone loss is entitled to both sentences.
```

```practice
Match duration to residual risk, because the absolute benefit scales with that risk and the toxicity
does not. The nodal and tumour-size table from [@pan2017] is the right starting point.

Ask what the patient actually tolerated in the first five years before proposing five more. A
nominal extension that is abandoned in month four has delivered toxicity and no benefit.

Reassess bone density before extending an aromatase inhibitor, and treat the fracture risk as part
of the decision rather than as a downstream consequence of it [[BS-5570]].

The setting-specific rules for early disease are in [[BS-6780]] and the toxicity management in
[[BS-5440]].
```

## BS-3850 Endocrine combination strategies

Endocrine therapy fails through a small number of pathways, and each has a class of inhibitor with
randomised evidence. This section is a map. The drug-level detail is in [[BC-450]] and the
metastatic ordering in [[BC-560]].

Cell cycle inhibition is the largest effect. CDK4/6 inhibitors added to endocrine therapy improve
progression-free survival in advanced disease [@finn2016; @hortobagyi2016; @goetz2024], improve
overall survival in some trials [@sledge2020], and improve invasive disease-free survival in
high-risk early disease [@johnston2020; @slamon2024]. The pathway is developed in [[BC-160]].

mTOR inhibition with everolimus improves progression-free survival after aromatase inhibitor failure
[@baselga2012]. PI3K, AKT and PTEN alterations define populations for alpelisib [@andre2019] and
capivasertib [@turner2023capivasertib], and the network is developed in [[BC-170]].

The timing question is separate from the choice of partner, and {{trial:sonia}} answered it in a way
worth remembering. Giving a CDK4/6 inhibitor first line rather than second line did not improve
progression-free survival from randomisation to second progression, the trial's primary endpoint
[@sonke2024]. Nor did it improve overall survival, which was 47.9 against 48.1 months with a
hazard ratio of 0.91 [@wortelboer2026]. More exposure was not better. That result belongs to the
de-escalation argument in [[BS-3580]] as much as to sequencing, and what a null strategy comparison
does and does not establish is set out in [[BS-6370]].

The general pattern across all these combinations is that progression-free survival improves
reliably and overall survival improves inconsistently, at a real cost in toxicity and in cumulative
treatment burden. Why that gap exists, and what it means for interpreting a progression-free
survival result, is the subject of [[BS-6360]].

## BS-3860 Adherence, symptom burden, and the gap between prescribed and received

Every efficacy estimate in this chapter comes from a trial with monitored adherence. Practice does
not look like that, and the difference is large enough to change the expected benefit.

In a cohort of 8769 women with hormone-sensitive stage I to III disease who filled at least one
prescription, 32% had discontinued endocrine therapy by 4.5 years [@hershman2010]. Among those who
continued, 72% were fully adherent by refill records. Taken together, 49% of the cohort took the
therapy for the full duration on the intended schedule. Women under 40 had the highest risk of
discontinuation, with a hazard ratio of 1.51 and an interval of 1.23 to 1.85.

The dominant reason is musculoskeletal. In a survey of 200 consecutive postmenopausal women
receiving an adjuvant aromatase inhibitor, 94 reported joint pain that began or worsened after
starting the drug and 88 reported joint stiffness [@crew2007]. Prior taxane chemotherapy was
associated with higher odds of both. That prevalence is higher than the figures reported in the
registration trials, which is itself a finding about how adverse events are captured.

Vasomotor symptoms, sexual dysfunction and mood change contribute, and they are frequently not
volunteered. The systematic management of these symptoms is in [[BS-5440]] and [[BS-5560]].

```practice
Treat a 15% relative risk reduction delivered to half the prescribed population as what it is. The
population-level benefit is not the trial-level benefit, and improving adherence has the same
arithmetic effect as finding a better drug.

Ask about symptoms with a specific question rather than an open one. "Are you managing" reliably
returns yes.

Switching within the aromatase inhibitor class helps some patients, and switching to tamoxifen is a
legitimate option rather than a failure. A tolerated drug taken for five years outperforms a better
drug stopped in year one.

Identify the high-risk groups in advance. Age under 40 and previous taxane exposure both predict
discontinuation [@hershman2010; @crew2007], and the access and cost drivers are in [[BS-5970]].
```

## BS-3870 Resistance patterns and what they predict for the next line

Resistance to endocrine therapy is not one phenomenon, and the clinically useful question is what
each pattern predicts about the next line. The biological taxonomy is in [[BS-1190]] and the
cross-class version in [[BC-345]].

An ESR1 ligand-binding domain mutation means the receptor is active without ligand. It predicts poor
return from further oestrogen deprivation and preserved sensitivity to receptor-directed treatment.
In the {{trial:sofea}} trial, patients with an ESR1 mutation had better progression-free survival on
fulvestrant than on exemestane, with a hazard ratio of 0.52 and an interval of 0.30 to 0.92
[@fribbens2016]. Patients with wild-type ESR1 did equally well on either. In the same analysis,
palbociclib added to fulvestrant helped both groups, with hazard ratios of 0.43 in mutant and 0.49
in wild-type disease. Cell cycle inhibition is therefore not the thing the mutation escapes.

The mutation also carries prognostic weight independent of what is given next. In a secondary
analysis of baseline plasma from 541 patients, 156 carried Y537S or D538G [@chandarlapaty2016].
Median overall survival was 32.1 months in wild-type disease, 26.0 months with D538G, 20.0 months
with Y537S, and 15.2 months in patients carrying both.

Loss of receptor expression is a different failure and predicts nothing endocrine will help. It
reflects lineage change rather than pathway rewiring, and it is developed in [[BS-1180]]. A receptor
result that has converted at biopsy should be interpreted with the discordance framework in
[[BC-290]].

Bypass through PI3K, AKT or PTEN predicts benefit from pathway inhibition and identifies the
populations for the agents named in [[BS-3850]]. Escape from cell cycle inhibition through RB1 loss
or cyclin E amplification is a separate axis again, and the evidence that the drug class selects for
it is in [[BS-2500]] and [[BS-2980]].

```caution
A negative plasma ESR1 result is not evidence of endocrine sensitivity. Detection depends on how
much tumour DNA is being shed and on the sensitivity of the assay, and both vary with disease burden
and site [[BS-3460]], [[BS-3540]].

The same caution applies to timing. Testing before aromatase inhibitor exposure tests for a mutation
that the exposure has not yet selected [[BS-2490]], so a negative result then says little about what
will be present at progression.
```

```interplay target=BC-300
Endocrine resistance is the book's cleanest example of treatment producing the resistance it then
encounters. The ESR1 mutation is not found in untreated disease at meaningful frequency, and it
appears under the specific pressure of oestrogen deprivation. That makes the choice of next line a
question about what the previous line selected for, rather than a question about what the tumour was
at diagnosis.
```
