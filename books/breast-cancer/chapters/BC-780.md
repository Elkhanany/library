## BS-6660 Preventing metastatic dissemination

Adjuvant therapy is built on a premise that the biology has already contradicted. The premise is that
dissemination happens late, so removing the primary early and treating residual cells with systemic
therapy should prevent metastasis.

Dissemination occurs early, often before a primary is clinically detectable, and this is set out in
[[BC-320]]. Cells leave during the earliest stages of lesion development. By the time a tumour is
palpable or screen-detected, dissemination has in many cases already happened. The implication is
uncomfortable. Earlier detection of the primary cannot prevent what has already occurred, which is one
reason screening's mortality benefit is smaller than the shift in stage distribution suggests.

Preventing dissemination therefore requires acting on cells that have already left, or on the process
that lets them survive after leaving. Neither is currently addressable. The steps are known
individually: local invasion, intravasation, survival in circulation including the clustered form that
is disproportionately efficient [@aceto2014], extravasation, and survival at the distant site. What is
not known is which step is rate-limiting in human disease, and a therapy aimed at a step that is not
rate-limiting will not change outcomes.

The honest statement is that no current or near-term therapy targets dissemination. Everything in this
book treats cells that have already disseminated or removes the tissue they might disseminate from.

## BS-6670 Eliminating dormant and residual disease

Late recurrence in hormone-receptor-positive disease is a distinct problem that adjuvant trial design
is poorly shaped to address. The recurrence hazard continues for two decades, and half of recurrences
occur after five years, which is the finding discussed in [[BC-430]].

Three specific obstacles stand between the dormancy biology in [[BC-330]] and a therapy.

Measurement. Dormant disseminated tumour cells cannot be counted with adequate sensitivity by any
routine test. ctDNA detects proliferating disease, not quiescent cells. A therapy targeting a
population that cannot be measured cannot demonstrate that it worked except through a twenty-year
endpoint.

Mechanism. Dormancy is not one state. Cellular quiescence, immune-mediated equilibrium and angiogenic
dormancy are different mechanisms with different vulnerabilities, and a tumour cell population may
contain more than one. A therapy targeting a survival dependency of one state leaves the others.

Trial feasibility. The endpoint is recurrence at ten to twenty years in a population where most
patients will not recur. This is the extreme case of the adjuvant design problem in [[BS-6330]], and
no current funding or regulatory structure accommodates it.

The realistic path is a validated intermediate endpoint for dormant burden. Without one, this problem
does not become tractable.

## BS-6680 Overcoming heterogeneity and acquired resistance

Resistance is not a single failure mode and treating it as one has produced a decade of combination
trials with disappointing results.

Pre-existing resistant subclones are present at diagnosis, below detection, and are selected by
treatment [@gundem2015; @yates2017]. Nothing is acquired here except visibility. Acquired genetic
alterations arise under treatment pressure, of which ESR1 mutation is the cleanest example
[@schiavon2015; @oleary2018]. Non-genetic tolerance is a reversible transcriptional state that does not
require mutation and is described in [[BC-310]]. Microenvironmental protection is resistance conferred
by the stroma or immune compartment rather than by the tumour cell.

Each of these requires a different response and only the second is addressed by sequencing a resistant
tumour. A patient whose resistance is non-genetic will have no actionable finding on a resistance
biopsy, and the negative result will be reported as absence of a target rather than as evidence about
mechanism.

The unresolved question is whether resistance can be prevented rather than treated. Preventing it
means acting before selection occurs, which means acting on a population that is not yet detectable,
which returns to the measurement problem in [[BS-6670]]. {{trial:serena6}} is the first evidence that
acting at the moment of resistance emergence rather than at progression delays progression.
Switching to camizestrant when an ESR1 mutation appeared gave median progression-free survival of
16.8 against 9.2 months, with a hazard ratio of 0.45. Second progression-free survival was 25.7
against 19.1 months, with a hazard ratio of 0.63 [@turner2026serena6]. Overall survival is not
established, so what has been shown is a delay in progression rather than longer life. That is not
prevention either, but it is the closest the field has come.

## BS-6690 Achieving durable immune control

Immunotherapy works in a minority of breast cancers and works durably in fewer. {{trial:keynote522}}
and {{trial:keynote355}} established benefit in triple-negative disease [@schmid2020;
@cortes2022keynote355]. Hormone-receptor-positive disease, the majority, has shown little benefit.

The unresolved questions are specific.

Why is hormone-receptor-positive disease cold. Low mutational burden is part of it and is not
sufficient, since some low-burden tumours in other diseases respond. Oestrogen signalling has
immunomodulatory effects that are incompletely characterised. The answer determines whether the
problem is addressable at all.

Which patients benefit. PD-L1 expression, tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes and mutational burden each
predict imperfectly and none predicts well enough to allocate therapy confidently. The TIL work in
[[BS-1660]] is the strongest signal and is not sufficient as a single marker.

Why does benefit not persist. Some responses are durable and most are not. The mechanisms of acquired
immune escape in [[BS-1930]] are described and not yet targetable.

What produces durable control. In the small number of patients with prolonged disease-free survival
after immunotherapy, whether the tumour is eradicated or held in immune equilibrium is unknown. That
distinction determines whether treatment can ever stop, which connects directly to [[BS-6700]].

## BS-6700 Defining cure in metastatic disease

A small number of patients with metastatic breast cancer live many years without progression. Whether
any of them are cured is a question the field avoids because it cannot currently be answered.

The difficulty is definitional before it is empirical. Cure means no remaining malignant cells capable
of producing disease. Available tools measure the absence of detectable disease, which is a different
statement. ctDNA below the limit of detection is consistent with eradication and with a dormant
population that sheds nothing, and [[BC-390]] cannot distinguish them.

The clinical consequence is concrete. A patient in prolonged complete response on maintenance therapy
asks whether she can stop. There is no test that answers this. Stopping risks reactivation, and
continuing carries cumulative toxicity and cost for years.

Three things would change this. An assay sensitive to residual and non-shedding disease. Evidence from
prospective cessation trials in prolonged responders, which are difficult to run because patients and
clinicians are reluctant to randomise. And a biological account of what sustains prolonged control,
which would say whether it is eradication or equilibrium.

Until then the field should say what it knows. Some patients with metastatic disease live a very long
time. Whether they are cured is not known, and describing them as cured is a claim the evidence does
not support.

## BS-6710 Where heterogeneity sets a ceiling on precision medicine

This is the payoff of Part VI and the argument the book has been building toward. It is stronger than
a caveat.

Precision medicine assumes that a measurement on a sample predicts the behaviour of the disease. Three
findings, taken together, say that assumption fails in a specific and bounded way.

Spatial protein-level heterogeneity within single tumours produces co-existing receptor phenotypes
that the tumour's assigned immunohistochemical classification does not resolve, and that
heterogeneity rises with grade and is independent of genomic heterogeneity [@mardamshina2025].
Temporal instability means the
measurement was made on a tumour that no longer exists in that form, and receptor status changes
between primary and metastasis [@priedigkeit2017]. And measurement itself is unreliable at the
thresholds that now determine treatment, with pathologist concordance for HER2 at the low end of 25%
[@robbins2023] and computational models agreeing with each other at 65.1% [@mckelvey2026].

The consequence is a ceiling, not a correction. If a biomarker measured on one core cannot represent
the tumour, then a fraction of what is recorded as treatment failure is allocation failure, and no
improvement in the drug addresses it. The conjugate cross-resistance problem in [[BC-460]] has the
same shape: a patient may be assigned by a target measurement that does not describe the tumour being
treated.

This does not mean precision medicine fails. It means its achievable accuracy is bounded by sampling
and measurement rather than by biological knowledge, and that the binding constraint has moved. Raising
the ceiling requires sampling the tumour differently, through imaging that surveys all disease
[@fes2025] or through ctDNA that samples all shedding lesions, rather than knowing more about targets.

```interplay target=BC-350
The argument of Part VI is not that heterogeneity complicates treatment. It is that heterogeneity
limits what can be known from a biopsy, and that everything downstream, including trial results,
inherits that limit. A negative biomarker-selected trial is partly a statement about the biomarker.
```

## BS-6720 Aligning innovation with affordability and global access

The therapeutic advances in this book are unavailable to most women with breast cancer. Survival
differs several-fold between high-income and low-income settings, and the gap is driven by stage at
presentation and access to treatment rather than by biology [@allemani2018; @anderson2021].

The structural problem is that innovation and access are optimised by different systems. Drug
development responds to markets that can pay. Essential medicines for breast cancer, including
tamoxifen and basic cytotoxics, remain unavailable or unaffordable in many settings [@fundytus2021],
while conjugates costing many multiples of annual income per patient enter the same guidelines.

Financial toxicity is not confined to low-income countries. Patients in high-income systems experience
material hardship, deplete savings and abandon treatment because of cost, and this is associated with
worse outcomes [@ramsey2016; @desouza2017]. Cost is a clinical variable, and treating it as an
administrative one is a category error.

Three directions have evidence behind them. Resource-stratified guidelines that specify what to do at
each level of available resource rather than describing one standard most systems cannot meet
[@anderson2006]. De-escalation research, which reduces cost and toxicity simultaneously and is the one
area where the interests of high and low resource settings align exactly. And biosimilar and generic
availability, which has done more for global access to trastuzumab and endocrine therapy than any new
agent.

The uncomfortable observation is that most of this book describes therapy that widens the gap it
documents in [[BC-700]]. A field that measures progress only by what is newly possible will keep
doing that.

## BS-6730 What a revised edition should contain

This section exists because the book is designed to be revised and because naming what is expected to
change is more useful than pretending it will not.

Several questions should be answerable next edition. Whether ctDNA-guided intervention improves
outcomes beyond the timing result in {{trial:serena6}}, with {{trial:dare-ctdna}} and {{trial:survive}}
reading out. Whether conjugate sequencing after conjugate failure is effective, which practice already
requires and evidence does not yet support. Whether the dormancy-directed approach in
{{trial:clever}} produces a signal worth developing. Whether PROTAC degraders extend beyond the
ESR1-mutant population where {{trial:veritac2}} showed benefit [@campone2025]. And whether
computational HER2 scoring reaches the reproducibility that [[BS-6440]] says it has not.

Several will not be settled and should be restated rather than quietly dropped. The surrogacy status
of ctDNA. Whether dormant disease can be eliminated. Whether durable immune control is achievable in
hormone-receptor-positive disease. Whether cure in metastatic disease can be defined operationally.

Two structural commitments should survive any revision. Numbers stay attached to their sources, and a
claim that cannot be sourced is rewritten or removed rather than softened. And the distinction between
biology and measurement is maintained in every chapter, because most apparent disagreement in this
field is disagreement about assays being reported as disagreement about disease.

The chapters most likely to need rewriting are the ones in Part XIV. That is by design. A book
organised so that the most volatile material is isolated and identified is a book that can be revised
without being rebuilt.
