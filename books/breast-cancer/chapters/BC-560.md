## BS-4810 Defining endocrine sensitivity and resistance in clinical language

Endocrine resistance is a clinical label applied to a heterogeneous set of failures. The
consensus definitions divide it by timing. Primary resistance means relapse within the first two
years of adjuvant endocrine therapy, or progression within six months of starting endocrine
therapy for advanced disease. Secondary resistance means failure after a longer period of
benefit.

These are useful conventions and they are conventions. Nobody has shown that six months marks a
point where biology changes. The cut-points exist so that trial populations can be defined
reproducibly, which is the same argument made about assay thresholds in [[BC-280]]. Reading a
trial eligibility boundary as a biological category is the recurring error in this literature.

What the timing does encode is real, and it is worth naming. A tumour that never responded
probably never depended on oestrogen receptor signalling in a way the drug could exploit. A
tumour that responded for four years and then failed has produced something. The first situation
argues for changing class. The second argues for characterising what changed before choosing.
The mechanisms behind each are separated in [[BS-2970]] and the pathway detail is in [[BC-140]].

One practical consequence follows. The single most informative number at progression is how long
the last line worked. It is free, it is already in the chart, and it predicts the yield of a
molecular test better than any feature of the scan [@oleary2018].

This chapter is about how to approach the patient. Its subjects are disease burden, visceral
crisis, endocrine sensitivity, the goal of each line, and when to stop. It is not the trial
record for this disease, and does not try to be.

Three neighbouring chapters hold that record. [[BC-870]] owns endocrine therapy and the receptor
degraders. [[BC-880]] owns CDK4/6 inhibition, including agent choice and continuation beyond
progression. [[BC-890]] owns the later-line conjugates and single-agent chemotherapy. Each is
read here as the source of a table rather than repeated.

The tables below are generated from the trial registry when the book is built. Each is filtered
on the decision its own section argues rather than on a drug class. A figure stays in the prose
only where the sentence is about that figure. Everything else has been left to the table, so a
new readout enters the registry once and appears wherever its filter matches.

## BS-4820 First-line endocrine therapy with CDK4/6 inhibition

The table below looks like ten trials of one class in one line. Sorted by what was actually
randomised, it separates into three different questions.

```evidence setting=metastatic subtype=HR+/HER2- line=1L modality=cdk4-6 phase=3 status=reported cols=acronym,n,arms,result,os caption=Randomised_phase_III_first-line_regimens_built_on_a_CDK4/6_inhibitor._The_overall_survival_column_is_where_the_agents_separate._The_full_class_record,_including_the_phase_II_trials_and_everything_after_first_progression,_is_in_BC-880.
```

Six rows randomise a CDK4/6 inhibitor against placebo on an endocrine backbone. Between them
they cover four agents and every common form of that backbone. Two used a non-steroidal
aromatase inhibitor in postmenopausal women. One used fulvestrant. One enrolled pre- and
perimenopausal women, all on goserelin. One was run entirely in Chinese hospitals. Every one
lengthened progression-free survival, and none has failed. That is the class finding, and it is
the only finding in this table that is about a class.

The survival column is where the rows part, and it invites a comparison nobody randomised. No
two of these inhibitors have been tested against each other in the first line. Each survival
figure is measured against that trial's own placebo arm, in that trial's population, followed by
whatever those patients received afterwards. What can be said structurally is narrower and more
useful than a ranking. The ribociclib survival result is replicated across three separate
first-line populations, postmenopausal on an aromatase inhibitor, postmenopausal on fulvestrant
and premenopausal on goserelin [@hortobagyi2022; @slamon2021; @lu2022]. Palbociclib and
abemaciclib each carry one first-line survival analysis [@slamon2024paloma2; @goetz2024].
Replication across populations is a different kind of evidence from a single analysis, whichever
way that analysis fell. {{trial:monarch3}} is the awkward row, because its point estimate favours
abemaciclib and does not clear its boundary [@goetz2024]. Not significant there is a statement
about the alpha remaining after the interim looks, not about the direction of the curve.

The other four rows are not that experiment at all. {{trial:inavo120}} holds palbociclib and
fulvestrant constant in both arms and randomises a PI3K inhibitor on top, in PIK3CA-mutated
disease that relapsed during adjuvant endocrine therapy or within twelve months of finishing it
[@turner2024inavo120]. {{trial:pada1}} and {{trial:serena6}} hold the inhibitor constant and
randomise a change of endocrine partner in the middle of the first line, which is the subject of
[[BS-4840]]. {{trial:sonia}} randomises neither drug. It randomises the position of the inhibitor
in the sequence.

That last row is the one this section rests on. It is the only design here whose clock runs past
the first progression. Both arms received an aromatase inhibitor and then fulvestrant, in that
order, and the randomisation decided which of the two lines carried the inhibitor. The endpoint
was progression after the second line. Neither that endpoint nor overall survival separated the
two strategies [@sonke2024; @wortelboer2026]. A trial that stops measuring at first progression
cannot answer this question, which is why the other nine rows do not.

The price of the first-line position is the part that never appears in a hazard ratio. Median
inhibitor exposure was 24.6 months with early use against 8.1 months with deferred use, and the
early arm accumulated more grade 3 or worse adverse events [@sonke2024]. Every patient pays
that. Only the subgroup that benefits collects.

```caution
{{trial:sonia}} is often read as showing that first-line CDK4/6 inhibition does not matter. It shows
something narrower. The trial permitted investigator choice of agent, and the agent chosen
determines how far the result travels, since the three inhibitors do not carry the same survival
evidence. Two further findings restrain the simple reading. A post hoc subgroup analysis suggested
a survival benefit from first-line use in premenopausal patients, with a hazard ratio of 0.53.
Postmenopausal patients showed none, and the test for interaction gave P equal to 0.01
[@wortelboer2026]. A prespecified circulating tumour DNA analysis of 409 {{trial:sonia}} patients divided
them by baseline genome-wide aneuploidy score. Those with a high score did benefit from first-line
use, with a hazard ratio of 0.58. Those with a low score did not, at 1.36 [@jongbloed2025]. A
negative trial overall is not a negative trial in everyone.
```

Three things the table cannot carry. A blank in the survival column means immature rather than
negative, and {{trial:dawna-2}}, {{trial:pada1}} and {{trial:serena6}} are all in that state. What
the control arms received after progression was not controlled and differed between protocols.
{{trial:sonia}} alone guaranteed its control arm an inhibitor by design. The survival column is
therefore not comparing like with like across rows. And no trial here enrolled a patient who had already
had a CDK4/6 inhibitor in the adjuvant setting. That is a growing share of the clinic and the one
first-line population with no randomised evidence at all.

## BS-4830 Choosing among CDK4/6 inhibitors and continuing beyond progression

The three inhibitors differ on three axes that a class label hides.

They differ in evidence position. Ribociclib has a positive first-line survival result
[@hortobagyi2022]. Abemaciclib has a positive survival result in the second line, while its
first-line survival analysis did not reach significance [@sledge2020; @goetz2024]. The trials
behind each of those statements are tabulated in [[BC-880]].

They differ in toxicity. Neutropenia dominates with palbociclib and ribociclib. Diarrhoea
dominates with abemaciclib. Ribociclib carries a QT and hepatic monitoring requirement that the
others do not. Agent detail belongs to [[BC-450]].

They differ in what they do to a schedule. Continuous dosing and intermittent dosing produce
different lives, and patients notice.

Continuing CDK4/6 inhibition beyond progression is a separate question and it is weakly
answered. The reason to doubt it is mechanistic. Most progression on these agents is not
explained by any measurable mechanism, and the marker with the best evidence, cyclin E1,
describes a cell cycle that does not depend on the kinase being blocked [@turner2019ccne1]. Emergent
RB1 mutations are real and uncommon, appearing in 6 of 127 progressing patients in one paired
analysis [@oleary2018]. A population that progressed because it stopped needing CDK4 and CDK6 is
not obviously a population to keep blocking them in. The randomised continuation and switch
trials are set out in [[BC-880]] and the mechanisms in [[BS-2980]].

## BS-4840 ESR1-mutant disease and receptor-degrading strategies

ESR1 mutation is the cleanest branch in this chapter because the mechanism and the action line
up. The mutation confers ligand independence. It is acquired under aromatase inhibitor exposure
and is not found in patients who have not had it [@schiavon2015]. Oestrogen deprivation
therefore fails as a class. A drug that acts on the receptor still has a target.

What each degrader is worth after progression, and how the trials split by mutation status, is
the argument of [[BC-870]]. The decision this chapter owns is earlier and narrower. It is whether
to act on a rising mutation in a patient whose scan is still reassuring.

```story SQ-0500
```

Two trials randomised at that moment and they agree. {{trial:pada1}} swapped the aromatase
inhibitor for fulvestrant and kept palbociclib [@bidard2022pada]. {{trial:serena6}} swapped it for
camizestrant and kept whichever inhibitor the patient was already taking [@bidard2025]. One
degrader is parenteral and one is oral, one trial was open-label in a single country and the
other was double-blind across twenty-three. The agreement therefore looks like a finding about
the timing rather than about the molecule.

What neither randomised is the comparison a clinic actually weighs. Both control arms continued
an aromatase inhibitor already known at the molecular level to be failing, so the contrast is
against something close to inert. Nobody has compared switching now with switching at
radiological progression, which is the choice in front of the oncologist. The clock is the second
problem. Progression-free survival here is measured from a randomisation triggered by an assay,
so the starting point is set by a detection threshold on a ctDNA panel rather than by anything
the patient experienced. Molecular progression is a laboratory convention as much as a
biological event, and patients whose resistance takes a route that panel does not report never
enter.

One figure speaks past the first line. In {{trial:serena6}} median second progression-free
survival was 25.7 months with the switch against 19.1 months without it [@bidard2025]. The
separation had not been repaid by the following line at that analysis, which is the thing a
strategy trial has to show. Overall survival is not yet reported in either trial.

The record at clinical progression is what makes the mechanism credible. {{trial:emerald}},
{{trial:veritac2}} and {{trial:ember3}} each set a receptor-directed drug against standard
endocrine monotherapy after progression, and in each the effect concentrates in the ESR1-mutant
patients [@bidard2022emerald; @campone2025; @jhaveri2025ember3]. Three trials, three molecules,
one dependence on mutation status. They establish that the mutation marks a treatable state. They
say nothing about when to act on it, because all three randomised after the scan had changed.

The precedent that should restrain enthusiasm is {{trial:swog-s0500}}. It changed treatment on a
persistent circulating tumour cell count rather than on imaging, and survival did not improve
[@smerage2014]. A blood marker can be prognostic without acting on it being therapeutic. That is
why a progression-free survival result measured from a molecular randomisation needs a survival
result behind it before it becomes a standard.

That strategy has a cost the progression curves do not show, because it requires plasma testing
every two to three months in patients who are well and responding. It then commits the service to changing
treatment on a laboratory result while the imaging is unchanged. A clinic that cannot deliver the
testing reliably should not adopt the strategy, because an irregularly tested patient gets the
control arm with extra appointments.

```caution
{{trial:serena6}} is the strongest available case for acting on a molecular result before the scan changes,
and its denominator deserves attention. Of 3,256 patients who had at least one ESR1 test during
first-line therapy, 548 tested positive and 315 were randomised [@turner2026serena6]. The trial
establishes the strategy in one mutation, with one drug, in patients who stayed on first-line
therapy long enough to be tested repeatedly. Whether pre-emptive switching generalises to other
resistance alterations is an open question, not an extension of this result.
```

## BS-4850 PI3K, AKT, and mTOR directed therapy and its toxicity burden

This branch has three approved options, and the differences between them are about population
and tolerability rather than about pathway. The trials, their biomarker definitions and their
efficacy are tabulated in [[BC-870]]. What belongs here is the toxicity, because it decides how
much of the benefit a patient actually receives.

{{trial:solar1}} established PIK3CA mutation as a predictive marker and also established the
reason the drug is hard to give. Grade 3 or 4 hyperglycaemia occurred in 36.6% of patients
against 0.7% on placebo. One quarter of patients discontinued alpelisib for adverse events,
against 4.2% in the control arm [@andre2019]. A quarter is the figure to hold when consenting a
patient, because it is the rate at which the treatment ends for a reason other than the cancer.

Capivasertib is better tolerated and its toxicity is differently shaped. Grade 3 or higher rash
occurred in 12.1% of patients against 0.3%, and diarrhoea in 9.3% against 0.3%
[@turner2023capivasertib]. Rash is early and manageable, whereas hyperglycaemia is not and needs
a plan that exists before the first prescription.

Inavolisib, given as a triplet in a deliberately endocrine-resistant population, is the only
regimen in this group carrying a survival benefit [@turner2024inavo120; @jhaveri2025inavo].
Whether that reflects the population, the triplet or the drug cannot be separated from one trial.

Everolimus with exemestane remains available and predates biomarker selection [@baselga2012].
Stomatitis, pneumonitis and metabolic toxicity limit it. Pathway biology is in [[BC-170]] and
agent detail in [[BC-450]].

```interplay target=BC-350
PIK3CA mutation frequency is not evenly distributed. In The Cancer Genome Atlas, comparing 154
patients of African ancestry with 776 of European ancestry, the African-ancestry group had more
TP53 mutations and fewer PIK3CA mutations. Most of those molecular differences disappeared after
adjusting for intrinsic subtype, which means the mutation-frequency difference is entangled with
the subtype distribution rather than independent of it [@huo2017]. Two consequences follow and
they belong together. Biologically, an alteration frequency is a property of a population's
subtype mix, not a fixed attribute of ancestry. Operationally, a drug whose eligibility is defined
by that alteration will be prescribed at different rates across populations for reasons that are
partly biological and partly about who gets sequenced at all. Access and representation are
developed in [[BC-690]].
```

## BS-4860 PARP inhibition in germline-altered disease

Germline BRCA status is the branch most often checked late and it changes both treatment and
family counselling.

```evidence setting=metastatic subtype=BRCA modality=parp status=reported cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,result,os caption=PARP_inhibition_in_advanced_disease._Two_of_these_set_a_single_agent_against_physician's_choice_chemotherapy._One_adds_a_PARP_inhibitor_to_platinum_and_a_taxane_rather_than_replacing_them,_and_one_is_the_expansion_study_that_tested_genes_other_than_BRCA1_and_BRCA2.
```

Two of these four rows ask one question with two drugs. Each set a single oral PARP inhibitor
against the physician's choice of single-agent chemotherapy in germline BRCA1 or BRCA2 carriers,
with progression-free survival by blinded central review as the primary endpoint. Both favoured
the inhibitor and both raised the response rate. Both then failed to separate on overall survival
at their final analyses [@robson2019; @litton2020]. The agreement runs through the negative half
as well as the positive half, which is what makes it a class finding rather than two results.

The third row is a different question wearing the same label. {{trial:brocade3}} did not replace
chemotherapy with a PARP inhibitor. It added veliparib to carboplatin and paclitaxel and compared
that with the same chemotherapy plus placebo [@diras2020]. Its control arm is platinum-based
combination treatment rather than a single agent, and its survival result is not on the record.
Reading it alongside the other two as though all three tested one strategy is the commonest error
made with this table.

{{trial:tbcrc048}} is single-arm and its subject is the gene list rather than the drug. Confirmed
responses appeared in two groups only, germline PALB2 carriers and patients with somatic BRCA1 or
BRCA2 mutations. No response occurred with ATM or CHEK2 alterations alone [@tung2020].
Homologous recombination related is therefore not an eligibility category. A panel that reports
those genes under one heading invites a decision the data do not support. Repair biology is in
[[BC-180]] and resistance in [[BS-3010]].

What no row tests is the comparison the biology most invites. Neither physician's-choice menu
included platinum. Both were built from capecitabine, eribulin and vinorelbine, with gemcitabine
added in one. A PARP inhibitor has therefore never been randomised against the other drug class
that exploits the same repair defect. Nor has one been randomised against endocrine-based
therapy, so these trials establish activity in hormone receptor-positive disease without
establishing where in the sequence it belongs. The populations were defined by a germline
mutation rather than by receptor status, which leaves the hormone receptor-positive patients
inside them a subgroup nobody powered.

## BS-4870 Transition to antibody-drug conjugates

The transition out of endocrine-based therapy is now a transition into conjugates rather than
into single-agent chemotherapy. The decision here is when to make it rather than which conjugate
is better, since the second question is argued in [[BC-890]].

```story SQ-0570
```

```evidence setting=metastatic subtype=HR+/HER2-,HER2-low modality=adc status=reported cols=acronym,phase,n,population,result,os caption=Conjugate_trials_reaching_this_population,_with_the_prior-therapy_requirement_in_the_population_column._Read_that_column_first,_because_the_trials_sit_at_different_depths_of_prior_chemotherapy_and_cannot_be_stacked_into_a_sequence._The_agents_are_compared_in_BC-890.
```

Four of the six rows are randomised comparisons against the physician's choice of chemotherapy,
and all four lengthened progression-free survival. That is the class finding. It is also the only
thing those four share, because the population column puts each of them at a different point in
the course. {{trial:tropics02}} required two to four previous chemotherapy regimens for
metastatic disease. {{trial:tropion-b01}} and {{trial:destiny-b04}} required one or two.
{{trial:destiny-b06}} required none and excluded anyone who had had chemotherapy for metastatic
disease at all. A hazard ratio from the last of those describes a decision the first never tested.

The survival column splits the class, and the split does not follow the payload. Both
TROP2-directed conjugates lengthened progression-free survival against chemotherapy. One then
lengthened overall survival [@rugo2023tropics02]. The other returned a final hazard ratio for
death sitting on the null [@bardia2025tropionb01]. Three explanations compete and the table
cannot choose between them. The two trials sat at different depths of prior chemotherapy.
Physician's choice is not a fixed quantity either, because it names a different menu in each
protocol and a different habit in each country. Subsequent treatment is the third. In
{{trial:tropion-b01}} the proportion going on to another conjugate was 12.3% in the datopotamab
arm against 24.0% in the chemotherapy arm, which offers an explanation without confirming one
[@pistilli2026].

{{trial:destiny-b06}} is the row this section rests on. It is the only randomised trial that put a
conjugate ahead of any chemotherapy for metastatic disease, against the chemotherapy that would
otherwise have started [@bardia2024destinyb06]. That is the comparison the clinic makes. Its
overall survival was immature at the primary analysis, so the case for moving the drug forward is
currently a longer time to first progression and nothing else.

{{trial:daisy}} is in the table because its subject is the assay rather than the position. It gave
trastuzumab deruxtecan across three immunohistochemistry cohorts, including tumours scored zero,
and produced responses in all three [@mosele2023]. Within the cohort scored zero, response did not
track ERBB2 expression above or below the median. The threshold defining [[term:her2-low]] is a
cut applied to an assay built to find amplification [[BC-590]], and this is the evidence that the
cut does not mark where the drug stops working. The study is single-arm, so it can say that and
nothing about sequence.

{{trial:saci-io-hr}} asked whether a checkpoint inhibitor adds anything to a conjugate, and it did
not lengthen progression-free survival [@garridocastro2026]. The PD-L1-positive analysis that
reads more favourably was a secondary endpoint, confined to the patients with evaluable tissue,
and it did not reach significance. A combined positive score of at least one is a scoring
convention applied to archival tissue, which is a weak instrument for a decision of this size.

The toxicity is not shared across the class. Adjudicated interstitial lung disease or pneumonitis
occurred in 11.3% of patients receiving trastuzumab deruxtecan in {{trial:destiny-b06}}, including
three grade 5 events, against 0.2% on chemotherapy [@bardia2024destinyb06]. That figure is why the
next section still exists.

Two comparisons are missing and both matter. No conjugate has been randomised against another, so
the order of this class is currently set by eligibility and habit rather than by evidence. And no
trial here measured progression on the line after the conjugate. Whether giving one earlier
lengthens the whole sequence or only its first segment is therefore untested. {{trial:ascent07}}
and {{trial:mk2870-010}} move a TROP2 conjugate into the first chemotherapy slot, and neither is
built to answer that question either. {{trial:icarus-breast01}} carries a HER3-directed conjugate into disease that
has had a CDK4/6 inhibitor and a line of chemotherapy, in a single arm, which can establish
activity and no position.

```caution
The two trials in the story above that set a CDK4/6 inhibitor against capecitabine do not belong
to this position. Both gave the inhibitor to patients who had never had one, which is not the
patient this section is about. They are argued where they belong, in [[BS-4880]]. Sequential
conjugate use is developed in [[BS-3030]] and the chemistry in [[BC-460]].
```

## BS-4880 When cytotoxic chemotherapy is still the right answer

Conjugates have displaced single-agent chemotherapy from several positions and they have not
abolished it.

```story SQ-0680
```

```evidence setting=metastatic subtype=HR+/HER2- modality=chemo line=1L,2L status=reported cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,result,os caption=Randomised_trials_in_which_chemotherapy_was_the_comparator_or_the_experimental_arm_early_in_the_course_of_this_disease._The_later-line_chemotherapy_record_is_in_BC-890.
```

The consistent finding across these six rows is negative, and it is the reason the working set of
cytotoxic agents has not changed in a decade. {{trial:calgb-40502-ncctg-n063h}} set two newer
agents against weekly paclitaxel in the first chemotherapy line, with bevacizumab in all three
arms, and neither beat it [@rugo2015]. {{trial:study301}} set eribulin against capecitabine after
an anthracycline and a taxane, with overall survival as a co-primary endpoint, and neither was
superior [@kaufman2015]. Nothing in this table has displaced weekly paclitaxel or capecitabine on
survival.

Two rows did beat a chemotherapy arm, and both did it on progression-free survival with no
survival result behind it yet. {{trial:right-choice}} is one, and it is argued in [[BS-4890]].
{{trial:brocade3}} is the other, and its experimental arm contains the chemotherapy rather than
replacing it [@diras2020].

{{trial:pearl}} and {{trial:young-pearl-kcsg-br15-10}} look like a disagreement and are not. Both
set palbociclib with endocrine therapy against capecitabine. One found capecitabine at least as
good on progression-free survival and the other found the combination better. The difference is
the endocrine history of the two populations rather than the drugs. PEARL enrolled postmenopausal
patients resistant to aromatase inhibitors, so its experimental arm was built on a class the
tumour had already defeated. Young-PEARL enrolled premenopausal patients progressing on
tamoxifen and gave them exemestane with ovarian suppression, which is a manoeuvre their disease
had not seen. The two agree on the endpoint that decides the matter. Neither produced an overall
survival difference [@martn2022; @ahn2025]. Both also enrolled patients who had never received a
CDK4/6 inhibitor, which no longer describes anyone reaching a second line.

Chemotherapy remains the answer in four situations. The first is after conjugate exposure, where
the remaining options are cytotoxic and the expectations are covered in [[BS-4910]]. The second
is where a conjugate is contraindicated, and interstitial lung disease history is the common
example given the 11.3% pneumonitis rate in {{trial:destiny-b06}} [@bardia2024destinyb06]. The
third is where access to a conjugate is not available, which is a real constraint in most of the
world [[BC-710]]. The fourth is rapid progression requiring a response now, covered in
[[BS-4890]].

One principle is old and still correct. Sequential single agents and combination chemotherapy
produce different trade-offs. Combinations raise response rate and toxicity. Single agents
preserve function and allow more lines. In disease that is not immediately threatening, the
single agent is usually right.

The choice among agents is driven by prior exposure, residual neuropathy, marrow reserve and
schedule. Capecitabine, eribulin, vinorelbine, gemcitabine and weekly taxane are the working
set. Agent detail is in [[BC-440]].

What no trial in this table did is randomise the order of the same drugs. The design that answers
a sequencing question randomises the position and then measures progression on the line after
next, which is how {{trial:sonia}} settled the first-line CDK4/6 argument. No conjugate trial and
no chemotherapy trial in this disease has been built that way. Until one is, moving a drug earlier
is demonstrated for the first segment of the sequence and assumed for the rest.

## BS-4890 Rapid progression and visceral crisis

Visceral crisis is a clinical diagnosis and it is overused. It means organ dysfunction from
tumour burden that requires a rapid response, judged by symptoms, examination and laboratory
values. Extensive liver metastases with normal liver function are not a visceral crisis. They
are a large burden.

The distinction matters because the reflex answer, combination chemotherapy, is not supported in
the population where it is most often used.

```story SQ-0480
```

{{trial:right-choice}} is the only randomised test of that reflex, and it tests it narrowly. It is
a phase II trial. It enrolled pre- and perimenopausal women only. Entry turned on the
investigator's judgement that chemotherapy was indicated, which is a decision rather than a
measurement, and no protocol reproduces it. Its comparator was combination chemotherapy, chosen
for toxicity contrast as much as for efficacy, rather than the sequential single agents most
clinics would give. Its endpoint was progression-free survival [@lu2024]. The practice it
challenges does not rest on progression-free survival. It rests on how quickly the disease turns
around.

The two capecitabine trials in the story are not about urgency at all. {{trial:pearl}} and
{{trial:young-pearl-kcsg-br15-10}} enrolled on endocrine resistance and on menopausal status, and
they are argued in [[BS-4880]]. They matter here only in showing an endocrine combination holding
disease as long as a cytotoxic agent in one population and not in another. That is a reason to
ask which population is in front of you. It is not a licence to generalise from either.

{{trial:abigail}} is the only design that addresses the manoeuvre clinicians actually use, which
is giving chemotherapy first and handing over to the combination. It randomises immediate
abemaciclib with endocrine therapy against a short course of paclitaxel before the same
combination. It has no verified peer-reviewed result on the record, so nothing about induction can
be read from it yet.

```caution
{{trial:right-choice}} is a phase II trial and its population is not a population in organ failure. Of 222
patients, 106 had investigator-assessed visceral crisis, which is a clinical judgment rather than
a physiological threshold [@lu2024]. The result argues strongly against reaching for combination
chemotherapy in aggressive but compensated disease. It does not establish endocrine therapy with a
CDK4/6 inhibitor as the answer in a patient with failing liver function. There the binding
constraint is time to response, and median time to response was 4.9 months with the endocrine
combination against 3.2 months with chemotherapy [@lu2024].
```

Nobody has randomised a patient in organ failure. No trial named in this section carries time to
symptomatic or biochemical improvement as a primary endpoint, which is the endpoint the decision
needs. The operational question in true crisis is how fast a response is required, measured in
weeks. If the answer is under four weeks, the agent with the fastest response is right, and that
is usually chemotherapy.

## BS-4900 Invasive lobular disease in the metastatic setting

Lobular carcinoma behaves differently in the metastatic setting, and most of the difference is a
measurement problem rather than a treatment problem.

The pattern of spread is distinctive. Serosal surfaces, peritoneum, retroperitoneum,
gastrointestinal tract and gynaecological organs are involved more often than in ductal disease.
Bone involvement is common and is frequently diffuse rather than focal.

That pattern defeats the assessment tools. Lobular deposits infiltrate in single files without
forming a mass, so they produce little to measure on computed tomography and can be invisible
until an organ stops working. Linitis plastica of the stomach and diffuse peritoneal
thickening are the classic traps. Response assessment by unidimensional criteria is therefore
less reliable in this histology than in ductal disease, and a stable scan carries less
reassurance.

The systemic treatment algorithm is the same. Lobular disease is overwhelmingly hormone receptor
positive and HER2-negative, so it sits in this chapter by default. Two differences are worth
holding. Loss of E-cadherin defines the histology and does not currently select a therapy.
And the higher frequency of HER2-activating mutations in lobular disease makes sequencing
worthwhile, which links this section to [[BS-4990]].

Histology is developed in [[BC-090]] and staging in [[BC-370]]. The practical point here is to
choose a modality that can see the disease before relying on it to declare response.

## BS-4910 Long-term disease control and the cost of staying on treatment

Many patients now spend years on continuous treatment for this disease. That is the achievement
of the last decade and it creates its own problem.

Three costs accumulate and none of them appear in a progression-free survival curve.

The first is physiological. Cumulative neuropathy, marrow suppression, ocular toxicity and
metabolic effects build over lines. The distinction between cumulative and exposure-dependent
toxicity, and what it implies for treatment breaks, is in [[BS-4790]].

The second is financial. Oral targeted agents given for years produce cost exposure that
persists as long as the response does. This is the one cost that improves with a longer
remission and worsens with it at the same time, and it is developed in [[BC-700]].

The third is what a treatment schedule does to a life. Three-weekly infusions, monthly
monitoring and the interval before each scan are the structure of the year. Patients rarely
raise this unprompted.

```practice
Ask at every line what the treatment is for. Control of symptoms, control of a threatened organ,
or extension of time are different aims, and only the first is verifiable within a month. Name the
expected magnitude before starting. Medians in the pretreated setting are short, and a patient
told that a line offers months rather than years is better placed to decide what to spend them on.
Review the whole regimen annually rather than at progression only. Agents accumulate. Supportive
medications outlive the episode that prompted them. Ask directly about cost. Patients who are
rationing doses report it when asked and rarely otherwise, and a rationed dose will present as
resistance [[BS-3040]].
```
