## BS-5430 Acute and chronic cytotoxic toxicity

Myelosuppression sets the schedule, and the drug classes that produce it are described in
[[BC-440]]. Febrile neutropenia risk is a property of the regimen rather than of the patient. That
is why primary growth factor prophylaxis is decided at the point of regimen selection rather than
after the first episode. Dose intensity and its trade-offs are in [[BS-3900]].

Nausea and vomiting are now largely preventable and the failure mode is undertreatment of a
regimen whose emetic risk was misclassified. Prophylaxis is selected from the emetic risk category
of the regimen, and an anthracycline with cyclophosphamide sits in the highly emetogenic group
[@hesketh2020]. Delayed emesis is undertreated more often than acute emesis because it happens
after the patient has gone home.

Alopecia has a prevention with randomised evidence. One trial enrolled women with stage I or II
disease receiving a taxane, an anthracycline, or both. Scalp cooling made hair loss of less than
50% after the fourth cycle significantly more likely than no cooling [@nangia2017]. In a parallel
study of non-anthracycline adjuvant regimens, scalp cooling reduced hair loss measured 4 weeks
after the last dose. Feeling less physically attractive was reported by 27.3% of cooled patients
against 56.3% of controls [@rugo2017]. Further cohorts found scalp cooling effective with anthracycline
and taxane regimens and faster hair recovery when it failed [@vasconcelos2018; @kinoshita2019]. A
systematic review found no increase in scalp metastases with cooling [@rugo2017b], and a
practice review addressed its logistics and insurance [@kruse2018].

Peripheral neuropathy is the toxicity most likely to persist. In a meta-analysis of 31 studies and
4,179 patients across tumour types, prevalence was 68.1% within the first month of chemotherapy,
60.0% at 3 months, and 30.0% at 6 months or beyond [@seretny2014]. No agent is recommended for
prevention, and duloxetine is the only agent with reasonable evidence for treating established
painful neuropathy [@loprinzi2020]. Physical methods are the exception. Frozen gloves and socks
reduced objective and subjective neuropathy from paclitaxel in a self-controlled trial
[@hanai2018], and a review of cooling found benefit from greater degrees of cooling [@sphar2020]. In
the POLAR randomised trial, hand cooling and compression significantly reduced high-grade
taxane-induced neuropathy [@michel2025]. Dose reduction remains the intervention that actually works,
and it has to be made before the deficit becomes fixed.

Fluoropyrimidine toxicity has a pharmacogenetic prevention. Genotype-guided dose reduction in DPYD
variant carriers before treatment reduced severe toxicity in a prospective safety analysis
[@henricks2018]. Testing happens before the first dose or it is pointless.

## BS-5440 Endocrine therapy symptom burden

Endocrine therapy is the best tolerated effective systemic treatment in this disease by any
objective grading scale, and it is the treatment patients most often stop. Those two statements
are both true and the gap between them is the subject of this section.

The symptom set is predictable. Vasomotor symptoms, arthralgia and the aromatase inhibitor
musculoskeletal syndrome, vaginal dryness and dyspareunia, reduced libido, sleep disturbance, and
mood change. Tamoxifen adds thromboembolic and endometrial risk. Aromatase inhibitors add
accelerated bone loss, which is [[BS-5500]].

The consequence is measured in discontinuation. In a cohort of 8,769 women with early-stage
disease, 32% had discontinued adjuvant hormonal therapy by 4.5 years, and of those still taking it
only 72% were fully adherent. Overall, 49% completed the full duration at the intended schedule.
Women under 40 years had the highest risk of discontinuation, with a hazard ratio of 1.51 and a
95% confidence interval of 1.23 to 1.85 [@hershman2010].

```caution
Grade 1 and grade 2 are the categories in which this whole problem lives. A symptom that never
reaches grade 3 is invisible to a toxicity table and is the commonest reason a curative treatment
is abandoned. Reading tolerability off a trial safety table understates the burden of endocrine
therapy more than that of any other class in this book.
```

For vasomotor symptoms, venlafaxine reduced hot flushes in survivors in a randomised trial, with
side effects to set against the benefit [@loprinzi2000]. Clonidine was slower than venlafaxine
but gave lower scores by 12 weeks [@boekhout2011]. An evidence review rated venlafaxine and
gabapentin likely to be effective and noted a large placebo effect [@kaplan2011]. A randomised
crossover trial in breast cancer survivors compared venlafaxine and gabapentin. Both reduced hot flash scores by a similar amount. Of 56 patients
expressing a preference, 68% preferred venlafaxine and 32% gabapentin [@bordeleau2010]. Selective
serotonin reuptake inhibitors that strongly inhibit CYP2D6 are conventionally avoided alongside
tamoxifen on pharmacological grounds. The genotype evidence behind that caution is weaker than the
caution implies, since CYP2D6 genotype was not associated with recurrence in a tamoxifen-treated
cohort [@rae2012]. The drug interaction was measured before the genotype question was settled.
Coadministered CYP2D6 inhibitors lowered plasma endoxifen [@jin2005], and the interactions between
antidepressants and tamoxifen were reviewed for psychiatrists [@henry2008]. A population study found
no increase in recurrence with concurrent CYP2D6-inhibiting medications [@ahern2009].

Switching within or between classes is a legitimate intervention rather than an admission of
failure. A patient intolerant of one aromatase inhibitor may tolerate another, and the alternative
to a switch is frequently no treatment at all. Adherence and its determinants are developed in
[[BS-3860]].

## BS-5450 Targeted agent toxicity by class

Targeted agent toxicity is mostly on-target toxicity in normal tissue. This is worth stating
plainly because it predicts which toxicities are unavoidable and which are idiosyncratic.

Cell cycle inhibitors suppress proliferating marrow. Neutropenia is a class effect and its
severity differs by agent, and abemaciclib produces diarrhoea as its dominant toxicity while the
other two agents in the class produce more myelosuppression. Ribociclib prolongs the QT interval
and requires electrocardiographic and electrolyte monitoring. Interstitial lung disease is rare
across the class and is a recognised event.

PI3K and AKT inhibition disrupts insulin signalling in normal tissue, so hyperglycaemia is the
mechanism working as designed outside the tumour. In {{trial:solar1}}, grade 3 or 4 hyperglycaemia
occurred in 36.6% of patients receiving alpelisib with fulvestrant against 0.7% receiving placebo
with fulvestrant, and grade 3 or 4 rash in 9.9% against 0.3%. Discontinuation for adverse events
occurred in 25.0% against 4.2% [@andre2019]. In {{trial:capitello291}}, grade 3 or higher rash
occurred in 12.1% of patients receiving capivasertib with fulvestrant against 0.3% with placebo.
Grade 3 or higher diarrhoea occurred in 9.3% against 0.3%. Discontinuation for adverse events
occurred in 13.0% against 2.3% [@turner2023capivasertib].

mTOR inhibition produces stomatitis and non-infectious pneumonitis, and the stomatitis is an
aphthous ulceration distinct from cytotoxic mucositis [@baselga2012]. PARP inhibition produces
anaemia, fatigue, and nausea [@robson2017; @litton2018], and carries a small long-term risk of
myeloid neoplasm taken up in [[BS-5510]]. HER2-directed tyrosine kinase inhibitors produce
diarrhoea and hand-foot reactions, and tucatinib adds transaminase elevation [@murthy2020].

The combination problem is that these toxicities overlap. Two agents that each suppress the
marrow, or each cause diarrhoea, do not simply add their published rates. Overlapping toxicity as
a constraint on combination design is in [[BS-4010]], and dose optimisation as a regulatory
question is in [[BS-6380]].

## BS-5460 Conjugate-specific toxicity and monitoring

The mechanism of conjugate toxicity belongs to [[BS-4100]]. This section is the monitoring
algorithm, and the two should not be confused. Understanding why a payload reaches lung epithelium
does not tell a clinician when to order a scan.

Interstitial lung disease is the event that changes management. A pooled analysis of nine
trastuzumab deruxtecan monotherapy studies covering 1,150 patients found adjudicated drug-related
interstitial lung disease or pneumonitis in 15.4%, grade 5 in 2.2%. Among those with an event,
77.4% had only grade 1 or grade 2 disease and 87.0% had their first event within 12 months of the
first dose. Median time to first event was 5.4 months. Independent adjudication placed onset
earlier than the investigator had for 53.2% of events, with a median difference of 43 days
[@powell2022]. In the randomised comparison against trastuzumab emtansine, the adjudicated rate
was 10.5% against 1.9% [@cortes2022destinyb03].

```practice
Screen by symptom at every visit. Ask specifically about new or changed cough, breathlessness, and
exercise tolerance, and treat any positive answer as an indication for computed tomography.

Hold the drug while investigating. A held cycle costs little. A grade 3 event costs the drug
permanently.

Do not wait for hypoxia or for a grade to declare itself. The adjudication gap says the radiological
change precedes the clinical impression by about six weeks.

Treat the first 12 months as the high-risk window, since 87.0% of patients who had an event had it
inside that year. Vigilance should not decay at six months [@powell2022].

For grade 1 events, hold and treat with corticosteroids, and resume only after full radiological
resolution. For grade 2 or above, discontinue permanently.

For datopotamab deruxtecan, stomatitis and ocular surface events are managed prophylactically
rather than reactively, with mouth care and ocular lubrication started at the first cycle
[@heist2024].

For sacituzumab govitecan, expect neutropenia and diarrhoea rather than pneumonitis. Grade 3 or
higher neutropenia occurred in 51% of patients against 33% on single-agent chemotherapy, and grade
3 or higher diarrhoea in 10% against under 1% [@bardia2021ascent].

For trastuzumab emtansine, monitor platelets and transaminases, which are its characteristic
toxicities rather than the lung [@verma2012].
```

```interplay target=BC-460
The monitoring schedules above are not interchangeable between conjugates, and the reason is in
Part VIII. Toxicity tracks the payload and linker rather than the antigen, so two agents against
the same target need different surveillance and two agents against different targets may need the
same. A clinician who groups these drugs by their antibody will monitor the wrong organ.
```

## BS-5470 Immune-related adverse events and endocrinopathies

Immune-related toxicity does not resemble cytotoxic toxicity in timing, organ distribution, or
reversibility, because it is the mechanism rather than a side effect of it. Management is codified
in guideline form with organ-specific branches [@schneider2021], and the general structure is set
out in [[BS-4180]].

Three properties matter more than any individual algorithm.

Onset is not dose-related and is frequently delayed. Events appear months into treatment and after
it has stopped, so tolerance of six cycles does not establish low risk.

Reversibility differs by organ. Colitis, hepatitis, and pneumonitis usually resolve with
immunosuppression. Endocrinopathy frequently does not. Hypophysitis, primary thyroid failure, and
immune-mediated diabetes leave lifelong replacement whether or not the drug is stopped.

Grade does not mean the same thing in every organ. Grade 2 dermatitis and grade 2 pneumonitis are
not comparable events, which is why the algorithms branch by organ rather than by number.

The consideration specific to breast cancer is the population. Checkpoint blockade is used in
early triple-negative disease with curative intent, and in {{trial:keynote522}} more than half of
the patients in the control arm reached a pathological complete response without pembrolizumab
[@schmid2020]. Permanent adrenal insufficiency in a patient who would have been cured without the
drug is a different calculation from the same event in metastatic disease. That argument is
developed in [[BS-4510]] and belongs in the conversation held before the first dose.

```caution
Fatigue in a patient on checkpoint blockade is not a nonspecific complaint. Hypothyroidism,
hypophysitis, and adrenal insufficiency all present this way, and adrenal insufficiency is the one
that kills. Check thyroid function and a morning cortisol before attributing fatigue to treatment
or to the cancer.
```

## BS-5480 Cardio-oncology, surveillance and prevention

Anthracycline injury and HER2-directed injury differ in reversibility, and that single difference
drives everything about how they are watched.

Anthracycline cardiotoxicity is cumulative-dose related and is largely irreversible once
established. Its timing is narrower than clinical folklore suggests. In a cohort of patients
receiving anthracycline-containing therapy, cardiotoxicity occurred in 9%. The median interval
from the end of chemotherapy to the event was 3.5 months. Of all events, 98% occurred within the
first year. Among affected patients treated with heart failure therapy, 11% had full recovery and
71% had partial recovery, and recovery depended on how early treatment was started
[@cardinale2015].

Trastuzumab-associated dysfunction behaves differently. A referral series examined 38 patients
with suspected trastuzumab-related cardiotoxicity, all previously anthracycline-exposed. Mean
ejection fraction fell from 0.61 to 0.43 and recovered to 0.56 after withdrawal. Mean time to
recovery was 1.5 months. Of 25 patients re-treated with trastuzumab, three had recurrent left
ventricular dysfunction [@ewer2005].

The surveillance consequence follows directly. For anthracyclines the aim is early detection in
the first year, because the window in which the injury is modifiable is short. For HER2-directed
therapy the aim is serial monitoring across the treatment course, because a fall is expected to be
recoverable and rechallenge is often possible. Treating both with one schedule wastes imaging in
one setting and misses events in the other. The HER2-specific schedule is in [[BS-7030]].

Baseline risk stratification before potentially cardiotoxic treatment is now formalised, using
pre-existing cardiovascular disease, prior cardiotoxic exposure, age, and conventional risk
factors [@lyon2022].

Radiation is the third exposure and behaves as a dose-response rather than a threshold. In a
case-control study of 2,168 women irradiated for breast cancer, comprising 963 with major coronary
events and 1,205 controls, the overall average mean heart dose was 4.9 Gy. Rates of major coronary
events rose by 7.4% per gray of mean heart dose, with no apparent threshold [@darby2013]. Cardiac
sparing technique is in [[BS-3780]].

```practice
Measure the baseline before the first cardiotoxic dose, not after the first symptom.

For anthracyclines, concentrate surveillance in the first 12 months after the last cycle.

Start heart failure therapy at the point of detection rather than at the point of symptoms. Recovery
depends on the interval.

Treat blood pressure, lipids, glucose, and smoking. Conventional risk factor management is the only
part of this that is fully modifiable.
```

## BS-5490 Pulmonary, neurologic, and hematologic complications

The pulmonary problem in current practice is that four different causes present identically.
Conjugate-associated interstitial lung disease, checkpoint pneumonitis, radiation pneumonitis, and
mTOR inhibitor pneumonitis all appear as new dyspnoea with ground-glass change. Infection and
lymphangitic progression complete the differential. The distinction matters because the treatments
diverge. Conjugate events require permanent discontinuation at grade 2 and above [@powell2022].
Checkpoint events require immunosuppression with a specified taper [@schneider2021]. Radiation
pneumonitis is confined to the treated volume and follows a defined time course. Infection
requires the opposite of steroids. A patient on several of these agents at once presents the
hardest version of the problem, and no test resolves it quickly.

Neurologic complications are dominated by peripheral neuropathy, covered in [[BS-5430]], and by
cognitive change, covered in [[BS-5550]]. Both are chronic rather than acute, and both are
underdocumented because neither generates an abnormal laboratory value.

Haematologic complications extend beyond the nadir. Prolonged cytopenias after multiple lines of
treatment reflect cumulative marrow damage rather than a single regimen. PARP inhibitors produce
anaemia that is dose-limiting and often requires transfusion [@robson2017]. Thrombosis risk is
elevated by the cancer itself, by tamoxifen, by central venous access, and by immobility, and
these are additive in a way that no single risk score captures well.

## BS-5500 Bone, metabolic, and endocrine late effects

Aromatase inhibition and ovarian suppression both accelerate bone loss, and they do so on top of
whatever age-related loss is already occurring. Baseline bone density measurement before starting,
with a defined monitoring interval, is the standard of care rather than a refinement.

Two separate indications for antiresorptive therapy exist in this disease and conflating them is
common. Every randomised trial of a bone-modifying agent in early disease is in the table below,
and the endpoint column is the one that separates the two indications.

```evidence setting=early modality=bone sort=year cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,endpoint,result,os caption=Bone-modifying_agents_given_alongside_adjuvant_treatment._One_of_these_trials_was_designed_around_a_fracture._The_others_were_designed_around_a_recurrence._Read_the_endpoint_column_before_the_result_column.
```

The first indication is skeletal protection, and {{trial:abcsg18}} is the trial that establishes it
[@gnant2015]. Two features of it are worth holding. Its endpoint was a fracture rather than a
recurrence. And the benefit held in women whose baseline bone density was still in the normal
range, which makes this a treatment decision rather than an osteoporosis decision.

The second indication is antitumour effect, and it belongs to bisphosphonates rather than to bone
protection in general. A meta-analysis of individual patient data from randomised trials examined
adjuvant bisphosphonates. In women who were postmenopausal when treatment began, recurrence fell
with a rate ratio of 0.86 and a 95% confidence interval of 0.78 to 0.94. Distant recurrence fell
with a ratio of 0.82 and bone recurrence with a ratio of 0.72. Breast cancer mortality fell with a
ratio of 0.82 and an interval of 0.73 to 0.93. Fractures were reduced with a ratio of 0.85.
Benefit was definite only in women postmenopausal at the start of treatment [@ebctcg2015].

The two individual trials in that table are where the distinction is easiest to see.
{{trial:azure}} gave adjuvant zoledronic acid and separated nothing across the whole population
[@coleman2018]. Its first report had crossed a boundary for lack of benefit, with disease-free
survival of 77% in each group [@coleman2011]. ABCSG-12 added zoledronic acid to ovarian suppression
with anastrozole or tamoxifen in 1,803 premenopausal women, and its benefit persisted at 62 months
[@gnant2011]. A trial-level meta-analysis found fewer fractures and a survival benefit over placebo
or no treatment, with osteonecrosis of the jaw in 0.52% [@valachis2013]. What benefit it found sat in the postmenopausal stratum, which is the
meta-analysis result appearing inside a single randomisation. {{trial:d-care}} gave denosumab at
the dose and interval used for skeletal protection in metastatic disease, and it did not improve
bone metastasis-free survival [@coleman2019]. The drug that prevents fractures did not prevent
metastases, at a far higher exposure than the fracture indication uses.

```caution
These are different questions with different answers. A drug that prevents fractures has not thereby
been shown to prevent recurrence, and a drug that reduces recurrence in postmenopausal women has not
been shown to do so in premenopausal women. The menopausal status restriction in the bisphosphonate
data is the sharpest illustration, because the same drug in the same disease produced benefit in one
group and not the other.
```

Premature menopause from chemotherapy or ovarian suppression compresses years of endocrine change
into months, and its bone and cardiovascular consequences arrive early in a young patient
[[BS-5290]]. Metabolic late effects include weight gain during adjuvant treatment, corticosteroid
exposure, and the hyperglycaemia of PI3K and AKT inhibition described in [[BS-5450]]. Thyroid
dysfunction follows supraclavicular irradiation and checkpoint blockade through entirely different
mechanisms, and both need a check rather than an assumption.

## BS-5510 Treatment-related second malignancies and clonal hematopoiesis

The best current quantification comes from a population cohort of 476,373 women with breast cancer
as their first invasive cancer, followed for up to 28 years. By 20 years, 13.6% had developed a
non-breast primary cancer, which was 2.1 percentage points more than expected in the general
population. A contralateral breast cancer had developed in 5.6%, which was 3.1 percentage points
more than expected. Radiotherapy was associated with increased contralateral breast and lung
cancer, endocrine therapy with increased uterine cancer and reduced contralateral breast cancer,
and chemotherapy with increased acute leukaemia. Absolute excess risk at 20 years was under 1% for
every individual non-breast cancer type. Roughly 7% of the excess second cancers in the cohort
were estimated to be attributable to adjuvant therapies [@mcgale2025].

```caution
Standardised incidence ratios above 1.5 and absolute excesses below 1% are both correct descriptions
of the same data. The ratio is the right number for understanding causation. The absolute excess is
the only number that belongs in a treatment conversation. Quoting the ratio to a patient deciding
about adjuvant chemotherapy misrepresents the magnitude by an order of magnitude or more.
```

Therapy-related myeloid neoplasms arise by two recognised routes. Alkylating agents produce a
latency of several years with characteristic chromosomal losses, and topoisomerase II inhibitors
produce a shorter latency with balanced translocations. Both are rare after standard breast cancer
regimens.

Clonal haematopoiesis is the mechanistic bridge and it is now measurable. Somatic mutations in
blood cells without cytopenia increase steeply with age and are associated with adverse outcomes
including subsequent haematologic cancer [@jaiswal2014]. In patients with non-haematologic
cancers, therapy-related clonal haematopoiesis is common and associated with adverse clinical
outcomes [@coombs2017]. Cancer therapy is not a neutral observer of this process. It shapes which
clones expand, with specific therapy classes selecting for mutations in defined DNA damage
response genes [@bolton2020]. The ageing marrow context sits in [[BS-2040]].

There is a measurement consequence that reaches beyond haematology. Clonal haematopoiesis is a
leading source of false positive results in plasma sequencing, because the variant is genuinely in
the blood and is not from the tumour. That problem is developed in [[BS-3540]].

## BS-5520 Toxicity monitoring and coordinated care

Most monitoring failures are not knowledge failures. They are ownership failures. Everyone assumed
someone else was checking the echocardiogram, the thyroid function, the bone density, or the
glucose.

The remedy is structural and unglamorous. Every regimen should have a written monitoring plan that
names the test, the interval, and the person responsible. That is three fields, and their absence
accounts for most of the missed surveillance in [[BS-5480]] and [[BS-5500]].

Three transitions are where plans break. The first is from intravenous to oral therapy, because
the patient stops attending an infusion unit where monitoring happened by default. The second is
from the cancer centre to community or primary care, which is the subject of [[BS-5600]]. The
third is between lines of therapy, because the monitoring requirements change completely and the
previous plan is often carried forward.

There is a measurement asymmetry worth naming. Clinician-graded adverse events record the
clinician's impression of the patient's experience. When the same events are graded by patients
directly, the reported severity and frequency are systematically higher. Both are measurements,
and they are measurements of different things. Routine collection of patient-reported outcomes is
developed in [[BS-5660]].

```practice
Write the monitoring plan when you write the prescription, not at the first review.

Name one person per recurring test. Shared responsibility for a six-monthly echocardiogram means no
responsibility.

Reconcile the plan at every line change and at every handover to another service.

Ask the patient directly about the symptoms that do not generate laboratory abnormalities. Neuropathy,
arthralgia, fatigue, sexual function, and cognitive change will not otherwise be recorded.
```

Toxicity in older adults carries additional predictors that this chapter does not repeat, and they
are in [[BS-5320]].
