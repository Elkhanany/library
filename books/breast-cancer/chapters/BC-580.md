## BS-5020 Biomarker-driven treatment selection

This chapter is about how to approach the patient. Risk, disease burden, symptom load, biomarker
triage and what the line of treatment is for. The trial record itself, arranged by line, is in
[[BC-920]]. Where a decision here rests on a result, the evidence for it is tabulated there and is
pointed to rather than restated.

The division matters because the two questions come apart in clinic. Which regimen has the better
hazard ratio is answered by [[BC-920]]. Whether this patient should start a regimen at all, and what
to measure to find out if it worked, is answered here.

Triple-negative is defined by what is not there. Three receptor assays report below threshold and
the tumour is assigned to a group on that basis. The category is therefore an artefact of an assay
panel, and the biological diversity inside it is real [@lehmann2011]. The classification argument is
developed in [[BC-120]].

What has changed is that management no longer runs on the absence. Three positive markers now
determine the first decision, and they are checked in a fixed order.

PD-L1 determines whether first-line treatment includes a checkpoint inhibitor [[BS-5030]]. It is an
assay output with an antibody clone, a scoring algorithm and a cut-point attached, not a property of
the tumour.

Germline BRCA1 and BRCA2 status, extended to PALB2 and to somatic BRCA alterations, determines
eligibility for PARP inhibition [[BS-5040]]. It also changes family counselling, which is a second
reason to test early.

HER2 expression level determines whether trastuzumab deruxtecan is available, because a proportion
of tumours called triple-negative score immunohistochemistry 1+ or 2+ without amplification. The
eligibility argument is in [[BC-590]].

Trop-2 expression is measured in trials and is not a selection criterion. In the biomarker analysis
of {{trial:ascent}}, sacituzumab govitecan outperformed chemotherapy across high, medium and low
Trop-2 expression, and the low-expression group was too small for a firm conclusion
[@bardia2021ascentbio]. Tumour mutational burden is high in only a small minority of breast cancers
and is not a routine selection tool here [@barrososousa2020].

```practice
Order all three at the first metastatic presentation, not sequentially as options run out.

PD-L1 on the most recent available tissue, with the assay and cut-point named in the report.

Germline BRCA1 and BRCA2 if never tested, with PALB2 included.

HER2 immunohistochemistry on recent tissue, read for the 0 against 1+ boundary specifically.

The order matters because the first-line decision depends on PD-L1 and cannot wait for the
others. The others determine the second and third decisions and there is time to get them right.
```

## BS-5030 Immunotherapy-based strategies and PD-L1 assessment

Checkpoint blockade works in this disease in a defined subgroup. The boundary of that subgroup is
an assay boundary rather than a biological one.

```evidence setting=metastatic subtype=TNBC line=1L modality=immunotherapy cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,result,os caption=The_first-line_chemoimmunotherapy_record._Read_the_assay_column_before_the_result_column.
```

Four of those trials added a checkpoint inhibitor to first-line chemotherapy. None of them
lengthened overall survival in the unselected population. Every survival claim that exists here
belongs to a PD-L1-defined subgroup, and one of the four produced nothing even inside its own.
{{trial:keynote355}} shows the dependence on the marker as a gradient rather than as a step.
Survival separates at a combined positive score of 10 or more. It weakens and loses significance at
a score of 1 or more. It is absent across the whole randomised population [@cortes2022keynote355].
A marker whose effect decays as its threshold is lowered is behaving predictively. That is what
makes the cut-point load-bearing rather than administrative.

The apparent disagreement in the table is between the two atezolizumab trials. It is not a
disagreement about atezolizumab. {{trial:impassion130}} and {{trial:impassion131}} used the same
antibody and scored PD-L1 on immune cells with the same assay [@schmid2018impassion130; @miles2021].
They differ in the taxane. The explanation usually offered is that paclitaxel requires corticosteroid
premedication and nab-paclitaxel does not. Nobody has randomised the two backbones against each
other with the checkpoint inhibitor held constant, so that remains a hypothesis about the partner
drug rather than a finding about the antibody.

The second design point explains why atezolizumab carries no confirmed survival claim.
IMpassion130 improved progression-free survival in both of its co-primary populations. Its testing
hierarchy put overall survival in the intention-to-treat population first, and formal testing
stopped when that gate did not open [@schmid2018impassion130; @emens2021]. The survival result in
the PD-L1-positive subgroup was therefore never tested. That is an alpha-control decision, not a
statement about the drug.

{{trial:torchlight}} is the third checkpoint inhibitor programme here. It improved both
progression-free and overall survival in its PD-L1-positive population [@jiang2024]. Survival was a
secondary endpoint there, read at a prespecified interim analysis. The row does not say which
antibody clone or cut-point defined that population, and the trial tested a single chemotherapy
backbone. A PD-L1-positive population is not a portable object between trials. The TORCHLIGHT
estimate cannot be added to the pembrolizumab estimate or read as a larger version of it.

The decision rests on KEYNOTE-355. It is the only trial here that carried overall survival in a
prespecified biomarker population as a primary endpoint and met it, against the chemotherapy the
clinic would otherwise have given [@cortes2022keynote355]. The decision that follows is narrow.
Most triple-negative disease does not reach the high cut-point, so this branch covers a minority of
patients seen in clinic. For that minority the first line is settled and should not be delayed
while other results are chased.

{{trial:ascent04}} then asked whether the chemotherapy partner can be replaced by a conjugate in
exactly this PD-L1-positive population, and on a progression-free survival endpoint it can
[@tolaney2026ascent04]. The practical consequence is that a positive PD-L1 result no longer
determines the whole regimen. It determines only that a checkpoint inhibitor belongs in it.

What the table cannot show is where the boundary belongs. No trial randomised patients with a
combined positive score between 1 and 9 as a stratum of their own. The weak result at a score of 1
or more is therefore a diluted superset rather than a test of that band. No trial scored the same specimens
with both assays and reported how many patients change side. And no trial randomised how long a
checkpoint inhibitor should continue, or whether it should be carried past progression.

```caution
A PD-L1 result is the output of one antibody clone scored by one algorithm at one cut-point. It is
not a property of the tumour, and the same specimen scored by a different assay can cross the
boundary in either direction. The predictive biomarker problem is developed in [[BS-4130]] and the
immune escape biology in [[BS-3020]]. One further point restrains over-reading. The early disease
result was obtained without PD-L1 selection at all [@schmid2020; @schmid2024k522os]. The marker
predicts benefit in the metastatic setting and does not in the neoadjuvant one, and nobody has
explained why.
```

## BS-5040 DNA repair directed treatment

Germline BRCA status identifies a population in which a targeted oral agent outperforms
chemotherapy. Both registration trials enrolled hormone receptor-positive and triple-negative
patients, so the evidence is shared with [[BC-560]].

```evidence setting=metastatic subtype=TNBC,BRCA modality=parp status=reported cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,result,os caption=Reported_PARP_inhibitor_trials_reaching_this_population.
```

Three of those five are randomised phase 3 trials in germline BRCA1 or BRCA2 carriers, and they
agree. A PARP inhibitor lengthens progression-free survival in germline-mutated HER2-negative
advanced disease. Three drugs, three sponsors, one direction.

They do not agree on what that delay is bought against, and the control arm is the reason.
{{trial:olympiad}} and {{trial:embraca}} substituted an oral agent for the single-agent
chemotherapy a clinic would otherwise prescribe [@robson2017; @litton2018]. {{trial:brocade3}}
added veliparib to carboplatin and paclitaxel, and both arms received that doublet [@diras2020].
BROCADE3 therefore asks about addition to an active platinum-containing regimen rather than about
replacement of a weak one. Its control arm is the strongest in the table and its margin is the
narrowest. Reading the three result cells as a ranking of PARP inhibitors is the error that column
invites.

Neither substitution trial improved overall survival [@robson2019; @litton2020]. BROCADE3 has not
reported survival at all. The table has no column for what patients received after progression, and
that is the column the survival reading depends on. In both substitution trials the control arm
could go on to a PARP inhibitor or to platinum. EMBRACA's investigators reported an analysis
adjusted for that subsequent treatment, and its interval still included no difference
[@litton2020]. What that pattern means for a progression-free survival endpoint generally is worked
through in [[BS-7740]].

The decision rests on OlympiAD and EMBRACA taken together rather than on either alone. Their
comparator is the thing that would otherwise be prescribed, which is what makes them load-bearing.
Two drugs tested the same way with the same result is what makes the class claim safe.

The other two rows answer different questions and should not be read alongside the first three.
{{trial:tbcrc048}} widened the eligible set beyond germline BRCA and then narrowed it again.
Confirmed responses occurred only with germline PALB2 and with somatic BRCA1 or BRCA2 alterations.
No confirmed response followed an ATM or CHEK2 mutation alone [@tung2020]. A repair gene on a panel
is not by itself an indication. Expansion cohorts have since been reported separately
[@tung2026a]. {{trial:dora}} asks whether a patient responding to platinum can be maintained off
chemotherapy. It enrolled by response to platinum rather than by germline status, and sustained
benefit tracked neither germline BRCA nor PD-L1 [@tan2024]. Each of its two arms was measured
against a historical control of continued platinum rather than against the other, so its result
cell cannot say whether durvalumab added anything.

Two comparisons nobody randomised sit behind this whole table. A PARP inhibitor has never been
tested against platinum in germline-mutated disease, which is the choice actually faced. Nor has
the order of the two been randomised, so cross-resistance is inferred rather than measured.

What the table does not carry is the reason to test early. The eligible population is defined by a
germline result, so a patient never tested is a patient never eligible, and the result also changes
counselling for relatives. Testing is therefore part of the first consultation rather than part of
the second-line decision [[BC-690]].

Platinum belongs in this section because it exploits the same defect. That shared mechanism also
predicts cross-resistance, and the prediction holds. Secondary intragenic mutations that restore the
BRCA2 reading frame confer resistance both to platinum and to PARP inhibition [@sakai2008]. The full
account is in [[BS-3010]] and the platinum trial record is in [[BS-7760]].

```caution
A homologous recombination deficiency score records past genomic instability. Scars persist after
repair function returns, so a tumour can score [[term:hrd]] high while repairing normally at the
moment of testing. The score describes history. The drug needs the present. Repair biology is in
[[BC-180]] and agent detail in [[BC-450]].
```

## BS-5050 Antibody-drug conjugates

Conjugates have moved from the refractory setting to the first line in this subtype. The four
randomised trials should be read as three populations rather than as one story.

```evidence setting=metastatic subtype=TNBC modality=adc cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,result,os caption=Randomised_TROP2_conjugate_trials_in_triple-negative_disease._The_control_arm_column_is_the_one_to_read_for_expectation_setting.
```

All four agree. A TROP2-directed conjugate delayed progression against the control regimen each
trial would otherwise have given. That holds in the third line and beyond, in the first line
without a checkpoint inhibitor, and in the first line with one. Both conjugates carry a
topoisomerase I payload, so this is one delivery mechanism tested in three positions rather than
two independent classes.

Where the four diverge is the control arm, and that is the column to read before the result column.
{{trial:ascent}} compared against single-agent chemotherapy in patients who had already had at least
two chemotherapy regimens [@bardia2021ascent]. What that arm achieved is the baseline every later
conversation with a patient should start from. {{trial:ascent03}} and {{trial:tropion-b02}} were set
against first-line chemotherapy, which in ASCENT-03 could be a gemcitabine and carboplatin doublet
[@cortes2025ascent03; @dent2026tropionb02]. {{trial:ascent04}} was set against chemotherapy plus
pembrolizumab, the strongest comparator in the table [@tolaney2026ascent04]. The conjugate's margin
narrows as the comparator strengthens. None of that is a statement about the conjugates.

The second divergence is endpoint maturity, and it governs what can be claimed today.
TROPION-Breast02 carried overall survival as a dual primary endpoint and met it. ASCENT-03 and
ASCENT-04 report progression-free survival with survival not yet reported. For first-line patients
who are not candidates for immunotherapy, which is most of them, TROPION-Breast02 is therefore the
load-bearing trial. It is the only first-line conjugate trial in which the delay in progression has
so far been shown to be paid out as time alive.

{{trial:ascent}} remains load-bearing further down the disease course. It is the only trial in this
subtype to have improved overall survival against single-agent chemotherapy in a population already
treated with two or more regimens. Its final analysis also reported outcomes by HER2 and TROP2
expression [@bardia2024], which is the closest the class has come to a selection assay and is not
one.

The practical result is that a conjugate is now the default first cytotoxic for nearly every
patient in this chapter. Three things the table cannot show qualify that. No conjugate has been
randomised against another conjugate, and ASCENT-03 and TROPION-Breast02 enrolled the same intended
population without ever being compared. No trial selected patients by TROP2 expression
[@bardia2021ascentbio]. And no trial randomised the order, so whether starting with a conjugate
beats reserving it for the second line is unknown.

```caution
The first-line conjugate trials differ in what they have shown. {{trial:tropion-b02}} carried overall
survival as a dual primary endpoint and met it [@dent2026tropionb02]. {{trial:ascent03}} reports
progression-free survival with survival not yet reported, and a published critique argues that
crossover-adjusted analysis is needed before first-line sacituzumab govitecan is treated as settled
[@poggio2026]. The two are frequently presented as equivalent first-line options. They are
equivalent in indication and not in the strength of what has been demonstrated.
```

```interplay target=BC-350
Triple-negative disease is where the heterogeneity argument of Part VI meets a category built from
three negative results. Trop-2 is expressed across most of these tumours and its level does not
select patients [@bardia2021ascentbio], while HER2 at immunohistochemistry 0 still supports conjugate
responses [@mosele2023]. Both findings point the same way. Antigen abundance is not the variable
governing conjugate benefit at the low end of the range, and a subtype defined by receptor absence is
not thereby a subtype without targets. What varies within these tumours is not only how much antigen
is present but how the payload is handled once delivered, and no routine assay reports that.
```

## BS-5060 Chemotherapy sequencing and realistic expectations

Chemotherapy still carries most of the treatment time in this disease. The expectations attached to
it should be stated rather than implied, and the honest source for them is the control arm column of
the conjugate table in [[BS-5050]].

Read the {{trial:ascent}}, {{trial:ascent03}} and {{trial:tropion-b02}} control arms together.
Single-agent chemotherapy in the refractory setting delays progression by a matter of weeks. In the
first line without immunotherapy it delays progression by several months. The difference between
them is the effect of line rather than of agent. It is the most useful single thing to have in mind
before a fourth-line conversation.

Three principles follow.

Sequential single agents are preferred to combinations outside a situation requiring immediate
response. Combinations raise response rate and toxicity without a consistent survival gain.

Platinum has a specific indication in germline BRCA-altered disease and no general superiority
otherwise [[BS-5040]].

Response duration shortens with each line, and the shortening is steeper here than in hormone
receptor-positive disease. A regimen that works in the fourth line is more often working for weeks
than for months.

Agent-level detail belongs to [[BC-440]] and the randomised comparisons between cytotoxics to
[[BS-7760]]. The point specific to this chapter is that expectation setting is a clinical act, and
it is done with absolute medians rather than with hazard ratios.

## BS-5070 Androgen receptor and other subtype-directed targets

```story SQ-1100
```

Transcriptional subtyping divides triple-negative disease into groups with different biology,
including a luminal androgen receptor group [@lehmann2011]. The clinical question is whether any of
those groups has a therapy attached. Mostly they do not.

```evidence setting=metastatic subtype=TNBC modality=endocrine weight=exploratory cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,endpoint,result caption=Subtype-directed_targets_tested_inside_triple-negative_disease._Every_row_is_exploratory_and_none_supports_an_indication.
```

Three of those four rows tested androgen receptor blockade, and they agree on the shape of the
answer. A minority of selected patients avoid progression for a few months. The measure is a
clinical benefit rate at a fixed timepoint. No trial carried a chemotherapy control arm. Activity is
real and it is not a treatment, and no androgen receptor directed agent is approved in this setting.

What separates the three is the assay rather than the drug. {{trial:tbcrc-011}} required more than
10% nuclear staining on central immunohistochemistry [@gucalp2013]. {{trial:mdv3100-11}} admitted
any nuclear staining above zero, then reported an evaluable subgroup defined at 10% or more
[@traina2018]. {{trial:tbcrc-032}} set its threshold at 10% and randomised between enzalutamide and
enzalutamide with taselisib [@lehmann2019]. The clocks differ as well. Clinical benefit was scored
at six months in the first trial and at sixteen weeks in the other two. Three studies of the same
target with three eligibility definitions and two scoring windows cannot be ranked against each
other.

The measurement problem sits underneath all of it. In MDV3100-11 benefit was higher in the
receptor-defined subgroup than in the whole enrolled population, which is what an imperfect
selection assay produces. TBCRC 032's investigators concluded that androgen receptor protein
expression alone does not identify androgen receptor-dependent tumours, and that the luminal
androgen receptor subtype and receptor splice variants may [@lehmann2019]. A threshold that has not
been reproduced between laboratories cannot support an indication however the responses distribute
around it.

The assay also discards most patients before it selects any. TBCRC 011 screened 424 patients with
oestrogen and progesterone receptor-negative disease, and 12% scored androgen receptor-positive
[@gucalp2013]. A single-arm study inside a selected fraction that small has to show a large effect
to earn a randomised test. None of these did. The one randomisation in the table, inside TBCRC 032,
compared two experimental arms, closed early, and left no clinical benefit rate on the record for
the enzalutamide-alone arm.

{{trial:sgntuc-019}} is in this table for a different reason, and its population column is the part
to read. It enrolled heavily pretreated patients selected by an activating HER2 mutation in disease
that local testing called HER2-negative, across receptor status rather than within triple-negative
disease [@okines2025]. It is one cohort of a single-arm basket study. Its response rate belongs to a
genotype and not to this subtype. The alteration-defined options that do cross this subtype are
collected in [[BS-7770]].

The general lesson recurs across this chapter. A transcriptional subtype is a research
classification. Converting it into an eligibility criterion requires an assay that ordinary
laboratories can run reproducibly, and the gap between those two things is the same gap described for
HER2 scoring in [[BS-2360]]. The trial design consequences are set out in [[BC-740]].

Immune-directed and repair-directed options are covered in [[BS-5030]] and [[BS-5040]]. Everything
else in this subtype remains investigational, and saying so is more useful than listing targets
without evidence.

## BS-5080 Rapidly progressive and refractory disease

This subtype produces the fastest clinical deterioration in breast cancer, and the interval between
a decision and its consequences is short.

Two practical features follow. The first is that a line of therapy must be assessed early. Waiting
twelve weeks for a scan in a patient deteriorating over three is a decision to spend the remaining
time on a failing regimen. The second is that performance status falls faster than in other
subtypes, so eligibility for anything, including trials, is lost quickly.

The judgment that is hardest and most important is when not to treat. The evidence for it is
indirect and consistent. Response rates on single-agent chemotherapy in the refractory setting are
in single figures, as the control arm of {{trial:ascent}} shows [[BS-5050]]. A patient with declining
performance status has a low probability of benefit and a high probability of toxicity, and the
toxicity arrives first.

```practice
Set a reassessment interval that matches the tempo of the disease, not the convention of the
department. In rapidly progressive disease that is often six weeks.

Decide in advance what would make you stop, and say it out loud before starting. A named
stopping rule is easier to act on than a judgment made in the room.

Involve palliative care in parallel with active treatment rather than after it
[@temel2010]. The referral trigger that works is disease status, not exhaustion of options
[[BC-680]].

Performance status is the variable that closes doors. Record it at every visit, because the
trial that is open today will not be open at the next decline.
```

## BS-5090 The unmet need after conjugate failure

The population that defines this disease has moved. The unmet need used to sit after chemotherapy.
It is now after conjugates, and the evidence base behind that position is retrospective.

The relevant data are the sequential conjugate series, and they were not assembled inside this
subtype. Among 85 patients with HER2-low metastatic breast cancer treated with both trastuzumab
deruxtecan and sacituzumab govitecan, progression-free survival on the second conjugate was shorter
than on the first in 75.2%. Fourteen of the 85 were still on treatment at the data cutoff
[@mai2025]. Longer progression-free survival on the first conjugate predicted longer
progression-free survival on the second, which is the signature of partial rather than complete
cross-resistance. Hormone receptor status was not associated with that second interval, which is
what allows the series to be read here at all. A second conjugate is a reasonable option with a
smaller expected magnitude.

What makes the position difficult is that three of the payloads now in routine use are
topoisomerase I inhibitors. Irinotecan works through the same mechanism, and its active metabolite
is the payload of sacituzumab govitecan. A patient who has received sacituzumab govitecan,
datopotamab deruxtecan and trastuzumab deruxtecan has had three exposures to the same payload class
delivered by three different antibodies. That is not three lines of non-cross-resistant therapy,
whatever the line count says.

The mechanisms that are not antigen loss remain unmeasurable in clinic. PTEN loss was associated
with de novo resistance to trastuzumab deruxtecan and not to sacituzumab govitecan, despite the
shared payload class [@mai2025]. That association is retrospective and it points at handling rather
than at target. The full taxonomy is in [[BS-3000]].

```evidence setting=metastatic subtype=TNBC,HER2-low modality=adc status=ongoing cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,endpoint,status caption=The_only_prospective_study_addressing_this_position.
```

{{trial:trade-dxd}} will give the first prospective estimate of how much activity remains after a
conjugate fails. It enters patients on the state of their disease after a previous conjugate, which
is the population every retrospective series has had to assemble after the fact. It enrols by HER2
result rather than by subtype, with separate hormone receptor-positive and negative cohorts, so
only part of it will speak to this chapter. Its cohorts are non-comparative, so it will not rank
sequences. What it will and will not settle is set out in [[BS-7780]].

The honest summary is that this is now the largest gap in the management of the disease. Trial
enrolment is the most useful thing on offer, and it should be discussed at the start of the
post-conjugate line rather than after it fails [[BS-4800]]. Emerging platforms are in [[BC-760]] and
[[BC-770]].
