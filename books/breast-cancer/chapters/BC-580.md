## BS-5020 Biomarker-driven treatment selection

Triple-negative is defined by what is not there. Three receptor assays report below threshold and
the tumour is assigned to a group on that basis. The category is therefore an artefact of an
assay panel, and the biological diversity inside it is real [@lehmann2011]. The classification
argument is developed in [[BC-120]].

What has changed is that management no longer runs on the absence. Three positive markers now
determine the first decision, and they are checked in a fixed order.

PD-L1 combined positive score determines whether first-line treatment includes pembrolizumab
[[BS-5030]]. It is an assay output with an antibody clone, a scoring algorithm and a cut-point
attached, not a property of the tumour.

Germline BRCA1 and BRCA2 status, extended to PALB2 and to somatic BRCA alterations, determines
eligibility for PARP inhibition [[BS-5040]]. It also changes family counselling, which is a
second reason to test early.

HER2 expression level determines whether trastuzumab deruxtecan is available, because a
proportion of tumours called triple-negative score immunohistochemistry 1+ or 2+ without
amplification. The eligibility argument is in [[BC-590]].

Trop-2 expression is measured in trials and is not a selection criterion. In the biomarker
analysis of {{trial:ascent}}, sacituzumab govitecan outperformed chemotherapy across high,
medium and low Trop-2 expression, and the low-expression group was too small for a firm
conclusion [@bardia2021ascentbio]. Tumour mutational burden is high in only a small minority of
breast cancers and is not a routine selection tool here [@barrososousa2020].

```practice
Order all three at the first metastatic presentation, not sequentially as options run out.

PD-L1 on the most recent available tissue, with the assay and cut-point named in the report.

Germline BRCA1 and BRCA2 if never tested, with PALB2 included.

HER2 immunohistochemistry on recent tissue, read for the 0 against 1+ boundary specifically.

The order matters because the first-line decision depends on PD-L1 and cannot wait for the
others. The others determine the second and third decisions and there is time to get them right.
```

## BS-5030 Immunotherapy-based strategies and PD-L1 assessment

Checkpoint blockade works in this disease in a defined subgroup and the boundary of that
subgroup is an assay boundary.

{{trial:keynote355}} randomised 847 patients with previously untreated locally recurrent
inoperable or metastatic triple-negative disease to pembrolizumab with chemotherapy or placebo
with chemotherapy. In the subgroup with a combined positive score of 10 or more, median overall
survival was 23.0 months against 16.1 months. The hazard ratio for death was 0.73, with a 95%
confidence interval of 0.55 to 0.95. In the subgroup with a score of 1 or more, it was 17.6
against 16.0 months, with a hazard ratio of 0.86 that did not reach significance. In the whole
randomised population it was 17.2 against 15.5 months [@cortes2022keynote355].

The gradient across those three populations is the result. Benefit concentrates where the
marker is high and disappears as the threshold is lowered. That is what a genuine predictive
biomarker looks like, and it is also what makes the cut-point load-bearing.

{{trial:ascent04}} tested whether the chemotherapy partner can be replaced. Among 443 patients
with previously untreated PD-L1-positive disease, median progression-free survival was 11.2
months with sacituzumab govitecan and pembrolizumab against 7.8 months with chemotherapy and
pembrolizumab, with a hazard ratio of 0.65. Objective responses occurred in 60% against 53%, and
median duration of response was 16.5 months against 9.2 months. Overall survival data were
immature. Discontinuation for adverse events occurred in 12% against 31%
[@tolaney2026ascent04].

```caution
A PD-L1 result is the output of one antibody clone scored by one algorithm at one cut-point. It
is not a property of the tumour, and the same specimen scored by a different assay can cross the
boundary in either direction. The predictive biomarker problem is developed in [[BS-4130]] and
the immune escape biology in [[BS-3020]]. Two further points restrain over-reading. Most
triple-negative disease is not PD-L1 high, so this branch covers a minority. And the early
disease result, where 60-month overall survival was 86.6% with pembrolizumab against 81.7%
without [@schmid2024k522os], was obtained without PD-L1 selection at all [@schmid2020]. The
marker predicts benefit in the metastatic setting and does not in the neoadjuvant one, and
nobody has explained why.
```

## BS-5040 DNA repair directed treatment

Germline BRCA status identifies a population in which a targeted oral agent outperforms
chemotherapy. Both registration trials enrolled hormone receptor positive and triple-negative
patients, so the evidence is shared with [[BC-560]].

In {{trial:olympiad}}, 302 patients with germline BRCA mutations and HER2-negative disease
received olaparib or physician's choice single-agent chemotherapy. Median progression-free
survival was 7.0 months against 4.2 months, with a hazard ratio of 0.58 and a response rate of
59.9% against 28.8% [@robson2017]. In {{trial:embraca}}, 431 patients received talazoparib or
single-agent chemotherapy. Median progression-free survival was 8.6 months against 5.6 months,
with a hazard ratio of 0.54 [@litton2018].

{{trial:tbcrc048}} widened the target set and, more usefully, narrowed it. Among 54 patients,
confirmed responses occurred in only two groups. Germline PALB2 alterations gave a response rate
of 82% and median progression-free survival of 13.3 months. Somatic BRCA1 or BRCA2 alterations
gave 50% and 6.3 months. No responses occurred with ATM or CHEK2 alterations alone [@tung2020].

Platinum belongs in this section because it exploits the same defect. That shared mechanism also
predicts cross-resistance, and the prediction holds. Secondary intragenic mutations that restore
the BRCA2 reading frame confer resistance both to platinum and to PARP inhibition [@sakai2008].
The full account is in [[BS-3010]].

```caution
A homologous recombination deficiency score records past genomic instability. Scars persist
after repair function returns, so a tumour can score [[term:hrd]] high while repairing normally
at the moment of testing. The score describes history. The drug needs the present. Repair
biology is in [[BC-180]] and agent detail in [[BC-450]].
```

## BS-5050 Antibody-drug conjugates

Conjugates have moved from the refractory setting to the first line in this subtype within five
years, and the trials should be read as three separate populations rather than as one story.

{{trial:ascent}} established the class in refractory disease. It enrolled 468 patients without
brain metastases who had received at least two prior chemotherapy regimens. Median
progression-free survival with sacituzumab govitecan was 5.6 months against 1.7 months with
single-agent chemotherapy, with a hazard ratio of 0.41. Median overall survival was 12.1 months
against 6.7 months, with a hazard ratio of 0.48. Objective responses occurred in 35% against 5%
[@bardia2021ascent]. The comparator arm result is the striking number.

{{trial:ascent03}} moved the same agent to the first line in patients who were not candidates
for PD-1 or PD-L1 inhibitors. Among 558 patients, median progression-free survival was 9.7
months against 6.9 months with chemotherapy, with a hazard ratio of 0.62. Objective responses
were similar at 48% against 46%, while median duration of response was 12.2 months against 7.2
months [@cortes2025ascent03].

{{trial:tropion-b02}} tested datopotamab deruxtecan in the same first-line
immunotherapy-ineligible population. Among 644 patients, median progression-free survival was
10.8 months against 5.6 months with chemotherapy, with a hazard ratio of 0.57. Median overall
survival was 23.7 months against 18.7 months, with a hazard ratio of 0.79 and a 95.01%
confidence interval of 0.64 to 0.98 [@dent2026tropionb02].

```caution
The first-line conjugate trials differ in what they have shown. {{trial:tropion-b02}} reports a
significant overall survival result [@dent2026tropionb02]. {{trial:ascent03}} reports progression-free
survival with survival not yet mature, and a published critique notes that crossover-adjusted
survival analysis is needed before first-line sacituzumab govitecan is treated as settled
[@poggio2026]. The two are frequently presented as equivalent first-line options. They are
equivalent in indication and not in the strength of what has been demonstrated.
```

```interplay target=BC-350
Triple-negative disease is where the heterogeneity argument of Part VI meets a category built
from three negative results. Trop-2 is expressed across most of these tumours and its level does
not select patients [@bardia2021ascentbio], while HER2 at immunohistochemistry 0 still supports
conjugate responses [@mosele2023]. Both findings point the same way. Antigen abundance is not
the variable governing conjugate benefit at the low end of the range, and a subtype defined by
receptor absence is not thereby a subtype without targets. What varies within these tumours is
not only how much antigen is present but how the payload is handled once delivered, and no
routine assay reports that.
```

## BS-5060 Chemotherapy sequencing and realistic expectations

Chemotherapy still carries most of the treatment time in this disease, and the expectations
attached to it should be stated rather than implied.

The honest baseline comes from the control arms. In refractory disease, single-agent
chemotherapy produced a median progression-free survival of 1.7 months and an objective response
rate of 5% [@bardia2021ascent]. In the first line without immunotherapy, it produced a median
progression-free survival of 6.9 months [@cortes2025ascent03]. The difference between those two
numbers is the effect of line, not of agent.

Three principles follow.

Sequential single agents are preferred to combinations outside a situation requiring immediate
response. Combinations raise response rate and toxicity without a consistent survival gain.

Platinum has a specific indication in germline BRCA-altered disease and no general superiority
otherwise [[BS-5040]].

Response duration shortens with each line, and the shortening is steeper here than in hormone
receptor positive disease. A regimen that works in the fourth line is more often working for
weeks than for months.

Agent-level detail belongs to [[BC-440]]. The point specific to this chapter is that expectation
setting is a clinical act, and the numbers above are the ones to set it with.

## BS-5070 Androgen receptor and other subtype-directed targets

Transcriptional subtyping divides triple-negative disease into groups with different biology,
including a luminal androgen receptor group [@lehmann2011]. The clinical question is whether any
of those groups has a therapy attached. Mostly they do not.

Androgen receptor directed therapy is the best tested and the results are modest. A phase II study
gave enzalutamide to 118 patients with advanced androgen receptor expressing triple-negative
disease. The clinical benefit rate at 16 weeks was 25% in the whole enrolled population. It was
33% in the 78 patients whose tumours expressed androgen receptor in at least 10% of nuclei. Median
progression-free survival was 2.9 months in the whole population and 3.3 months in the evaluable
subgroup [@traina2018].

That is activity and it is not a treatment. No androgen receptor directed agent is approved in
this setting, and the immunohistochemical threshold used to define eligibility was set within
the study rather than validated across observers.

The general lesson is worth stating because it recurs. A transcriptional subtype is a research
classification. Converting it into an eligibility criterion requires an assay that ordinary
laboratories can run reproducibly, and the gap between those two things is the same gap
described for HER2 scoring in [[BS-2360]]. Trial design consequences are in [[BC-740]].

Immune-directed and repair-directed options are covered in [[BS-5030]] and [[BS-5040]].
Everything else in this subtype remains investigational, and saying so is more useful than
listing targets without evidence.

## BS-5080 Rapidly progressive and refractory disease

This subtype produces the fastest clinical deterioration in breast cancer, and the interval
between a decision and its consequences is short.

Two practical features follow from that. The first is that a line of therapy must be assessed
early. Waiting twelve weeks for a scan in a patient deteriorating over three is a decision to
spend the remaining time on a failing regimen. The second is that performance status falls
faster than in other subtypes, so eligibility for anything, including trials, is lost quickly.

The judgment that is hardest and most important is when not to treat. The evidence for it is
indirect and consistent. Response rates in the refractory setting are low, at 5% for single-agent
chemotherapy in {{trial:ascent}} [@bardia2021ascent]. A patient with declining performance
status has a low probability of benefit and a high probability of toxicity, and the toxicity
arrives first.

```practice
Set a reassessment interval that matches the tempo of the disease, not the convention of the
department. In rapidly progressive disease that is often six weeks.

Decide in advance what would make you stop, and say it out loud before starting. A named
stopping rule is easier to act on than a judgment made in the room.

Involve palliative care in parallel with active treatment rather than after it
[@temel2010]. The referral trigger that works is disease status, not exhaustion of options
[[BC-680]].

Performance status is the variable that closes doors. Record it at every visit, because the
trial that is open today will not be open at the next decline.
```

## BS-5090 The unmet need after conjugate failure

The population that defines this disease has moved. Five years ago the unmet need was after
chemotherapy. It is now after conjugates, and the evidence base behind that position is
retrospective.

The relevant data are the sequential conjugate series. Among 85 patients treated with both
trastuzumab deruxtecan and sacituzumab govitecan, progression-free survival on the second
conjugate was shorter than on the first in 75.2%, with 14 of the 85 still on treatment at the data
cutoff [@mai2025]. Longer progression-free survival on the first conjugate predicted longer
survival on the second, which is the signature of partial
rather than complete cross-resistance. A second conjugate is therefore a reasonable option with
a smaller expected magnitude.

What makes the position difficult is that three of the payloads now in routine use are
topoisomerase-I inhibitors, and one of the standard salvage chemotherapies, irinotecan, shares
that mechanism. A patient who has received sacituzumab govitecan, datopotamab deruxtecan and
trastuzumab deruxtecan has had three exposures to the same payload class delivered by three
different antibodies. That is not three lines of non-cross-resistant therapy, whatever the line
count says.

The mechanisms that are not antigen loss remain unmeasurable in clinic. PTEN loss was associated
with de novo resistance to trastuzumab deruxtecan and not to sacituzumab govitecan despite the
shared payload class [@mai2025], which shows that the relevant variable is handling rather than
target. The full taxonomy is in [[BS-3000]].

The honest summary is that this is now the largest gap in the management of the disease. Trial
enrolment is the most useful thing on offer, and it should be discussed at the start of the
post-conjugate line rather than after it fails [[BS-4800]]. Emerging platforms are in
[[BC-760]] and [[BC-770]].
