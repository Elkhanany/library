## BS-4270 Clinical and genomic risk assessment

Risk in this subtype runs on two axes that are routinely collapsed into one. The anatomic axis is
tumour size and nodal burden. The biological axis is grade, proliferation and the output of a
multigene assay. They correlate weakly and they answer different questions.

This chapter is about how to approach the patient. It covers what to measure, what to give, to
whom, for how long, and what to do once treatment stops. The randomised trial record it draws on
lives next door and is not repeated here. Adjuvant endocrine therapy is in [[BC-790]]. Adjuvant
CDK4/6 inhibition is in [[BC-800]]. Preoperative systemic therapy in this subtype is in
[[BC-810]]. Adjuvant PARP inhibition is in [[BS-7230]]. Where a decision below rests on one of
those records, the text points forward rather than restating it.

The two axes are not academic, because they have been used to define eligibility for different
treatments. {{trial:monarche}} enrolled on clinicopathological criteria [@johnston2020].
{{trial:natalee}} enrolled on anatomic stage instead [@hortobagyi2025]. Neither used a genomic
assay to select. The population column of the table in [[BS-4320]] is where the difference is
legible.

The anatomic axis also does most of the work in predicting late events. Size and nodal status
still stratify distant recurrence risk between years 5 and 20. The figures are in [[BS-4350]]
[@pan2017]. Nothing in routine use has superseded them.

Two measurement cautions belong with all of this. Ki-67 is the least reproducible of the routine
variables, and the scoring conventions that make it usable are set out in [@dowsett2011] and
[@nielsen2021]. And a genomic assay reports a risk estimate conditioned on the treatment the
validation cohort received, which is developed in [[BC-380]]. Tools that combine the recurrence
score with clinicopathological features report a different quantity again [@sparano2021rsclin].

## BS-4280 Selection of endocrine therapy by menopausal status

Menopausal status is not a patient characteristic in this decision. It is a statement about where
oestrogen is being made, and therefore about which blockade will work.

In postmenopausal women an aromatase inhibitor outperforms tamoxifen. A patient-level
meta-analysis of 31,920 women found that recurrence rate ratios favoured aromatase inhibitors
while the treatments differed, at 0.70 with a 95% confidence interval of 0.64 to 0.77, and not
significantly thereafter. Ten-year breast cancer mortality across all comparisons was reduced,
with a rate ratio of 0.86 and an interval of 0.80 to 0.94 [@ebctcg2015ai]. The same analysis
priced the trade. Ten-year endometrial cancer incidence was 0.4% against 1.2% with tamoxifen, and
five-year bone fracture risk was 8.2% against 5.5%.

In premenopausal women the ovary is the dominant source, so an aromatase inhibitor alone does not
work. It requires ovarian suppression, and the comparison then changes character. A patient-level
meta-analysis of 7,030 women receiving ovarian suppression found that an aromatase inhibitor
reduced breast cancer recurrence against tamoxifen, with a rate ratio of 0.79 and an interval of
0.69 to 0.90. The absolute reduction in five-year recurrence risk was 3.2 percentage points, from
10.1% to 6.9%. Breast cancer mortality did not differ, with a rate ratio of 1.01 and an interval
of 0.82 to 1.24. Fractures were more common, at 6.4% against 5.1% [@ebctcg2022ofs].

The randomised trials underneath both estimates are tabulated in [[BC-790]], where the agent
question and the duration question are separated. The two estimates do not have the same shape.
Breast cancer mortality fell alongside recurrence in the postmenopausal comparison and did not
under ovarian suppression. A named toxicity rises in both. The agents themselves are covered in
[[BC-430]].

## BS-4290 Ovarian function suppression and who benefits

Ovarian suppression is the clearest example in early breast cancer of a treatment whose absolute
benefit is concentrated in a minority of the people offered it.

```evidence setting=early subtype=HR+/HER2- line=adjuvant modality=endocrine topic=ovarian-suppression sort=year cols=acronym,n,population,arms,result,os caption=The_randomised_trials_of_ovarian_suppression._Read_the_experimental-versus-control_column_first,_because_these_four_ask_three_different_questions.
```

Four rows, three questions. Only two of these trials randomised suppression at all.
{{trial:soft}} set tamoxifen plus suppression, and exemestane plus suppression, against tamoxifen
alone. {{trial:astrra}} set two years of goserelin added to tamoxifen against tamoxifen alone.
{{trial:soft-text}} and {{trial:abcsg12}} gave a gonadotropin-releasing hormone agonist to
everybody and randomised the partner drug instead.

Where the four agree is narrower than it looks. Adding ovarian suppression to tamoxifen lowers
recurrence in premenopausal hormone receptor-positive disease, and in {{trial:soft}} and
{{trial:astrra}} it also moved overall survival. Upgrading the partner drug on top of suppression
is a separate claim. The two trials that tested that upgrade disagree on recurrence, and the one
that reports overall survival for the comparison finds no difference. The trial-level argument
across all four is made in [[BS-6770]].

The apparent disagreement is between {{trial:soft-text}} and {{trial:abcsg12}}, and it is not a
disagreement about aromatase inhibitors. Exemestane beat tamoxifen on disease-free survival in
the joint SOFT and TEXT analysis. Anastrozole did not beat tamoxifen in ABCSG-12. Four things
differ besides the drug. ABCSG-12 treated for three years and SOFT and TEXT for five. ABCSG-12
enrolled a largely chemotherapy-naive and lower-risk population. Its primary report came before
most late events in this subtype have occurred [@gnant2009]. And its final analysis was powered
around a second randomisation, to zoledronic acid, which the row reports and which belongs to the
adjuvant bisphosphonate argument rather than to this one [@gnant2014]. Reading the two rows as a
verdict on anastrozole against exemestane is the error the arms column exists to prevent.

{{trial:astrra}} diverges by population rather than by design. It enrolled women aged 45 or
younger who had already had chemotherapy and were confirmed still premenopausal on
follicle-stimulating hormone and bleeding history at six-monthly intervals [@kim2020astrra]. That
is a prospective enrichment of precisely the {{trial:soft}} stratum in which suppression looked
worthwhile. Its agreement with SOFT is therefore close to circular, and its effect size should not
be transported to a woman selected any other way. What it does add is that the effect survives
being looked for on purpose, and it holds at eight years [@baek2023].

The decision rests on {{trial:soft}}. It randomised suppression against no suppression in a
premenopausal population that was not pre-selected on recovered ovarian function. That is the
population a clinic actually faces, which is what makes the row load-bearing.

The subgroups are where the decision lives, and the table carries none of them. In HER2-negative
disease, which was 86.0% of the joint population, the 12-year overall survival advantage of
exemestane with suppression was 2.0 percentage points. It was 3.3 points in the women who had
received chemotherapy [@pagani2023]. The gain tracks baseline risk rather than the drug, so a
single average for this comparison is not usable in clinic.

One inversion inside {{trial:soft}} is worth more attention than it gets. Exemestane with
suppression gave the best eight-year disease-free survival of the three arms and did not give the
best eight-year overall survival [@francis2018]. The arm that beat tamoxifen alone on survival was
tamoxifen with suppression. The trial was not powered to rank the two suppression arms against
each other on survival. Read the recurrence ordering as a survival ordering and you are reading
something the design did not test. Final outcomes from both trials are now on the record
[@francis2026].

Three things nobody randomised are missing from every row. Duration of suppression is one, at two
years in ASTRRA, three in ABCSG-12 and five in SOFT and TEXT, never varied inside a trial. Method
is the second. ABCSG-12 and ASTRRA specified a gonadotropin-releasing hormone agonist, and no
trial here set one method of suppression against another, so oophorectomy and radiation ablation
are inferred rather than tested. The third is confirmation of suppression.
ASTRRA measured ovarian function repeatedly and the others did not, which means the treatment
being compared is biochemically the same drug and not reliably the same state.

```practice
Suppression is a risk-stratified decision, not a menopausal-status decision. Receipt of
chemotherapy marks the subgroup with the larger twelve-year survival gain [@pagani2023].

In a woman with a small, grade 1, node-negative tumour the absolute gain sits at the bottom of
that range and the toxicity is not proportionally smaller. Quote the absolute difference before
prescribing rather than the hazard ratio.

Remaining premenopausal after chemotherapy is itself a selection marker, because it identifies the
women in whom ovarian function was never removed [@francis2018].

If suppression is used, the aromatase inhibitor is the better partner on recurrence and has not
been shown to be better on survival [@ebctcg2022ofs; @pagani2023]. Fractures rise, so bone management
starts at the same visit.
```

## BS-4300 Genomic assays and the decision to omit chemotherapy

```story SQ-0040,SQ-0050
```

This is the chapter's own question, in the two forms the trials were built to answer. The first
asks whether a score measured once before treatment can allocate chemotherapy. The second asks
whether the tumour's behaviour under a short course of endocrine therapy allocates it better.

The predictive claim came before any of the randomised trials. It was made retrospectively, inside
three trials designed to answer something else.

```evidence setting=early subtype=HR+/HER2- line=adjuvant topic=recurrence-score-cohort sort=year cols=acronym,n,population,arms,result caption=The_archival_cohorts_in_which_the_21-gene_assay's_predictive_claim_was_first_made._Each_row_is_a_randomised_trial_repurposed,_not_a_trial_of_the_assay.
```

Each of the three contributed a different piece, and none of them was designed to. The randomised
comparison in {{trial:nsabp-b-14}} was tamoxifen against placebo, so the assay work used one arm
of it and could show only that the score is prognostic under tamoxifen [@paik2004].
{{trial:nsabp-b-20}} randomised chemotherapy added to tamoxifen, so it could ask about an
interaction, and reported one [@paik2006]. {{trial:swog-8814-int-0100}} carried the same structure
into postmenopausal node-positive disease with an anthracycline regimen [@albain2010]. Prognostic
and predictive are different claims, and only the second and third rows were built in a way that
could support the predictive one.

What all three share matters more than what separates them. Each measured the score in banked
blocks from a minority of the enrolled patients, and tissue availability is not a random event.
Each conditioned the score on tamoxifen alone as the endocrine backbone, because that was the
treatment of the era. Nobody has recalibrated the assay against an aromatase inhibitor or against
tamoxifen with ovarian suppression, which is what the patient in front of you will receive. That
is a measurement problem rather than a biological one, and it propagates into every prospective
trial below, all of which inherited the thresholds.

```evidence setting=early subtype=HR+/HER2- line=adjuvant topic=genomic-assay cols=acronym,n,population,arms,endpoint,result,os,status caption=The_prospective_trials_that_tested_assay-guided_and_response-guided_chemotherapy_omission.
```

Four reported trials and one still running, and they converge on one finding. In node-negative or
limited node-positive hormone receptor-positive disease, a large fraction of patients can be
treated with endocrine therapy alone without a measurable loss of distant disease control over the
follow-up so far accrued. That is the finding. Everything else in the table is a difference in how
it was established.

Those differences are design, population and endpoint, not assay performance.
{{trial:tailorx}} randomised chemotherapy against no chemotherapy inside a midrange score band in
node-negative disease, and was powered as a non-inferiority comparison [@sparano2018].
{{trial:mindact}} randomised only the patients whose clinical and genomic risk disagreed, and its
primary question was a single-arm rate tested against a fixed boundary, with the randomised
comparison secondary [@cardoso2016]. {{trial:rxponder}} moved to one to three positive nodes and
capped the score at 25 [@kalinsky2021]. {{trial:wsg-adapt-hr-her2}} compared two prospectively
defined groups rather than two randomised ones, because allocation followed biology
[@nitz2022]. The assays differ too, with a 70-gene signature in MINDACT and a 21-gene score
elsewhere, on thresholds that are not interchangeable. And the endpoint differs, because a distant
metastasis-free survival endpoint and an invasive disease-free survival endpoint do not count the
same events. The second counts second primaries and non-breast deaths, which dilutes a
chemotherapy effect in an older population and inflates it in none.

The decision in node-negative disease rests on {{trial:tailorx}}. It is the only row that
randomised the thing a clinic would otherwise do against the thing it would otherwise omit. It did
so in the population where the question is actually asked, with enough events to bound the
difference.

{{trial:rxponder}} is the one that did not settle its question, and the reason sits in the
interaction rather than in either arm. Chemotherapy benefit differed by menopausal status, with an
interaction p value of 0.008 [@kalinsky2021]. The two strata point in opposite directions. A
single hazard ratio for that trial would describe nobody. One sentence in that report matters more
than the headline. The relative chemotherapy benefit did not increase as the recurrence score
increased. In node-positive premenopausal women the assay identified who was at risk. It did not
identify who benefited from chemotherapy.

Read alongside {{trial:tailorx}}, where the residual chemotherapy signal also sat in women aged 50
or younger, that is the same finding twice. {{trial:mindact}} looked in the same place, reporting
an exploratory analysis by age in its update [@piccart2021mindact]. Age and menopausal status
travel together, and no trial in this table randomised ovarian suppression. The benefit attributed
to chemotherapy in young women cannot be separated from the benefit of chemotherapy-induced
menopause.

{{trial:wsg-adapt-hr-her2}} answers the second question and answers it in a form that needs care.
Three weeks of preoperative endocrine therapy separated the population, with central Ki-67 read
before and after. The endocrine-responsive midrange group then did as well on endocrine therapy
alone as the low-score control group [@nitz2022]. The comparison was between two arms
defined by the marker, so nobody with an endocrine-responsive midrange tumour was treated the
other way. The response rate also depends on which drug was given, at 78.1% on an aromatase
inhibitor against 41.1% on tamoxifen. Endocrine response as measured here is a property of the
test as much as of the tumour.

{{trial:adaptcycle}} is still running. It asks whether ribociclib with an aromatase inhibitor can
take the place of the chemotherapy that a recurrence score and an endocrine response test would
otherwise mandate. That is the only substitution question in the table. Its reported concordance
work is the part to read now. Local and central Ki-67 disagreed on the endocrine-response call
often enough that 13.1% of patients called responders locally were non-responders centrally
[@hamann2025]. A response-guided strategy is only as portable as the assay that defines response.

All five rows should be read alongside the non-inferiority methods in [[BC-740]] and specifically
[[BS-6370]]. Three things the table cannot give you are worth naming. Nobody randomised assay use
against no assay use, so the value of testing itself is inferred from the outcomes of the tested.
Nobody enrolled four or more positive nodes into an omission question. And the overall survival
column is where the gap shows, because the node-positive trial has not reported it and this
subtype recurs for twenty years [[BS-4350]].

```caution
A trial that declares endocrine therapy non-inferior has not shown that chemotherapy does nothing.
It has shown that the difference is smaller than a margin chosen in advance. The {{trial:mindact}}
intention-to-treat comparison makes the point concretely, because its eight-year distant
metastasis-free survival favoured chemotherapy inside a design whose primary question was a
single-arm rate against a fixed boundary [@piccart2021mindact]. The general form of this error is
set out in [[BS-6370]].
```

```caution
The premenopausal {{trial:rxponder}} result is widely read as showing that chemotherapy works in young
node-positive women with low genomic risk. The flat relationship between score and benefit is
compatible with a different reading, which is that some of what chemotherapy delivered was ovarian
suppression [@kalinsky2021]. The trial did not randomise suppression, so it cannot separate the
two. A patient offered chemotherapy on this evidence should be told that ovarian suppression is
the alternative hypothesis, not a consolation prize.
```

## BS-4310 Chemotherapy selection when it is indicated

Once chemotherapy is chosen, the remaining questions are which regimen and on what schedule. The
pharmacology of each agent belongs to [[BC-440]].

```evidence setting=early subtype=HR+/HER2- line=adjuvant topic=adjuvant-backbone sort=year cols=acronym,n,population,arms,result,os caption=The_randomised_trials_that_built_the_adjuvant_backbone._Three_questions_are_interleaved_here,_and_the_experimental-versus-control_column_is_what_separates_them.
```

The eight trials answer three questions that are habitually read as one, and they agree on one
thing across all three. Every increment here is a few percentage points of disease-free survival,
and none of the modern comparisons found a regimen that was clearly wrong.

Whether a taxane should follow an anthracycline was asked by {{trial:calgb-9344}} and
{{trial:nsabp-b-28}}. Both found the same relative reduction in disease-free survival events and
diverged on survival, which CALGB 9344 improved and NSABP B-28 did not [@henderson2003;
@mamounas2005]. Nothing about the taxane differs between them. CALGB 9344 also randomised three
doxorubicin doses and found no dose effect, which is the more durable result in that row. Its
unplanned subgroup analysis put the recurrence hazard ratio at 0.72 in oestrogen receptor-negative
and 0.91 in receptor-positive tumours [@henderson2003]. That single comparison does more to
explain the position of taxanes in this subtype than either trial's headline.

Inside that same question sits the choice of taxane and schedule, which {{trial:ecog-e1199}} asked
directly. The answer is a pairing rather than a class. Weekly paclitaxel beat three-weekly paclitaxel on disease-free and overall
survival. Docetaxel every three weeks also beat three-weekly paclitaxel on disease-free survival.
Weekly docetaxel did not [@sparano2008]. A trial with four arms is the only design that could have
shown that. Its price was grade 2 to 4 neuropathy in 27% of the weekly paclitaxel arm against 20%
of the control.

Whether the interval between cycles carries an independent effect was asked by {{trial:calgb9741}}
and {{trial:gim2}}, twelve years apart, on different backbones. Both are positive on disease-free
and overall survival. Both also carried a second randomisation that added nothing, to concurrent
against sequential scheduling in CALGB 9741 and to fluorouracil in GIM2 [@citron2003;
@delmastro2015]. The schedule effect is the only claim in this table reproduced on two independent
backbones, which is why it is more secure than any single agent addition.

Whether the anthracycline can come out altogether was asked by {{trial:us-oncology-9735}},
{{trial:abc-trials}} and {{trial:wsg-planb}}. Those three appear to disagree and largely do not,
because they did not use the same control arm. {{trial:us-oncology-9735}} compared four cycles of
docetaxel and cyclophosphamide with four cycles of doxorubicin and cyclophosphamide, which is an
anthracycline regimen containing no taxane [@jones2009]. It is evidence that a taxane doublet beats
an old doublet. It is not evidence about the modern comparator.

{{trial:abc-trials}} and {{trial:wsg-planb}} both used that modern comparator, setting six cycles
of docetaxel and cyclophosphamide against a taxane and anthracycline sequence. Both returned small
differences, and the two point estimates do not favour the same arm. Their verdicts differ because
their margins were set on different scales. ABC set an inferiority boundary on the hazard ratio at 1.18
and returned 1.202, so non-inferiority could not be declared [@blum2017]. PlanB set a margin of
4.4 percentage points of disease-free survival and cleared it [@nitz2019]. The trials also differ
in follow-up at the time of the verdict and in how their randomised populations were assembled,
because PlanB removed its lowest genomic risk stratum from randomisation altogether. ABC has since
reported with longer follow-up [@geyer2024]. Read the margin before reading the conclusion.

Two structural points are specific to this subtype, and one of them is about the table itself. Six
of these eight trials enrolled without regard to receptor status, so they appear here on the
all-comers wildcard rather than because they enrolled this population. Their estimates are
averages over a population in which hormone receptor-positive disease was the part least likely to
benefit. The absolute gain from anthracycline-containing over taxane-based regimens is small in
endocrine-responsive disease, and the trial-level comparisons that establish it are in
[@ebctcg2012]. Schedule, by contrast, carries an independent effect established across 37,298
women in 26 trials [@ebctcg2019].

The practical consequence is that dose density is available without adding an agent. In a subtype
where the marginal benefit of chemotherapy is contested, changing the schedule is a cheaper lever
than changing the drugs.

## BS-4320 Adjuvant CDK4/6 inhibition, patient selection and open questions

```evidence setting=early subtype=HR+/HER2- line=adjuvant,post-neoadjuvant modality=cdk4-6 sort=year cols=acronym,n,population,arms,result,os,status caption=The_four_reported_adjuvant_CDK4/6_trials_and_the_one_still_running._The_population_column_carries_most_of_the_difference_between_them.
```

Four reported trials, two positive and two negative, and one still running that asks a different
question. Treating them as a single class question is the most consequential error available in
this chapter.

The agreement is thin and should be said plainly. All four reported trials added a CDK4/6
inhibitor to adjuvant endocrine therapy, used invasive disease-free survival as the primary
endpoint, and were large enough to detect the effect they were designed for. {{trial:monarche}}
and {{trial:natalee}} separated on that endpoint. {{trial:pallas}} and {{trial:penelope-b}} did
not, and neither has separated on survival with longer follow-up [@mayer2025; @loibl2025].

The two negative trials used palbociclib, which is the observation that drives the class-effect
reading. It is not the only thing that differs. Duration runs from one year in
{{trial:penelope-b}} through two in {{trial:monarche}} and {{trial:pallas}} to three in
{{trial:natalee}}. Schedule differs between abemaciclib given continuously twice daily, ribociclib
at a reduced dose three weeks on and one week off, and palbociclib for three weeks in four. No
trial varies one of those while holding the others fixed.

Population is where the split is usually explained, and the explanation does not survive contact
with the table. {{trial:pallas}} enrolled stage II to III disease with no high-risk qualifier,
which is a lower-risk population than {{trial:monarche}} required [@gnant2022]. {{trial:penelope-b}}
enrolled residual invasive disease after taxane-containing neoadjuvant chemotherapy with a CPS-EG
score of 3 or more, which is a higher-risk population than either positive trial enrolled
[@loibl2021penelopeb]. The two failures therefore bracket the two successes on risk rather than
sitting below them. A population account has to explain both ends and explains neither.

{{trial:penelope-b}} also differs on an axis the filter has to name explicitly. It is a
post-neoadjuvant trial, so its patients had already demonstrated chemotherapy resistance in vivo.
That is a biologically different starting point from an adjuvant population selected on pathology
alone. It is the one difference in this table that is about the tumour rather than about the
protocol.

The decision rests on {{trial:monarche}}. It is the only one of the five that has shown an overall
survival advantage [@johnston2026os], and its entry criteria are written in variables a pathology
report already contains. {{trial:natalee}} is load-bearing for the wider stage II population and
for node-negative disease, on recurrence alone. It has reported at successive data cuts with
different amounts of follow-up, so its absolute rates are not comparable across reports. The
hazard ratio for an invasive disease-free survival event is the quantity that has stayed stable
across them [@slamon2024; @hortobagyi2025; @fasching2025; @crown2025].

{{trial:adaptcycle}} is in this table because of its modality and belongs to the argument in
[[BS-4300]]. It is the only row asking whether a CDK4/6 inhibitor can replace chemotherapy rather
than follow it.

Two things the table cannot show. The result column reports different timepoints in every row, so
reading down it compares three-year rates with four-year and seven-year rates. And no patient has
ever been randomised between abemaciclib and ribociclib in this setting, which is the comparison a
clinic makes weekly. [[BC-800]] does the axis-by-axis comparison. [[BS-6860]] sets the outcomes
side by side.

```caution
Class effect is the word doing the damage here. The three agents have different target selectivity,
different schedules and different tolerability, and the two positive trials used the two agents
that were not palbociclib. That is consistent with a drug difference. It is equally consistent with
a duration difference, an adherence difference or a population difference. Reading a positive
result for abemaciclib as support for palbociclib runs against two randomised trials that looked
for exactly that and did not find it [@gnant2022; @loibl2021penelopeb].
```

```practice
Use the agent in the population in which it was tested, for the duration for which it was tested.
That is the only reading these four trials support.

For node-positive high-risk disease meeting the {{trial:monarche}} criteria, two years of
abemaciclib buys an absolute overall survival difference of 1.8 percentage points at seven years,
alongside a 6.5-point recurrence difference [@johnston2026os]. Quote both absolute numbers rather
than the hazard ratios, which are what a patient will otherwise hear.

For stage II to III disease including selected node-negative tumours, three years of ribociclib
has a recurrence benefit with immature survival data [@crown2025]. Say that the survival question
is open rather than implying it is answered.

Where a patient meets both sets of criteria there is no randomised comparison. Choose on
tolerability, duration and what the patient will take, and say that is the basis.

Do not substitute palbociclib for either agent on grounds of access or familiarity. In the
adjuvant setting it has been tested twice and has failed twice [@gnant2022; @loibl2021penelopeb].
```

## BS-4330 Adjuvant PARP inhibition for germline carriers

```evidence setting=early subtype=BRCA line=adjuvant modality=parp cols=acronym,n,population,arms,endpoint,result,os caption=The_only_randomised_adjuvant_PARP_inhibitor_trial_in_early_breast_cancer._Its_population_spans_two_subtypes_with_different_entry_criteria.
```

One row, and the population cell is the part to read twice. {{trial:olympia}} enrolled both
hormone receptor-positive and triple-negative disease, with risk criteria that differed between
the two [@tutt2021]. A hormone receptor-positive carrier entered on stage and nodal count. A
triple-negative carrier usually entered on residual disease after neoadjuvant chemotherapy. The
trial was not designed to separate them, so the transferable claim is about germline carriers at
high risk rather than about either subtype on its own. The triple-negative half of the same
argument is in [[BS-4500]]. Invasive disease-free survival separated first and overall survival
followed [@geyer2022].

Two observations matter for the consent conversation and neither is in the table. The separation
was in distant disease-free survival as well as in invasive disease-free survival, at 87.5%
against 80.4% at three years [@tutt2021]. The effect is therefore not carried by second primaries
and non-breast events. And olaparib was added after chemotherapy rather than in place of it, so a
carrier who declines chemotherapy sits outside this evidence [@tutt2021].

A single row also means a single set of choices that nobody varied. Duration was one year in
everyone. The comparator was placebo, not another agent. Eligibility required a germline BRCA1 or
BRCA2 variant, so carriers of PALB2 and of other homologous recombination genes are outside the
evidence entirely. The mechanism is developed in [[BC-180]] and the agents in [[BC-450]]. Germline
testing should precede the adjuvant decision rather than follow it, and the testing strategy is in
[[BC-070]].

```caution
Sequencing olaparib with adjuvant CDK4/6 inhibition in a dually eligible patient is unresolved and
should be presented that way. {{trial:olympia}} did not permit a CDK4/6 inhibitor and {{trial:monarche}} did not
require germline testing, so neither trial observed the combination or the sequence. Choosing one,
choosing the other, or giving them in series are three strategies with no comparative evidence
behind any of them. A patient who is offered a definite order should be told that the order is a
judgement.
```

## BS-4340 Extended endocrine therapy and duration

The case for extending endocrine therapy rests on the residual hazard quantified in [[BS-4350]].
The case against rests on toxicity and on adherence. A patient-level meta-analysis of 12 trials in
22,031 postmenopausal women who had already completed at least five years of endocrine therapy now
prices both sides [@ebctcg2025]. The individual trials underneath it are tabulated in [[BS-6780]].

Allocation to further aromatase inhibition reduced recurrence, with a rate ratio of 0.73 and a 95%
confidence interval of 0.67 to 0.80 [@ebctcg2025].

The subset that matters most in current practice is the one already treated with an aromatase
inhibitor. There, five further years reduced recurrence with a rate ratio of 0.71, taking the risk
from year 5 to year 15 from 15.2% to 11.6%. Distant recurrence fell with a rate ratio of 0.73,
from 8.6% to 6.6%. Breast cancer mortality fell non-significantly, with a rate ratio of 0.90, an
interval of 0.70 to 1.15 and a p value of 0.40, from 5.0% to 4.4% [@ebctcg2025].

Because the proportional reduction was similar across tumour characteristics, the absolute benefit
scales with baseline risk. Ten years against five years took node-positive risk from 20.1% to
16.3% and node-negative risk from 11.8% to 9.1% [@ebctcg2025]. Five-year fracture risk rose with a
rate ratio of 1.35, from 3.4% to 4.6%.

```practice
Extended therapy is a recurrence decision with an unproven mortality effect. Present it that way
rather than as a survival intervention [@ebctcg2025].

Anchor the offer to nodal status, because the proportional benefit is flat and the absolute
benefit is not. Quote the node-positive and node-negative recurrence risks between years 5 and 15
rather than the rate ratio [@ebctcg2025].

Put the fracture risk in the same sentence as the recurrence risk. The same analysis prices both,
and the five-year fracture rate is what a patient will weigh against the recurrence she cannot
see [@ebctcg2025].

A patient who has tolerated five years badly is unlikely to tolerate ten. Non-adherence in these
placebo-controlled trials ran at 39.0% on further treatment and 37.6% on placebo [@ebctcg2025],
which is the honest baseline for what extension means in practice.
```

## BS-4350 Late recurrence risk and surveillance implications

This subtype recurs on a timescale that outlasts most follow-up schedules and most trials. The
annual hazard of recurrence does not fall to zero after five years, and the pattern across 24
years of follow-up is set out in [@colleoni2016] and [@saphner1996].

The size of that residual risk is now precisely quantified. Among 62,923 women with oestrogen
receptor-positive disease who were disease-free after five years of scheduled endocrine therapy,
recurrences occurred at a steady rate from year 5 to year 20. Distant recurrence risk over that
window was 13% for T1N0 disease, 20% for T1N1-3, 34% for T1N4-9, 19% for T2N0, 26% for T2N1-3 and
41% for T2N4-9 [@pan2017].

Grade added moderately within a stage stratum. For T1N0 disease the risk over years 5 to 20 was
10% for low-grade, 13% for moderate-grade and 17% for high-grade tumours [@pan2017]. Progesterone
receptor and HER2 status were not predictive of late distant recurrence in that dataset.

Two implications follow, and only one of them is comfortable.

The comfortable one is that the original pathology report retains predictive power for two
decades. Size and nodal status are not superseded by anything currently in routine use.

The uncomfortable one is that no surveillance strategy has been shown to convert that knowledge
into benefit. Detecting asymptomatic distant recurrence earlier has not improved survival, and the
evidence against surveillance intensity is in [[BC-650]]. Circulating tumour DNA detects molecular
relapse before imaging does, and whether acting on that lead time helps is unresolved and is the
subject of [[BC-390]]. Late recurrence is a dormancy problem, and its biology is in [[BC-330]].

```interplay target=BC-350
Heterogeneity in this subtype is mostly temporal rather than spatial, which is why the chapter
reads as a series of duration decisions. The adjuvant setting applies years of selective pressure
to a population that cannot be sampled, and what emerges at year eight has been shaped by that
pressure exactly as the metastatic sequences in [[BS-2490]] are. Extended endocrine therapy and
adjuvant CDK4/6 inhibition are not simply longer treatment. They are longer selection, and the
ESR1 and RB1 escape routes that define the metastatic setting [[BS-2500]] are being selected for
during the years in which the patient is called disease-free.
```

## BS-4360 Adherence and long-term tolerability

Every hazard ratio in this chapter is an intention-to-treat estimate over a population in which a
large minority stopped taking the drug. That is not a footnote to the effect size. It is part of
what the effect size means.

In a cohort of 8,769 women with stage I to III hormone-sensitive breast cancer who filled at least
one prescription, 32% had discontinued endocrine therapy by 4.5 years. Among those who continued,
72% were fully adherent by a medication possession ratio of 80% or more. Combining the two, 49%
took adjuvant endocrine therapy for the full duration on the intended schedule. Women under 40 had
the highest discontinuation risk, with a hazard ratio of 1.51 and a 95% confidence interval of
1.23 to 1.85 [@hershman2010].

The randomised evidence agrees. In the placebo-controlled extended-therapy trials, non-adherence
was 39.0% on further aromatase inhibition and 37.6% on placebo [@ebctcg2025]. Almost as many
patients stopped an inert tablet as stopped an active one, which says that the burden is the
regimen as much as the pharmacology.

The toxicities that drive this are known and are quantified elsewhere in the chapter. Fractures
rise with aromatase inhibition in postmenopausal women [@ebctcg2015ai] and with ovarian
suppression in premenopausal women [@ebctcg2022ofs]. Endometrial cancer and thrombosis rise with
tamoxifen [@ebctcg2015ai]. Arthralgia and vasomotor symptoms are the commonest reasons given for
stopping, and the symptom burden is developed in [[BC-430]] and [[BC-650]].

```practice
Ask about adherence at every visit and ask in a form that permits a true answer. Half of patients
do not complete the prescribed course [@hershman2010].

Treat the symptom rather than shortening the course wherever that is possible. A switch between
agents, or a dose reduction with evidence behind it [@lazzeroni2023], keeps a patient on treatment
that stopping does not.

Flag women under 40 as the highest-risk group for discontinuation and follow them accordingly
[@hershman2010].

When quoting a benefit from any trial in this chapter, remember it was achieved under the same
adherence that the patient in front of you will have. The number is not an estimate of perfect
compliance and should not be presented as one.
```
