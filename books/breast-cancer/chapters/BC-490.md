## BS-4270 Clinical and genomic risk assessment

Risk in this subtype runs on two axes that are routinely collapsed into one. The anatomic axis is
tumour size and nodal burden. The biological axis is grade, proliferation and the output of a
multigene assay. They correlate weakly and they answer different questions.

This chapter is about how to approach the patient. It covers what to measure, what to give, to
whom, for how long, and what to do once treatment stops. The randomised trial record it draws on
lives next door and is not repeated here. Adjuvant endocrine therapy is in [[BC-790]]. Adjuvant
CDK4/6 inhibition is in [[BC-800]]. Preoperative systemic therapy in this subtype is in
[[BC-810]]. Adjuvant PARP inhibition is in [[BS-7230]]. Where a decision below rests on one of
those records, the text points forward rather than restating it. The exception is adjuvant
chemotherapy, whose randomised record was built in trials that enrolled every subtype. Those
trials are tabulated once, in [[BS-4310]], and argued there and in [[BC-440]].

The two axes are not academic, because they have been used to define eligibility for different
treatments. {{trial:monarche}} enrolled on clinicopathological criteria [@johnston2020].
{{trial:natalee}} enrolled on anatomic stage instead [@hortobagyi2025]. Only {{trial:natalee}}
accepted a high-risk genomic assay result, and only as one way for grade 2, node-negative stage IIA
disease to qualify [@fasching2025]. The population column of the table in [[BS-6820]] is where the
difference is legible.

The anatomic axis also does most of the work in predicting late events. Size and nodal status
still stratify distant recurrence risk between years 5 and 20. The figures are in [[BS-4350]]
[@pan2017]. Nothing in routine use has superseded them.

Two measurement cautions belong with all of this. Ki-67 is the least reproducible of the routine
variables, and the scoring conventions that make it usable are set out in [@dowsett2011] and
[@nielsen2021]. And a genomic assay reports a risk estimate conditioned on the treatment the
validation cohort received, which is developed in [[BC-380]]. Tools that combine the recurrence
score with clinicopathological features report a different quantity again [@sparano2021rsclin].

## BS-4300 Genomic assays and the decision to omit chemotherapy

```story SQ-0040,SQ-0050,SQ-1050
```

This is the chapter's own question, in the two forms the trials were built to answer. The first
asks whether a score measured once before treatment can allocate chemotherapy. The second asks
whether the tumour's behaviour under a short course of endocrine therapy allocates it better.

The predictive claim came before any of the randomised trials. It was made retrospectively, inside
three trials designed to answer something else.

```evidence setting=early subtype=HR+/HER2- line=adjuvant topic=recurrence-score-cohort sort=year cols=acronym,n,population,arms,result caption=The_archival_cohorts_in_which_the_21-gene_assay's_predictive_claim_was_first_made._Each_row_is_a_randomised_trial_repurposed,_not_a_trial_of_the_assay.
```

Each of the three contributed a different piece, and none of them was designed to. The randomised
comparison in {{trial:nsabp-b-14}} was tamoxifen against placebo, so the assay work used one arm
of it and could show only that the score is prognostic under tamoxifen [@paik2004].
{{trial:nsabp-b-20}} randomised chemotherapy added to tamoxifen, so it could ask about an
interaction, and reported one [@paik2006]. {{trial:swog-8814-int-0100}} carried the same structure
into postmenopausal node-positive disease with an anthracycline regimen [@albain2010]. The parent
trial had randomised chemotherapy followed by tamoxifen against tamoxifen alone, and chemotherapy
given before tamoxifen against the two given together [@albain2009]. Prognostic
and predictive are different claims, and only the second and third rows were built in a way that
could support the predictive one.

What all three share matters more than what separates them. Each measured the score in banked
blocks from a minority of the enrolled patients, and tissue availability is not a random event.
Each conditioned the score on tamoxifen alone as the endocrine backbone, because that was the
treatment of the era. The score was later shown to be prognostic under anastrozole, in node-negative
and node-positive disease, in TransATAC [@dowsett2010b]. That established risk under an aromatase
inhibitor, not chemotherapy benefit. Nobody has recalibrated the chemotherapy thresholds against an
aromatase inhibitor or against tamoxifen with ovarian suppression, which is what the patient in
front of you will receive. That
is a measurement problem rather than a biological one, and it propagates into every prospective
trial below, all of which inherited the thresholds.

The same archival route produced the score's other associations. In node-negative NSABP trials it
was associated with locoregional recurrence as well as distant recurrence [@mamounas2010].
Prognostic estimates were better when the score was combined with clinicopathological information
than when it was combined with the online Adjuvant calculator [@tang2011]. Registry and prospective cohorts then
allocated treatment by the score. In PlanB, 348 patients with a score of 11 or less omitted chemotherapy, and their
three-year disease-free survival was 98% [@gluz2016]. In a registry of node-positive disease with
micrometastases or one to three positive nodes, outcomes with a score below 18 supported endocrine
therapy alone [@stemmer2017].

```evidence setting=early subtype=HR+/HER2- line=adjuvant topic=genomic-assay cols=acronym,n,population,arms,endpoint,result,os,status caption=The_prospective_trials_that_tested_assay-guided_and_response-guided_chemotherapy_omission.
```

Four reported trials and one still running, and they converge on one finding. In node-negative or
limited node-positive hormone receptor-positive disease, a large fraction of patients can be
treated with endocrine therapy alone without a measurable loss of distant disease control over the
follow-up so far accrued. That is the finding. Everything else in the table is a difference in how
it was established.

Those differences are design, population and endpoint, not assay performance.
{{trial:tailorx}} randomised chemotherapy against no chemotherapy inside a midrange score band in
node-negative disease, and was powered as a non-inferiority comparison [@sparano2018].
{{trial:mindact}} randomised only the patients whose clinical and genomic risk disagreed, and its
primary question was a single-arm rate tested against a fixed boundary, with the randomised
comparison secondary [@cardoso2016]. {{trial:rxponder}} moved to one to three positive nodes and
capped the score at 25 [@kalinsky2021]. {{trial:wsg-adapt-hr-her2}} compared two prospectively
defined groups rather than two randomised ones, because allocation followed biology
[@nitz2022]. The assays differ too, with a 70-gene signature in MINDACT and a 21-gene score
elsewhere, on thresholds that are not interchangeable. And the endpoint differs, because a distant
metastasis-free survival endpoint and an invasive disease-free survival endpoint do not count the
same events. The second counts second primaries and non-breast deaths, which dilutes a
chemotherapy effect in an older population and inflates it in none.

The decision in node-negative disease rests on {{trial:tailorx}}. It is the only row that
randomised the thing a clinic would otherwise do against the thing it would otherwise omit. It did
so in the population where the question is actually asked, with enough events to bound the
difference.

{{trial:rxponder}} is the one that did not settle its question, and the reason sits in the
interaction rather than in either arm. Chemotherapy benefit differed by menopausal status, with an
interaction p value of 0.008 [@kalinsky2021]. The two strata point in opposite directions. A
single hazard ratio for that trial would describe nobody. One sentence in that report matters more
than the headline. The relative chemotherapy benefit did not increase as the recurrence score
increased. In node-positive premenopausal women the assay identified who was at risk. It did not
identify who benefited from chemotherapy.

Read alongside {{trial:tailorx}}, where the residual chemotherapy signal also sat in women aged 50
or younger, that is the same finding twice. {{trial:mindact}} looked in the same place, reporting
an exploratory analysis by age in its update [@piccart2021mindact]. Age and menopausal status
travel together, and no trial in this table randomised ovarian suppression. The benefit attributed
to chemotherapy in young women cannot be separated from the benefit of chemotherapy-induced
menopause.

{{trial:wsg-adapt-hr-her2}} answers the second question and answers it in a form that needs care.
Three weeks of preoperative endocrine therapy separated the population, with central Ki-67 read
before and after. The endocrine-responsive midrange group then did as well on endocrine therapy
alone as the low-score control group [@nitz2022]. The comparison was between two arms
defined by the marker, so nobody with an endocrine-responsive midrange tumour was treated the
other way. The response rate also depends on which drug was given, at 78.1% on an aromatase
inhibitor against 41.1% on tamoxifen. Endocrine response as measured here is a property of the
test as much as of the tumour.

{{trial:adaptcycle}} is still running. It asks whether ribociclib with an aromatase inhibitor can
take the place of the chemotherapy that a recurrence score and an endocrine response test would
otherwise mandate. That is the only substitution question in the table. Its reported concordance
work is the part to read now. Local and central Ki-67 disagreed on the endocrine-response call
often enough that 13.1% of patients called responders locally were non-responders centrally
[@hamann2025]. A response-guided strategy is only as portable as the assay that defines response.

All five rows should be read alongside the non-inferiority methods in [[BC-740]] and specifically
[[BS-6370]]. Three things the table cannot give you are worth naming. Nobody randomised assay use
against no assay use, so the value of testing itself is inferred from the outcomes of the tested.
Nobody enrolled four or more positive nodes into an omission question. And the overall survival
column is where the gap shows, because the node-positive trial has not reported it and this
subtype recurs for twenty years [[BS-4350]].

```caution
A trial that declares endocrine therapy non-inferior has not shown that chemotherapy does nothing.
It has shown that the difference is smaller than a margin chosen in advance. The {{trial:mindact}}
intention-to-treat comparison makes the point concretely, because its eight-year distant
metastasis-free survival favoured chemotherapy inside a design whose primary question was a
single-arm rate against a fixed boundary [@piccart2021mindact]. The general form of this error is
set out in [[BS-6370]].
```

```caution
The premenopausal {{trial:rxponder}} result is widely read as showing that chemotherapy works in young
node-positive women with low genomic risk. The flat relationship between score and benefit is
compatible with a different reading, which is that some of what chemotherapy delivered was ovarian
suppression [@kalinsky2021]. A later serum analysis in women younger than 55 found the benefit
confined to those whose anti-Mullerian hormone showed normal ovarian reserve [@kalinsky2026rxponder].
The trial did not randomise suppression, so it cannot separate the two. A patient offered
chemotherapy on this evidence should be told that ovarian suppression is
the alternative hypothesis, not a consolation prize.
```

## BS-4310 Chemotherapy selection when it is indicated

```story SQ-1040
```

Once chemotherapy is chosen, the remaining questions are which regimen and on what schedule. The
pharmacology of each agent belongs to [[BC-440]].

```evidence setting=early subtype=HR+/HER2- line=adjuvant topic=adjuvant-backbone sort=year cols=acronym,n,population,arms,result,os caption=The_randomised_trials_that_built_the_adjuvant_backbone,_all_but_ABC_and_PlanB_open_to_every_receptor_subtype._Three_questions_are_interleaved_here,_and_the_experimental-versus-control_column_is_what_separates_them._GONO-MIG-1,_which_enrolled_from_1992,_and_TACT2_have_no_taxane_in_either_arm.
```

The thirteen trials answer three questions that are habitually read as one, and they agree on one
thing across all three. Every increment here runs to single percentage points of disease-free or
event-free survival, and none of the modern comparisons found a regimen that was clearly wrong.

Whether a taxane should follow an anthracycline was asked by {{trial:calgb-9344}} and
{{trial:nsabp-b-28}}. Both found the same relative reduction in disease-free survival events and
diverged on survival, which CALGB 9344 improved and NSABP B-28 did not [@henderson2003;
@mamounas2005]. Nothing about the taxane differs between them. CALGB 9344 also randomised three
doxorubicin doses and found no dose effect, which is the more durable result in that row. Its
unplanned subgroup analysis put the recurrence hazard ratio at 0.72 in oestrogen receptor-negative
and 0.91 in receptor-positive tumours [@henderson2003]. That single comparison does more to
explain the position of taxanes in this subtype than either trial's headline.

Two later rows ask the same question against controls of the same length as the experimental arm.
The answer depends on what the taxane displaced. {{trial:geicam-9805}} put docetaxel in place of
fluorouracil in high-risk node-negative disease and lowered recurrence, consistently across hormone
receptor status, although its survival analysis rested on few deaths [@martin2010geicam9805].
{{trial:tact}} set docetaxel after four cycles of FEC against eight cycles of anthracycline-based
chemotherapy, so docetaxel took the place of the second half of an anthracycline-based course. It
found no difference [@ellis2009tact]. Why that control matters is argued in [[BS-3890]]. In this
subtype the more useful line is TACT's exploratory one. Docetaxel benefit was most apparent in
oestrogen receptor-negative, HER2-positive tumours, with lesser or no apparent benefit elsewhere.
The investigators proposed that receptor-positive, HER2-negative disease gains little from a taxane
[@ellis2009tact]. That is the CALGB 9344 subgroup again, in a second trial, and still a hypothesis.

Inside that same question sits the choice of taxane and schedule, which {{trial:ecog-e1199}} asked
directly. The answer is a pairing rather than a class. Weekly paclitaxel beat three-weekly
paclitaxel on disease-free and overall survival. Docetaxel every three weeks also beat
three-weekly paclitaxel on disease-free survival.
Weekly docetaxel did not [@sparano2008]. A trial with four arms is the only design that could have
shown that. Its price was grade 2 to 4 neuropathy in 27% of the weekly paclitaxel arm against 20%
of the control.

Whether the interval between cycles carries an independent effect was asked by five of these trials.
Only two answer it cleanly. {{trial:calgb9741}} and {{trial:gim2}}, twelve years apart, on different
backbones, are both positive on disease-free and overall survival. Both also carried a second
randomisation that added nothing, to concurrent against sequential scheduling in CALGB 9741 and to
fluorouracil in GIM2 [@citron2003; @delmastro2015]. {{trial:ago-etc}} is positive on both endpoints
too. It also changed the sequence along with the interval [@moebus2018agoetc]. {{trial:gono-mig-1}}
and {{trial:tact2}} found no significant difference [@blondeaux2020mig1; @cameron2017tact2]. Why
those two do not overturn the effect is argued in [[BS-3900]]. So is why AGO ETC cannot isolate it.
The schedule effect is still the claim in this table with the broadest support, because the
patient-level meta-analysis pooled the flat trials with the positive ones and kept the gain
[@ebctcg2019].

For this subtype the row to read is GONO-MIG-1's. Its subgroup analysis found the gain in hormone
receptor-negative tumours and none in receptor-positive ones. Its own test found no significant
difference in effect by receptor status [@blondeaux2020mig1]. The meta-analysis found the
proportional reduction similar in oestrogen receptor-positive and -negative disease, with a somewhat
smaller absolute gain in the first [@ebctcg2019]. A single trial's receptor split is not a reason to
withhold the schedule here.

TACT2's second randomisation belongs to the argument about ovarian function in [[BS-4300]].
Capecitabine in place of CMF after epirubicin was non-inferior on time to recurrence. Permanent
amenorrhoea was far less common with capecitabine than with CMF in women who were premenopausal at
entry [@cameron2017tact2]. Its investigators drew no conclusion about whether that difference
mattered for recurrence. It shows only that two regimens of similar efficacy can differ sharply in
the menopause they cause.

Whether the anthracycline can come out altogether was asked by {{trial:us-oncology-9735}},
{{trial:abc-trials}} and {{trial:wsg-planb}}. Those three appear to disagree and largely do not,
because they did not use the same control arm. {{trial:us-oncology-9735}} compared four cycles of
docetaxel and cyclophosphamide with four cycles of doxorubicin and cyclophosphamide, which is an
anthracycline regimen containing no taxane [@jones2009]. It is evidence that a taxane doublet beats
an old doublet. It is not evidence about the modern comparator.

{{trial:abc-trials}} and {{trial:wsg-planb}} both used that modern comparator, setting six cycles of
docetaxel and cyclophosphamide against a regimen containing a taxane and an anthracycline. Both
returned small differences, and the two point estimates do not favour the same arm. Their verdicts
differ because their margins were set on different scales. ABC set an inferiority boundary on the
hazard ratio at 1.18 and returned 1.202, so non-inferiority could not be declared [@blum2017]. PlanB
set a margin of 4.4 percentage points of disease-free survival and cleared it [@nitz2019]. The
trials also differ in follow-up at the time of the verdict and in how their randomised populations
were assembled, because PlanB removed its lowest genomic risk stratum from randomisation altogether.
ABC has since reported with longer follow-up [@geyer2024]. Read the margin before reading the
conclusion.

Two structural points are specific to this subtype, and one of them is about the table itself.
Eleven of these thirteen trials enrolled without regard to receptor status. So did every trial in
the next table. They appear here because this chapter is where the book tabulates the all-comers
adjuvant chemotherapy record, not because they enrolled this population. Their estimates are
averages over a population in which hormone receptor-positive disease was the part least likely to
benefit. The absolute gain from anthracycline-containing over taxane-based regimens is small in
endocrine-responsive disease, and the trial-level comparisons that establish it are in
[@ebctcg2012]. Schedule, by contrast, carries an independent effect established across 37,298 women
in 26 trials [@ebctcg2019].

The practical consequence is that dose density is available without adding an agent. In a subtype
where the marginal benefit of chemotherapy is contested, changing the schedule is a cheaper lever
than changing the drugs.

The rest of the all-comers record asked other questions, and the second table holds it. Two rows
come from before the backbone. {{trial:nsabp-b-05}} set a single alkylating agent against placebo
after mastectomy [@fisher1975b05; @fisher1986b05], and {{trial:ma5}} put epirubicin in place of
methotrexate in premenopausal node-positive disease [@levine2005ma5]. Three added a third drug to a
full anthracycline and taxane course. {{trial:finxx}} and {{trial:us-oncology-01062}} added
capecitabine [@joensuu2012; @oshaughnessy2015uso01062], and {{trial:success-a}} added gemcitabine
[@degregorio2020successa]. Two pushed dose further. {{trial:swog-s9313}} raised the intensity of
each drug at an unchanged total dose [@linden2007s9313]. {{trial:wsg-am-01}} gave tandem high-dose
chemotherapy with stem-cell support to patients with nine or more involved nodes [@nitz2005wsgam01].
{{trial:ice-ii}} tested a taxane-based regimen in patients aged 65 or older, with tolerability as
its endpoint [@vonminckwitz2015iceii]. {{trial:chemo-n0}} asked the question of [[BS-4300]] a
generation early, allocating CMF in node-negative disease by two invasion proteins measured in
tumour tissue [@harbeck2013chemon0].

```evidence setting=early subtype=HR+/HER2- line=adjuvant topic=beyond-backbone sort=year cols=acronym,n,population,arms,result,os caption=All-comers_adjuvant_chemotherapy_trials_outside_the_backbone's_three_questions:_what_came_before_it,_what_was_added_to_it,_how_far_dose_was_pushed,_older_patients,_and_selection_by_a_tumour_marker._NSABP_B-05,_MA.5,_SWOG_S9313_and_Chemo-N0,_which_enrolled_between_1972_and_1998,_and_WSG_AM-01_have_no_taxane_in_either_arm.
```

None of the three additions met its primary endpoint, which is the finding to carry into clinic. The
two capecitabine trials each reported a survival difference as a secondary endpoint. What that can
and cannot mean is argued in [[BS-3910]]. For this subtype the more useful result in
{{trial:us-oncology-01062}} is a measurement. Among its oestrogen receptor-positive, HER2-negative
patients, 70% of them node-positive, about a quarter had a central Ki-67 below 10%. Only 17 of those
had a disease-free survival event by seven years [@oshaughnessy2015uso01062]. Its investigators
concluded that such patients should not be enrolled in adjuvant trials measured by five-year
disease-free survival. That is a statement about trial design. It is also, from inside a
chemotherapy trial, a description of the tumour the assays in [[BS-4300]] were built to find.

What neither table can show is a regimen comparison in this subtype alone. Apart from the two
HER2-negative trials, ABC and PlanB, the regimen record for hormone receptor-positive, HER2-negative
disease is an average taken over everyone.
