## BS-3950 HER2-directed antibodies

The receptor biology is in [[BC-150]] and is not restated. What belongs here is that an antibody
against HER2 has three mechanisms and a kinase inhibitor has one of them. It blocks signalling,
it drives receptor internalisation and degradation, and it engages immune effector cells through
its constant region.

The third mechanism has human evidence. Fc gamma receptor polymorphisms were associated with
clinical outcome on trastuzumab-based therapy, which is difficult to explain if effector function
contributes nothing [@musolino2008]. It also explains why loss of the antibody epitope and loss of
pathway dependence are separate resistance mechanisms [[BS-2990]].

Adjuvant trastuzumab remains the largest single effect in this chapter. In the joint analysis of
two adjuvant trials, 133 events occurred in the trastuzumab group against 261 in the control
group. The hazard ratio for recurrence, second primary cancer or death was 0.48. The absolute
difference in disease-free survival at three years was 12 percentage points, and the risk of
death fell by 33%. Three-year cumulative incidence of class III or IV congestive heart failure or
cardiac death was 4.1% and 2.9% in the two contributing trials [@romond2005].

Pertuzumab blocks the dimerisation interface rather than the trastuzumab epitope, and the
structural basis is separate [@franklin2004]. In first-line metastatic HER2-positive disease,
adding pertuzumab to trastuzumab and docetaxel gave a median overall survival of 56.5 months
against 40.8 months at the 2015 confirmatory analysis of {{trial:cleopatra}}. The hazard ratio for
death was 0.68, with a 95% confidence interval of 0.56 to 0.84 [@swain2015]. At the final 8-year
end-of-study analysis the medians were 57.1 months against 40.8 months, with a hazard ratio of
0.69 [@swain2020]. That is the current figure, and it is the one used in [[BS-4920]]. Neither
analysis was adjusted for crossover, so both are conservative rather than optimistic.

Eligibility for all of this is defined by an assay, and the assay has a heterogeneity problem
[[BC-280]] and an analytic validity framework [[BC-110]].

## BS-3960 HER2 and pan-HER tyrosine kinase inhibitors

A small molecule reaches the kinase domain rather than the extracellular epitope. Three
consequences follow and they are the whole case for the class.

A truncated receptor lacking the antibody binding site remains a target in principle
[@scaltriti2007]. A mutant receptor without amplification is addressable, with sensitivity that
depends on which allele is present [@bose2013; @hyman2018]. And a small molecule crosses into the
central nervous system in a way an antibody largely does not.

That third point is where the class earns its place. Tucatinib was added to trastuzumab and
capecitabine in heavily pretreated HER2-positive metastatic disease. The hazard ratio for
progression or death was 0.54, with a 95% confidence interval of 0.42 to 0.71, and median
progression-free survival was 7.8 against 5.6 months. The hazard ratio for death was 0.66, with an
interval of 0.50 to 0.88. Among patients with brain metastases, progression-free survival at one
year was 24.9% against 0% [@murthy2020]. A rate of zero in the control arm is the reason this
result changed practice.

Selectivity is the design variable that decides tolerability. Neratinib inhibits HER1, HER2 and
HER4 irreversibly. Given for twelve months after chemotherapy and trastuzumab, it improved
two-year invasive disease-free survival. There were 70 events in 1420 patients against 109 in
1420, giving a stratified hazard ratio of 0.67 with a 95% confidence interval of 0.50 to 0.91.
Grade 3 diarrhoea occurred in 561 of 1420 patients, which is 40% [@chan2016]. EGFR inhibition
bought the diarrhoea. It did not buy the HER2 activity.

Antibodies and kinase inhibitors also fail differently, which is why progression on one is weak
evidence about the other [@wangyc2011]. Sequencing after progression is developed in [[BS-4930]].

## BS-3970 CDK4/6 inhibitors and next-generation cell cycle agents

The cell cycle mechanism is in [[BC-160]]. The point that belongs here is that this class was
predicted to work in luminal disease before it was tested. Oestrogen receptor positive lines were
preferentially sensitive in vitro, and receptor-negative lines were not [@finn2009]. The
prediction held.

Two first-line trials give the size of the effect. Palbociclib with letrozole gave median
progression-free survival of 24.8 months against 14.5 months with letrozole alone, with a hazard
ratio of 0.58 and a 95% confidence interval of 0.46 to 0.72 [@finn2016]. Ribociclib with
letrozole gave a hazard ratio of 0.56, with an interval of 0.43 to 0.72, and progression-free
survival at 18 months of 63.0% against 42.2% [@hortobagyi2016].

The toxicity is worth reading carefully because it is routinely misread. Grade 3 or 4 neutropenia
occurred in 66.4% of patients on palbociclib against 1.4% on placebo, while febrile neutropenia
occurred in 1.8% against none [@finn2016]. Grade 3 or 4 neutropenia occurred in 59.3% on
ribociclib against 0.9% on placebo [@hortobagyi2016]. Neutropenia at that rate with febrile
neutropenia under 2% is a different clinical object from chemotherapy neutropenia. The mechanism
is reversible arrest of a dividing marrow compartment rather than killing of progenitors.

In early disease the class produces a consistent effect in two differently selected populations.
Abemaciclib with endocrine therapy in node-positive high-risk disease gave a hazard ratio for
invasive disease-free survival of 0.75, with a 95% confidence interval of 0.60 to 0.93. Two-year
rates were 92.2% against 88.7% [@johnston2020]. Ribociclib with a non-steroidal aromatase
inhibitor in stage II or III disease gave a hazard ratio of 0.75, with an interval of 0.62 to
0.91. Three-year rates at that prespecified interim analysis were 90.4% against 87.1%
[@slamon2024]. The final preplanned analysis gave 0.749 and three-year rates of 90.7% against
87.6%, and the later data cuts are set out in [[BS-4320]] [@hortobagyi2025]. The same hazard ratio
in two different eligible populations is the useful observation, because what differs is the
selection rule rather than the drug.

Next-generation agents follow from the escape biology rather than from screening. Cyclin E1
expression marks a cycle that does not depend on the blocked kinase [@turner2019ccne1], which is the
argument for targeting further down. Resistance is developed in [[BS-2980]].

## BS-3980 PI3K, AKT, and mTOR inhibitors

Pathway architecture is in [[BC-170]]. What this section adds is that every agent in the class is
fighting the feedback its own mechanism creates. Inhibiting mTOR raises insulin receptor
substrate 1 and reactivates Akt [@oreilly2006]. Inhibiting PI3K raises oestrogen receptor
transcriptional activity and receptor protein [@bosch2015], partly through a chromatin regulator
[@toska2017]. Every trial in this class therefore pairs the inhibitor with endocrine therapy, and
that is a mechanistic requirement rather than a convention.

Four randomised results define current practice, and each selected patients differently.
{{trial:bolero2}} added everolimus to exemestane without molecular selection [@baselga2012].
{{trial:solar1}} selected PIK3CA-mutated disease for alpelisib [@andre2019].
{{trial:capitello291}} selected PIK3CA, AKT1 or PTEN alteration for capivasertib
[@turner2023capivasertib]. {{trial:inavo120}} selected PIK3CA-mutated disease progressing during
or shortly after adjuvant endocrine therapy, which is a clinical selection layered on a molecular
one [@turner2024inavo120; @jhaveri2025inavo].

Toxicity is the binding constraint and it is on-mechanism. Hyperglycaemia follows from inhibiting
a pathway that mediates insulin signalling. Rash and stomatitis follow from inhibiting it in
epithelium. Neither is avoidable by better patient selection, because the patients selected are
those whose tumours depend on the pathway being inhibited. The practical limit is dose intensity
maintained over months, and the clinical detail sits in [[BS-4850]].

Selection has a measurement caveat. PIK3CA status from plasma and from archival tissue are not
the same test, and matched white cell sequencing is needed to keep clonal haematopoiesis out of
a plasma result [@razavi2019]. PTEN loss is a convergent resistance mechanism as well as a
selection criterion [@juric2015], so its meaning depends on when it was measured.

## BS-3990 PARP inhibitors and DNA repair directed agents

Trapping and synthetic lethality are in [[BS-1450]]. This section is about who the drugs are for
and what the boundary of that group is.

Two randomised trials established activity in germline BRCA-mutated metastatic disease.
{{trial:olympiad}} compared olaparib with physician's choice chemotherapy [@robson2017].
{{trial:embraca}} compared talazoparib with chemotherapy [@litton2018]. {{trial:olympia}}
established adjuvant olaparib in high-risk germline BRCA-mutated early disease [@tutt2021].

The boundary of the group is the interesting part. {{trial:tbcrc048}} tested olaparib beyond
germline BRCA and found activity in germline PALB2 and in somatic BRCA1 or BRCA2 alterations
[@tung2020]. The unifying property is loss of homologous recombination in the tumour, and germline
status is a proxy for it rather than the thing itself.

The proxy problem is sharpest for platinum, and one trial settles how far genomic scores can be
trusted. In unselected advanced triple-negative disease, carboplatin was not more active than
docetaxel, with objective response rates of 31.4% against 34.0% in 376 patients. In the germline
BRCA-mutated subgroup carboplatin doubled the response rate, at 68% against 33%, with an
interaction p value of 0.01. Benefit was not seen in patients selected by BRCA1 methylation, by
low BRCA1 messenger RNA, or by a high score on a commercial homologous recombination deficiency
assay [@tutt2018].

```caution
A homologous recombination deficiency score measures accumulated genomic scarring, which is a
record of past instability [[term:hrd]]. Scars do not disappear when repair function is restored.
In advanced triple-negative disease a high score did not identify platinum benefit while a
germline mutation did [@tutt2018]. The score answers a question about history and the drug needs
an answer about the present. Reversion and other routes to restored tolerance are in [[BS-1460]]
and [[BS-3010]].
```

## BS-4000 Rare alteration directed and tumor-agnostic approaches

A tumour-agnostic claim is an inference, and its strength depends on how many histologies were
tested and whether breast cancer was one of them. Three current examples differ sharply on that
measure.

TRK fusion is the strongest case. Larotrectinib produced an overall response in 75% of 55
patients by independent review, with a 95% confidence interval of 61 to 85. Those patients carried
17 distinct tumour types and ranged in age from 4 months to 76 years [@drilon2018].
Fusion-positive tumours are rare in breast cancer outside secretory carcinoma, so the practical
issue is finding them rather than treating them.

Mismatch repair deficiency is the second. Pembrolizumab produced objective responses in 53% of
patients and complete responses in 21%, across 12 tumour types [@le2017]. The proposed mechanism
is neoantigen supply, which makes the inference to other histologies mechanistically reasonable.

Tumour mutational burden is the weakest and it is the one most often applied loosely. One
prospective biomarker analysis of a multi-cohort study set the threshold at 10 mutations per
megabase. Objective responses occurred in 30 of 102 patients above it, which is 29%, and in 43 of
688 below it, which is 6% [@marabelle2020]. The ten enrolled cohorts were anal, biliary, cervical,
endometrial, mesothelioma, neuroendocrine, salivary, small-cell lung, thyroid and vulvar. No
breast cohort was included. High tumour
mutational burden is also uncommon in breast cancer [@barrososousa2020], so both the evidence and
the eligible population are thin.

HER2 mutation without amplification is the alteration-directed case specific to this disease. It
is a different lesion from amplification, with allele-dependent sensitivity
[@bose2013; @hyman2018], and it is missed by any assay that reports copy number alone.

## BS-4010 Combination strategies and overlapping toxicity

There are three defensible reasons to combine targeted agents, and only the first is a mechanism.

The first is to close the feedback loop the primary drug opens. mTOR inhibition reactivates Akt
[@oreilly2006], PI3K inhibition raises receptor signalling [@bosch2015], and HER-family inhibition
is buffered by a shift in HER3 phosphorylation [@sergina2007]. Each of those names its own
partner, which is what makes the reasoning productive rather than combinatorial [[BS-1090]].

The second is to cover two dependencies that exist simultaneously. Hormone receptor positive,
HER2-positive disease is the clean example, because oestrogen receptor signalling rises
adaptively under anti-HER2 pressure [@giuliano2015]. Triple blockade with endocrine therapy, HER2
antibodies and a CDK4/6 inhibitor follows from that [@ciruelos2026], as does chemotherapy-free
neoadjuvant treatment in selected disease [@tpii2026].

The third is depth of response where the endpoint requires it, which is mainly a neoadjuvant
argument [[BS-3910]].

Overlapping toxicity decides most of what is feasible. Myelosuppression stacks, so a CDK4/6
inhibitor and a PARP inhibitor are difficult together. Diarrhoea stacks, so a pan-HER inhibitor
and capecitabine constrain each other. Hyperglycaemia and QT prolongation do not stack with much,
but each narrows the eligible population before the trial starts.

```practice
The comparison a combination has to win is not against the weaker single agent. It is against the
sequence of the same two agents given one after the other.

Most combination trials do not run that comparison, so a positive combination result usually
leaves open whether the benefit came from using both drugs or from using both drugs at once.

Where the mechanism is feedback relief, simultaneous administration is genuinely required, because
the escape appears within weeks [[BS-2960]]. Where the mechanism is covering two independent
dependencies, sequence may cost little and will cost less toxicity.
```

## BS-4020 Cross-resistance between classes

Cross-resistance follows shared dependence. It does not follow shared indication, and the general
argument is in [[BS-3030]]. What belongs here is the class-by-class version, because the strength
of the prediction differs sharply between pairs.

Where the dependence is the same, the prediction is strong. Platinum and PARP inhibition both
exploit defective homologous recombination, and a secondary mutation restoring the reading frame
removes sensitivity to both [@sakai2008]. Selection by germline mutation works for both and
selection by genomic scar works for neither [@tutt2018].

Within the PI3K pathway the prediction is moderate. Alpelisib, capivasertib and inavolisib act at
different nodes of one pathway, so a lesion that bypasses the pathway rather than the node, such
as convergent PTEN loss, should affect all of them [@juric2015]. A lesion at the node should not.

Between HER2-directed antibodies and kinase inhibitors the prediction is weak, because the escape
routes are demonstrably different [@wangyc2011]. Failure of one is poor evidence about the other,
and that is a reason to try rather than a reason to expect.

Between endocrine therapy and CDK4/6 inhibition the relationship is asymmetric. An ESR1 mutation
confers ligand independence, so it predicts failure of oestrogen deprivation as a class while
leaving a receptor-directed drug with a target [@bidard2022emerald]. It does not predict failure
of cell cycle inhibition, which depends on cyclin E1 and Rb rather than on ligand
[@turner2019ccne1; @oleary2018].

Conjugates are the unsolved case and they are treated separately in [[BS-4080]].

```interplay target=BC-350
Every class in this chapter selects, and each selects for something different. An antibody selects
for the compartment that does not display the antigen. A kinase inhibitor selects for the cell
that does not depend on the kinase. A PARP inhibitor selects for the clone that restored repair.
The composition of the tumour at progression was therefore partly fixed before the first dose,
which is the heterogeneity argument arriving as a treatment-selection problem.
```
