## BS-2250 Definitions and thresholds, spatial, clonal, and the ten percent convention

HER2 heterogeneity has no single definition. The working conventions describe different things
and are not interchangeable. Keep the genetic and the protein-level claim apart from the first
paragraph onward, because most published confusion collapses the two [@konecny2003].

## BS-2280 Genetic and protein-level heterogeneity and their imperfect correlation

Amplification and overexpression are measured by different assays with different failure modes.
The analytic validity framework set out in [[BC-110]] applies here without restatement.

## BS-2290 Amplicon topology, extrachromosomal DNA, and the mechanistic origin of instability

Extrachromosomal amplification segregates unequally at mitosis. That is a mechanism for
heterogeneity rather than a description of it, and it predicts instability under treatment
pressure, which is the argument carried forward in [[BC-300]].

## BS-2350 Implications for conjugate payload delivery and the bystander effect

Bystander killing is the reason a heterogeneous tumor can still respond. The payload chemistry
that permits it is covered in [[BC-460]] and the clinical consequence in {{trial:destiny-b04}}.

```interplay target=BC-350
HER2 heterogeneity, payload sensitivity, and bystander killing behave as one system. A tumor
with low mean expression and high spatial variance is not the same problem as a tumor with low
expression everywhere, even though the pathology report may read the same.
```
