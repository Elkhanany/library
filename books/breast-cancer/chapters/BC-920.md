## BS-7720 Scope, and the biomarker triage at diagnosis

This chapter is the trial record for metastatic triple-negative disease, arranged by line. It says
what was tested, what came back, and what each design cannot settle. How to approach the patient
who carries this disease is in [[BC-580]], which covers risk, burden, symptoms and goals. Where the
two chapters touch, [[BC-580]] points forward to the tables here.

Triple-negative is a description by absence, and treating it as one disease is the error this
chapter exists to prevent. The transcriptional and genomic diversity inside the label is set out in
[[BC-120]] [@lehmann2011].

Three tests decide first-line therapy. All three should be sent at metastatic diagnosis rather than
at progression, because the first-line decision cannot wait for a result ordered later.

PD-L1, with the assay and the threshold named in the report. The antibody clone and the scored
compartment differ between the trials that produced the indications, so a bare positive is not a
statement about the patient.

Germline BRCA1 and BRCA2, extended to PALB2 and to somatic BRCA alterations. A patient never tested
is a patient never eligible, which makes the access argument in [[BC-690]] a treatment argument
here.

And HER2 immunohistochemistry, read specifically at the 0 against 1+ boundary. A tumour called
triple-negative may be HER2-low, and therefore eligible for a conjugate approved on expression that
the older classification called negative. The ultralow category below that boundary carries
randomised evidence only in hormone receptor-positive disease, so it does not open the same route
here [[BS-5100]].

That third point is the one most often missed. HER2-low is not a new biology. It is the same tumours
under a threshold that moved, which is the measurement argument in [[BC-110]] and [[BS-2360]]. The
order in which to send the three tests, and what to do while they are outstanding, is set out in
[[BS-5020]] rather than here.

Each section below opens with the question its trials were built to answer, taken from the
[Chapter Stories](stories.html) map, and closes with a table filtered on that same question. The
story says what was being looked for and what the design cannot settle. The table says what else
is in the cell. The prose argues the decision.

## BS-7730 First-line chemoimmunotherapy and the PD-L1 threshold

```story SQ-0820
```

```evidence setting=metastatic subtype=TNBC line=1L modality=immunotherapy cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,result,os caption=Randomised_first-line_trials_adding_a_checkpoint_inhibitor_to_a_chemotherapy_backbone._They_differ_in_the_antibody,_the_backbone_and_the_PD-L1_assay.
```

Four of those rows test one idea. Add a checkpoint inhibitor to a chemotherapy backbone in untreated
disease, and compare it against the same backbone alone. On the direction of selection they agree.
Where overall survival was read in the whole enrolled population, it did not separate. Every survival
signal that survives scrutiny sits inside an assay-positive population. That is the class finding,
and it is narrower than the way the class is usually described.

There is no single PD-L1 test behind it. {{trial:impassion130}} used SP142 and scored staining on
immune cells as a percentage of tumour area. {{trial:keynote355}} used 22C3 and scored a combined
positive score across tumour cells, lymphocytes and macrophages. A tumour positive by one is not
necessarily positive by the other. The practical consequence is that the request has to name the
assay the intended regimen was registered with. A report giving a score without a clone cannot be
mapped onto either indication. The general form of this problem is in [[BC-470]] and [[BS-4130]].

The trials diverge in three ways at once, and the antibody is the least important of them. The
backbone differs: nab-paclitaxel in {{trial:impassion130}} and {{trial:torchlight}}, plain
paclitaxel in {{trial:impassion131}}, and a choice of three regimens in {{trial:keynote355}}. The
assay differs, as above. The testing order differs, and that matters most. {{trial:impassion130}}
required overall survival to reach significance in the intention-to-treat population before the
PD-L1-positive subgroup could be tested formally. {{trial:impassion131}} tested progression-free
survival in the PD-L1-positive population first. {{trial:keynote355}} read overall survival at a
score of 10 or higher, at 1 or higher, and in everyone.

Read that way, the two atezolizumab trials are not a disagreement about atezolizumab. They differ in
the taxane, in the corticosteroid premedication a plain taxane requires, and in the population
enrolled [@miles2021]. The comparison between them is across trials and was never randomised. The
failure cannot be assigned to the backbone alone. {{trial:torchlight}} then reproduced the selection
pattern with a third antibody on a nab-paclitaxel backbone [@jiang2024], which is the best available
argument that the pattern belongs to the class rather than to one drug.

The first-line decision rests on {{trial:keynote355}}. It is the only trial here that carried overall
survival as a primary endpoint to a prespecified score threshold and met it, against the chemotherapy
the clinic would otherwise have given [@cortes2022keynote355]. Three backbones were permitted, so the
result does not belong to one taxane. Progression-free survival had already been reported separately [@cortes2020].
The trial also shows where selection stops working, because the survival curves at a score of 1 or
higher are not the curves at 10 or higher. That decay is itself the argument. A marker whose effect
weakens as its threshold is lowered is behaving predictively, which is what makes this cut-point
load-bearing rather than administrative.

{{trial:impassion130}} is the cautionary case rather than the load-bearing one, and it is a worked
illustration of the hierarchical testing point in [[BC-740]]. Overall survival missed in the
intention-to-treat population, so the hierarchy stopped there [@schmid2018impassion130]. Every
survival figure quoted for the assay-positive group descends from a test that was never formally
performed [@emens2021]. The drug is used on that basis anyway, which is a defensible clinical
judgement and not a statistical result. Progression-free survival there improved in the whole
enrolled population as well as in the assay-positive subgroup, which is the one place the selection
story is untidy.

{{trial:ascent04}} renders in this table because it carries a checkpoint inhibitor in both arms. It
is not a test of whether to add one. Both arms received pembrolizumab, and what was randomised was
the cytotoxic partner against a conjugate, on a progression-free survival endpoint that favoured
the conjugate [@tolaney2026ascent04]. It belongs to the argument in [[BS-7750]] and appears here
only because the filter is on modality. Its practical consequence belongs here, though. A positive
PD-L1 result no longer determines the whole regimen. It determines only that a checkpoint inhibitor
belongs in it.

What the table cannot show is the variable that decides eligibility. There is no column for the
antibody clone, the scored compartment or the cut-off, so assays that select different patients print
as one word.
Nobody ran two assays on the same tumours inside a randomised trial and compared who they selected.
Nobody randomised nab-paclitaxel against plain paclitaxel within a single checkpoint trial, which is
the only design that could settle the backbone question. No first-line trial was powered to exclude
a small benefit in the assay-negative population, which is not the same as showing there is none.
Nobody randomised the band between the two thresholds as a stratum of its own, so the weak result at
a score of 1 or higher is a diluted superset rather than a test of the patients between 1 and 9. And
nobody randomised how long a checkpoint inhibitor should continue, or whether it should be carried
past progression.

{{trial:keynote119}} completes the picture from outside this table, because its line of therapy puts
it elsewhere. Single-agent pembrolizumab after one or two prior treatments did not improve survival
at either score threshold tested [@winer2021keynote119]. Checkpoint blockade in this disease needs a
cytotoxic partner and needs to be given early. Nothing in the later-line record contradicts that.

```caution
The PD-L1 threshold selects in metastatic disease and does not select in the early setting, where
{{trial:keynote522}} showed benefit without selection at all [@schmid2020; @schmid2024k522os]. Do not
carry the metastatic selection rule backwards into the neoadjuvant decision, and do not carry a
neoadjuvant PD-L1 result forwards into this one. [[BC-860]] sets out why the same marker behaves
differently on either side of surgery.
```

## BS-7740 PARP inhibition for germline carriers

```evidence setting=metastatic subtype=TNBC,BRCA modality=parp status=reported cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,result,os caption=Reported_PARP_inhibitor_trials_reaching_this_population._Both_registration_trials_enrolled_hormone_receptor-positive_patients_as_well.
```

Who counts as a carrier is itself a measurement. In 8,085 unselected patients, 9.2% carried a
pathogenic germline variant in a susceptibility gene, and BRCA1 or BRCA2 variants were commonest in
triple-negative disease, at 11.2% [@sun2017].

What those five rows agree on is narrow and firm. Where a germline BRCA1 or BRCA2 mutation is
present, a PARP inhibitor delays progression better than the single agent a clinic would otherwise
reach for. It also produces a markedly higher response rate. Neither registration trial converted
that into longer survival on final analysis [@robson2019; @litton2020].

The rows differ in what the inhibitor was compared against rather than in the inhibitor.
{{trial:olympiad}} and {{trial:embraca}} gave it alone against the physician's choice of
chemotherapy in carriers. {{trial:brocade3}} added it to carboplatin and paclitaxel, so its control
arm already contained the platinum that the same repair defect selects for. That trial therefore
asks the smaller question of what a PARP inhibitor adds to a regimen already aimed at the defect.
{{trial:dora}} tested maintenance without chemotherapy after platinum, and judged each of its two
arms against a historical control rather than against the other [@tan2024]. It enrolled by response
to platinum rather than by germline status, and sustained benefit tracked neither germline BRCA nor
PD-L1. {{trial:tbcrc048}} is a
single arm, and its question is which gene rather than which drug.

The decision rests on {{trial:olympiad}} and {{trial:embraca}} together, because they are the only
randomised comparisons against the treatment a carrier would otherwise receive at that point. They
used different PARP inhibitors, different chemotherapy baskets and different randomisation ratios,
and they returned the same shape of answer. Two trials reproducing one another with different drugs
is the strongest evidence this section has.

The gap between the two endpoints is the interpretive problem, and it has a candidate explanation
rather than a settled one. Subsequent PARP inhibitor and platinum use was commoner in the
chemotherapy arm of {{trial:embraca}} than in the talazoparib arm. Adjusting for it moved the hazard
ratio for death in the right direction, with a bootstrap interval still crossing one [@litton2020].
Crossover is a reason the survival signal may be diluted. It is not evidence that the signal exists.

{{trial:tbcrc048}} widened the eligible set and, more usefully, narrowed it. Confirmed responses
occurred only with germline PALB2 and with somatic BRCA1 or BRCA2 alterations. Nothing responded
with ATM or CHEK2 alterations alone [@tung2020; @tung2026a]. That is a result about which gene rather
than about how much repair deficiency, and it does not license a panel-wide indication. Its
population was mostly oestrogen receptor-positive, so the row sits in this table on mechanism rather
than on receptor status.

Two things the table cannot show follow from that. Nobody has randomised a PARP inhibitor against
platinum, which is the other drug the same defect selects for, so the choice between them at first
progression rests on no head-to-head evidence at all. And there is no column for what patients
received afterwards, which is where the survival reading of both registration trials lives.

One result from outside this table belongs in the argument. In {{trial:tnt}}, germline BRCA status
raised the response rate to carboplatin and not to docetaxel [@tutt2018]. The same defect that PARP
inhibition exploits also selects for platinum, which is why the two decisions are made together
rather than in sequence. It is also why they fail together. Secondary intragenic mutations that
restore the BRCA2 reading frame confer resistance both to platinum and to PARP inhibition
[@sakai2008]. The mechanism is in [[BC-180]] and the shared resistance in [[BS-3010]].

```caution
A homologous recombination deficiency score records past genomic instability. Scars persist after
repair function returns, so a tumour can score [[term:hrd]] high while repairing normally at the
moment of testing. The score describes history. The drug needs the present.
```

## BS-7750 Conjugates in second line and beyond

```story SQ-0840
```

```evidence setting=metastatic subtype=TNBC modality=adc cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,result,os caption=Randomised_TROP2_conjugate_trials_in_triple-negative_disease,_from_the_refractory_setting_to_the_first_line.
```

Four randomised comparisons, two conjugates, one direction. In every one of them the conjugate beat
the chemotherapy it was built to replace, in refractory disease and in the first line, with a
checkpoint partner and without one. No other class in this chapter has that record.

{{trial:ascent}} established it in patients who had already been through several lines of
chemotherapy [@bardia2021ascent; @bardia2024]. The comparator arm is the part that changed practice,
because it records the honest baseline this disease had before conjugates, and that baseline is
short.

The class then moved forward in three trials that split the first line by whether checkpoint
blockade was an option. {{trial:ascent03}} took the patients for whom it was not
[@cortes2025ascent03]. {{trial:ascent04}} took the PD-L1-positive patients and held pembrolizumab
constant in both arms [@tolaney2026ascent04]. {{trial:tropion-b02}} took a second conjugate into the
immunotherapy-ineligible slot [@dent2026tropionb02].

Read the control arm column before the result column, because it explains the spread across those
four rows. {{trial:ascent}} was set against single-agent chemotherapy in heavily pretreated
disease. {{trial:ascent03}} and {{trial:tropion-b02}} were set against first-line chemotherapy,
which in ASCENT-03 could be a gemcitabine and carboplatin doublet. {{trial:ascent04}} was set
against chemotherapy with pembrolizumab, the strongest comparator in the table. The conjugate's
margin narrows as the comparator strengthens, and none of that is a statement about the
conjugates.

The divergence in that group is maturity and endpoint, not efficacy. {{trial:tropion-b02}} carried
overall survival as a dual primary endpoint and met it. {{trial:ascent03}} reports progression-free
survival with survival not yet mature, and a published methodological critique argues that
crossover-adjusted analysis is needed before the first-line position is treated as settled
[@poggio2026]. {{trial:ascent04}} reports its survival data as immature at the analysis published.
{{trial:ascent03}} and {{trial:tropion-b02}} were built for the same slot, immunotherapy-ineligible
first-line disease. They are not equivalent in what has been demonstrated.

So the first line in immunotherapy-ineligible disease rests on {{trial:tropion-b02}}, which is
currently the only first-line conjugate trial with a survival result against the chemotherapy a
clinic would otherwise give. {{trial:ascent03}} will become load-bearing when its survival reads out,
and ranking the two before then is a statement about follow-up rather than about drugs. One detail
of {{trial:ascent03}} is worth carrying into the consent conversation. Confirmed response rates were
close to identical between the arms while duration of response was not. What the conjugate buys
there is the length of a response rather than the chance of one.

{{trial:ascent04}} leaves its own question open. Its control arm was chemotherapy with
pembrolizumab, not the conjugate alone, so the trial cannot say whether the checkpoint inhibitor is
still needed once the conjugate is in the regimen. The single-arm cohorts of {{trial:begonia}} pair
the other conjugate with PD-L1 blockade and report the highest confirmed response rate recorded at
this position [@schmid2026]. Neither cohort has a control arm, and they are exploratory rather than
part of the record above. One enrolled all comers and the other only PD-L1-high tumours, which makes
the two rates a difference in selection rather than a comparison.

Three things the table cannot show. No trial has randomised one TROP2 conjugate against the other,
and none is planned, so the choice between them rests on indication and toxicity. No trial has
randomised the order of conjugate and chemotherapy, so the case for giving the class first rather
than keeping it in reserve is inferred from separate trials at separate lines. No trial selected patients on TROP2 expression, and the biomarker analysis of
{{trial:ascent}} found progression-free survival tracking an expression gradient without producing
a threshold anyone could act on [@bardia2021ascentbio]. And the population that established the
class in {{trial:ascent}}, heavily pretreated and conjugate-naive, has largely been abolished by
moving the class forward.

```evidence setting=metastatic subtype=HER2-low line=2L,3L+ modality=adc cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,result,os,status caption=The_HER2_route_out_of_the_triple-negative_label,_for_tumours_scoring_1+_or_2+_without_amplification.
```

{{trial:destiny-b04}} brought trastuzumab deruxtecan into a population the older classification
called HER2-negative, and it improved both progression-free and overall survival against the
physician's choice of chemotherapy [@modi2022; @modi2025]. Read it with its population in view. The
great majority of those enrolled were hormone receptor-positive, and the primary endpoint was read
in that cohort. The triple-negative patients are a minority inside an all-patient secondary
analysis. The row that opens the HER2-low route for this chapter is therefore mostly evidence from
another chapter's population.

{{trial:daisy}} argues that the boundary is arbitrary in the other direction, because confirmed
responses occurred in immunohistochemistry 0 disease at a rate well above zero [@mosele2023]. It is
a single-arm phase II trial in cohorts defined by staining, so it establishes that such tumours can
respond and not how often they will. The apparent disagreement between the two rows is about
measurement rather than biology. Pathologist concordance is poorest exactly at the 0 against 1+
boundary [[BS-6710]], which means a proportion of the patients in each cohort would have been
assigned to the other by a second reader.

{{trial:trade-dxd}} sits in this table without a result, because it is still enrolling. Its
contribution is discussed in [[BS-7780]], where the sequencing question it was built for belongs.

The comparison this table most needs does not exist. No trial has randomised a HER2-directed
conjugate against a TROP2 conjugate in a tumour eligible for both, which is now the commonest
choice a clinic faces in HER2-low triple-negative disease. Nor is there randomised evidence
restricted to HER2-low tumours without hormone receptor expression.

Toxicity rather than efficacy separates the three conjugates in practice, and the dominant toxicity
differs by linker and payload handling rather than by target [[BS-4100]]. Sacituzumab govitecan is
limited by neutropenia and diarrhoea, and UGT1A1 genotype is worth knowing before the first dose in a
patient with a suggestive history. Trastuzumab deruxtecan carries the interstitial lung disease risk
that should shape ordering in anyone with poor baseline reserve or prior thoracic radiotherapy.
Datopotamab deruxtecan is dominated by mucosal and ocular events instead, which are more tolerable in
a frail patient and more disruptive in a working one.

```interplay target=BC-350
Three conjugates now reach this population and all three carry a topoisomerase I inhibitor payload.
A patient who receives sacituzumab govitecan, datopotamab deruxtecan and trastuzumab deruxtecan has
had three exposures to one payload class delivered by three antibodies. The line count says three
lines. The biology says one. Every assay in clinical use reports antigen abundance, which answers
whether the antibody can bind and nothing about internalisation, lysosomal transport, efflux or
repair capacity [[BS-4090]]. A proportion of what is recorded here as conjugate failure is therefore
allocation failure. No improvement in the payload addresses it.
```

## BS-7760 Chemotherapy sequencing when no target is present

```story SQ-0810
```

A substantial fraction of patients have no PD-L1 positivity above threshold, no germline or somatic
repair alteration and no HER2 expression. For them the sequence is chemotherapy. Platinum doublets
were active in single-arm studies [@perez2000; @nelli2013]. In tnAcity, designed to find a new
first-line doublet for this population [@yardley2015], nab-paclitaxel with carboplatin gave longer
progression-free survival than nab-paclitaxel with gemcitabine or gemcitabine with carboplatin
[@yardley2018]. Adding iniparib to gemcitabine and carboplatin failed its coprimary endpoints
[@oshaughnessy2014]. The randomised record
behind it is older and thinner than the record behind everything above.

```evidence setting=metastatic subtype=TNBC modality=chemo topic=versus cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,result,os caption=Head-to-head_first-line_chemotherapy_comparisons_in_this_population.
```

One comparison in that table beat a taxane, and it did so in a population that does not generalise.
Platinum was not worse than a taxane anywhere in it. That is the whole of the agreement, and it is
enough to license either choice at the start.

The apparent disagreement inside it is an endpoint difference. {{trial:tnt}} read objective response
rate as its primary endpoint and found none between carboplatin and docetaxel in unselected advanced
disease [@tutt2018]. A difference in how long benefit lasted could not have shown.
{{trial:cbcsg006}} read progression-free survival, tested it for non-inferiority against a
prespecified margin and then for superiority, and met both for the platinum doublet [@hu2015]. The
populations differ as well. One enrolled advanced disease broadly, the other untreated disease at
Chinese centres only, with gemcitabine held constant in both arms. Neither result is a general
statement about platinum against a taxane at any line.

{{trial:calgb-40502-ncctg-n063h}} is in this table as a taxane comparison rather than as a
triple-negative trial. It enrolled all subtypes and gave bevacizumab in every arm. Neither
nab-paclitaxel nor ixabepilone beat weekly paclitaxel, and the nab-paclitaxel arm was the more toxic
[@rugo2015]. The newer agent was not the better one.

The decision rests on {{trial:tnt}}. It is the randomised comparison between the two drugs a clinic
actually chooses between at this point, and it is the trial that carried the marker question. Its germline BRCA finding was a prespecified interaction rather than a randomisation, and
it stood alone. BRCA1 methylation, low BRCA1 mRNA and a commercial homologous recombination
deficiency score all failed to select.

{{trial:tbcrc-009}} is the exploratory counterpart and is deliberately outside the table. It gave
single-agent platinum with a gene expression predictor written in as a coprimary endpoint, and the
predictor failed outright [@isakoff2015]. The choice between cisplatin and carboplatin was left to
the treating physician, so the trial does not compare the two agents. Its germline carriers were a
subgroup and there were very few of them.

The missing column is survival. Neither {{trial:tnt}} nor {{trial:cbcsg006}} has reported overall
survival, so the platinum question rests entirely on response and progression endpoints.
Nor has anyone randomised platinum against a taxane using germline carrier
status as an entry criterion, which is the trial the TNT interaction asked for.

```evidence setting=metastatic subtype=TNBC modality=chemo line=2L,3L+ cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,result,os caption=Randomised_single-agent_comparisons_after_the_first_line.
```

Those two rows carry the later-line record, and both enrolled metastatic breast cancer of any
subtype. {{trial:embrace}} showed that a single cytotoxic agent can extend survival after two to
five prior regimens [@cortes2011]. {{trial:study301}} then compared eribulin with capecitabine
directly and found neither superior on either co-primary endpoint [@kaufman2015].

The difference between those two outcomes is the comparator. A basket of the physician's choice is
easier to beat than one named active drug, and the second trial replaced the first comparator with
the second. Within the class there is no evidence of a best agent. Neither trial was restricted to
triple-negative disease, so both rows describe this population by inference from a subgroup rather
than by design.

What those rows are most useful for is not choosing an agent. It is setting an expectation, and
the honest source is the control arm column of the conjugate table in [[BS-7750]]. Read the
{{trial:ascent}}, {{trial:ascent03}} and {{trial:tropion-b02}} control arms together. Single-agent
chemotherapy in the refractory setting delays progression by a matter of weeks. In the first line
without immunotherapy it delays progression by several months. The difference between them is the
effect of line rather than of agent, and it is the most useful single thing to hold before a
fourth-line conversation. Response duration shortens with each line here more steeply than it does
in hormone receptor-positive disease. Expectation setting is a clinical act, and it is done with
absolute medians rather than with hazard ratios.

Three principles follow. All three are frequently violated. Sequential single agents are preferred to
combinations outside a situation demanding immediate response, because combinations raise response
rate and toxicity without a consistent survival gain. Treat to progression or intolerance rather
than to a fixed number of cycles. And choose the agent by prior exposure, residual neuropathy,
marrow reserve and what the patient will tolerate, because the comparative trials show no efficacy
difference to choose on. Agent detail is in [[BC-440]].

## BS-7770 Antigen-defined and alteration-defined options

```story SQ-0800,SQ-0850
```

The search for something inside this disease to aim at has produced one instructive failure and a
handful of small positives. The failure is the largest and is worth reading closely. Only the four
AKT trials are randomised enough to sit in the table below. Everything else named in this section is
exploratory or supporting weight, and is held out of the table by design rather than by oversight.

```evidence setting=metastatic subtype=TNBC modality=pi3k-akt cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,endpoint,result,os caption=The_AKT_axis,_carried_from_randomised_phase_II_to_two_phase_III_trials.
```

The pattern in that table is clean and it is the reason the axis is closed. Both randomised phase II
trials favoured the AKT inhibitor on progression-free survival. Both phase III trials missed their
primary endpoint. Two drugs, four trials, one paclitaxel backbone throughout.

The divergence is not the drug. It is that neither phase III trial repeated the phase II trial that
sent it forward. {{trial:lotus}} read progression-free survival in the intention-to-treat population
and in a prespecified PTEN-low population, and separated nothing in the selected group [@kim2017].
Selection had therefore already failed once inside the phase II work.
{{trial:ipatunity130-cohort-a}} kept progression-free survival as its endpoint but admitted only
tumours carrying a PIK3CA, AKT1 or PTEN alteration, so it never tested the unselected population its
signal came from [@dent2024]. {{trial:pakt}} read progression-free survival against a one-sided
threshold [@schmid2019a]. {{trial:capitello-290}} kept the unselected population but moved the
primary endpoint to overall survival, with alterations assessed by retrospective central testing
[@schmid2025]. One programme changed the population between phases and the other changed the
endpoint.

The verdict rests on {{trial:capitello-290}}. It is the largest trial in the table, it enrolled the
population the phase II signals came from, and it read the endpoint that decides. It also closed the
escape route, because the pathway-altered tumours inside it did no better than the rest. A negative
result in an unselected population can always be blamed on dilution. A negative result in the
selected subgroup of that same population cannot.

What the table cannot show is the gap inside {{trial:capitello-290}}. Progression-free survival
still favoured the combination there, in the whole population and in altered tumours, while overall
survival did not. The result column carries the primary endpoint and not that discrepancy. An agent
that shifts one endpoint without shifting the other is the general warning in [[BC-740]], and this
programme is its cleanest example in the chapter. Nothing in the table tests an AKT inhibitor with a
partner other than paclitaxel, and nothing randomised altered against unaltered tumours inside one
protocol.

Earlier candidate dependencies failed in ways their designs could not separate.
{{trial:tbcrc-001}} added EGFR blockade to carboplatin with protocol crossover, so the same patients
supply more than one response group [@carey2012]. {{trial:tbcrc-019}} set its primary endpoint as the
response rate within each arm rather than the difference between arms [@forerotorres2015].
{{trial:tbcrc-011}} gave bicalutamide to androgen receptor-positive tumours defined at a 10% nuclear
staining threshold, and asked for oestrogen and progesterone receptor negativity without specifying
HER2 [@gucalp2013]. Its enrolled population is therefore wider than the cell it is filed under.
{{trial:tbcrc-032}} added a PI3K inhibitor to enzalutamide and reported a clinical benefit rate for
the combination with none for the antagonist alone, so the record carries no comparison
[@lehmann2019]. Four trials, four designs that cannot attribute what they saw.

Four current options are defined by an alteration or a histology rather than by receptors, and all
four are single arms reporting response rate. {{trial:sgntuc-019}} treated activating HER2 mutations
in conventionally HER2-negative disease and responded most often of the four [@okines2025].
{{trial:talazoparib-beyond-brca}} treated repair gene mutations outside BRCA1 and BRCA2, and its
regressions were confined to a single gene [@gruber2022]. {{trial:nimbus}} selected on tumour
mutational burden within HER2-negative disease [@barrososousa2025]. {{trial:dart-metaplastic}}
entered patients on metaplastic histology alone, and its few responses were still running when it
reported [@adams2021].

None of these is a trial of triple-negative disease. Each enrolled on a criterion that crosses the
label, and several report no hormone receptor or HER2 split at all. Each cohort is small enough that
one more responder would move the rate, so none of them is a number to plan around. Match the
patient to the entry criterion rather than to the cell in the table. The alteration-defined logic and
its trial designs are in [[BC-590]], and the case for broad sequencing here is in [[BC-750]].

## BS-7780 Unresolved questions and what reads out next

```story SQ-0830
```

What to do after a conjugate is the dominant unanswered question. It is also now the commonest
clinical situation in later-line disease, created by the first-line results in [[BS-7750]]. The only
clinical evidence is a retrospective series of patients treated with two conjugates in sequence
[@mai2025]. It is consistent with partial rather than complete cross-resistance.

```evidence setting=metastatic subtype=TNBC,HER2-low modality=adc status=ongoing cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,endpoint,status caption=The_first_prospective_study_designed_around_conjugate_sequencing.
```

{{trial:trade-dxd}} is the one prospective study built around the question, and the single row is
the point. It treats patients whose disease has progressed on a previous conjugate, in cohorts
defined by which conjugate came first. It admits hormone receptor-positive and receptor-negative
disease, and immunohistochemistry 0 tumours alongside HER2-low ones. Its endpoint is response rate
and its cohorts are non-comparative, so it will not rank sequences and it cannot be read as a
randomised comparison. It will produce the first prospective estimate of how much activity remains
after a conjugate fails. Nothing else in the registry is designed to answer this, which is why the
table has one row rather than several.

Whether responsiveness to checkpoint blockade can be manufactured where the assay says it is absent
remains open. Each of the attempts produces disease control in a minority, and they disagree about
who that minority is. {{trial:topacio}} combined a PARP inhibitor with PD-1 blockade irrespective of
BRCA status, and its responses concentrated in tumours carrying a BRCA mutation [@vinayak2019].
{{trial:dora}} gave chemotherapy-free maintenance after platinum and found durable control that did
not track germline BRCA status [@tan2024]. Those two are not in conflict about the drug. They differ
in line, in selection and in endpoint, one reading response in advanced disease and the other
reading progression-free survival in platinum-sensitive disease against a historical control.

The attempts share one design problem. {{trial:tonic}} allocated patients between priming cohorts
and never compared them statistically, and only its first stage is reported [@voorwerk2019].
{{trial:pembrolizumab-plus-radiotherapy-in-metas}} had no control arm, so a shrinking unirradiated
lesion cannot be credited to an abscopal effect [@ho2019]. {{trial:mediola}} is a single arm
combining PARP and checkpoint inhibition, so neither drug's contribution can be isolated
[@domchek2020]. Eligibility drifts as well. {{trial:mediola}} asked for a germline BRCA mutation in
HER2-negative disease rather than in triple-negative disease, and {{trial:dora}} defined
triple-negative at a receptor staining threshold rather than at no expression. Hormone
receptor-positive tumours sit inside both.

Only {{trial:tbcrc-043}} randomised the immune agent itself, adding PD-L1 blockade to carboplatin,
and it favoured the combination on both progression-free and overall survival [@lehmann2024]. Its
benefit did not follow PD-L1 status, which is the reverse of the selection the first-line trials were
built on. It is an exploratory phase II trial and is not confirmatory evidence. It is also the only
design in this group that could have attributed anything to the immune agent, which is the argument
for repeating it at size rather than for acting on it.

Whether PD-L1 is the right marker at all is the question underneath the whole first line. It selects
imperfectly, the assays disagree with each other, and the biology in [[BC-250]] suggests
tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes carry information the receptor stain does not. A better marker would
change first-line practice more than a better drug would.

And the whole first line now rests on a conjugate for almost everybody, with no randomised evidence
on what should follow it. Until a conjugate arrives carrying a payload from a different class, the
choices after failure will continue to be made on antigen expression, which answers one question out
of six [[BS-4090]].
