## BS-7720 Scope, and the biomarker triage at diagnosis

Triple-negative is a description by absence, and treating it as one disease is the error this chapter
exists to prevent. The transcriptional and genomic diversity within it is set out in [[BC-120]], and
the clinical decision architecture is in [[BC-580]].

Three tests decide first-line therapy and all three should be sent at metastatic diagnosis rather than
at progression. PD-L1, with the assay and threshold named, because they differ between trials.
Germline BRCA1 and BRCA2. And HER2 immunohistochemistry, because a tumour called triple-negative may
be HER2-low or HER2-ultralow and therefore eligible for a conjugate approved on expression the older
classification called negative.

That third point is the one most often missed. HER2-low is not a new biology, it is the same tumours
under a threshold that moved, which is the measurement argument in [[BC-110]].

The story below is the argument the rest of this chapter is evidence for, and it carries no
figures at all. It states what each group of trials was built to find out, what came back, and
what the design cannot settle, so the tables are read as answers to a question rather than as a
list. It is one node of the [Chapter Stories](stories.html) map.

## BS-7730 First-line chemoimmunotherapy and the PD-L1 threshold

Two trials established chemoimmunotherapy in this setting and they used different antibodies,
different scoring systems and different thresholds, which is why their results are not interchangeable.

{{trial:impassion130}} used the SP142 assay scoring PD-L1 on immune cells. {{trial:keynote355}} used
22C3 scoring a combined positive score across tumour and immune cells. A tumour can be positive by one
and negative by the other. The claim "PD-L1-positive" without naming the assay is not a claim about
the patient, and [[BC-470]] makes the general version of this argument.

The formal statistical structure of {{trial:impassion130}} matters and is instructive. Overall survival
was to be tested in the intention-to-treat population first, and only if significant there would it be
tested in the PD-L1-positive subgroup. It was not significant in the intention-to-treat population, so
the hierarchy stopped, and the PD-L1-positive figures are descriptive rather than confirmatory. That is
a textbook illustration of the hierarchical testing point in [[BC-740]].

{{trial:keynote119}} is the negative that completes the picture. Single-agent pembrolizumab in
pretreated disease did not significantly improve survival at any PD-L1 threshold. Checkpoint blockade
in this disease needs a chemotherapy partner and needs to be given early.

```caution
The PD-L1 threshold selects in metastatic disease and does not select in the early setting, where
KEYNOTE-522 showed benefit regardless. Do not carry the metastatic selection rule backwards into the
neoadjuvant decision. [[BC-860]] explains why the same marker behaves differently.
```

## BS-7740 PARP inhibition for germline carriers

{{trial:olympiad}} and {{trial:embraca}} established single-agent PARP inhibition against
physician's-choice chemotherapy in germline BRCA carriers. The mechanism, synthetic lethality with
homologous recombination deficiency, is in [[BC-180]].

Two points govern use. The benefit is in progression-free survival and quality of life more than in
overall survival, which is the interpretive problem [[BC-740]] describes for the metastatic setting
generally. And the eligible population is defined by germline testing, so a patient never tested is a
patient never eligible. That makes the access argument in [[BC-690]] a treatment argument here, not
only an equity one.

{{trial:tbcrc048}} extended activity beyond germline BRCA to PALB2 and somatic BRCA, which is a
biologically coherent extension because the same repair pathway is disabled.

## BS-7750 Conjugates in second line and beyond

Conjugates changed this setting more than any other class. {{trial:ascent}} established sacituzumab
govitecan against physician's choice in pretreated disease, and {{trial:tropion-b02}} and
{{trial:ascent03}} and {{trial:ascent04}} moved the class earlier.

For patients whose tumours express HER2 at low or ultralow levels, {{trial:destiny-b04}} brought
trastuzumab deruxtecan into a population previously classified as HER2-negative. The practical
consequence is that HER2 immunohistochemistry must be read and reported at the low end, where
concordance is poorest. The measurement problem is in [[BC-110]] and the computational attempt to fix
it in [[BC-750]].

The unresolved question is sequencing. A patient may now be eligible for two or three conjugates, and
whether activity persists after failure of one with the same payload class is not established. The
mechanism is in [[BC-460]] and the general form of the problem in [[BC-780]].

## BS-7760 Chemotherapy sequencing when no target is present

A substantial fraction of patients have no PD-L1 positivity, no germline alteration and no HER2
expression, and for them the sequence is single-agent chemotherapy.

The principles are settled and worth stating plainly because they are frequently violated. Sequential
single agents are preferred to combinations outside visceral crisis, because combinations improve
response rate and not survival while adding toxicity. Treat to progression or intolerance rather than
to a fixed number of cycles. And the choice among agents is driven by prior exposure, residual
neuropathy, and what the patient is willing to tolerate.

The evidence base here is older and thinner than for the targeted options, and that asymmetry is itself
informative. The patients with no target are the patients with the least recent evidence, and they are
not a small group.

## BS-7770 Antigen-defined and alteration-defined options

A small number of patients have alterations that cross subtype boundaries, and finding them requires
testing that is not routine in this disease.

Somatic alterations in the homologous recombination pathway, HER2 mutations treated in [[BC-900]],
and high tumour mutational burden or mismatch repair deficiency all define eligibility for agents not
otherwise indicated in triple-negative disease. The alteration-defined logic and its trial designs are
in [[BC-590]].

The argument for broad sequencing in this subtype is stronger than the yield suggests, because the
alternative is a chemotherapy sequence with modest expectations. A low probability of finding an
actionable alteration is weighed against what is available if nothing is found.

## BS-7780 Unresolved questions and what reads out next


What to do after a conjugate is the dominant unanswered question, and it is now the commonest clinical
situation in later-line triple-negative disease.

Whether checkpoint blockade has any role after first line. {{trial:keynote119}} says single-agent does
not. Whether combinations do, and in whom, is unsettled.

Whether PD-L1 is the right marker at all. It selects imperfectly, the assays disagree, and the biology
in [[BC-250]] suggests tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes carry information PD-L1 does not. A better
marker would change first-line practice more than a better drug.
