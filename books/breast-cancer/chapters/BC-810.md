## BS-6900 Scope, and why the neoadjuvant setting is harder in this subtype

This chapter covers preoperative systemic therapy for operable and locally advanced hormone
receptor-positive, HER2-negative breast cancer. Hormone receptor-positive HER2-positive disease is
a different entity with its own algorithm, and it has its own chapter in [[BC-840]].

Several neighbours own things this chapter deliberately does not. The machinery of the neoadjuvant
platform belongs to [[BC-540]]. The surrogacy argument attached to pathological complete response
is methodology and belongs to [[BC-740]] and [[BS-6360]]. The decision architecture that puts a
patient into this pathway at all belongs to [[BC-490]]. Adjuvant endocrine therapy belongs to
[[BC-790]], adjuvant CDK4/6 inhibition to [[BC-800]], and the genomic assay decision made on the
surgical specimen to [[BS-4300]]. What this chapter owns is the trial record of what is given
before the operation.

The organising fact is uncomfortable. Everywhere else in early breast cancer the neoadjuvant
setting earns its place because a pathological complete response is both achievable and
informative. Here it is neither, because complete responses in this subtype are uncommon. The
pooled analysis that established the prognostic value of the endpoint found the association weakest
in exactly this group [@cortazar2014]. A separate pooled analysis of seven randomised trials found
no association at all between complete response and disease-free survival in luminal A tumours
[@vonminckwitz2012].

Preoperative treatment here is therefore an experiment at least as much as a treatment. Nearly
every endpoint read before the operation is a proliferation measurement standing in for an outcome.
Nearly every question in this chapter turns on whether that substitution holds.

Each section below opens with the question its trials were built to answer, taken from the
[Chapter Stories](stories.html) map, and closes with a table filtered on that same question. The
story says what was being looked for and what the design cannot settle. The table says what else is
in the cell. The prose argues the decision. No figure appears in the prose unless the sentence is
about that figure.

## BS-6910 Neoadjuvant chemotherapy and the low pathological complete response rate

```story SQ-0080
```

Two separate facts with different causes and different consequences are usually run together here.

The first is that complete responses are uncommon. That is a property of the disease rather than of
the regimen. Slowly proliferating, oestrogen-driven tumours are not eradicated by cytotoxic therapy
at the rate that highly proliferative ones are. Intensifying the regimen moves the rate a little.
It does not move it into the range where a binary endpoint discriminates usefully between patients.

The second is that, within this subtype, achieving a complete response separates outcomes less
sharply than it does elsewhere [@cortazar2014]. The two facts compound each other. Few patients
reach the category, and reaching it says less about them than it would in triple-negative disease.

The practical consequence is that the reason for giving chemotherapy before surgery here is almost
never the response itself. It is downstaging of the breast tumour to permit conservation, or
downstaging of the axilla, or the logistics of a delayed operation. All three are legitimate
reasons, provided the expectation set with the patient before the first cycle matches whichever one
applies.

What the surgical specimen is for is a separate question whose answer is not the binary endpoint.
Residual cancer burden is continuous and remains prognostic in this subtype, with a shallower
gradient than in triple-negative disease [@symmans2017; @yau2022]. The index and its measurement
caveats are set out in [[BS-4680]]. A report giving only the presence or absence of a complete
response has discarded the part of the measurement that still carries information here
[[term:rcb]].

The harder version of the question is whether response can be used to withhold chemotherapy
altogether. Two families of trial have tried and they did not reach the same standard of proof.
Response-guided omission has a noninferiority result behind it, from a programme that allocated by
genomic score and by endocrine response rather than by randomisation. Substituting a CDK4/6
inhibitor for chemotherapy has only similar estimates behind it, read on endpoints scored at
surgery. Similar estimates are not noninferiority. None of the substitution trials carried a margin
or the size to test one [[BS-6370]].

```caution
A low complete response rate is routinely read as chemoresistance, which is a different claim.
Adjuvant chemotherapy reduces recurrence in selected hormone receptor-positive disease, in
populations where almost nobody would have achieved a complete response had they been treated
preoperatively [[BS-4310]]. Sensitivity measured by eradication and benefit measured by recurrence
are different quantities. The first systematically understates the second whenever the tumour grows
slowly.
```

## BS-6920 Neoadjuvant endocrine therapy and who it suits

```story SQ-0090,SQ-0100
```

Neoadjuvant endocrine therapy is the one preoperative strategy in this subtype whose rationale
never depended on a pathological endpoint.

The trials that established it do not all point the same way, and the disagreement is instructive.
Two found an aromatase inhibitor better than tamoxifen. Two found the difference small and not
significant. The divergence is more a measurement artefact than a pharmacological one. Response was
scored by callipers and by ultrasound, and the two methods gave different rates inside a single
trial. One of the null trials also permitted concurrent chemotherapy, which contaminates the
endocrine comparison [@cataliotti2006].

Escalating within the receptor axis has not moved the ceiling. A randomised comparison of the three
aromatase inhibitors found no difference between them in proliferation suppression or in the
post-treatment prognostic index [@ellis2011]. Adding fulvestrant to an aromatase inhibitor did not
improve on it either, over a full preoperative course and on that trial's own prognostic endpoint
[@ma2024alternate]. The oral receptor degraders have so far been tested only in two-week and
four-week windows, whose endpoint is a ratio of two Ki67 scores [[BS-4700]].

Who it suits follows from the biology rather than from the trials. Strongly oestrogen
receptor-positive, low-grade tumours are the ones that respond. Older or less fit patients are the
group in whom the toxicity comparison against chemotherapy is most one-sided.

Lobular carcinoma has the strongest prior claim on this strategy and the weakest evidence behind
it. Lobular tumours lose E-cadherin and infiltrate in single files rather than forming a discrete
mass. They are almost always receptor-positive, low grade and poorly responsive to cytotoxic
treatment. Both halves of the neoadjuvant argument therefore change at once. No trial has yet
reported a lobular-only comparison of endocrine agents. The one randomised trial named for lobular
disease enrolled any histology, and the only publication on its record is an axillary analysis
[@weiss2025]. The histology argument rests on biology and on subgroups borrowed from elsewhere.

Duration is where practice most often goes wrong, because response accrues over months rather than
weeks. The trials that read out used three to six months of exposure. There is no reason to expect
an eight-week course to produce comparable downstaging. Shortened courses are the commonest
avoidable cause of a disappointing result.

```practice
Fix the goal before the first prescription. Surgical downstaging and measurement of endocrine
sensitivity are different objectives with different stopping rules.

Plan at least four months, reassessing by examination and imaging at intervals rather than waiting
for a fixed date.

Take a baseline Ki67 and a repeat at two to four weeks only if the result will change a decision.
If it will not, the biopsy is not justified by the information.

Treat progression on neoadjuvant endocrine therapy as a significant event rather than an ordinary
one. It is uncommon, and it identifies a tumour unlikely to be controlled by the same drug class
given for the next five years.
```

## BS-6930 Ki67, PEPI and response measures for a setting where pCR fails

```story SQ-0060
```

If the binary endpoint carries little information, something has to replace it. Two measures do,
answering two different questions.

Ki67 measured after two to four weeks of endocrine therapy is a pharmacodynamic read-out. It
reports whether proliferation in that patient's tumour is oestrogen-driven. The strongest evidence
that the measurement carries prognostic weight comes from a trial whose intervention achieved
nothing, which is worked out in [[BS-4700]]. A two-week value separated five-year recurrence risk
across a nearly fivefold range among treated HER2-negative women [@smith2020].

The preoperative endocrine prognostic index answers the other question, which is what to do after
surgery. It combines pathological tumour size, nodal status, Ki67 and oestrogen receptor status
measured in the surgical specimen. It was derived in the P024 trial and validated in IMPACT. A
score of zero identified a group at very low risk of relapse on endocrine therapy alone
[@ellis2008]. That is a prognostic statement and not a predictive one. It does not establish that
chemotherapy would have added nothing, because nobody in either cohort was randomised to find out.

The measurement side deserves as much attention as the biology. Ki67 scoring varies between
laboratories and observers even under a common protocol [@polley2013], which is why successive
working group statements concentrate on method rather than on cut-point [@dowsett2011;
@nielsen2021]. The largest contemporary test of that is worth the arithmetic. In a trial using
post-endocrine Ki67 to allocate treatment, local and central scoring agreed on the binary
endocrine-response call for about four women in five [@hamann2025]. One in five would have been
allocated differently depending on which laboratory read the slide.

Two conventions coexist and they are not interchangeable. Complete cell-cycle arrest is set at a
Ki67 of 2.7% or below, which is a pharmacodynamic threshold used in window studies. Endocrine
response is set at 10% or below, which is an allocation threshold used to direct treatment. Neither
boundary was placed where biology changes. Both were placed where observers could agree.

```caution
A fall in Ki67 is a change in a proliferation marker rather than a change in outcome. The single
trial large enough to separate the two found a measurement that predicted recurrence and a
treatment that prevented none of it [@smith2020]. Reading a suppressed Ki67 as evidence that the
drug is helping that patient runs the two together.
```

## BS-6940 Neoadjuvant CDK4/6 inhibition and window-of-opportunity designs

```story SQ-0070
```

This is the cleanest demonstration in the book that arresting proliferation and eradicating a
tumour are different events.

Adding a CDK4/6 inhibitor to an aromatase inhibitor suppresses proliferation well beyond what
endocrine therapy achieves alone. The randomised trials of palbociclib and of abemaciclib show it
in the same direction and at similar magnitude. Neither produced a clinical response advantage over
the endocrine comparator, with pathological complete responses close to absent in both.

That combination is what a cytostatic agent should produce, because blocking the G1 restriction
point stops cells dividing [[BC-160]]. It does not kill them, so the tumour bed is still cellular
at surgery. A trial judging such a drug by a pathological complete response will call an active
agent inactive.

{{trial:neopal}} makes the point sharpest by disagreeing with itself. Judged on residual cancer
burden, the chemotherapy arm did better. Judged on the preoperative endocrine prognostic index, the
combination arm did better. One randomisation of one set of patients produces opposite rankings
depending on which post-treatment measurement is chosen. Neither measurement has been validated
against outcome in this setting. {{trial:coralleen}} read a third measure, a molecular risk score
on the surgical specimen, and found the two arms level.

The composite index deserves a further warning, because proliferation is one of its own components.
A drug that suppresses proliferation improves that index partly by construction. Reading such an
improvement as evidence of deeper tumour control is circular.

There is a second reason to distrust the arrest threshold once a cell-cycle inhibitor is in the
regimen. Complete cell-cycle arrest was calibrated on tumours given endocrine therapy alone, and it
was built to grade endocrine dependence. A drug that stops the cycle whether or not the tumour
depends on oestrogen crosses that line in nearly everyone. Crossing it then says nothing about the
receptor, which is exactly what destroys its power to select.

One finding from these trials bears directly on adjuvant practice. In {{trial:neomonarch}}, tumours
that had reached complete cell-cycle arrest showed Ki67 rebound when treatment stopped more than
four days before the specimen was taken [@hurvitz2020neomonarch]. Suppression requires continuing
exposure, which makes the discontinuation and adherence questions in [[BS-6880]] questions about
efficacy rather than about administration.

Window-of-opportunity designs sit alongside all of this. Their logic, and the reason a
pharmacodynamic effect does not license an outcome claim, is worked out in [[BS-4700]] using
{{trial:poetic}} and is not repeated here.

## BS-6950 Immunotherapy in hormone receptor-positive early disease

```story SQ-0110
```

Two phase 3 trials have added a checkpoint inhibitor to neoadjuvant chemotherapy in this subtype.
Both raised the pathological complete response rate against placebo while remaining immature for
event-free survival.

Whom they enrolled matters more than what they showed, because both restricted entry to high-grade
disease. One admitted grade 2 tumours only where oestrogen receptor expression fell between 1% and
10% [@loi2025cm7fl]. These are the tumours that behave least like typical luminal disease.
Transferring the result to a grade 1, strongly receptor-positive tumour is not supported by either
trial.

The effect concentrates further inside those populations. In {{trial:checkmate7fl}} the response
increment among PD-L1-positive tumours was roughly double the increment in the trial as a whole.
That is a group defined by a marker rather than a randomised comparison. The marker was scored with
one antibody clone against one cut-off. [[BS-6390]] sets out what such an observation can and
cannot carry.

The endpoint problem now arrives at its sharpest point. A gain in pathological complete response
has been demonstrated in the one subtype where that endpoint is least prognostic [@cortazar2014;
@vonminckwitz2012]. The gain may well translate into fewer recurrences. Nothing published so far
shows that it has.

A practical conflict is already visible, because adjuvant abemaciclib and adjuvant ribociclib are
options for the same high-risk population. Neither is given alongside a checkpoint inhibitor. A
patient eligible for both escalations can have only one of them, and no trial compares the two.
There is also a mechanistic reason to doubt that the two would combine. A single-arm profiling
study found that a CDK4/6 inhibitor on an endocrine backbone diminished adaptive anti-tumour
immunity [@papagiannis2025]. That is a reason for doubt rather than a test of the combination.

The other mechanism being tried in this cell is a conjugate rather than a checkpoint inhibitor,
using HER2 expression below the amplification threshold as an address rather than as a driver.
Nothing on its record can yet be read as a result [[BS-6960]].

```caution
The intuitive reading is that a doubled complete response rate in a poor-prognosis population must
translate into fewer recurrences. In triple-negative disease that reading has been supported by
subsequent survival data. Here the same inference is being drawn in the subtype where the
trial-level link between response and outcome is weakest. Late recurrence also means the follow-up
needed to test it is measured in decades rather than years [[BS-4350]].
```

## BS-6960 Unresolved questions and what reads out next

Four questions are open, and only three of them are being asked by a trial.

The first is whether a response gain translates. Event-free survival from the two checkpoint
inhibitor trials is the readout that will settle whether the argument in [[BS-6950]] was pessimism
or realism. A positive result would make pathological complete response useful in this subtype for
the first time. A null result would confirm that the endpoint measured the wrong thing.

The second is whether endocrine response can take the place of chemotherapy in intermediate-risk
disease. {{trial:adaptcycle}} allocates on a genomic score together with the post-endocrine Ki67
described in [[BS-6930]], so its invasive disease-free survival readout tests the allocation rule
and the treatment at once. That design makes a null result ambiguous, because the rule and the drug
fail together. {{trial:ribolaris}} asks a narrower version of the same question. It routes on a
molecular risk score read at surgery and carries distant metastasis-free survival as its endpoint,
which is the right endpoint and a long wait.

The third is what CDK4/6 inhibition does to a human tumour over months rather than weeks.
{{trial:neoletrib}} samples serially through six months of exposure, so its value is mechanistic
rather than clinical.

The fourth question has no trial behind it. Nobody is testing whether preoperative treatment of any
kind improves outcome over upfront surgery in this subtype. Nobody has prospectively validated a
preoperative endocrine prognostic index of zero as a gate for omitting adjuvant chemotherapy. Both
would change practice. Neither is being studied.

```interplay target=BC-350
Part VI argues that a tumour is a population rather than a value, and this chapter is where that
argument has a bedside consequence for the commonest subtype. Every response measure used here
averages over that population. A complete response asks whether the whole population was
eradicated, which in slowly proliferating disease is almost never true. Residual cancer burden
keeps the variance and is therefore the more informative instrument [@symmans2017]. A two-week Ki67
measures the fraction of the population still cycling under oestrogen deprivation, which is a
statement about composition rather than about size [@smith2020]. Read this way, the low complete
response rate in hormone receptor-positive disease is not a failure of the drugs. It is what
averaging over a heterogeneous, slowly dividing population looks like when the measurement is
forced into two categories.
```
