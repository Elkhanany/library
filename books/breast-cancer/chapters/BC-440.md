## BS-3880 Major drug classes and mechanisms

Cytotoxic drugs are usefully sorted by the lesion they make rather than by the era they arrived
in. The lesion predicts the resistance mechanism and it predicts the dose-limiting toxicity, so
one fact does two jobs.

**Anthracyclines** poison topoisomerase II, intercalate into DNA and generate reactive oxygen
species. The first of those is the antitumour mechanism. The third is the cardiac one, which is
why cardiac risk tracks cumulative dose rather than dose intensity [@cardinale2015].

**Taxanes** stabilise microtubules and arrest cells in mitosis. Neuropathy follows from the same
mechanism acting on axonal transport in long sensory neurons, which is why it is cumulative and
frequently irreversible [@loprinzi2020].

**Non-taxane microtubule agents** destabilise rather than stabilise. Eribulin inhibits microtubule
dynamics through a distinct mode, and it retains activity after taxane exposure [@cortes2011].

**Alkylating agents and platinum salts** create adducts and interstrand crosslinks that require
homologous recombination for repair. That is why germline BRCA status predicts platinum benefit
and why a genomic scar score does not [@tutt2018].

**Antimetabolites** act in S phase. Capecitabine is a prodrug, so its exposure depends on host
enzyme activity rather than on tumour biology. Dihydropyrimidine dehydrogenase deficiency is a
germline property of the patient, and genotype-guided dosing reduces the toxicity that follows
from it [@henricks2018].

The organising claim is that each class has a matched pair of consequences. A drug that damages
DNA is escaped by repair. A drug that acts on the spindle is escaped by efflux or by not dividing.
Neither is escaped the way a targeted agent is, which is why no assay predicts cytotoxic
sensitivity in this disease.

## BS-3890 Regimen selection and scheduling

The adjuvant meta-analyses settle more than the individual trials do, and one result inside them
is almost never quoted.

Adding four cycles of a taxane to a fixed anthracycline-based regimen reduced breast cancer
mortality. The rate ratio was 0.86 with a standard error of 0.04. Some trials counterbalanced
those extra taxane cycles in the control arm with extra cycles of other cytotoxic drugs, roughly
doubling the non-taxane dose. In those trials there was no significant difference, with a rate
ratio of 0.94 and a standard error of 0.06 [@ebctcg2012]. Part of what looks like a taxane class
effect in the first comparison is a total-dose effect.

The same analysis separates regimens by cumulative dose rather than by name. Standard four-cycle
anthracycline and cyclophosphamide was equivalent to standard CMF, with a rate ratio of 0.98.
Anthracycline regimens with substantially higher cumulative dose were superior to standard CMF,
with a rate ratio of 0.78 and a standard error of 0.06 [@ebctcg2012]. Regimen names encode dose,
and comparisons between names are often comparisons between doses.

Schedule is a separate axis from dose. Weekly paclitaxel after standard adjuvant doxorubicin and
cyclophosphamide improved disease-free and overall survival compared with paclitaxel every three
weeks, with an odds ratio for survival of 1.32. Grade 2, 3 or 4 neuropathy was more common on the
weekly schedule, at 27% against 20% [@sparano2008]. More frequent administration of a spindle
agent buys efficacy and costs nerve.

Platinum is a selection question rather than a scheduling question in advanced triple-negative
disease, and the selection that works is germline BRCA status [@tutt2018].

## BS-3900 Dose intensity, dose density, and dose modification

Three quantities are routinely conflated and they come apart in practice. Dose intensity is dose
per unit time. Dose density is the same total dose delivered at shorter intervals. Cumulative dose
is the total received over the course. A regimen can raise one and lower another.

The randomised demonstration compared dose-dense and conventionally scheduled adjuvant therapy in
node-positive disease. Dose-dense treatment improved disease-free survival, with a risk ratio of
0.74, and overall survival, with a risk ratio of 0.69. Four-year disease-free survival was 82%
against 75%. There was no difference between concurrent and sequential administration and no
interaction between density and sequence. Severe neutropenia was less frequent on the dose-dense
regimens than on the conventional ones [@citron2003]. The last of those is counterintuitive and it
is the reason the strategy was adoptable.

The patient-level meta-analysis puts a size on it. Data were available for 37,298 of 40,070 women
randomised in 26 trials. Ten-year recurrence risk was 28.0% with dose-intense treatment against
31.4% with standard scheduling, giving a rate ratio of 0.86 and a 95% confidence interval of 0.82
to 0.89. Ten-year breast cancer mortality was 18.9% against 21.3%, and all-cause mortality was
22.1% against 24.8%. In the seven trials comparing two-weekly with three-weekly delivery of the
same chemotherapy, ten-year recurrence was 24.0% against 28.3% among 10,004 women [@ebctcg2019].

The objection this is usually met with is that intensification kills people. The meta-analysis
answers it directly. Ten-year risk of death without recurrence was 4.1% with dose-intense
treatment and 4.6% with standard scheduling, with a rate ratio of 0.88 and an interval of 0.78 to
0.99 [@ebctcg2019]. Mortality from causes other than breast cancer was not higher.

Dose modification is where planned intensity is lost silently. A reduction made during a toxicity
episode commonly persists after the toxicity has resolved. The reasoning for treating delivered
dose as a standing question rather than an assumption is in [[BS-3040]].

## BS-3910 Neoadjuvant and adjuvant applications

The drugs are the same and the information is not. Preoperative treatment converts a course of
chemotherapy into an experiment with a tissue readout, and the readout is what later escalation is
built on [[term:rcb]].

Three trials instantiate the same structure with three different classes. In {{trial:create-x}},
capecitabine after residual invasive disease improved five-year disease-free survival, with a
hazard ratio for recurrence, second cancer or death of 0.70 and a 95% confidence interval of 0.53
to 0.92. Five-year overall survival was 89.2% against 83.6%, with a hazard ratio for death of
0.59. In the triple-negative subgroup, disease-free survival was 69.8% against 56.1% and overall
survival was 78.8% against 70.3% [@masuda2017]. In {{trial:katherine}}, a conjugate replaced
trastuzumab after residual HER2-positive disease [@vonminckwitz2019] [[BC-460]]. In
{{trial:keynote522}}, checkpoint blockade was added before surgery rather than after
[@schmid2020; @schmid2024k522os] [[BC-470]].

The common element is worth naming, because later chapters instantiate it repeatedly. Observed
response to treatment is a stronger selection variable in this disease than any baseline
biomarker currently available. The de-escalation and escalation framework that follows from that
is set out in [[BC-400]].

Two cautions belong with it. Pathologic complete response is defined by a sampling protocol, so
how much of the bed was examined affects the answer. And a trial that escalates only non-responders
tells you nothing about whether the escalation would have helped the responders.

## BS-3920 Metastatic use and cumulative exposure

The goal changes and so does the arithmetic. Sequential single agents are preferred over
combinations unless a rapid response is needed for organ function, because combinations raise
response rate and toxicity without reliably extending survival.

The benchmark for late-line single-agent chemotherapy is lower than most conversations assume.
One randomised trial compared sacituzumab govitecan with physician's choice of eribulin,
vinorelbine, capecitabine or gemcitabine in relapsed metastatic triple-negative disease. Median
progression-free survival on the chemotherapy arm was 1.7 months in 233 patients, with a 95%
confidence interval of 1.5 to 2.6 [@bardia2021ascent]. That figure is the comparator against which
newer classes should be read.

Eribulin is the one agent with a survival result in heavily pretreated disease. Among 762 women
randomised 2:1, median overall survival was 13.1 months with eribulin against 10.6 months with
physician's choice. The hazard ratio for death was 0.81, with a 95% confidence interval of 0.66 to
0.99. Peripheral neuropathy was the commonest reason for discontinuing eribulin, in 24 of 503
patients treated [@cortes2011].

Cumulative exposure constrains the sequence in three ways. Anthracycline cardiac risk tracks
lifetime dose [@cardinale2015]. Taxane neuropathy accumulates and often does not resolve
[@loprinzi2020]. And conjugates now occupy lines that single-agent chemotherapy used to occupy, so
cumulative payload exposure has become a version of the same constraint in a new class
[[BS-4080]].

Body surface area is a poor proxy for the volume in which a drug distributes. Sarcopenia was a
determinant of chemotherapy toxicity and of time to tumour progression in metastatic breast cancer
[@prado2009], which means two patients with the same calculated dose can receive quite different
exposures.

## BS-3930 Resistance mechanisms

Cytotoxic resistance is less often a single lesion than targeted-agent resistance, which is the
main reason no assay predicts it. Four mechanisms account for most of it and they are not mutually
exclusive.

Efflux moves drug out of the cell. The transporters that do it, including MDR1 and ABCC1, are the
same ones that defeat conjugate payloads [@li2018; @loganzo2015], which is why conjugate and
chemotherapy resistance are not independent problems.

Target alteration changes what the drug acts on. Tubulin isotype composition alters taxane
binding, and topoisomerase II level alters anthracycline effect. Neither is measured routinely.

Repair capacity determines what a DNA lesion costs. Restoration of homologous recombination
removes platinum sensitivity and PARP inhibitor sensitivity together [@sakai2008], and the
detailed argument is in [[BS-3010]].

Drug-tolerant states account for the rest and they are not genetic. A small subpopulation survives
through a reversible chromatin-mediated state [@sharma2010]. Cells in that state acquire a
different dependency, which is itself targetable [@hangauer2017]. Rare cell variability precedes
and predicts which cells will reprogram [@shaffer2017]. Persisters cycle rather than simply
resting [@oren2021]. Metabolic state contributes, with MYC and MCL1 cooperating to sustain
chemotherapy-resistant populations through mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation [@lee2017].

One immune-adjacent mechanism belongs here rather than in [[BC-470]]. CD73 promotes anthracycline
resistance and poor prognosis in triple-negative disease [@loi2013], which means a single
molecule links cytotoxic escape and adenosine-mediated immune suppression.

The taxonomy that puts these in clinical order, and distinguishes a population that was never
sensitive from one that was made resistant, is in [[BS-2960]].

## BS-3940 Toxicity mitigation and supportive standards

Four areas have evidence worth acting on, and they differ in how much they change.

**Myelosuppression.** Growth factor support makes shorter intervals deliverable, and the
randomised evidence is that severe neutropenia was less frequent on supported dose-dense regimens
than on conventional ones [@citron2003]. The supportive drug changed which schedules were
possible rather than making an existing schedule safer.

**Alopecia.** Scalp cooling has two prospective datasets. In a randomised trial stopped early for
efficacy, hair was preserved in 48 of 95 women who received cooling, which is 50.5%, and in 0 of
47 who did not [@nangia2017]. In a prospective cohort, hair loss of 50% or less occurred in 67 of
101 evaluable women with cooling and 0 of 16 controls. No participant in the cooling group in that
study received an anthracycline [@rugo2017]. That exclusion is the reading caution, because it is
the anthracycline-containing regimens where patients most want the intervention.

**Neuropathy.** There is no established preventive agent, so dose modification and schedule choice
are the intervention [@loprinzi2020]. Telling a patient that nothing prevents it, early, is more
useful than adding a supplement.

**Host genotype.** Fluoropyrimidine toxicity is partly predictable before the first dose, and
genotype-guided dosing based on DPYD reduces it [@henricks2018]. This is one of the few places in
this chapter where a pre-treatment test changes a decision.

```practice
Three questions are worth asking before each cycle, and none of them is on a standard toxicity
form.

What proportion of the planned dose has actually been delivered so far, counting reductions and
delays. Planned intensity and received intensity diverge quietly [[BS-3040]].

Is the current reduction still needed. Reductions made for a resolved episode tend to persist for
the rest of the course.

Which toxicity is cumulative and which is not. Neuropathy and cardiac injury accumulate, so a
tolerable cycle four is not evidence about cycle eight. Myelosuppression does not accumulate the
same way, so it can be managed cycle by cycle.

Cardiac surveillance belongs in the first year after anthracycline exposure. In a cohort of 2625
patients, 98% of cardiotoxicity events occurred within that year. Median onset was 3.5 months
after the end of chemotherapy. Recovery was more likely when heart failure therapy was started
early [@cardinale2015].
```
