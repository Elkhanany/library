## BS-5100 HER2-low and HER2-ultralow disease

Three categories now sit inside what the guideline still calls HER2-negative disease, and all
three are assay outputs rather than entities.

HER2-low means immunohistochemistry 1+, or 2+ with negative in situ hybridisation. HER2-ultralow
means immunohistochemistry 0 with faint incomplete membrane staining in a small proportion of
cells. HER2-negative without qualification means everything below that. The categories were
created by trial eligibility criteria rather than by a finding about biology [@wolff2023].
[[term:her2-low]]

Prevalence follows from where the lines were drawn. Roughly a third to a half of all breast
cancers score HER2-low, and among HER2-negative tumours the proportion is higher in hormone
receptor-positive than in triple-negative disease [@tarantino2020].

The question underneath all of this is whether the amount of antigen selects anyone, or whether
the antigen only has to be there. The story below states what each group of trials was built to
find out, and it carries no figures at all.

The studies in that table agree on one thing. Activity does not stop at the bottom of the
expression scale. In both randomised comparisons trastuzumab deruxtecan outperformed the
physician's choice of chemotherapy in hormone receptor-positive disease. In the single-arm cohort
study it produced responses in tumours the assay scored as non-expressing [@mosele2023].

Where they diverge, the antigen is not the reason. {{trial:destiny-b04}} enrolled after one or two
lines of chemotherapy [@modi2022]. {{trial:destiny-b06}} enrolled patients who had never had
chemotherapy for metastatic disease, after endocrine therapy [@bardia2024destinyb06]. The same
drug was therefore tested at two positions in the disease, against whatever a physician would
choose at each. Each result describes the position it was measured at rather than the score that
governed entry. {{trial:daisy}} differs again, in design rather than in population alone. It is
single-arm, its endpoint is response, and its cohorts are expression strata [@mosele2023].
{{trial:trade-dxd}} enters patients already progressing on a conjugate, which is a fourth
population and a different question.

The decision rests on DESTINY-Breast04. It is the randomised comparison against the treatment the
clinic would otherwise give. It is also the only study here with overall survival on the record
[@modi2022; @modi2025]. DESTINY-Breast06 changed who is eligible and when the drug is given. Its
survival data were immature at the primary analysis. Giving a conjugate before any chemotherapy
rests on a progression-free survival result alone.

What nobody did is randomise anyone on the score. The ultralow group in DESTINY-Breast06 was an
exploratory cohort recruited under a rule rather than an arm. Its result tracked the HER2-low
population it sat beside [@bardia2024destinyb06]. DAISY's response falls as staining falls, but
each cohort received the same drug with no comparator inside it. The gradient therefore cannot say
whether the advantage over chemotherapy narrows as expression falls. That is the missing column. A
patient and her clinician want the gain relative to her own alternative, and no design here
measures it by expression.

The same question put to a different antigen came back differently. {{trial:tropics02}} and
{{trial:tropion-b01}} imposed no expression requirement at entry. Their results are argued in
[[BC-890]]. Where TROP2 was measured three ways in {{trial:saci-io-hr}}, by immunohistochemistry,
by immunofluorescence and in plasma, no measure tracked outcome. An antigen acquires a threshold
when somebody draws one, not when the drug demands it.

Toxicity belongs beside efficacy here, because the table has no column for it. Adjudicated
interstitial lung disease or pneumonitis occurred in 11.3% of patients given trastuzumab
deruxtecan in DESTINY-Breast06, against one patient given chemotherapy [@bardia2024destinyb06].
That is the quantity to weigh against a progression-free survival gain in a patient who has not
yet had chemotherapy.

## BS-5110 Antigen thresholds as eligibility criteria, the general lesson

The structure of this problem recurs across the field, and it is worth setting out in the abstract
before returning to HER2.

A drug's activity varies continuously with a target that is continuously distributed across
tumours. A trial needs a binary rule to decide who enters. So a cut-point is placed on the
continuum. Everything downstream inherits it. The label, the reimbursement decision, the reported
efficacy, the patient's access, and the number a pathologist has to produce all rest on that one
line.

Three separate questions then get collapsed into one.

Does the drug work better at higher target expression? For trastuzumab deruxtecan, response falls
as the score falls, and the cohort gradient in the table above is the measurement [@mosele2023].
That is a gradient in response rate across single-arm cohorts rather than a gradient in benefit
over chemotherapy.

Is there a level below which it does not work? Not established. Activity at immunohistochemistry 0
is well above zero. Within that group the amount of ERBB2 expression did not separate responders
from non-responders [@mosele2023].

Can observers reliably place a case on one side of the line? No. When 18 breast pathologists from
15 institutions scored 170 breast cancer biopsies, agreement on cases scored immunohistochemistry
0 was 25%, with a Fleiss kappa of 0.49. Agreement across the full four-category system was 28.8%.
On the question the assay was built to answer, 3+ against not 3+, agreement reached 87.1%
[@robbins2023].

That third answer is the one to say out loud rather than to imply. A treatment decision affecting
a large number of patients now rests on an assay boundary that trained specialist observers
reproduce about a quarter of the time. This is the clearest case in oncology of a therapeutic
eligibility line drawn where reproducibility is poorest. The evidence that the line marks a real
difference in drug behaviour is weak.

If the assay cannot place the case, the obvious response is to measure the target some other way.
That has been tried, and what came back is argued in [[BS-4980]].

The guideline is unusually candid about the same problem. The 2023 ASCO and College of American
Pathologists update affirmed the existing recommendations and declined to create new result
categories, calling it premature to do so [@wolff2023]. It acknowledged in the same document that
the distinction between 0 and 1+ is now clinically relevant, because of the entry criteria of the
trial that supported regulatory approval. The analytic validity framework is in [[BC-110]] and
[[BS-0930]]. The same problem read as a heterogeneity problem is [[BS-2360]].

The lesson generalises to every threshold of this kind. PD-L1 combined positive score at 10
governs first-line immunotherapy in triple-negative disease [[BS-5030]]. An androgen receptor
percentage governed eligibility in a trial that set its own threshold [[BS-5070]]. Ki67 governs
risk categories [[BS-0950]]. In each case the same three questions apply, and in each case the
third is the one nobody asks.

Trop-2 is the instructive counterexample, because somebody checked. In the biomarker analysis of
{{trial:ascent}}, sacituzumab govitecan outperformed chemotherapy across high, medium and low
Trop-2 expression, so no threshold was imposed [@bardia2021ascentbio]. An antigen whose benefit
does not require a cut-point does not acquire one.

```caution
A single immunohistochemistry 0 result is weak evidence that a patient is ineligible for a
HER2-directed conjugate. Two legitimate actions follow and they differ from each other. Asking
whether the slide was read with the 0 against 1+ distinction specifically in mind is a request
that the assay answer the question being asked of it. Testing a different or more recent specimen
is a request for a different sample. Repeating the same test on the same block until it crosses
the line is neither, and the difference is whether a reason for the retest existed before it was
ordered [[BS-5150]].
```

## BS-5120 Germline and somatic DNA repair alterations

Germline BRCA1 and BRCA2 pathogenic variants identify a population in which an oral PARP inhibitor
outperforms chemotherapy. The two registration trials entered HER2-negative advanced disease and
selected on the germline mutation alone.

The question that followed was how far out from those two genes the benefit extends. The story
below is what the trials were built to settle, and it carries no figures.

```story SQ-0600
```

```evidence setting=metastatic subtype=BRCA modality=parp sort=year cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,endpoint,result,os caption=PARP_inhibitor_trials_entered_on_a_DNA_repair_alteration_rather_than_on_a_line_of_treatment._The_two_registration_trials_sit_alongside_the_studies_that_went_looking_for_a_wider_eligible_population.
```

{{trial:olympiad}} and {{trial:embraca}} are read together and they agree [@robson2017;
@litton2018]. In germline BRCA1 or BRCA2 carriers with HER2-negative disease, an oral PARP
inhibitor delays progression and produces responses more often than the single-agent chemotherapy
a physician would otherwise choose. Neither converted that into longer overall survival
[@robson2019; @litton2020]. The EMBRACA investigators repeated the survival analysis with
adjustment for later PARP inhibitor and platinum exposure, and the estimate moved towards benefit
without excluding no difference. Crossover is a candidate explanation that the trials cannot test,
and the general form of that problem is in [[BS-7740]].

{{trial:brocade3}} reads as the weaker result in the table and is a different experiment
[@diras2020]. Its control arm is carboplatin with paclitaxel. It therefore asks what veliparib
adds to a platinum doublet, not whether PARP inhibition beats the alternative to chemotherapy. The
dose was one a full-dose doublet tolerates, and it was raised only when chemotherapy stopped. A
combination design of that shape trades target engagement for haematological headroom. Reading its
hazard ratio beside OlympiAD's sets a drug-against-chemotherapy result next to a
drug-added-to-chemotherapy result. The drug is not the variable that differs.

{{trial:tbcrc048}} went outside germline BRCA, and the useful part of its answer is which genes
did not respond [@tung2020; @tung2026a]. Confirmed responses were confined to germline PALB2 and
to somatic BRCA1 or BRCA2. Carriers of ATM or CHEK2 alterations alone had none.
{{trial:talazoparib-beyond-brca}} gave talazoparib to patients wild-type for BRCA1 and BRCA2 who
carried a mutation in another repair gene. Its activity again concentrated in germline PALB2
[@gruber2022]. A homologous recombination gene list is not one biomarker. It is several, and they
do not behave alike.

Two cautions travel with that gradient. The cohorts hold a few patients per gene, so a gene that
looks inert is as uncertain as a gene that looks active. And the TBCRC 048 row carries a BRCA
subtype tag although most of its patients had hormone receptor-positive, HER2-negative disease.
The row reads as a germline BRCA result. That is a filing convention rather than a population.

What no reported trial here has done is randomise a PARP inhibitor against chemotherapy outside
germline BRCA. Somatic BRCA and germline PALB2 are treated on response rates from single arms,
against a comparator that exists only in the germline trials. {{trial:evopar-breast01}} does not
close that gap, and it asks the question the clinic now has instead. It sets a PARP1-selective
inhibitor with an oral SERD against a CDK4/6 inhibitor with endocrine therapy in the first line,
in disease carrying a germline or somatic BRCA1, BRCA2 or PALB2 alteration. That is the comparison
which decides whether a repair defect displaces the endocrine sequence or waits behind it. It has
not reported.

[[term:hrd]] scores are a different kind of measurement and a common source
of error. They record genomic scarring accumulated in the past. Scars persist after repair
function returns, so a tumour can score high while repairing normally at the moment of testing. The score predicts response to platinum-containing neoadjuvant chemotherapy in
triple-negative disease [@telli2016], which is a validated use. It is not a measurement of current
repair capacity.

Resistance follows the same logic in reverse. Secondary intragenic mutations restoring the BRCA2
reading frame confer resistance to platinum and to PARP inhibition alike [@sakai2008], because
both exploit the same defect.

Germline testing carries a second purpose that somatic testing does not, which is the implication
for relatives. That is a reason to order it early rather than when a treatment decision forces it
[[BC-070]].

## BS-5130 Rare actionable mutations and fusions

Activating HER2 mutations occur in tumours without HER2 amplification and are genuine oncogenic
drivers [@bose2013]. This is a different disease from HER2-positive breast cancer and it is
routinely filed under the same heading, which produces the wrong drug. The agent that works is an
irreversible pan-HER kinase inhibitor rather than an antibody. In a basket study, neratinib
produced responses across HER2-mutant and HER3-mutant cancers, with the rate depending on the
specific allele [@hyman2018].

```story SQ-0580
```

```evidence setting=metastatic subtype=HR+/HER2- modality=her2 topic=HER2-mutant weight=any sort=year cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,endpoint,result caption=Trials_entered_on_an_activating_HER2_mutation_in_disease_the_standard_assays_call_HER2-negative._All_three_are_single-arm_in_substance,_and_all_three_pair_a_HER2_inhibitor_with_endocrine_therapy.
```

Every cohort in that table selected on the mutation. None required amplification and none required
protein overexpression. All three paired a HER2 inhibitor with endocrine therapy in hormone
receptor-positive disease, and all three produced activity. Where response was the endpoint, it
occurred across kinase-domain and extracellular-domain mutations [@okines2025], and in lobular as
well as ductal disease [@jhaveri2023summit]. Selecting on the mutation finds a treatable
population the amplification assay cannot see.

The result columns are not three measurements of the same object. {{trial:muther}} reports a
clinical benefit rate, which counts durable stable disease. Its cohorts are strata of oestrogen
receptor status and previous fulvestrant exposure rather than of mutation [@ma2022].
{{trial:summit}} and {{trial:sgntuc-019}} report objective response. SGNTUC-019 is one cohort of a
solid-tumour basket and gives its intervals at 90% confidence, which is not the convention the
rest of this registry uses [@okines2025]. Two of the three used neratinib and one used tucatinib.
The drug is the smallest of the differences between them, and ranking the three by result column
ranks three different things.

{{trial:summit}} carries the decision, and not because of its response rate [@jhaveri2023summit].
It is the only study here with anything randomised inside it. Neither fulvestrant alone nor
fulvestrant with trastuzumab produced a response. Patients crossing over to the triplet responded.
That is what attributes the effect to the kinase inhibitor rather than to endocrine therapy or to
antibody blockade of an unamplified receptor. The randomised substudy is small and allowed
crossover, so it fixes the direction and settles nothing about magnitude.

Nobody randomised any of this against the chemotherapy these patients would otherwise be offered.
That is the comparison the table cannot show and the one the choice actually turns on. Mutations
are pooled for the reported result within each trial. MutHER saw clinical benefit associated with
lobular histology and against L755 alterations, in an exploratory analysis far too small to act
on. It also found acquired HER2 mutations at progression [@ma2022]. Allele-level selection is a
hypothesis here rather than a rule. HER2-negative status was also assigned locally in these
studies, so the population is defined by whichever laboratory reported the case.

NTRK fusions are the tumour-agnostic example. Larotrectinib produced an overall response rate of
75% by independent review, with a 95% confidence interval of 61% to 85%, in 55 patients with 17
different TRK fusion-positive tumour types [@drilon2018]. The relevance to breast cancer is
limited by prevalence rather than by the size of the effect. These fusions are very rare outside
secretory carcinoma, so the pre-test probability determines whether looking for them is sensible.

PIK3CA, AKT1 and PTEN alterations form the largest actionable group in this disease and they
belong to [[BC-560]], where the trials and the sequencing are set out [@andre2019;
@turner2023capivasertib]. ESR1 mutations belong there too, and the selection that produces them is
described in [[BS-2490]].

The organising principle is that actionable is not a binary property. A tiering framework ranks
alterations by the strength of the evidence linking them to benefit in a specific tumour type. The
breast-specific application of that framework separates alterations with randomised support from
those resting on case reports [@condorelli2019].

The practical rule follows from prevalence rather than from biology. A rare alteration is worth
looking for when a panel is already being run for another reason, or when histology raises the
pre-test probability, as lobular histology does for HER2 mutation [[BS-4900]].

## BS-5140 Tumor-agnostic biomarkers

A tumour-agnostic approval says that a biomarker predicts benefit across tissue of origin. It does
not say that the biomarker is common in any particular disease, and in breast cancer that
distinction does most of the work.

Mismatch repair deficiency was the first. Objective radiographic responses occurred in 53% of
patients with advanced mismatch repair-deficient cancers across 12 tumour types treated with PD-1
blockade, and complete responses in 21% [@le2017]. The proposed mechanism is that defective
mismatch repair generates a large burden of mutant neoantigens.

High tumour mutational burden followed. A prospective biomarker analysis within a multi-cohort
phase 2 study set the threshold at 10 mutations per megabase [@marabelle2020]. Objective responses
to pembrolizumab occurred in 30 of 102 patients above it, which is 29%, against 43 of 688 patients
below it, which is 6%.

That study is worth reading for what it did not include. The eligible tumour types were anal,
biliary, cervical, endometrial, mesothelioma, neuroendocrine, salivary, small-cell lung, thyroid
and vulvar [@marabelle2020]. Breast cancer was not among them. The tumour-agnostic claim reaches
breast cancer by inference rather than by measurement, and that should be said when the option is
offered to a patient.

The inference has since been tested in breast cancer directly. The story below is what those
studies set out to do, and it carries no figures.

```story SQ-0620
```

The three studies behind that question do not point the same way, and the disagreement is about
what each one selected on rather than about the drugs.

{{trial:nimbus}} is the study built on the burden argument [@barrososousa2025]. It gave nivolumab
with low-dose ipilimumab to HER2-negative disease entered on a tumour mutational burden above a
fixed threshold, with a prespecified correlative analysis at a higher one. Responses occurred.
They were commoner above the higher cut-off. Two things stop that from selecting a patient in
clinic. The trial does not report how many of its patients had hormone receptor-positive disease,
so no result for this subtype reads out of it. And the threshold analysis rests on a handful of
patients and was a correlative outcome rather than a randomised comparison. It nominates a
cut-off. It does not validate one.

{{trial:saci-io-hr}} is the randomised comparison, and it did not select on burden at all
[@garridocastro2026]. Adding pembrolizumab to a TROP2 conjugate in hormone receptor-positive,
HER2-negative disease did not improve progression-free survival. Its PD-L1-defined subgroup gives
a direction rather than a result. That analysis was secondary, confined to the patients with
evaluable tissue, and not significant. This is the load-bearing study here, because it is the only
one that set checkpoint blockade against the treatment it was being added to.

{{trial:dart-metaplastic}} cuts the other way [@adams2021]. A rare-histology cohort of a basket
study produced few responses, and those it produced were durable. They arose in tumours with low
burden, low PD-L1 and absent tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes, which is the opposite of what the
burden argument predicts. The report gives no hormone receptor or HER2 status, so the cohort
cannot be placed in this subtype at all. Selection was on histology, and histology appears to be
doing the work.

Burden is also a property of the panel that counts it. A figure expressed as mutations per
megabase depends on the size of the panel, on what it calls a somatic variant, and on how germline
variants are filtered out. It travels between assays less well than it reads on a report. A
patient is not above or below ten on a biological scale. She is above or below ten on one
laboratory's.

NTRK fusions complete the set [@drilon2018], with the prevalence caveat in [[BS-5130]].

The breast-specific arithmetic governs whether to test. High tumour mutational burden occurs in a
small minority of breast cancers and is concentrated in particular contexts rather than
distributed evenly [@barrososousa2020]. Mismatch repair deficiency is rarer still. A test with a
low positive rate is worth running as part of a panel ordered for other reasons, and is hard to
justify as a standalone investigation.

One reading habit is worth acquiring here. When a tumour-agnostic result is quoted, look for the
number of patients with the disease in front of you. The per-disease denominator inside a basket
trial is frequently in single figures, and a response rate pooled across tissue types is not a
response rate in any one of them.

## BS-5150 Discordant or changing biomarker results

A result that disagrees with a previous result has three possible explanations and they call for
different responses. True biological change. Assay variation, including antibody clone, platform,
fixation and scoring. Sampling, because two specimens are two different pieces of tissue
[[BC-290]].

HER2-low is the most unstable of the categories now governing eligibility. Across 547 patients
with matched primary and relapse specimens, overall HER2 discordance was 38.0%. Movement from
HER2-0 to HER2-low occurred in 15% of cases and from HER2-low to HER2-0 in 14% [@miglietta2021].
Two movements of similar size in opposite directions are what measurement variation looks like.
Some of it is certainly real change, and that design cannot separate the two components.

Hormone receptor and HER2 conversion between primary and distant metastasis is the
better-characterised version of the same problem [@schrijver2018].

Plasma and tissue disagree for reasons that are not error. A lesion that does not shed is
invisible to plasma. A plasma result integrates across lesions and cannot say which one it came
from. And variants arising in blood cells rather than in tumour appear in plasma unless matched
white cell sequencing removes them [@razavi2019]. The blind spots on each side are set out in
[[BC-390]].

The rule that follows is decision-theoretic rather than biological, and saying so is better than
pretending otherwise. When two adequate specimens disagree and the result governs eligibility,
acting on the one that opens a treatment option is usually right. The justification is asymmetric
consequences, not a claim about which measurement was correct.

```caution
That rule sits one step away from testing repeatedly until a result crosses a threshold, and the
two are not the same thing. The difference is whether a reason for the repeat existed before the
repeat was ordered. A newer specimen, a different lesion, a change in disease behaviour and a
laboratory reporting with the relevant boundary in mind are all reasons. Dissatisfaction with the
previous result is not.
```

## BS-5160 Competing actionable targets and how to prioritize

The common situation is not an absence of targets. It is several at once. One report can carry a
PIK3CA mutation, HER2-low expression, a germline BRCA variant and a fusion of unknown significance
on the same page.

Five criteria order them, and applying them in sequence is better than weighting them informally.

Evidence level for that alteration in this disease. A tiering framework exists for exactly this
purpose and it separates randomised support from anecdote [@condorelli2019].

Magnitude and durability of the expected benefit, with the comparator named. A hazard ratio
against the physician's choice of chemotherapy is not measured on the same scale as a response
rate in a single-arm cohort of germline PALB2 carriers. The tables under [[BS-5100]] and
[[BS-5120]] carry both quantities, and ranking one against the other requires saying which each
is.

Whether the option is line-restricted. An agent available only before chemotherapy is lost by
giving chemotherapy first, which is a reason to sequence it earlier rather than a reason to prefer
it.

Toxicity against the patient's current function. Interstitial lung disease on a deruxtecan
conjugate is the example that changes most decisions, and its frequency is argued in [[BS-5100]].
That is a different proposition in a patient with baseline lung disease than in one without.

Whether the alteration is truncal or subclonal. A target present in a fraction of cells leaves a
compartment untouched, which is the argument in [[BC-280]] and [[BC-300]]. Variant allele fraction
in plasma gives a crude indication and tissue gives a better one.

A molecular tumour board exists to do this ordering, and the reporting standards that make it
possible are in [[BS-3430]].

```practice
Order the full panel once and early, rather than sequentially as options run out [[BS-5020]].

Write the competing options as a list with the comparator attached to each, before discussing
them with the patient.

Say which option is being deferred and why, so that the deferral is a recorded decision rather
than an omission.

Re-check a panel result against the most recent specimen before acting on an alteration found
years earlier [[BS-5150]].
```

```interplay target=BC-350
This chapter is the heterogeneity argument of Part VI arriving as an eligibility form. Every
biomarker on that form is a summary statistic over a structured population. An
immunohistochemistry score is a mean over cells that vary. A variant allele fraction is a
proportion over clones that vary. A panel result is a snapshot of a population that is changing
under the treatment the patient is currently receiving. The thresholds applied to those summaries
were chosen for reproducibility and for trial design, not because biology changes at those values
[[BS-2250]]. Treating the form as a description of the tumour, rather than as a set of
measurements with known failure modes, is the error this part of the book is written against.
```
