## BS-7970 The biology this part assumes

This chapter carries no figures. It is the spine of the part, and the chapters
after it are the evidence.

The defining fact of this subtype is a definition by exclusion. Three assays are
negative, and the category is what remains. That is a statement about
measurement rather than about biology, and almost everything difficult in this
part follows from it. [[BC-120]] is the chapter that takes the category apart,
and [[BC-110]] is the one about the thresholds that draw its boundary.

Three consequences matter here.

**The category is heterogeneous because nothing selected for homogeneity.**
Tumours with different transcriptional programmes, different immune
infiltration and different repair capacity all satisfy the same three negatives.
Trials enrol the category, so a positive trial is a statement about a mixture.
[[BC-100]] carries the molecular classifications that cut it differently.

**The disease is fast, and the timing of that speed is the clinical fact.**
Proliferation is high, recurrence is early, and the risk falls rather than
persists. A patient who has not recurred by five years is in a different
position from one with hormone receptor-positive disease at the same point.
[[BC-330]] is the chapter about why the two curves have different shapes.

**Speed is also what makes the neoadjuvant setting work here.** Response is
common, it arrives quickly, and it carries more prognostic information in this
subtype than in hormone receptor-positive disease. That is the structural reason
the platform is used so heavily in this part, and [[BC-540]] argues what the
setting is for.

The three things that turned out to be present, after a category defined by
absence, are the organising story of the part. Repair deficiency, in the
germline and beyond it [[BC-180]]. An immune microenvironment that can be
engaged [[BC-210]]. And surface antigens that a conjugate can reach, whether or
not the tumour depends on them [[BC-460]].

## BS-7980 What the subtype does to the treatment decision

**Chemotherapy is the backbone and remains so.** In the other two parts the
backbone is targeted and chemotherapy is the thing being spared. Here it is the
other way round, and every addition is an addition to cytotoxic treatment. That
changes what a trial is testing and what its toxicity means.

**The three positives are not alternatives. They are different questions asked
of different patients.** Germline status is inherited and binary. Immune
infiltration is continuous, scored on an assay, and measured differently by
different tests. Antigen expression is continuous and may not need to be high.
Reading them as three branches of one algorithm is the commonest error in this
part.

**A biomarker threshold in this subtype is usually an eligibility criterion
rather than a biological boundary.** The clearest case is the assay used to
select for checkpoint blockade, where the threshold moved and the populations of
two trials therefore differ in a way that has nothing to do with the drug.
[[BC-580]] carries that argument and [[BC-110]] carries the measurement.

**Early and advanced disease diverge more sharply here than elsewhere.** In
early disease the aim is cure and the evidence supports escalation. In advanced
disease the disease is usually incurable and the record is a sequence of modest
gains. The same drug class can be standard in one setting and marginal in the
other, and this is not an inconsistency.

## BS-7990 The spine, from diagnosis to last line

**Almost all early disease of any size is treated before surgery.** The reason
is the platform rather than the tumour: preoperative treatment turns an
unmeasurable question into a measurable one, and what is found at surgery
directs what follows. [[BC-510]] frames the early decision.

**The preoperative regimen now includes checkpoint blockade for most patients,
and what that settled is narrower than it is often read to be.** [[BC-860]] is
the chapter, and it separates what the evidence establishes from the four things
it did not.

**Residual disease after preoperative chemotherapy is the highest-yield finding
in this part.** It identifies a group whose risk is high and whose treatment
changes because of it. [[BC-850]] carries the post-neoadjuvant decision, and it
is the place where germline status stops being an epidemiological fact and
becomes a treatment allocation.

**Advanced disease begins with the three tests.** Germline status, the immune
assay and antigen expression are established before the first line where
possible, because each opens a different door and two of them close if the first
line is chosen without them. [[BC-580]] is the decision chapter and states the
order in which to send them.

**First line is chemoimmunotherapy for the assay-positive group and
chemotherapy for the rest**, with a PARP inhibitor as the alternative for
germline carriers. [[BC-920]] carries the record from first line onwards.

**After first line the part becomes a conjugate sequence.** Two antigens, more
than two drugs, and an unsettled question about what to do when one has already
failed. [[BC-920]] argues it and [[BC-590]] carries the general form of the
antigen-threshold problem it depends on.

## BS-8000 Where this part disagrees with itself

**The early and advanced chapters price checkpoint blockade differently.** In
[[BC-860]] it is standard for most of the preoperative population regardless of
the immune assay. In [[BC-580]] and [[BC-920]] it is confined to the
assay-positive group. The settings really do differ, and the honest statement is
that the early result does not license the advanced use.

**The category is used and criticised in the same part.** [[BC-120]] argues that
triple-negative disease is not one thing. Every trial in [[BC-920]] enrolled it
as though it were. The part does not resolve this, because the trials do not,
and the resolution would require trials that have not been run.

**Germline status changes the drug in some chapters and the risk in others.**
[[BC-850]] treats it as an allocation criterion after residual disease.
[[BC-070]] treats it as an inherited risk with implications for the family. Both
are correct and they are not the same use of the same fact.

**Response means two different things across the part.** In [[BC-860]] a
pathological complete response is a prognostic endpoint strong enough to allocate
further treatment. In [[BC-920]] a radiological response is a description of
tumour shrinkage with a weaker relationship to how long the patient lives. The
word is the same and the inferential weight is not.
