## BS-4610 Diagnostic confirmation and restaging

Biopsy before treating. A new nodule in a treated breast or on a chest wall has several possible
explanations and only one of them is recurrence. Fat necrosis, post-radiation change, a suture
granuloma, a new primary and a non-breast malignancy all present in similar ways on imaging.

The distinction between a true recurrence and a new ipsilateral primary is made on location
relative to the original tumour bed, on histology and on receptor profile. It is frequently not
resolvable. It matters less for the immediate treatment decision than the literature implies, and
more for the prognosis that should be quoted to the patient.

Restaging is justified here for the same reason as in [[BS-4540]]. The probability of concurrent
distant disease at locoregional recurrence is high enough that finding it changes intent from
curative to palliative. That is the criterion for ordering the investigation.

Circulating tumour DNA adds something at this point and what it adds is asymmetric. A positive
plasma result raises the probability of occult distant disease substantially. A negative result
does not exclude it, because shedding depends on burden and on site [[BS-3460]]. The analogous
experience in the residual disease setting is instructive. In {{trial:ctrak-tn}}, most patients
with detectable circulating tumour DNA already had radiologically evident metastases by the time
the result could be acted on [@turner2022ctrak].

## BS-4620 Reassessment of tumor biology at recurrence

This is where receptor discordance stops being a conceptual problem and becomes a prescription.

Re-test oestrogen receptor, progesterone receptor and HER2 on the recurrence. The result governs
systemic selection, and there are three separate reasons why it may differ from the primary. It
may be true biological change. It may be assay variation, including different antibody clones,
platforms and fixation. It may be sampling, because the primary and the recurrence are different
pieces of tissue. Only the first is biology, and [[BC-290]] sets out how the three are told
apart.

The published discordance literature is dominated by distant metastases rather than by
locoregional recurrence [@schrijver2018]. Rates taken from it should be applied here with that
caveat attached rather than quoted as though the settings were equivalent.

HER2-low is the least stable of the routinely reported categories and it now determines
eligibility. Among 547 patients with matched primary and relapse samples, overall HER2
discordance was 38.0%. Tumours scored HER2-0 became HER2-low in 15% of cases, and HER2-low became
HER2-0 in 14% [@miglietta2021]. Two movements of similar size in opposite directions are the
signature of measurement variation as much as of biology, and the design cannot separate the two
components.

The practical consequence holds whichever explanation applies. The result on the recurrence is
the result that determines what the patient can be offered. Repeating the test is justified by
the decision it feeds, not by a claim about what changed [[BC-590]].

## BS-4630 Salvage surgery

Complete excision with clear margins is the objective, and achieving it is the strongest
modifiable predictor of subsequent local control.

Axillary restaging at recurrence is possible. Repeat sentinel node mapping succeeded in 80.4% of
patients in one series, sparing most of them a completion dissection [@cox2008]. A systematic
review put its feasibility at 64% and its negative predictive value at 96.5% [@poodt2018].

After breast-conserving surgery the standard salvage is mastectomy. A second breast conservation
is possible in selected patients. In {{trial:rtog1014}}, a single-arm phase 2 trial, 58 evaluable
patients underwent a second lumpectomy followed by three-dimensional conformal partial breast
reirradiation [@arthur2020]. The five-year cumulative incidence of a further recurrence in the
ipsilateral breast was 5%, with a 95% confidence interval of 1% to 13%. Seven patients
subsequently underwent ipsilateral mastectomy, a five-year cumulative incidence of 10%. Distant
metastasis-free survival and overall survival were both 95%. Grade 3 late adverse events occurred
in four patients, which
is 7%, with none of grade 4 or higher.

The selection should be read alongside those numbers. Eligibility required a unifocal recurrence
under 3 cm, at least one year after the original breast-conserving therapy, clinically node
negative, excised with negative margins. In 53 of the 58 patients the tumour was 2 cm or smaller.
The result describes what is achievable in that population rather than in every in-breast
recurrence.

After mastectomy the operation is excision of the chest wall recurrence, with a flap where
primary closure is not achievable.

The axilla needs a plan in both situations. If it was never dissected, stage it. If it was
dissected, imaging-directed sampling of an abnormal node is more useful than a repeat
dissection.

## BS-4640 Radiation and reirradiation

If the field was never irradiated, irradiate it. That is the simple case and it applies to chest
wall recurrence after a mastectomy given without radiation.

If the field was irradiated, reirradiation is possible and the constraints are cumulative. Skin
and subcutaneous tissue, brachial plexus, heart, lung and any reconstruction all carry dose from
the first course. The question is therefore not whether the patient has had radiation but how
much, where, when and with what technique [[BS-3770]].

Partial breast reirradiation after a second lumpectomy has prospective evidence behind it, with
the control and toxicity figures given in [[BS-4630]] [@arthur2020]. It is the only reirradiation
strategy in this disease supported by a trial rather than by case series.

Hyperthermia is used as a radiosensitiser for chest wall recurrence. The supporting evidence is
old and largely non-randomised, and availability of the technique varies widely between centres.
Both of those facts belong in the discussion rather than only the first.

The case for treating local disease hard rests on a relationship measured elsewhere. After
breast-conserving surgery, radiotherapy halved the ten-year recurrence rate and reduced the
breast cancer death rate by about a sixth [@ebctcg2011rt]. Roughly one death was avoided by year 15
for every four recurrences avoided by year 10. That was the primary setting. It supports
taking local control seriously at recurrence. It is not evidence that salvage radiation delivers
the same ratio.

Hyperthermia is the one adjunct to radiation with randomised support in superficial tumours of 3 cm
depth or less, the category into which a chest wall recurrence usually falls. A thermal dose above a
defined threshold improved local control, without an overall survival benefit [@jones2005].

## BS-4650 Systemic therapy selection after locoregional recurrence

One randomised trial addresses whether chemotherapy helps after a completely excised isolated
locoregional recurrence, and its final analysis is more useful than its first.

{{trial:calor}} randomised 162 patients with completely excised isolated locoregional recurrence
to chemotherapy or to no chemotherapy [@aebi2014]. At a median follow-up of 4.9 years, five-year
disease-free survival was 69% with chemotherapy against 57% without, with a hazard ratio of 0.59
and a 95% confidence interval of 0.35 to 0.99.

The final analysis at nine years of median follow-up separated the population [@wapnir2018].
Among 58 patients with oestrogen receptor negative recurrence, the hazard ratio for a
disease-free survival event was 0.29, with a 95% confidence interval of 0.13 to 0.67, and
ten-year disease-free survival was 70% against 34%. Among 104 patients with oestrogen receptor
positive recurrence, the hazard ratio was 1.07, with an interval of 0.57 to 2.00, and ten-year
disease-free survival was 50% against 59%. The interaction test gave P equal to 0.013.

Two things about that pattern are unusual. The subgroup effect strengthened with longer follow-up
rather than regressing, which is the opposite of what usually happens to subgroup findings. And
the direction is directly actionable, because it separates the patients for whom chemotherapy is
worth its toxicity from those for whom it is not.

The limits are the denominators. Fifty-eight patients carry the positive result. Overall survival
did not separate significantly in either group, with hazard ratios of 0.48 and 0.70 and intervals
crossing one [@wapnir2018].

```practice
For an oestrogen receptor negative isolated locoregional recurrence, offer chemotherapy
[@wapnir2018].

For an oestrogen receptor positive recurrence, endocrine therapy is the backbone and chemotherapy
is not supported by this trial.

Select the endocrine agent on what the patient received before and how long ago, not on what is
conventionally first.

For a HER2-positive recurrence, HER2-directed therapy is given by extrapolation from the adjuvant
setting rather than from a trial conducted in this position. Say that it is an extrapolation.

Base every one of these decisions on the receptor result from the recurrence rather than from the
primary [[BS-4620]].
```

## BS-4660 Curative intent, prognosis, and aligning with patient goals

An isolated locoregional recurrence is potentially curable and frequently is not cured. Both
halves of that sentence belong in the conversation, and omitting either one produces a
predictable failure.

The variables carrying prognosis are the interval since the primary, the receptor status of the
recurrence, whether complete excision was achieved, and what systemic therapy the patient
received the first time. In two randomised trials of mastectomy against breast conservation, an
early locoregional recurrence marked a biologically aggressive tumour after either operation, and
salvage cured only a limited number [@vantienhoven1999]. A recurrence during adjuvant treatment says something different from one
appearing eight years after completing it [[BS-4730]].

Two failure modes recur. The first is treating a curable recurrence as palliative, on an unstated
assumption that recurrence means incurable. The second is putting a patient with occult distant
disease through salvage surgery, reirradiation and chemotherapy, when the distant disease will
declare itself within months.

The second is harder to avoid, because the information that would prevent it does not exist at
the time of the decision. Restaging lowers the probability and does not eliminate it
[[BS-4610]].

```practice
Say the word curable if it applies, and say what the probability is rather than leaving it
implied.

Say what the treatment will cost in time and in function before it starts, in weeks and in named
losses.

Agree in advance what finding would end the curative attempt. New distant disease during salvage
is the obvious one, and naming it beforehand makes it much easier to act on.

Ask what the patient is trying to protect. Six months of planned locoregional treatment is
something they are spending, and what they are spending it for is theirs to say [[BC-670]].
```

```interplay target=BC-350
Locoregional recurrence is temporal heterogeneity presenting as a lump. The tumour that recurs is
the population that survived the first treatment, so its receptor profile is a measurement of
what selection left rather than a re-measurement of what was originally there [[BS-2470]]. That
is why the result from the recurrence governs and the result from the primary does not, and it is
why HER2-low status moves in both directions between the two samples [@miglietta2021]. The
specimen is new. The tumour is the old one, filtered.
```
