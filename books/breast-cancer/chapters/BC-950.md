## BS-7930 The biology this part assumes

This chapter carries no figures. It is the spine of the part, and the chapters
after it are the evidence.

The defining fact of this subtype is an amplification. A segment of chromosome
seventeen is present in many copies, the receptor it encodes is expressed far
above normal, and the excess drives signalling without a ligand. [[BC-150]] sets
out the receptor family and what the excess does to it.

Three properties follow.

**The target is truncal.** The amplification is an early event and it is carried
by most cells in most of these tumours. Three things follow from that. Blockade
keeps working across lines. The convention is to continue it while changing the
partner. And this subtype has more active drugs against one target than any
other in the disease. [[BC-300]] is the chapter about which events are truncal
and which are not.

**The target is also heterogeneous, and the two statements are compatible.**
Truncal means most cells. It does not mean all cells, and the fraction that
lacks the amplification is where resistance lives. [[BC-280]] is the chapter
about that fraction, and it is the reason a conjugate can work where an antibody
has failed.

**The assay is a threshold on a continuum, and the threshold has moved.** What
counts as positive is a scoring convention over immunohistochemistry and in situ
hybridisation. The category below it was not a category at all until a drug made
it into one. [[BC-110]] is the measurement chapter and [[BC-590]] carries the
argument about antigen thresholds as eligibility criteria, which is the same
argument in its general form.

## BS-7940 What the subtype does to the treatment decision

Every regimen in this part is an answer to one of four questions. Naming them is
the fastest way to read the chapters that follow.

**What do you add to the antibody?** The first question historically, and the
one that built the adjuvant standard. Chemotherapy, then a second antibody, then
a kinase inhibitor, each tested as an addition to a backbone that already
worked.

**What can you take away?** This subtype invented modern de-escalation, for a
structural reason. Response rates are high enough that a few hundred patients
can separate two regimens, response arrives in months rather than years, and
response carries more prognostic information here than anywhere else in the
disease. That combination makes the neoadjuvant setting a usable instrument
rather than a convenience, which [[BC-540]] argues in general and [[BC-830]]
argues here.

**What do you do when disease remains at surgery?** Residual disease after
adequate preoperative treatment identifies a group at high risk, and it is
actionable rather than merely prognostic. This is the single most consequential
use of the neoadjuvant platform in this book, and [[BC-820]] carries it.

**What do you give when the antibody stops working?** The convention is that
blockade continues and the partner changes. The convention is well founded and
worth knowing what founds it, because the claim it does not support is that
every continued regimen is equivalent. [[BC-900]] is the chapter about the
sequence.

A fifth question applies to part of this population and is easy to miss. When
the oestrogen receptor is also present, the two receptors converge on the cell
cycle, and the tumour has a route out of each that it does not have out of both.
That is why triple-positive disease sits in this part, and why it has two
chapters of its own.

## BS-7950 The spine, from diagnosis to last line

**Early disease starts with a decision about the operation, not the drug.**
Whether treatment is given before or after surgery changes what can be learned
and what can be adapted. [[BC-500]] frames the early decision and [[BC-830]]
argues the preoperative regimens.

**The preoperative regimen is chosen for what it will reveal as well as for what
it will do.** A complete response allows de-escalation of what follows.
Residual disease allows escalation. Both are handled in [[BC-820]], which is
where the post-neoadjuvant decision lives.

**Adjuvant treatment for a small node-negative tumour is a different problem.**
Those patients were largely excluded from the registration trials, so their
regimen was built rather than inherited, and what is known about it comes from
single-group and toxicity-endpoint studies. [[BC-820]] states the margin a real
de-escalation trial would need and why none exists.

**Triple-positive early disease is where the two receptors first collide.**
Response to HER2-directed treatment is lower when the oestrogen receptor is
present, which is a class finding rather than a property of one drug.
[[BC-840]] argues it and reads the chemotherapy-sparing strategies that follow
from it.

**Advanced disease has a settled first line and an unsettled order after it.**
[[BC-570]] frames the metastatic decision and [[BC-900]] carries the line-by-line
record, including the trials that have changed what first line means.

**Triple-positive advanced disease asks whether the endocrine partner is
maintenance or treatment.** [[BC-910]] is the chapter, and its evidence is
thinner than the equivalent evidence one part earlier.

**The central nervous system is a separate compartment in this subtype more
than in any other.** It is not in this part. [[BC-930]] is the chapter, and the
reason it is elsewhere is that the compartment does not sort by receptor.

## BS-7960 Where this part disagrees with itself

**The de-escalation chapters and the escalation chapters read the same endpoint
in opposite directions.** A pathological complete response licenses giving less
in [[BC-830]] and predicts a good outcome in [[BC-820]]. The trials that
established the endpoint as prognostic are not the trials that established it as
a licence to de-escalate, and the second claim is weaker than the first.

**The duration trials disagree with each other, and the disagreement is not
about trastuzumab.** [[BC-820]] carries four trials that set different
non-inferiority margins on different scales, some anchored to a time point and
some not. A reader who treats them as four attempts at one question will find
them irreconcilable.

**Triple-positive disease is filed here and argued partly elsewhere.**
[[BC-840]] and [[BC-910]] sit in this part because their decisions are HER2
decisions. Several of the trials they turn on are endocrine trials, and the
endocrine reasoning is in the previous part. The cross-references are load
bearing rather than decorative.

**The first-line record keeps moving, and the later-line record was built
against an older first line.** [[BC-900]] says this about itself. Every
later-line trial in it enrolled patients who received a first-line regimen that
may no longer be what a clinic gives, which limits what the sequence can be read
to establish.
