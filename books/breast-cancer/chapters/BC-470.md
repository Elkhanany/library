## BS-4120 Immune checkpoint blockade

A checkpoint inhibitor releases a brake. It does not create a response. The size of the effect is
therefore set by what was already happening before the first dose [@chen2017; @sharma2023]. In
breast cancer that starting context is usually unfavourable, and [[BS-4190]] is where that
argument is made in full.

Where the class is established is early triple-negative disease given with chemotherapy. In
{{trial:keynote522}}, pathological complete response was 64.8% with pembrolizumab plus
chemotherapy against 51.2% with chemotherapy alone, among the first 602 patients randomised
[@schmid2020]. Median follow-up later reached 75.1 months. Overall survival at 60 months was 86.6%
in 784 patients on pembrolizumab and chemotherapy, with a 95% confidence interval of 84.0 to 88.8.
It was 81.7% in 390 patients on chemotherapy alone, with an interval of 77.5 to 85.2
[@schmid2024k522os].

In metastatic triple-negative disease the effect is concentrated in an assay-defined subgroup.
PD-L1 positive is not one measurement. The trials below do not share an antibody, a scored cell
population or a cut-point, which is why the assay and threshold are named at each first use here.
The full argument is in [[BS-4130]].

{{trial:keynote355}} added first-line pembrolizumab to chemotherapy. Among patients with a PD-L1
combined positive score of 10 or more on the 22C3 assay, median overall survival was 23.0 months
against 16.1 months. The hazard ratio for death was 0.73, with a 95% confidence interval of 0.55
to 0.95. Among patients with a score of 1 or more the figures were 17.6 and 16.0 months. There
the hazard ratio was 0.86, with an interval of 0.72 to 1.04, and it did not meet the
significance criterion [@cortes2022keynote355]. Loosening the threshold diluted the effect to
nothing.

Atezolizumab with nab-paclitaxel gave a consistent pattern in the same setting. Median
progression-free survival in the intention-to-treat population was 7.2 against 5.5 months, with
a hazard ratio of 0.80. In the subgroup called positive by the SP142 immune cell assay, meaning
PD-L1-expressing immune cells covering at least 1% of tumour area, it was 7.5 against 5.0
months, with a hazard ratio of 0.62. That is a different antibody and a different scored
population from the combined positive score above, and the two subgroups are not
interchangeable. Median overall survival in the intention-to-treat population was 21.3 against
17.6 months, with a hazard ratio for death of 0.84 and an interval of 0.69 to 1.02
[@schmid2018].

## BS-4130 Predictive biomarkers and patient selection

Four candidate biomarkers exist and each has a measurement problem that is larger than its
biological problem.

PD-L1 is the one in use and it is not one test. The combined positive score, read on the 22C3
antibody, counts PD-L1-staining tumour cells, lymphocytes and macrophages, divides by the number
of viable tumour cells and multiplies by 100 [@cortes2022keynote355]. The immune cell score used
in the atezolizumab trials is read on the SP142 antibody and records the proportion of tumour
area covered by PD-L1-expressing immune cells [@schmid2018; @mittendorf2020]. Those are
different quantities, computed from different denominators with different antibodies. They are
not two measurements of one property. A result is interpretable only alongside the assay and
cut-point that produced it.

Tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes are prognostic and reproducibly scored under a published
convention. They are not established as predictive of checkpoint benefit. The scoring convention
and its pitfalls are in [@salgado2015; @kos2020]. The subtype distribution is in
[@denkert2018; @stanton2016] and the prognostic value in triple-negative disease in
[@loi2019; @park2019]. The biology is developed in [[BS-1660]].

Tumour mutational burden and mismatch repair deficiency carry tumour-agnostic evidence
[@marabelle2020; @le2017], and neither study included a breast cohort. High burden is also
uncommon in this disease [@barrososousa2020], so the eligible population is small before the
question of whether the inference transfers is even reached.

Spatial organisation predicts better than any single marker and is not measurable in routine
practice. Triple-negative tumours stratify into distinct spatial immune microenvironments
[@gruosso2019; @keren2018], and exclusion driven by TGF-beta signalling in the stroma identifies
non-responders in other diseases [@mariathasan2018].

```caution
PD-L1 positive is the output of a specified antibody, scoring algorithm and cut-point. It is not a
property of a tumour. A patient scored positive by one assay and negative by another has not
changed, and neither has the tumour. The general form of this problem, where an assay boundary
becomes an eligibility boundary, is in [[BS-5110]].
```

## BS-4140 Combination with chemotherapy and targeted agents

Checkpoint blockade in this disease is a combination therapy, and there are three mechanistic
reasons rather than one.

Cytotoxic therapy supplies antigen and changes the infiltrate. Neoadjuvant chemotherapy alters
tumour-infiltrating lymphocyte density and PD-L1 expression in breast cancer tissue
[@pelekanou2017]. S-phase-specific DNA damage activates STING-dependent innate immune signalling
in breast cancer [@parkes2017], and cytosolic DNA is a general route from genome damage to innate
sensing [@mackenzie2017; @bakhoum2018].

The neoadjuvant evidence is consistent across two trials with different agents. In
{{trial:keynote522}}, chemo-immunotherapy raised pathological complete response and later overall
survival [@schmid2020; @schmid2024k522os]. With atezolizumab, pathological complete response was
documented in 95 of 165 patients, which is 58%, against 69 of 168, which is 41%. The rate
difference was 17 percentage points, with a 95% confidence interval of 6 to 27. In the population
called positive by the SP142 immune cell assay at the 1% threshold, the figures were 53 of 77
against 37 of 75 [@mittendorf2020].

Conjugates are the combination partner with the strongest preclinical rationale and no phase 3
breast cancer result, and that case is made in [[BS-4110]].

Endocrine therapy is the combination that has not been shown to work. There is a coherent argument
that luminal immune quiescence is part of the endocrine-dependent transcriptional program rather
than a separate property, which would predict that relieving the dependence changes the immune
context [[BS-3080]]. There is no established clinical evidence that endocrine manipulation improves
benefit from checkpoint blockade in this disease.

## BS-4150 Vaccines and immune priming

The obstacle is not antigen delivery. It is that there is often no response worth amplifying, and
that the antigens available are mostly self.

Breast cancer carries a low mutational burden relative to the histologies where checkpoint
blockade works [@barrososousa2020], so the supply of tumour-specific neoantigens is small. Vaccines
against shared overexpressed proteins are therefore targeting self antigens against which central
and peripheral tolerance already operate. No breast cancer vaccine has an established place in
treatment, and the reason is mechanistic rather than a failure of formulation.

Priming is the more useful framing, and it is what the neoadjuvant setting provides without a
vaccine. An intact primary tumour and its draining nodes place antigen and naive T cells in the
same anatomical compartment, which is the basis of the systemic immunity and nodal priming
argument in [[BS-1710]]. The clinical consequence of that is developed in [[BS-4190]] and it is
the strongest available evidence that priming matters.

In situ approaches, meaning intratumoural injection or focal radiation given to convert a lesion
into an antigen source, follow the same logic. They have no established result in breast cancer
and should be described as a rationale rather than a strategy.

## BS-4160 Cellular therapies and immune-engaging antibodies

Adoptive cell transfer has a proof of principle in this disease and it is one patient. A woman
with chemorefractory hormone receptor positive metastatic breast cancer received
tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes reactive against four mutant proteins, given with interleukin-2
and checkpoint blockade. The targets were mutant SLC3A2, KIAA0368, CADPS2 and CTSB. She had a
complete durable regression ongoing beyond 22 months at report [@zacharakis2018]. That establishes
that mutation-reactive T cells exist in luminal breast cancer and can be expanded. It does not
establish a therapy, and the distance between those two statements is the whole current
problem.

Engineered T cells face three obstacles in solid tumours and antigen heterogeneity is the one this
book has already characterised. Trafficking into the tumour is the first. The suppressive
microenvironment is the second [[BC-230]]. The third is that a receptor-directed effector requires
the receptor, which is the same constraint that limits a naked antibody [[BS-2320]]. The modalities
that tolerate heterogeneity are collected in [[BS-3170]].

T cell engaging antibodies redirect a T cell to a surface antigen without requiring that antigen
to be a driver. That is attractive for the same reason conjugates are, because it converts a
dependency problem into an access problem. There is no established breast cancer result.

Effector-engaging antibodies are already in routine use and are not usually described as
immunotherapy. Trastuzumab acts partly through antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity
[@musolino2008] [[BS-1690]], and blocking the macrophage checkpoint CD47 alongside it eliminated
HER2-positive cells and overcame trastuzumab tolerance in preclinical models [@upton2021].

## BS-4170 Immune resistance mechanisms

Primary and acquired resistance are different problems and in breast cancer the first dominates.
The reasons are developed in [[BS-3020]] and [[BS-1930]] and the clinical statement is that most
tumours in this disease were never going to respond, rather than that they escaped.

Acquired resistance has a defined mechanistic core, and the clearest human evidence is from
melanoma. Four patients relapsed after an initial response to pembrolizumab, and paired
whole-exome sequencing was performed. Two carried loss-of-function mutations in JAK1 or JAK2 with
deletion of the wild-type allele. A third carried a truncating B2M mutation [@zaretsky2016]. The
first two lost the ability to respond to interferon gamma. The third lost surface class I major
histocompatibility complex. Those are the two axes, and the breast cancer versions are in
[[BS-1890]] and [[BS-1930]].

Exclusion is a third route and it is stromal rather than tumour-intrinsic. TGF-beta signalling in
fibroblasts keeps T cells out of the tumour bed and attenuates response to PD-L1 blockade
[@mariathasan2018]. A tumour resisting by exclusion has intact antigen presentation and an intact
interferon response, so no tumour-cell assay will detect it.

Host factors are a fourth. Interferon signalling diminishes with age and is associated with
checkpoint blockade efficacy in triple-negative models [@sceneay2019], which makes age a variable
in the immune context rather than only in tolerance of treatment.

The measurement limitation should be stated rather than implied. Rebiopsy at progression on
immunotherapy is uncommon in breast cancer practice and rarer still in trials, so the acquired
resistance literature in this disease is thin. Most of what is written about it is transferred
from other tumour types.

## BS-4180 Immune-related toxicity and its management

The toxicity is the mechanism, which is why it does not resemble chemotherapy toxicity in timing,
in organ distribution or in reversibility. Management is codified in guideline form
[@schneider2021] and three structural facts matter more than any individual regimen.

Onset is not dose-related and it is often delayed. Events appear months into treatment and after
it has stopped, so a patient who tolerated six cycles is not thereby low risk.

Reversibility differs by organ. Colitis, hepatitis and pneumonitis usually resolve with
immunosuppression. Endocrinopathy frequently does not, and hypophysitis or primary thyroid failure
leaves lifelong replacement whether or not the drug is stopped.

The same numerical grade carries different urgency in different organs. Grade 2 dermatitis and
grade 2 pneumonitis are not comparable clinical events, which is why grade-based algorithms have
organ-specific branches [@schneider2021].

The consideration specific to breast cancer is the population. In {{trial:keynote522}} more than
half the patients in the control arm reached a pathological complete response without
pembrolizumab [@schmid2020]. Permanent endocrinopathy in a patient who would have been cured
without the drug is a different calculation from the same event in metastatic disease, and the
conversation held before treatment should reflect that.

```caution
Tumour burden can increase before it falls under checkpoint blockade. That is why the response
framework for these agents requires confirmation of progression on a subsequent assessment
[@seymour2017]. A single scan is not definitive. Stopping an effective drug on one scan is the
avoidable error here. Continuing an ineffective one on the same reasoning is the other.
```

## BS-4190 Why response rates in breast cancer sit where they do

This is the chapter's carried argument, and it has three components that are separable and all
prior to the drug.

The first is antigen supply. Breast cancer carries a low mutational burden relative to the
histologies where single-agent checkpoint blockade works, and high burden is uncommon within it
[@barrososousa2020]. The populations defined by mismatch repair deficiency and by high mutational
burden respond well and are small [@le2017; @marabelle2020].

The second is immune context by subtype, which is the luminal deficit developed in [[BC-210]] and
[[BC-230]]. Tumour-infiltrating lymphocyte density is lowest in hormone receptor positive disease
and highest in triple-negative and HER2-positive disease [@denkert2018; @stanton2016], and the
subtype pattern is set out in [[BS-1700]]. There is a further possibility, which is that immune
quiescence is part of the oestrogen-driven transcriptional program rather than a property that
happens to accompany it [[BS-3080]]. If that is right, the deficit is a consequence of the
dependency being treated, and it is modifiable in principle. It is not established.

The third is timing relative to an intact tumour, and this is where the evidence is sharpest. One
trial added atezolizumab to postoperative chemotherapy in stage II or III triple-negative disease,
with no neoadjuvant phase. Invasive disease-free survival events occurred in 141 of 1101 patients
receiving atezolizumab and chemotherapy, which is 12.8%. They occurred in 125 of 1098 receiving
chemotherapy alone, which is 11.4%. The final stratified hazard ratio was 1.11, with a 95%
confidence interval of 0.87 to 1.42 and a p value of 0.38. Enrolment was halted at 2199 of a
planned 2300 patients after a futility analysis. Grade 3 or 4 treatment-related adverse events
occurred in 54% against 44%, and chemotherapy exposure was similar in the two groups
[@ignatiadis2025].

Read that against {{trial:keynote522}}, where the same class given before surgery improved
pathological complete response and then overall survival [@schmid2020; @schmid2024k522os]. Same
disease, same broad class of agent, benefit when the tumour was in place and none when it was not.

The mechanistic reading is that an intact primary and its draining nodes are the compartment where
antigen and naive T cells meet, which is the priming argument in [[BS-1710]]. Resection removes the
antigen source and disrupts the nodal architecture, so adjuvant checkpoint blockade is asked to
amplify a response with nothing left to prime it.

```caution
The two trials differ in more than timing. One used a PD-1 antibody with a platinum-containing
regimen and the other a PD-L1 antibody without platinum, and the eligible populations were defined
differently. Timing is the most economical explanation for the contrast. It is not the only one,
and no trial has isolated it. Presenting the comparison as a controlled test of timing overstates
what these two results can carry.
```

```interplay target=BC-350
The immune argument and the heterogeneity argument are the same argument. Immune pressure selects
among a mixed population exactly as drug pressure does, so a tumour with variable antigen
presentation loses the presenting compartment first. What makes the immune case distinctive is
that the effector recognises a context rather than a receptor. That is why immune mechanisms sit
alongside bystander-capable conjugates in [[BS-3170]], as one of the few heterogeneity-tolerant
strategies available.
```
