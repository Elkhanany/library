## BS-7120 Why hormone receptor-positive HER2-positive disease behaves as its own entity

This chapter covers early hormone receptor-positive, HER2-positive breast cancer as a disease
in its own right. The word triple-positive is clinical shorthand rather than a guideline
category. It names the intersection of three separate assays, each with its own threshold and
its own reproducibility, and [[BC-110]] sets those out.

The intersection is not a small corner. In {{trial:aphinity}}, the adjuvant pertuzumab trial,
the hormone receptor-positive group was the clear majority. The population column of the table
below records that split. Whatever the exact figure in an unselected population, this is most
of HER2-positive breast cancer rather than a fraction of it.

Four things make it behave differently, each of them developed in a section below.

Pathological complete response rates are consistently lower than in hormone receptor-negative
HER2-positive disease, across regimens and across two decades [[BS-7140]].

Pathological complete response also carries less prognostic information here. In a pooled
analysis of seven randomised trials it was associated with disease-free survival in
HER2-positive non-luminal disease and was not associated with it in luminal B HER2-positive
disease [@vonminckwitz2012].

The two dependencies run on different clocks, because HER2-directed therapy finishes at one
year while endocrine therapy runs for five to ten. The late recurrence hazard described in
[[BS-4350]] applies here as it does to any other hormone receptor-positive group.

Almost everything known about the entity comes from hormone receptor subgroups of trials
designed for HER2-positive disease as a whole [[BS-7170]].

What this chapter leaves to neighbours. The HER2-directed neoadjuvant backbone belongs to
[[BC-830]], and adjuvant and post-neoadjuvant HER2-directed therapy to [[BC-820]]. Choice and
duration of adjuvant endocrine therapy belongs to [[BC-790]]. The metastatic setting belongs to
[[BC-910]]. [[BS-4430]] carries the short-form treatment algorithm, and this chapter the
reasoning underneath it.

Each section below opens with the question its trials were built to answer, taken from the
[Chapter Stories](stories.html) map, and closes with a table filtered on that same question.
The story says what was being looked for and what the design cannot settle. The table says what
else is in the cell. The prose argues the decision.

That table is the map of the chapter rather than a menu of options. It holds eight distinct
questions, and the reason two rows disagree is usually that they were asked different ones.

Four trials built the backbone that everything else in the table sits on. {{trial:hera}},
{{trial:nsabp-b31-n9831}} and {{trial:bcirg006}} put adjuvant trastuzumab against chemotherapy
alone, and {{trial:noah}} did the same before surgery. None of the four was designed to say
anything about the oestrogen receptor.

Five asked how long that backbone has to run. {{trial:hera}} carried a two-year arm,
{{trial:persephone}} and {{trial:phare}} tested six months, and {{trial:shorther}} and
{{trial:sold}} tested nine weeks. Why those four do not agree with one another is argued in
[[BS-7170]].

Six asked what should be added to that backbone, or exchanged for part of it.
{{trial:neoaltto}} and {{trial:neosphere}} added a second HER2-directed agent before surgery,
and {{trial:destiny-b11}} exchanged the anthracycline for a conjugate ahead of the same
triplet. {{trial:altto}}, {{trial:aphinity}} and {{trial:extenet}} added an agent after
surgery.

Two asked which chemotherapy belongs underneath the HER2-directed treatment.
{{trial:tryphaena}} and {{trial:train2}} both removed the anthracycline, the first as a cardiac
safety study and the second as a randomised efficacy comparison.

Three substituted an antibody-drug conjugate for the cytotoxic chemotherapy. {{trial:kristine}}
did it before surgery, {{trial:kaitlin}} after it, and {{trial:wsg-adapt-tp}} did it in a
twelve-week window confined to this population.

Two asked how little a small node-negative tumour needs. {{trial:apt}} gave twelve weeks of
paclitaxel with trastuzumab in a single group, which {{trial:atempt}} then used as the
comparator for a conjugate.

Nine tried to manage without a full course of cytotoxic chemotherapy. {{trial:tbcrc006}},
{{trial:tbcrc-023}}, {{trial:pamela}}, {{trial:perelisa}}, {{trial:wsg-tpii}} and
{{trial:touch}} relied on endocrine therapy to hold the second pathway instead.
{{trial:phergain}}, {{trial:phergain2}} and {{trial:compassher2pcr}} let an early or
pathological response decide how much was given, which is the argument [[BS-7160]] carries.

Three asked what to do when the specimen still contains invasive disease, of which
{{trial:katherine}} and {{trial:destiny-b05}} have reported and {{trial:compassher2-rd}} has
not.

Only four of those thirty-three enrolled this population and no other. The rest contain it,
which is the fact [[BS-7170]] is about.

The seven metastatic trials are set out in [[BC-910]] and are not argued here. They are worth
reading once, because they show what the same biology is allowed to do when the disease is
incurable. {{trial:tandem}} and {{trial:egf30008}} established that adding HER2 blockade to an
aromatase inhibitor delays progression. The second of those enrolled regardless of HER2 status
and made the HER2-positive group its primary analysis. {{trial:pertain}} then added a second
antibody to that pair. {{trial:sysucc-002}} went further and tested endocrine therapy against
chemotherapy as the partner for trastuzumab, on a non-inferiority design. {{trial:patina}}
added a CDK4/6 inhibitor to maintenance endocrine and HER2-directed therapy. It is the one
trial in that table built on the convergence of the two receptors on the cell cycle.

{{trial:cleopatra}} and {{trial:destiny-b09}} sit in the same table for a different reason.
Both enrolled HER2-positive disease of any receptor status and neither treats the oestrogen
receptor as a target. The contrast is the point. In advanced disease the endocrine axis is
something to aim at, and the cytotoxic is negotiable. In early disease nobody has run the
equivalent trial.

## BS-7130 Reciprocal cross-talk and what it predicts for treatment

The mechanism is set out in [[BS-1260]] and [[BC-140]] and is not restated. The short form is
that the two pathways regulate each other in both directions, so blocking one raises traffic on
the other. This section is about what that predicts in clinic.

**It predicts that single-axis therapy selects for the other axis.** Oestrogen receptor
signalling is upregulated as an adaptive survival mechanism in HER2-positive tumours under
anti-HER2 treatment [@giuliano2015]. A tumour carrying both receptors therefore has an escape
route that a receptor- negative one does not, which is the direct handoff from [[BS-1280]]. The
clinical form of that prediction is simple. HER2 blockade without endocrine therapy should
underperform here, as should endocrine therapy given without HER2 blockade.

**It predicts that the two blockades should be given together rather than in sequence.** Every
gap between them is a window in which the escape route is open. The prediction is clean and the
supporting randomised evidence in the adjuvant setting does not exist [[BS-7150]].

**It predicts a lower complete response rate.** If the oestrogen receptor supplies a survival
route that HER2-directed therapy does not close, then eradication should be less frequent when
the receptor is present. That is what the neoadjuvant trials show [[BS-7140]].

**It predicts that endocrine sensitivity should be measurable early and should matter.** In
{{trial:perelisa}}, a fall in Ki67 after two weeks of letrozole selected a group treated to
surgery without chemotherapy [@guarneri2019perelisa]. A two-week endocrine response was being
used to predict the result of a HER2-directed regimen, which only makes sense if the pathways
are coupled.

**It predicts convergence on the cell cycle.** Both axes end at the same restriction point
[[BC-160]], which is the rationale for {{trial:patina}} and is developed in [[BS-7670]].

```interplay target=BC-280
A report calling both receptors positive has averaged twice, over a tumour that is uniform in
neither [[BS-2370]]. The compartment that survives HER2 blockade need not be the compartment that
survives oestrogen deprivation. Prospective assessment in the neoadjuvant setting showed that HER2
heterogeneity separated a group in which no patient reached a complete response from a group in
which most did [@filho2021], and [[BS-2330]] gives the numbers. The clinical reading for this
chapter is narrow. The lower complete response rate in triple-positive disease is partly a
statement about how much of the tumour lay outside the reach of the regimen. It is not a statement
about how much HER2 the average cell expressed.
```

## BS-7140 Lower pathological complete response rates and what that does to the post-neoadjuvant decision

```story SQ-0290,SQ-0330
```

The observation is unusually consistent, and it is a finding about the class rather than about
any member of it. Wherever the receptor split is reported, response is lower in the
receptor-positive stratum. It holds for a taxane with one antibody, for a taxane with an
antibody and a tyrosine kinase inhibitor, and for a taxane with two antibodies. It holds for a
conjugate substituted for the taxane. It holds in {{trial:compassher2pcr}}, the largest modern
cohort given a taxane with dual blockade. Intensifying the HER2 blockade raises response in
both strata and does not close the distance between them. [[BS-4430]] gives the most recent figures from the conjugate era. Twenty years of
drug development have moved the level and left the gradient alone, which is what makes it a
property of the disease.

The trials in that table are not disagreeing about the gap. They are measuring different
things. Three sources of variation account for most of the spread, and none of them is the
drug.

The first is the endpoint. Some of these trials scored the breast alone and some the breast and
the axilla. That convention costs more here than elsewhere, because low-volume residual disease
concentrates in receptor-positive tumours. {{trial:tbcrc006}} found residual ypT1a-b disease in
a third of its receptor-positive cases and in a small fraction of its receptor-negative ones.
Where the line between a complete response and a near-complete one is drawn therefore changes
the size of the gap it produces. That is measurement rather than biology.

The second is the comparator. {{trial:noah}}, {{trial:neoaltto}}, {{trial:neosphere}},
{{trial:tryphaena}}, {{trial:train2}} and {{trial:destiny-b11}} added or exchanged agents on
top of a cytotoxic backbone. {{trial:kristine}}, {{trial:wsg-adapt-tp}}, {{trial:wsg-tpii}} and
{{trial:touch}} took the cytotoxic away. {{trial:tbcrc006}}, {{trial:tbcrc-023}},
{{trial:pamela}}, {{trial:phergain}}, {{trial:phergain2}} and {{trial:compassher2pcr}} reported
response without testing it against a randomised cytotoxic comparator. Two response rates from
those three designs are not the same quantity.

The third is what was measured on the pretreatment biopsy. {{trial:perelisa}} found that the
receptor-positive tumours which clear are largely the ones called HER2-enriched.
{{trial:pamela}} found the same split in a cohort that admitted any receptor status. That is a
signature assay run on a single core, and it classifies the core rather than the tumour.
Hormone receptor status was a stratification factor or a reporting subgroup in these trials and
was randomised nowhere, so the two strata differ in more than the receptor.

Now put the lower response rate next to the second finding, because the combination is what
matters. Complete response was not associated with disease-free survival in luminal B
HER2-positive disease in the pooled German analysis, while it was strongly associated with it
in non-luminal HER2-positive disease [@vonminckwitz2012]. In this subtype the endpoint is both
rarer and less informative.

The consequence falls on the post-neoadjuvant decision, where {{trial:katherine}} and
{{trial:destiny-b05}} both assign escalation on the presence of residual invasive disease.
Those two are the load-bearing trials for this section even though neither is in its table.
{{trial:katherine}} randomised against trastuzumab, which is what the clinic would otherwise
have given. Its survival update is the only mature overall survival result in the
post-neoadjuvant record. {{trial:destiny-b05}} then moved the comparator to the older
conjugate, in a population that also had to carry nodal disease at surgery or inoperable
disease at diagnosis. They are a chain and not a disagreement. More triple-positive patients
are routed into that chain by a trigger that carries less prognostic information than it does in
receptor-negative disease.

That is not an argument against escalating, but an argument about what the trigger means. The
distinction is the one made in [[BS-4690]]. A response-adapted trial demonstrates that a
population defined by residual disease benefits from a specific drug. It requires neither that
the defining measure be prognostic nor that a regimen producing more complete responses produce
longer survival.

Three things the table cannot show are worth naming. It has no column for the volume of
residual disease, which is the axis on which this population actually differs. It has no column
for what was given after surgery, and in most of these protocols that was decided by the
specimen. And two of its most relevant rows cannot yet answer the question they were built for.
{{trial:destiny-b11}} reports the most recent neoadjuvant response gain in the table on
event-free survival data that are 4.5% mature. {{trial:compassher2pcr}} is the largest modern
cohort in this population. Its primary endpoint is recurrence-free survival among the patients
who reached a complete response. That endpoint has not reported.

```caution
The mirror error is more dangerous than the first one. A complete response in triple-positive
disease is regularly read as permission to de-escalate what follows. The trials that produced these
response rates did not test that. {{trial:phergain}} and {{trial:wsg-tpii}} both sent patients with
residual disease on to chemotherapy. In {{trial:wsg-tpii}} every patient also received dual HER2
blockade after surgery. A complete response inside such a design tells you the tumour responded. It
does not tell you that the rest of the plan was unnecessary.
```

## BS-7150 Endocrine therapy alongside HER2 blockade in the adjuvant setting

Every patient in this chapter receives both treatments. The question is whether they should
overlap, which no randomised trial in the adjuvant setting has answered.

The mechanistic case for overlapping them is the one in [[BS-7130]]. Oestrogen receptor
signalling rises under anti-HER2 pressure [@giuliano2015], so the months between finishing
HER2-directed therapy and starting endocrine therapy are months in which the escape route is
unopposed. On that reasoning the two should start as close together as the chemotherapy
schedule allows.

The case against is practical rather than biological, because nobody has shown harm from
concurrent administration and nobody has shown benefit either. Aromatase inhibitor arthralgia,
tamoxifen symptoms and trastuzumab infusions accumulate in the same weeks. Most of the
adherence that is ever lost is lost in that first year [[BS-6800]].

What the neoadjuvant trials contribute is limited but real. In {{trial:wsg-adapt-tp}}, adding
endocrine therapy to twelve weeks of trastuzumab emtansine changed the complete response rate
hardly at all. The conjugate itself accounted for a large difference against trastuzumab with
endocrine therapy [@harbeck2017adapt]. In {{trial:tbcrc006}}, letrozole was given concurrently
with dual HER2 blockade throughout, on an explicit hypothesis that blocking both receptors was
required [@rimawi2013]. Neither trial isolates the contribution of timing, because neither
randomised it.

Two practical points sit outside the trials. Endocrine therapy in this group runs long after
HER2-directed therapy has finished. The late recurrence hazard in [[BS-4350]] is therefore the
dominant long-term risk. Extended duration is decided on the same grounds as in HER2-negative
disease [[BS-6780]]. And in the metastatic setting, receipt of endocrine therapy at all has
been associated with better survival in this subtype in a real-world cohort [@carausu2022].
That is an association in advanced disease and not a randomised comparison in early disease,
but the direction is consistent with the mechanism.

```practice
Start endocrine therapy as soon as chemotherapy finishes. Give it alongside the remainder of the
HER2-directed year rather than after it. There is no evidence of harm from the overlap, and the
mechanism argues against leaving a gap.

Do not delay endocrine therapy for radiotherapy, since the two are routinely given together.

Treat the first year as the adherence problem it is. A patient managing infusions, radiotherapy and
a new aromatase inhibitor at the same time is the patient most likely to stop the tablet.

Plan the endocrine duration conversation for the point at which HER2-directed therapy ends. That is
when treatment appears to the patient to be finishing. It is also the point at which most of the
remaining benefit has yet to be delivered.
```

## BS-7160 Chemotherapy omission and the de-escalation questions specific to this group

```story SQ-0300,SQ-0310,SQ-0320
```

This is the live de-escalation question. It is more live here than in receptor-negative disease
for one reason. A triple-positive tumour has a second systemic therapy that works, which a
receptor-negative one does not.

Seven trials in the table agree on one thing. A chemotherapy-free regimen clears disease in a
minority of receptor-positive tumours, and that minority is real rather than anecdotal. They
also agree, without having been designed to, on what governs the size of it. In
{{trial:wsg-adapt-tp}} the response rate tracked what carried the payload and not what was
added alongside. Adding endocrine therapy to the conjugate changed the rate hardly at all,
while replacing the conjugate with a bare antibody on the same endocrine therapy cut it to a
fraction. {{trial:tbcrc-023}} points the same way from the other direction, since doubling the
duration of chemotherapy-free dual blockade raised response in the receptor-positive subgroup
rather than the receptor-negative one.

What separates these seven is evidentiary status, not drug. {{trial:tbcrc006}} and
{{trial:pamela}} are single-arm windows. {{trial:tbcrc-023}} combined a phase II design in its
experimental arm with a pick-the-winner design. It was not powered to compare its two
durations, so its duration gap is a numerical difference with no test behind it.
{{trial:wsg-adapt-tp}} randomised the partner drug rather than the strategy. {{trial:perelisa}}
randomised nothing and allocated on a marker. Only {{trial:wsg-tpii}} and {{trial:touch}} put a
chemotherapy-free arm against a cytotoxic one, and {{trial:touch}} was built to test a gene
signature rather than to compare the arms. Its two response rates landed in the same place,
which is a description and not a demonstration of equivalence, because no non-inferiority
margin was ever set.

{{trial:na-pher2}} is the trial a reader will not find in that table, and the reason is a
convention worth stating. An evidence table carries the record a decision rests on, and a
single-arm exploratory study is not part of that record. It has coprimary endpoints of the
change in proliferation at two weeks and at surgery and the change in apoptosis. Proliferation
collapsed within two weeks of the chemotherapy-free combination and had risen again by surgery.
Apoptosis fell over the same interval rather than rising. That is a statement about target
engagement and it belongs beside its question rather than beside a randomised phase III.

{{trial:wsg-tpii}} is the trial to read closely, because its design explains both its result
and its limits. Substituting endocrine therapy for the weekly taxane more than halved the
complete response rate. Five-year overall survival was nonetheless close to complete in both
arms. The invasive disease-free survival estimates did not follow the response gap either, on
an interval around the taxane arm that runs from 55.6% to 100%. That width is a statement about
the size of the trial rather than about the treatments. The reason the arms converge is in the
protocol rather than in the biology. Chemotherapy was mandatory after residual disease, so most
of the endocrine arm received it anyway. The trial is consistent with a response-guided strategy
being safe. It does not demonstrate that chemotherapy can be omitted, which is a different claim.

The selection tools are the weak point, and they fail in three different ways. A two-week Ki67
threshold and a PET metabolic response threshold are continuous measurements converted into a
gate, and the reproducibility problem set out in [[BS-6930]] applies directly to the first.
Allocation also confounds them. In {{trial:perelisa}} the molecular non-responders were sent to
paclitaxel, so their higher response rate measures the paclitaxel rather than the marker. The
baseline markers were never randomised either. An intrinsic subtype call in {{trial:pamela}},
and the prospective two-site HER2 scoring in {{trial:her2-heterogeneity}}, say who responds and
not who needed the chemotherapy that was withheld. Heterogeneity in particular is a sampling
convention rather than a property of the disease, and only 16 of 157 evaluable tumours met the
definition. Exactly one marker in this literature was tested as a treatment interaction rather
than as a prognostic split, the E2F signature in {{trial:touch}}, and it showed none.

The general shape of the problem is what the table cannot hold. Demonstrating omission requires
showing that events did not accumulate, which needs more patients, longer follow-up and a
non-inferiority margin somebody has to justify [[BS-6370]]. No trial confined to early
triple-positive disease has been built at that size. The one trial that tested a marker as an
interaction enrolled postmenopausal patients with a median age of 69. That is narrower than the
group the strategy is offered to.

```evidence setting=early subtype=HR+/HER2+ line=neoadjuvant modality=endocrine sort=year caption=Neoadjuvant_trials_combining_endocrine_therapy_with_HER2_blockade,_the_evidence_base_for_chemotherapy_omission.
```

```practice
Outside a trial, chemotherapy remains part of treatment for node-positive or larger triple-positive
tumours. Where it is omitted, say plainly that the supporting evidence is phase 2 and
response-adapted.

The de-escalation with the strongest evidence is not omission of chemotherapy. It is omission of
the anthracycline, which {{trial:train2}} supports irrespective of hormone receptor status.

In a small, node-negative tumour the de-escalated regimens in [[BS-7000]] apply to this group as
they do to receptor-negative disease. The endocrine therapy that follows is the longer half of the
treatment.
```

## BS-7170 Subgroup evidence from the HER2 trials, and its limits

Almost every statement made in this chapter rests on a hormone receptor subgroup of a trial
powered for HER2-positive disease as a whole. That is worth saying explicitly rather than
leaving implicit, and [[BC-740]] sets the standard this section is held to.

Start with what the design does well. Randomisation was never done on the receptor. It balanced
everything else inside each receptor stratum, so a receptor subgroup of a randomised trial
remains a randomised comparison of the treatments that trial gave. Some of these trials,
{{trial:extenet}} among them, stratified randomisation on hormone receptor status as well,
which makes the strata better behaved still. Stratification supplies neither power nor a
randomised comparison for the question of whether receptor status modifies the treatment
effect. In none of the reported trials in this table did a receptor subgroup reverse the
direction of the trial's own answer.

Five of the sixteen rows do not speak to the receptor at all, and saying so is part of reading
the table. {{trial:hera}}, {{trial:nsabp-b31-n9831}} and {{trial:bcirg006}} asked whether
adjuvant trastuzumab works. They answered it in the same direction in every subgroup they
examined. {{trial:altto}} asked whether lapatinib adds to trastuzumab and found nothing that
cleared its own revised threshold. That is a verdict on one tyrosine kinase inhibitor rather
than on dual blockade, and the pertuzumab question was asked separately.
{{trial:compassher2-rd}} has not reported, and its eligibility is the subject of [[BS-7180]].

The four duration trials look like the sharpest disagreement in the table and are the clearest
case of divergence by design. {{trial:persephone}} declared non-inferiority for six months.
{{trial:phare}} tested the same six months and could not. {{trial:shorther}} and {{trial:sold}}
tested nine weeks and could not either. The four used four different margins, a hazard ratio of
1.15, of 1.29 and of 1.3, and three percentage points of four-year disease-free survival.
{{trial:phare}} also randomised patients who were already several months into treatment rather
than at the start. Four margins and two durations produce four answers about study design and
one answer about trastuzumab. No shorter schedule has displaced the year.

{{trial:extenet}} is the one place in the table where the receptor looks as though it matters.
The benefit of extended neratinib concentrated in hormone receptor-positive disease, and a
final analysis of that subgroup exists [@chan2020]. The mechanism section above supplies a
reason to believe it. The trial's own eight-year overall survival comparison found nothing, in
either direction, which is the endpoint this chapter is spending five to ten years of endocrine
therapy on. A subgroup with a mechanism behind it is still a subgroup.

{{trial:aphinity}} is the instructive case for the general problem. The hormone
receptor-positive and receptor-negative subgroup hazard ratios for adjuvant pertuzumab differ
in point estimate and overlap substantially in interval [@piccart2021aphinity], and [[BS-4430]]
gives both. The defensible reading is that nothing in those data distinguishes the two groups.
The common reading, that pertuzumab works better in triple-positive disease, requires an
interaction the trial did not demonstrate. The axis that did separate in that trial was nodal
status rather than receptor status. A later exploratory subanalysis went further and located
the largest benefit in a receptor-positive subset defined by HER2 expression
[@agostinetto2026]. A subanalysis of that kind generates a hypothesis rather than testing one.

Two rows carry a negative result that is more useful than it looks. {{trial:kaitlin}}
substituted a conjugate for the taxane after an anthracycline in high-risk disease, and
{{trial:atempt}} substituted it for the whole regimen in stage I disease. {{trial:kaitlin}}
found no invasive disease-free survival difference against the taxane arm. {{trial:atempt}} was
randomised three to one and was never sized to rank the two on outcome. On its other co-primary
endpoint it failed to lower clinically relevant toxicity against the taxane comparator. Neither
trial gave a reason to move. The same answer at both ends of the risk range is worth more than
either trial alone. It is why {{trial:apt}} rather than a conjugate remains the reference
regimen for a small tumour.

The trial the decision rests on for a patient with residual disease is {{trial:katherine}}, for
the same reason as in [[BS-7140]]. It is the randomised comparison against what the clinic
would otherwise do. It is also the only post-neoadjuvant trial in this table with a mature
overall survival result. {{trial:destiny-b05}} raises the comparator and reports survival that
is immature at its primary analysis.

Now the ceiling on the direct evidence. Four trials in the whole record enrolled early
triple-positive disease and nothing else, and they enrolled 375, 207, 147 and 64 patients
[@harbeck2017adapt; @tpii2026; @malorni2025; @guarneri2019perelisa]. None of them is in this
table, because all four are neoadjuvant. Every adjuvant and post-neoadjuvant decision in this
chapter is therefore made on a subgroup.

The asymmetry that follows is worth naming. Escalation decisions in this group rest on large
randomised trials whose populations included it, which is a reasonable basis. De-escalation
decisions rest on small trials designed for it, which is a weaker basis for a stronger claim.
Practice has been more willing to de-escalate on the second than to question the first.

Three columns this table will never have. None of these trials reports overall survival within
the hormone receptor-positive stratum as a tested comparison. None randomised when endocrine
therapy should start relative to the HER2-directed year, which is the decision [[BS-7150]] is
about. And the duration trials read disease-free survival at four and five years, in a group
whose recurrence hazard is still running at ten [[BS-4350]].

```caution
A subgroup result does not become more reliable because the subgroup is large. The hormone
receptor-positive subgroup of {{trial:aphinity}} is large enough that its estimate is precise. It
is still not a randomised comparison between receptor-positive and receptor-negative disease.
Precision within a subgroup says nothing about whether the subgroups differ [[BS-6390]].
```

## BS-7180 Unresolved questions and what reads out next

The largest gap is an absence rather than a pending readout.

{{trial:compassher2-rd}} tests escalation of post-neoadjuvant treatment for residual
HER2-positive disease. Its eligibility is restricted to high-risk residual disease, defined as
oestrogen receptor-negative or node-positive [@osullivan2021compassher2rd]. Receptor-positive,
node-negative residual disease is therefore outside it. That is a large part of the
triple-positive residual population, and precisely the group in which [[BS-7140]] argues that
residual disease is least informative about risk. The trial that would settle what to do with
them is not running.

The other two trials in the table were built to test the opposite move. Both will read out a
recurrence endpoint at three years. {{trial:compassher2pcr}} asks whether a complete response
after a taxane with dual blockade licenses stopping there, measured as recurrence-free
survival among the patients who reached one. {{trial:phergain2}} asks the same of a
chemotherapy-free course in node-negative tumours of 5 to 30 mm, with a recurrence-free
interval outstanding alongside a quality-of-life endpoint that has reported. Between them they will say what a complete response
in this subtype is worth, which is the question [[BS-7140]] shows nobody can currently answer.

Two further readouts will change something. Overall survival from {{trial:destiny-b05}} will
show whether the invasive disease-free survival gain in residual disease converts into longer
life. Its population was assembled on residual disease with nodal involvement, in which this
chapter's group is over-represented for the reasons given in [[BS-7140]]. And the adjuvant
question raised by {{trial:patina}} has no early-disease trial behind it. Whether CDK4/6
inhibition belongs in early triple-positive disease is unasked rather than unanswered
[[BS-7670]].

Two questions will probably never be answered directly. Concurrent against sequential endocrine
therapy alongside HER2 blockade would need a large trial for a difference nobody expects to be
large [[BS-7150]]. Chemotherapy omission in an unselected triple-positive population would need
a non-inferiority trial whose margin would be hard to justify to a patient [[BS-6370]].
