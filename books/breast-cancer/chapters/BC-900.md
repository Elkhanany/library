## BS-7570 Scope, and the line structure this chapter assumes

This chapter enumerates the systemic evidence for HER2-positive metastatic disease, line by line. The
decision architecture, meaning how to weigh a patient's disease burden, symptoms and comorbidity
against these options, is in [[BC-570]]. Conjugate mechanism and toxicity are in [[BC-460]]. Disease
that is both hormone receptor-positive and HER2-positive is treated in [[BC-910]], because its
sequencing question is different. Brain and leptomeningeal disease has its own chapter in [[BC-930]].

Two features of this subtype shape everything below. Survival is long enough that a patient may
pass through many lines of treatment, so the order matters more here than in any other subtype. And the lines
were established sequentially against the standard of their own day. The trial that defined second
line enrolled patients who had never received what is now first line.

That second point is the one to hold onto. The evidence base is a stack of comparisons, each of them
valid against its own control. The stack does not tell you what to do after a modern first line. Every
table below therefore carries the population column, because the population is what dates a
comparison and the result column does not.

The word line is doing less work in these trials than a reader expects. Most later-line trials set a
minimum prior exposure and capped nothing above it, so a second-line label is a floor rather than a
description of who was enrolled. Trials that admit a wide range of prior treatment tend to report a
median number of prior regimens well above their stated minimum. Read that median, not the label.

The endpoint column dates a comparison as thoroughly as the population column does. The earliest
trials here measured time to progression or response rate. The later ones measure progression-free
survival by independent review. Those are not the same object, and a table sorted by year lines them
up as though they were.

Read the tables as a record of what was asked rather than as a ranking of the drugs in them. Two rows
may sit a generation apart and share no backbone at all. Nothing in the registry adjusts for that
and nothing could. The ordering a clinic uses is a reading across the stack, and no trial inside it
tested that ordering.

Each section below opens with the question its trials were built to answer, taken from the
[Chapter Stories](stories.html) map, and closes with a table filtered on that same question. The
story says what was being looked for and what the design cannot settle. The table says what else
is in the cell. The prose argues the decision.

## BS-7580 First-line dual blockade with a taxane

```story SQ-0690,SQ-0700
```

```evidence setting=metastatic subtype=HER2+ line=1L modality=her2,adc sort=year cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,result,os caption=First-line_trials_of_HER2_blockade,_newest_first._Each_was_run_against_the_standard_of_its_own_year,_so_no_two_control_arms_are_the_same_regimen._Read_the_population_column_before_the_result_column.
```

Pertuzumab, trastuzumab and a taxane held first line for a decade. The reason is durability rather
than response rate. Dual blockade covers the juxtamembrane domain and the dimerisation arm at once,
which is the mechanism set out in [[BC-150]].

Most randomised rows in that table add one more layer of HER2 blockade to the layer beneath it.
Each of those delayed progression against the regimen its extra layer was added to. None of them
failed. That is the class finding, and it is the only thing those rows say with one voice.
{{trial:marianne}} and {{trial:destiny-b09}} are not layering trials. Each substitutes a conjugate
for the free taxane, which is a different question.

What varies down the column is the measurement and the comparator rather than the drug.
{{trial:h0648g}} reported time to disease progression, the convention when trastuzumab was
registered [@slamon2001]. {{trial:m77001}} is a phase 2 trial whose primary endpoint was response
rate [@marty2005]. The 2015 confirmatory analysis of {{trial:cleopatra}} quoted in [[BS-3950]] is
an earlier data cut of that trial rather than a competing estimate [@swain2015]. From {{trial:cleopatra}} onwards the endpoint is progression-free survival,
though not by the same reader. {{trial:cleopatra}}, {{trial:marianne}} and {{trial:destiny-b09}}
used independent central review. {{trial:phila}} and {{trial:her2climb-05}} used the investigator.
An interval at the head of this table is therefore not the same object as an interval at its foot.
The two cannot be read as one scale.

The control arms diverge further than the endpoints do. {{trial:cleopatra}} was run against a
regimen carrying none of the drugs that now follow first-line progression, which places its survival
difference in a whole sequence rather than in pertuzumab alone [@swain2020]. {{trial:phila}} added a
kinase inhibitor to a trastuzumab and docetaxel doublet rather than to the triplet a clinic now
starts with [@ma2023; @ma2026]. It also enrolled in a single country. Part of its separation is
therefore the contribution pertuzumab was never allowed to make in it, and the overall survival it
has since reported inherits the same control arm. {{trial:her2climb-05}} randomised only patients
whose disease had not progressed during induction [@dieras2025]. Its population is selected for
disease the backbone already controls. It describes what a third layer adds to a responder rather
than what a three-drug start does for everyone.

The toxicity that decides between those two is different in kind as well as in size. Pyrotinib
produced grade 3 or higher treatment-related events in 90% of patients against 76% on placebo
[@ma2023]. Tucatinib produced diarrhoea in 72.7% of a maintenance population who were otherwise
well [@dieras2025]. A rate measured in a maintenance phase is paid by a patient with no symptoms
to weigh it against.

{{trial:destiny-b09}} is the one comparison whose control arm is the taxane triplet a clinic starts
with today, which is what makes it load-bearing rather than its effect size [@tolaney2026destinyb09]. Its
rationale is not that a conjugate is more active, which the later lines had already shown. It is
that giving the agent before resistance is selected may convert responders into durable responders.
That is the problem set out in [[BC-345]]. Two features belong with its result. Overall survival
was not the primary endpoint and was not mature at the reported interim analysis. And adjudicated
drug-related interstitial lung disease or pneumonitis occurred in 12.1% of patients on the
conjugate combination against 1.0% on the taxane triplet [@tolaney2026destinyb09].

{{trial:marianne}} is the reason to be careful with substitution arguments in general. The
first-generation conjugate was noninferior to the taxane backbone and was not superior to it, with
or without pertuzumab [@perez2016]. The easy reading of the two substitution trials is that trial
conduct improved across the intervening decade, and it is the wrong one. MARIANNE ran against the
weaker of the two available control arms, because its comparator carried no pertuzumab. A conjugate
that could not beat the weaker comparator, set beside one that beats the stronger, separates the
two drugs rather than the two designs. The chemistry behind that separation is in [[BC-460]]. Noninferiority is a claim about a prespecified margin rather
than a demonstration that two regimens are the same [[BS-6370]]. A failed superiority claim for one
conjugate is not evidence about another. The stronger version of the substitution argument drops the
cytotoxic rather than carrying it on the antibody, and that version is argued under [[BS-7620]].

Four things this table cannot show. It carries no overall survival for {{trial:destiny-b09}}, whose
third arm, the conjugate without pertuzumab, was still blinded at the interim analysis. Until that
arm reads out, nothing separates what the conjugate contributes from what the second antibody
contributes. {{trial:destiny-breast07}} is the programme giving the newer conjugate alone and in
combinations at this position, and it has no publication on the record. {{trial:peruse}} gave the
antibody pair with whichever taxane the investigator chose, with safety as its primary endpoint and
no comparator [@miles2021a]. Its figures describe a cohort and cannot rank three taxanes against one
another. And no trial here enrolled a patient progressing after a first-line deruxtecan conjugate,
which is the population first-line practice is now creating. Central confirmation of HER2 was
required in {{trial:marianne}} and {{trial:her2climb-05}}. The trials of the first decade record no
such step, so these populations differ by measurement as well as by era.

The taxane triplet has not become wrong. It remains the regimen with the longest follow-up, the
widest availability and the best characterised late toxicity. Where a deruxtecan conjugate is
unavailable, or where baseline lung function makes cumulative pneumonitis risk hard to accept, the
older triplet is a defensible first line rather than a compromise.

```caution
Interstitial lung disease is cumulative and does not respect line of therapy. Moving a deruxtecan
conjugate earlier lengthens the exposure of patients who will live for years. The toxicity data in
[[BC-460]] were generated in later-line populations with shorter exposure.

A chemotherapy-free first line is the obvious headline and it is not quite what the trial tested.
The conjugate carries a cytotoxic payload, so what has changed is delivery rather than the presence
of chemotherapy. What is genuinely lost is the finite induction model. The taxane triplet gives
chemotherapy for a defined period and then stops, leaving antibody maintenance. The conjugate
continues until progression. For a patient facing years of treatment, a gain in median
progression-free survival and a continuous pneumonitis risk are being traded against a regimen with
a known exit [@swain2020; @tolaney2026destinyb09].
```

## BS-7590 Second-line conjugate therapy

```story SQ-0710
```

```evidence setting=metastatic subtype=HER2+ line=2L modality=her2,adc sort=year cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,result,os caption=Second-line_trials._The_two_capecitabine_trials_at_the_foot_of_the_table_are_where_the_principle_of_blocking_HER2_through_progression_was_established.
```

The principle underneath this line is older than every drug in the table.
{{trial:gbg-26-big-03-05}} and {{trial:egf100151}} each held capecitabine fixed in both arms and
varied only the HER2 blockade after progression on trastuzumab [@vonminckwitz2009; @geyer2006]. One
continued the antibody through progression. The other added a kinase inhibitor. Both delayed
progression against capecitabine given alone. That is the evidence that the target survives
progression on the drugs aimed at it. It is why the answer to HER2 failure is usually a different
way of attacking the same receptor rather than a different target.

Neither of those trials lengthened life. The reason is a property of their designs rather than of
the principle. Both used time to progression as the endpoint, which was the era's convention.
{{trial:gbg-26-big-03-05}} randomised a small population, and its survival difference was not
significant. {{trial:egf100151}} stopped at an interim analysis for superiority in time to
progression, and its final survival analysis did not separate [@cameron2010]. A trial stopped early
on a progression endpoint is not built to answer a survival question.

The two conjugates in that table are not interchangeable and the difference is chemical.
Trastuzumab emtansine carries a non-cleavable linker and a microtubule payload with no bystander
effect. Trastuzumab deruxtecan carries a cleavable linker and a topoisomerase-I payload that
crosses membranes, so it reaches cells that do not display the antigen. The consequences are
developed in [[BC-460]]. That is the heterogeneity argument arriving as a treatment result, and
the evidence for it across the expression range comes from {{trial:daisy}} [@mosele2023].

{{trial:emilia}} and {{trial:destiny-b03}} are the same experiment run in two different eras. Each
set a conjugate against the standard its own era had established, and each separated on progression
and on survival [@verma2012; @cortes2022destinyb03; @corts2024]. {{trial:destiny-b03}} is the
load-bearing row. Its comparator was the agent {{trial:emilia}} itself had put in second line, and
its population was conjugate-naive [@hurvitz2023]. That lineage is what makes the ranking
interpretable, because each step was tested against the thing it replaced.

Two rows are answering a different question from the rest of the table. {{trial:phoebe}} set one
kinase inhibitor against another on a shared capecitabine backbone, in China, so it ranks members of
a class rather than asking whether the class belongs at this position [@xu2021]. Its result is a
prespecified interim analysis, and the arm it beat has since been displaced from this line by the
conjugates above it. A class ranking made against a comparator nobody now offers does not transfer.
{{trial:kate2}}
appears here because its backbone is a conjugate. Its question is immunological, and it is argued
under [[BS-7600]] with the other trials that added a mechanism from outside HER2.

{{trial:her2climb-02}} and {{trial:kate2}} are the only pair in this chapter with an identical
control arm. Both gave trastuzumab emtansine to everyone, in patients previously treated with
trastuzumab and a taxane. Adding a selective HER2 kinase inhibitor delayed progression. Adding a
checkpoint inhibitor did not, and that trial was unmasked early for futility [@hurvitz2025;
@emens2020]. With design, backbone and population matched, the difference between them is mechanism
rather than trial construction. Both were built on a backbone {{trial:destiny-b03}} has since
displaced, so the question has not been asked again on a deruxtecan backbone.

{{trial:her2climb-02}} is the row that should slow a reader down. It improved progression-free
survival against the conjugate alone, while its interim overall survival point estimate lay on the
wrong side of one [@hurvitz2025]. A progression endpoint moving one way and an interim survival
estimate moving the other is not a shape on which to build a sequencing decision. Longer follow-up
may resolve the discrepancy. Until it does, adding a kinase inhibitor at this position needs a
reason beyond the curve it was licensed on.

What the table cannot show is the population second line is about to contain.
{{trial:destiny-breast02}} carries a second-line label that is a floor rather than a description,
because entry required progression on trastuzumab emtansine and capped nothing before that
[@andr2023]. Its overall survival favoured the conjugate, without a published median or hazard
ratio, so the size of that advantage is not on the record. {{trial:emilia}} carries the opposite
problem. Its tabulated survival comes from the second interim analysis, while the 2017 final
analysis gave medians of 29.9 against 25.9 months with a hazard ratio of 0.75 [@diras2017]. That is
one trial reported twice rather than two estimates in conflict. No trial above enrolled a patient who had
already received a deruxtecan conjugate. If {{trial:destiny-b09}} moves that agent into first line,
this section describes a population that is leaving the clinic.

The practical consequence is that second line now has two answers, and which one applies is set by
what came first. After a taxane triplet, the answer is trastuzumab deruxtecan on direct randomised
evidence. After first-line trastuzumab deruxtecan, the answer is inferred. The candidates are a
tyrosine kinase inhibitor combination [[BS-7600]], trastuzumab emtansine, or a non-cross-resistant
conjugate. None has been tested in that position, and the inference rests on a class argument
rather than on a trial.

None of these trials required a repeat biopsy at progression. The persistence of the receptor is
inferred from the fact that drugs against it still work. It is nowhere measured.

What no trial answers is what a second conjugate is worth after the first has failed. The available
evidence is retrospective. In a series of patients given both trastuzumab deruxtecan and
sacituzumab govitecan in either order, progression-free survival on the second conjugate was
usually shorter than on the first [@mai2025]. Activity was retained and the expected magnitude was
smaller. The reasoning is developed in [[BS-3030]].

Trastuzumab emtansine has not disappeared with all this. It retains a defined role in residual
disease after neoadjuvant therapy, which is an early-disease question and belongs to [[BS-7010]].
In the metastatic setting it is now a later option rather than a second-line standard.

```practice
When a patient progresses after a conjugate, the question is not which conjugate next but whether the
resistance was to the target, the linker-payload, or the delivery. [[BC-460]] separates the three.
Only the first is addressed by switching target.

Establish whether brain disease is present before choosing, because it changes the ranking rather
than the plan [[BC-930]].

Say which of the two arguments you are making. Either the lineage argument, which assumes the patient
in front of you resembles the trial population, or a judgement about a patient who plainly does not.
```

```caution
Trial evidence attaches to a population, not to a line number. {{trial:emilia}} put trastuzumab
emtansine into the second line against a lapatinib doublet that no clinic would now offer.
{{trial:destiny-b03}} then displaced it in a population that had never seen a conjugate. Reporting
either as establishing a line number was accurate only while the line above it stood still.
Whenever a regimen moves earlier, every downstream result loses the population it was measured in.
```

## BS-7600 Third line and beyond

```story SQ-0720,SQ-0740
```

```evidence setting=metastatic subtype=HER2+ line=3L+ modality=her2,adc sort=year cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,result,os caption=Third-line_and_later_trials._Several_of_these_comparators_are_physician's_choice_in_pretreated_disease,_which_is_a_floor_rather_than_a_standard.
```

Beyond second line the evidence thins. The trials in that table were built to show different things,
and three patterns run through them. Each pattern limits what can be read off the result column.

Every randomised comparison here favoured the newer agent, or the more heavily blocked arm, on its
own progression endpoint. That is the whole of the agreement. Overall survival followed in some of
them and not in others, and the split tracks the control arm rather than the drug.

{{trial:her2climb}} is where the decision rests. It randomised tucatinib against placebo on a
backbone of trastuzumab and capecitabine, which carries activity of its own, and it separated on
progression and on survival [@murthy2020; @curigliano2021]. A contrast against an active backbone is
harder to achieve and easier to believe. {{trial:egf104900}} also separated on survival, against
lapatinib given alone [@blackwell2012]. No clinic would offer that control to anyone, so the size of
the separation belongs to the comparator as much as to the combination. {{trial:th3resa}} used
physician's choice in heavily pretreated disease, which is a floor rather than a standard, and it
varied the chemotherapy alongside the blockade [@krop2014th3resa; @krop2017]. {{trial:nala}} is the
mirror image of those two. It set one kinase inhibitor against another on a shared capecitabine
backbone, so both arms were active, and its survival comparison did not separate [@saura2020nala].
That is what an active control does to a survival endpoint. It is not a statement about neratinib.
The backbone was shared in name rather than in dose. Capecitabine was given at a lower dose in the
neratinib arm, which also carried mandatory loperamide prophylaxis. A head-to-head of two inhibitors
that adjusts the partner drug between arms is not a clean comparison of the inhibitors.

Two rows have no control arm and should not be ranked against the rows that do.
{{trial:destiny-breast01}} established activity after trastuzumab emtansine in a single arm, in a
heavily pretreated population, with response by independent review as its endpoint [@modi2020].
{{trial:daisy}} is not a line-of-therapy trial at all. It gave one conjugate across three cohorts
defined by HER2 immunohistochemistry, to ask whether activity tracks the antigen [@mosele2023]. Its
HER2-overexpressing cohort is the one that belongs in this section, and the other two belong to the
HER2-low argument. Neither study reports overall survival, and neither was designed to.

Two rows rest on a biological hypothesis rather than on a position in the sequence, and they point
in opposite directions about how well that works. {{trial:patricia-solti}} acted on its hypothesis
at entry. It prescreened for a PAM50 luminal subtype before randomising palbociclib, trastuzumab and
endocrine therapy against trastuzumab-based physician's choice [@ciruelos2026; @ciruelos2020].
Progression-free survival separated. The trial is small, its comparator is again a floor, and it
reports no survival. {{trial:sophia}} did not select at entry, and it is the instructive negative. Margetuximab is engineered to bind CD16A more tightly, which should improve
antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity. It separated on progression-free survival against
trastuzumab given with the same chemotherapy, and its final overall survival did not favour it
[@rugo2021a; @rugo2022sophia]. The CD16A genotype analysis was preplanned but exploratory, and it
pointed one way for the low-affinity allele and the other way for the high-affinity one. It
generates the hypothesis it is usually read as supporting. The standard for what an exploratory
subgroup can carry is set in [[BC-740]].

What the table cannot show is the thing a clinic most wants. No trial randomised the order. The
controls are era-specific, and the kinase inhibitor doublet {{trial:emilia}} displaced is no longer
what anyone would put in a control arm. {{trial:her2climb}} and {{trial:nala}} both admitted
patients with brain metastases, so part of what they measured belongs to [[BC-930]] rather than
here. And no row enrolled a patient who had received a deruxtecan conjugate earlier in the course.

```evidence setting=metastatic subtype=HER2+ line=2L,3L+ modality=immunotherapy,parp weight=any sort=year cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,endpoint,result,os,status caption=Adding_a_mechanism_other_than_HER2_blockade._Two_trials_that_asked_a_single_question_and_one_that_has_not_reported.
```

That second table is short because the field has tried this less often than the rationale deserves.
Part of trastuzumab's effect is immune. It engages Fc receptors on effector cells, and the response
to it tracks with the lymphocytic infiltrate in the tumour.

{{trial:panacea}} found responses only in PD-L1-positive tumours, and in a minority of those
[@loi2019a]. Its PD-L1 was scored centrally on a metastatic biopsy, which is not how the marker is
obtained in clinic. Its headline figure belongs to the PD-L1-positive cohort of the expansion stage
rather than to everyone treated. Being single-arm, it cannot separate those responses from
what trastuzumab alone would have done in the same selected group. {{trial:kate2}} asked the
randomised version of the question on a conjugate backbone, and it did not meet its primary
endpoint [@emens2020]. It was unmasked early for futility, so follow-up is short on both arms. The
PD-L1-positive subgroup its authors argue for is not reported as a subgroup result, which leaves
that case resting on an analysis nobody can read. {{trial:tbcrc-050}} is testing a PARP inhibitor
added to trastuzumab without germline selection, on the argument that HER2 amplification brings
replication stress. It has not reported, so it names a strategy under test.

Neither reported trial closes the immune question. Both enrolled after HER2 blockade had already
failed, which is where a lymphocytic infiltrate is least likely to be intact. The
design that would settle it puts the checkpoint antibody at the front of the course. Nobody has run
that trial in this subtype.

What all of this leaves for the clinic is a ranking assembled from unlike comparisons. An agent with
a survival result against an active comparator has a stronger claim than one with a progression
result against a floor. Brain disease, residual neuropathy, diarrhoea tolerance and hand-foot
syndrome then decide between candidates of similar rank. Those are patient variables rather than
trial variables, and no trial in either table helps weigh them.

## BS-7610 Central nervous system disease and the agents that reach it

The compartment now has its own chapter. [[BC-930]] carries the argument about whether the barrier
explains what it is taken to explain. It also sets out how an intracranial response rate depends on
who was admitted to the cohort, and what to do about leptomeningeal disease.

Two facts from that chapter bear on the choice of a systemic agent here, and only two.
{{trial:her2climb}} admitted patients whose brain metastases were untreated or progressing. Its
central nervous system analyses were prespecified rather than found afterwards [@murthy2020].
{{trial:destiny-b12}} gave a full-length antibody conjugate in a single arm, to a cohort with stable
or active brain metastases and to a cohort without [@harbeck2024]. It reported central nervous
system progression-free survival in the first of those cohorts. There is no comparator, so it
establishes activity in the compartment rather than a rank.

The practical consequence is narrow. Brain disease changes which HER2 agent you reach for first. It
does not change whether the disease is still HER2-driven. It is not a reason to abandon systemic
blockade in favour of local therapy alone. Everything else about the compartment belongs in
[[BC-930]] and is not repeated here.

## BS-7620 Maintenance, de-escalation and treatment interruption

A patient in prolonged response faces a question the registration trials did not ask. The taxane is
usually stopped for cumulative neuropathy while antibody therapy continues. That practice is
near-universal and rests on very little randomised evidence. None of the first-line trials randomised
the stopping point.

The chemotherapy-free version of the question has been asked twice. Neither answer transfers cleanly.
{{trial:tbcrc-003}} gave dual blockade without chemotherapy in two cohorts split by prior treatment.
The gap between those cohorts is a difference between populations rather than a comparison
[@lin2015]. {{trial:egf104900}} did randomise chemotherapy-free blockade, against a single kinase
inhibitor rather than against any chemotherapy-containing regimen [@blackwell2012]. Neither of them
speaks to removing the taxane from a modern triplet in a patient who is responding to it.

{{trial:her2climb-05}} is the closest thing this subtype has to a maintenance trial, because it
randomised after induction rather than at diagnosis [@dieras2025]. What it adds is a drug rather than
permission to remove one. Nothing in the record tests subtraction directly.

Whether HER2-directed therapy itself can ever stop is unresolved. It is the same question [[BC-780]]
raises about defining cure in metastatic disease. No assay distinguishes eradicated disease from
controlled disease, so the decision is made without the information that would settle it. Endocrine
maintenance after induction does have randomised support in the hormone receptor-positive half of
this subtype, and that evidence is in [[BC-910]].

```interplay target=BC-350
Every de-escalation decision in this section is taken on a HER2 measurement made once, usually on the
primary tumour, against a threshold chosen to select for trastuzumab. Continuing blockade through
progression works because the antigen persists in most patients. The ones in whom it does not are not
identified by anything in routine use, so they are treated as though they were the others. That is the
ceiling argued in [[BS-6710]] appearing as a treatment-duration problem rather than a diagnostic one.
```

## BS-7630 HER2-mutant disease as a distinct target

```story SQ-0550
```

```story SQ-0590
```

Activating HER2 mutations occur in tumours reported as HER2-negative by immunohistochemistry and in
situ hybridisation. That is the whole point of the category, and the separation of biology from
measurement inside it is exact. A HER2-negative report means the protein is not overexpressed and
the gene is not amplified. Neither assay can see a point mutation. Finding one requires sequencing
of tumour tissue or of circulating tumour DNA.

```evidence setting=metastatic subtype=HR+/HER2- modality=her2 line=3L+,any weight=any status=reported sort=year cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,endpoint,result,os caption=Trials_entered_on_an_activating_HER2_mutation_in_disease_the_standard_assays_call_HER2-negative._Almost_every_patient_in_them_was_hormone_receptor-positive,_and_every_regimen_pairs_a_HER2_inhibitor_with_endocrine_therapy.
```

Three features of that table matter more than any figure in it. Every regimen pairs a HER2 kinase
inhibitor with an endocrine partner where the disease was hormone receptor-positive. The mutation
reads as a route out of oestrogen dependence rather than as a driver that arrived on its own. Where
the disease was not hormone receptor-positive, the endocrine partner was withheld. Every population was entered on a
sequencing result rather than found inside a larger trial, so none of these is a subgroup. And every
cohort reported activity, measured as objective response in two of the studies and as clinical
benefit in the third. A receptor switched on by mutation is blockable at its kinase, and that is the
class finding.

{{trial:summit}} carries the only randomised comparison in the field, and it is small
[@jhaveri2023summit]. Read its denominators before its rate. Of the 71 patients with hormone
receptor-positive, HER2-mutant disease who had progressed on a CDK4/6 inhibitor, 57 received
neratinib with fulvestrant and trastuzumab. Their objective response rate was 39%, with a 95%
confidence interval of 26% to 52%, and that figure and the median progression-free survival beside
it are computed on the 57 rather than on the 71. Only 21 patients were randomised, seven to each
arm, so the controlled part of the comparison is very small. Neither fulvestrant alone nor
fulvestrant with trastuzumab produced a response, and patients crossing over to the triplet did
respond. That places the activity in the
kinase inhibitor rather than in the endocrine partner, which is the closest thing available to a
demonstration of which component works. What the design cannot do is measure what neratinib adds,
because no arm gave neratinib without fulvestrant in this population.

The three studies diverge on exposure history and on assay rather than on drug. {{trial:muther}}
defined its cohorts by whether fulvestrant had already been given rather than by line, which leaves
how heavily pretreated its patients were unrecorded [@ma2022]. Clinical benefit appeared in both
cohorts, so previous failure of fulvestrant does not exclude benefit from a combination containing
it. {{trial:sgntuc-019}} is the breast cohort of a basket study in HER2-altered solid tumours, and
it gave fulvestrant to its hormone receptor-positive patients while withholding it from the rest
[@okines2025]. It reports no split between those two groups, so the one within-trial test of the
endocrine partner it could have offered is not recoverable. It also reports at 90% confidence rather
than the 95% used elsewhere in this table. That is a reporting convention rather than greater
precision, and it prints a narrower interval than the same data would give at 95%.

Longitudinal circulating tumour DNA in {{trial:summit}} showed acquisition of further HER2
alterations and of PIK3CA mutations at progression [@jhaveri2023summit]. This branch escapes the
way the others do, rather than standing outside the argument in [[BC-300]].

Two limits apply to all three. The mutation was called by whatever assay the enrolling centre
happened to run, so the entry criterion is not standardised across the table. And each study pools
mutations in different domains of the receptor, which do not all respond alike. No overall survival
is reported anywhere in this table, and none of these designs could produce one.

Acquired HER2 mutation is also a described resistance mechanism to endocrine therapy, which connects
this section to the evolution argument in [[BC-300]]. The practical consequence is a testing decision
rather than a treatment one. A patient with hormone receptor-positive disease progressing through

## BS-7640 Unresolved questions and what reads out next

Four questions matter most and none of them has an answer.

What follows first-line trastuzumab deruxtecan. If {{trial:destiny-b09}} moves that agent forward,
every line below it rests on trials that enrolled patients for whom it was still available. Nobody
has randomised anything in the post-conjugate setting. {{trial:destiny-breast07}} does not fill that
gap. It gives the conjugate alone and in combinations in first line, and it has no publication on
the record.

Whether conjugates carrying the same payload class are cross-resistant. Practice already sequences
them and the evidence does not yet support that practice. This is the problem flagged in [[BC-460]]
and generalised in [[BC-780]].

Whether a mechanism other than HER2 blockade can extend what HER2 blockade does. {{trial:tbcrc-050}}
is testing a PARP inhibitor without germline selection, on the argument that amplification brings
replication stress. Nothing beyond conference material has been published. The immune route has been
tried only in late lines, which is the least favourable place in the disease course to test it.

Whether HER2-directed therapy can be stopped in prolonged responders. No trial has asked the question
in a randomised design. That absence reflects how hard such a trial is to run rather than how
important the question is to the patients who would be in it. A patient in year six of continuous
antibody therapy is being treated on an extrapolation from year one.
