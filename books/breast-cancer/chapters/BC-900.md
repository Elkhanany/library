## BS-7570 Scope, and the line structure this chapter assumes

This chapter enumerates the systemic evidence for HER2-positive metastatic disease, line by line. The
decision architecture, meaning how to weigh a patient's disease burden, symptoms and comorbidity
against these options, is in [[BC-570]]. Conjugate mechanism and toxicity are in [[BC-460]]. Disease
that is both hormone receptor-positive and HER2-positive is treated in [[BC-910]], because its
sequencing question is different.

Two features of this subtype shape everything below. Survival is long, so a patient may receive five
or more lines and the order matters more than in any other subtype. And the lines were established
sequentially against the standard of their day, which means the trial that defined second line
enrolled patients who had not received what is now first line.

That second point is the one to hold onto. The evidence base is a stack of comparisons, each valid
against its own control, and the stack does not tell you what to do after a modern first line. The
tables below give each trial with the population it actually enrolled.

## BS-7580 First-line dual blockade with a taxane

Pertuzumab, trastuzumab and a taxane was the first-line standard for a decade, and the reason is in
the durability rather than the response rate. Dual blockade at the dimerisation interface and the
juxtamembrane domain blocks two routes to signalling, which is the mechanism in [[BC-150]].

{{trial:destiny-b09}} is the change. Trastuzumab deruxtecan with pertuzumab against the taxane triplet
moves a conjugate into first line. The argument for doing so is not that the conjugate is more active, which was
already known. It is that giving it first may convert responders into durable responders, before the
resistance described in [[BC-345]] is selected.

The argument against is specific and worth stating. Moving the best agent to first line removes it
from later lines, and the trials that established later lines enrolled patients who still had it
available. A patient who progresses on first-line trastuzumab deruxtecan is in a setting no randomised
trial has studied.

```caution
Interstitial lung disease is cumulative and does not respect line of therapy. Moving a deruxtecan
conjugate earlier lengthens the exposure of patients who will live for years. The toxicity data in
[[BC-460]] were generated in later-line populations with shorter exposure.
```

## BS-7590 Second-line conjugate therapy

{{trial:destiny-b03}} established trastuzumab deruxtecan over trastuzumab emtansine in second line,
and it did so against the comparator that {{trial:emilia}} had established a decade earlier. That
lineage is what makes the result interpretable. Each step was tested against the standard it replaced.

What the trial does not settle is the second-line choice for a patient who received a deruxtecan
conjugate first. If {{trial:destiny-b09}} moves that agent into first line, this section's evidence
becomes evidence about a population that no longer exists in practice.

```practice
When a patient progresses after a conjugate, the question is not which conjugate next but whether the
resistance was to the target, the linker-payload, or the delivery. [[BC-460]] separates the three.
Only the first is addressed by switching target.
```

## BS-7600 Third line and beyond

Beyond second line the evidence thins and the trials diverge in what they were trying to show.

{{trial:her2climb}} combined tucatinib with trastuzumab and capecitabine and enrolled patients with
active brain metastases, which almost no other registration trial did. {{trial:th3resa}} established
trastuzumab emtansine over physician's choice in a heavily pretreated population.
{{trial:nala}} compared two tyrosine kinase inhibitors on the same capecitabine backbone and met one
of two coprimary endpoints.

{{trial:sophia}} is the instructive negative. Margetuximab is Fc-engineered to bind CD16A more
tightly, which should improve antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity, and the final overall survival
analysis did not favour it. The CD16A genotype analysis was exploratory and hypothesis-generating, and
the standard for what an exploratory subgroup can support is in [[BC-740]].

## BS-7610 Central nervous system disease and the agents that reach it

Central nervous system involvement is common in this subtype and was an exclusion criterion in most
registration trials. The evidence therefore comes from two kinds of study, and they answer different
questions.

The first kind is a systemic trial that admitted central nervous system disease and reported it as a
prespecified analysis. {{trial:her2climb}} enrolled patients with active brain metastases and did
exactly that. {{trial:destiny-b12}} studied trastuzumab deruxtecan with and without brain metastases.
{{trial:nala}} reported fewer interventions for central nervous system disease with neratinib. These
carry the most weight, because the comparison is randomised and the control arm is a real one.

The second kind is a study whose entry criterion was the central nervous system disease itself. There
are more of these than the systemic literature suggests, and most are small, single-arm and phase II,
so they establish activity rather than benefit. The population column is where they differ from each
other, because active, treated and progressive-after-radiotherapy disease are not the same population.

```evidence setting=cns subtype=HER2+,HR+/HER2+,HER2-low modality=her2,adc sort=year cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,result,status caption=Trials_whose_entry_criterion_was_HER2-positive_brain_or_leptomeningeal_disease._Read_the_population_column_first,_and_read_a_single-arm_response_rate_as_activity_rather_than_as_benefit.
```

The mechanistic framing that the blood-brain barrier excludes large molecules has not survived
contact with the conjugate data, and [[BC-770]] takes that up. Established metastases disrupt the
barrier. The practical consequence is that a large molecule can be active intracranially, and
excluding it on molecular weight alone is reasoning from a model rather than from data.

## BS-7620 Maintenance, de-escalation and treatment interruption

A patient in prolonged response on a taxane triplet faces a question the trials did not ask. Taxane is
usually stopped for cumulative neuropathy while antibody therapy continues, and that practice is
near-universal and rests on very little randomised evidence.

Whether HER2-directed therapy itself can ever stop is unresolved, and it is the same question
[[BC-780]] raises about defining cure in metastatic disease. There is no assay that distinguishes
eradicated disease from controlled disease, so the decision is made without the information that would
settle it.

For hormone receptor-positive HER2-positive disease, endocrine maintenance after induction has
randomised support, and that evidence is in [[BC-910]].

## BS-7630 HER2-mutant disease as a distinct target

Activating HER2 mutations occur in tumours that are HER2-negative by immunohistochemistry and in situ
hybridisation. That is the whole point: the gene is altered rather than amplified, so the assay that
defines HER2-positive disease does not detect it.

{{trial:summit}} tested neratinib with fulvestrant and trastuzumab in this population. The separation
of biology from measurement here is exact and is worth stating to trainees. A HER2-negative report
means the protein is not overexpressed and the gene is not amplified. It says nothing about whether
the gene is mutated, which requires sequencing.

Acquired HER2 mutation is also a described resistance mechanism to endocrine therapy, which connects
this section to the evolution argument in [[BC-300]].

## BS-7640 Unresolved questions and what reads out next


Three questions matter most and none has an answer.

What follows first-line trastuzumab deruxtecan. If {{trial:destiny-b09}} moves the agent forward, the
whole sequence below it is built on trials that enrolled patients for whom it was still available.
Nobody has randomised the post-conjugate setting.

Whether conjugates with the same payload class are cross-resistant. Practice already sequences them
and the evidence does not yet support it. This is the problem flagged in [[BC-460]] and generalised in
[[BC-780]].

Whether HER2-directed therapy can be stopped in prolonged responders. No trial has asked, and the
absence is a reflection of how hard such a trial is to run rather than of the question's importance.
