## BS-4920 First-line therapy and maintenance

For a decade the first line was a taxane with trastuzumab and pertuzumab, followed by antibody
maintenance after the taxane stopped. {{trial:cleopatra}} established it and the end-of-study
analysis shows why it held. Among 808 patients followed for a median of more than eight years,
median overall survival at the final 8-year analysis was 57.1 months with pertuzumab against 40.8
months with placebo, both with trastuzumab and docetaxel. The hazard ratio for death was 0.69, and
8-year survival was 37% against 23% [@swain2020]. The earlier 2015 confirmatory analysis, quoted in
[[BS-3950]], gave 56.5 months against 40.8 months at shorter follow-up [@swain2015]. The two are
successive data cuts of one trial and not competing estimates.

That standard has now been challenged directly. {{trial:destiny-b09}} randomised patients with no
previous therapy for metastatic disease to trastuzumab deruxtecan with pertuzumab or to a taxane
with trastuzumab and pertuzumab. Median progression-free survival was 40.7 months in 383 patients
on the conjugate combination and 26.9 months in 387 patients on the taxane triplet. The hazard
ratio for progression or death was 0.56, with a 95% confidence interval of 0.44 to 0.71. Confirmed
responses occurred in 85.1% against 78.6%, and median duration of response was 39.2 months against
26.4 months [@tolaney2026destinyb09].

Two features of that trial should be stated with the result. Overall survival was not the
primary endpoint and was not mature at the reported interim analysis. And adjudicated
drug-related interstitial lung disease or pneumonitis occurred in 12.1% of patients receiving
the conjugate combination against 1.0% on the taxane triplet, with two deaths
[@tolaney2026destinyb09].

```caution
A chemotherapy-free first line is the obvious headline and it is not quite what the trial
tested. The conjugate carries a cytotoxic payload, so what has changed is delivery rather than
the presence of chemotherapy. What is genuinely lost is the finite induction model. The taxane
triplet gives chemotherapy for a defined period and then stops, leaving antibody maintenance.
The conjugate continues until progression. For a patient facing 40 months of treatment, a
13.8-month gain in median progression-free survival and a continuous 12.1% pneumonitis risk are
being traded against a regimen with a known exit [@swain2020; @tolaney2026destinyb09].
```

## BS-4930 Sequencing HER2-directed agents after first progression

Second-line treatment is the position where the evidence is strongest and where the first-line
change has done the most damage to the reasoning.

{{trial:destiny-b03}} compared trastuzumab deruxtecan with trastuzumab emtansine in 524 patients
previously treated with trastuzumab and a taxane. At the primary analysis, 75.8% of patients on
the conjugate were alive without progression at 12 months against 34.1%, with a hazard ratio of
0.28 [@cortes2022destinyb03]. With longer follow-up, median progression-free survival was 28.8
months against 6.8 months, with a hazard ratio of 0.33. Median overall survival was not reached
in either arm, with a hazard ratio for death of 0.64 and a 95% confidence interval of 0.47 to
0.87 [@hurvitz2023].

That is one of the largest effects in the disease and it was measured in a specific population.
Everyone in it was conjugate-naive. If trastuzumab deruxtecan becomes the first-line standard on
the strength of {{trial:destiny-b09}}, no randomised trial describes what should follow it.

The practical consequence is that second line now has two different answers depending on what
came first. After a taxane triplet, the answer is trastuzumab deruxtecan on direct randomised
evidence. After first-line trastuzumab deruxtecan, the answer is inferred, and the candidates
are a tyrosine kinase inhibitor combination [[BS-4950]], trastuzumab emtansine, or a
non-cross-resistant conjugate. None of those has been tested in that position.

```caution
Trial evidence attaches to a population, not to a line number. Reporting {{trial:destiny-b03}} as
establishing trastuzumab deruxtecan in the second line was accurate when nobody received it
first. It is no longer a safe shorthand. Whenever a regimen moves earlier, every downstream
result loses the population it was measured in.
```

## BS-4940 Antibody-drug conjugates and the reordering of later lines

Three conjugates now act in this disease and they are not interchangeable. Trastuzumab emtansine
carries a non-cleavable linker and a microtubule payload with no bystander effect. Trastuzumab
deruxtecan carries a cleavable linker and a topoisomerase-I payload that crosses membranes. The
chemistry and its consequences are developed in [[BC-460]].

The reordering that followed {{trial:destiny-b03}} was not simply that one drug beat another. It
was that a conjugate whose payload kills neighbouring cells outperformed one whose payload does
not, in a disease where antigen expression is not uniform. That is the heterogeneity argument
arriving as a treatment result, and the evidence for it across the expression range comes from
{{trial:daisy}} [@mosele2023].

Trastuzumab emtansine has not disappeared. It retains a defined role in residual disease after
neoadjuvant therapy, which is the subject of {{trial:katherine}} and belongs to [[BC-500]]. In
the metastatic setting it is now a later option rather than a second-line standard.

What no trial answers is what a second conjugate is worth after the first has failed. The
available evidence is retrospective. Among 85 patients who received both trastuzumab deruxtecan
and sacituzumab govitecan in either order, progression-free survival on the second conjugate was
shorter than on the first in 75.2%, with 14 of the 85 still on treatment at the data cutoff
[@mai2025]. Activity was retained and the expected magnitude was smaller. The reasoning is
developed in [[BS-3030]].

## BS-4950 Tyrosine kinase inhibitor selection

Three oral inhibitors are in use and they differ in selectivity, which determines both their
toxicity and where they are useful.

Tucatinib is highly selective for HER2 and spares EGFR. That is why its dose-limiting toxicity
is not the rash and diarrhoea that constrain the less selective agents. In {{trial:her2climb}},
612 patients previously treated with trastuzumab, pertuzumab and trastuzumab emtansine received
tucatinib or placebo with trastuzumab and capecitabine. Median progression-free survival was 7.8
months against 5.6 months, with a hazard ratio of 0.54. Median overall survival was 21.9 months
against 17.4 months, with a hazard ratio of 0.66 [@murthy2020].

Neratinib is an irreversible pan-HER inhibitor. Its EGFR activity produces diarrhoea severe
enough to require prophylaxis, and it is also the reason it has activity in HER2-mutant disease
[[BS-4990]].

Lapatinib is reversible and dual, and is now largely displaced.

The selection logic is short. Central nervous system disease favours tucatinib on the evidence
in [[BS-4960]]. HER2-mutant disease favours neratinib. Otherwise the choice is driven by
toxicity tolerance and by what the patient can take orally.

One point from resistance biology belongs here. Antibodies and kinase inhibitors are escaped by
different routes, with distinct contributions from oestrogen receptor signalling and from
receptor reactivation [@wangyc2011]. Failure of an antibody is therefore weak evidence about a
kinase inhibitor, and the converse holds. Class detail is in [[BC-450]] and mechanism in
[[BS-2990]].

## BS-4960 Central nervous system active strategies

Brain metastases occur in a large minority of patients with HER2-positive metastatic disease,
and for many years the systemic options were assumed to be inactive there. That assumption was
wrong and the correction came from two directions.

{{trial:her2climb}} included patients with treated stable and with active untreated brain
metastases, which most trials exclude. In the brain metastasis subgroup, 24.9% of patients on
tucatinib were alive without progression at one year against 0% on placebo, with a hazard ratio
of 0.48. Median progression-free survival in that subgroup was 7.6 months against 5.4 months
[@murthy2020]. This remains the strongest randomised central nervous system evidence in the
disease.

{{trial:destiny-b12}} addressed the same question for trastuzumab deruxtecan in a single-arm
phase 3b/4 design. Among 263 patients with stable or active brain metastases, 61.6% were alive
without progression at 12 months and 58.9% without central nervous system progression
[@harbeck2024]. Investigator-reported interstitial lung disease occurred in 16% of that cohort.

```caution
These two results are not comparable and are often placed side by side. {{trial:her2climb}} is randomised
against placebo and reports a between-arm difference. {{trial:destiny-b12}} is single-arm and reports
a rate. A 58.9% 12-month central nervous system progression-free rate has no control arm behind
it [@harbeck2024]. Both agents are intracranially active. Only one has a randomised comparison
supporting that claim.
```

The strategy question is when to use local therapy. Systemic activity does not remove the need
for radiotherapy or surgery in a symptomatic or structurally threatening lesion. It does allow
whole-brain radiotherapy to be deferred more often than it once was. Organ-specific management,
including leptomeningeal disease, is developed in [[BC-600]].

## BS-4970 Hormone receptor-positive HER2-positive disease and endocrine integration

Roughly half of HER2-positive disease is also hormone receptor positive, and it has been treated
for years as though the second receptor were incidental. It is not.

The biological reason is reciprocal. Oestrogen receptor signalling is upregulated as an adaptive
survival mechanism in HER2-positive tumours under anti-HER2 therapy [@giuliano2015]. Blocking
one pathway increases dependence on the other. That predicts that blocking both should work
better than blocking either, and it predicts that endocrine maintenance should not be optional.

{{trial:patina}} tested the strongest version of that idea. Patients with hormone receptor
positive, HER2-positive metastatic disease who had not progressed after four to eight cycles of
chemotherapy with HER2-targeted therapy were randomised to maintenance HER2-targeted and
endocrine therapy with or without palbociclib. Among 518 patients followed for a median of 53.5
months, median progression-free survival was 44.3 months with palbociclib against 29.1 months
without. The hazard ratio was 0.75, with a 95% confidence interval of 0.59 to 0.96. Grade 3
adverse events occurred in 79.7% against 30.6%, predominantly neutropenia
[@metzger2026patina].

That toxicity figure deserves to sit next to the efficacy figure. A 15.2-month gain in median
progression-free survival is being bought with grade 3 events in four patients out of five, in a
maintenance setting where patients are well.

Real-world data support endocrine maintenance itself rather than the addition of palbociclib
[@carausu2022]. Heterogeneity in both receptors, and why a report describing both as positive
has averaged twice, is in [[BS-2370]]. Pathway cross-talk is in [[BC-150]].

## BS-4980 Heterogeneous or changing HER2 expression

HER2-positive is a binary category applied to a continuous and spatially variable quantity. Two
consequences follow for metastatic management.

The first is heterogeneity at diagnosis. A tumour can contain amplified and unamplified
compartments, and the arrangement matters as much as the proportion. In a neoadjuvant study of
trastuzumab emtansine with pertuzumab, no patient with HER2 heterogeneity achieved a pathologic
complete response against 55% of those without it [@filho2021]. The full argument is in
[[BC-280]].

The second is change over time. A biopsy at progression that reports HER2-negative disease is
frequently an accurate measurement of what treatment has selected, not a laboratory error
[[BS-2340]]. Preclinical models with mixed high and low expressing populations reproduce this
during the evolution of resistance [@goyette2026].

The practical rule is to obtain recent tissue before a decision that depends on expression
level, and to treat the result as a statement about the biopsied lesion at that time. Receptor
discordance and how to interpret it is in [[BC-290]].

What a falling HER2 result does not mean is that HER2-directed treatment is finished. Activity
at low and absent expression is established, with confirmed responses in 29.7% of patients
scored immunohistochemistry 0 in {{trial:daisy}} [@mosele2023]. The expression threshold is an
eligibility boundary rather than a biological one, and the reproducibility of the boundary
itself is poor [@robbins2023; @wolff2023]. The generalised version of this argument is
[[BC-590]].

```interplay target=BC-350
HER2-positive disease is where the heterogeneity argument of Part VI is most directly
operational. The mean expression level determines eligibility. The variance determines what
survives. A conjugate with a membrane-permeable payload converts part of that variance problem
into a delivery solution, which is why bystander-capable agents outperformed
non-bystander agents in a disease whose antigen is unevenly distributed
[@hurvitz2023; @mosele2023]. Mean and variance are separate axes, and current reporting gives
only the mean.
```

## BS-4990 HER2-mutant disease as a separate therapeutic problem

Activating HER2 mutations occur in tumours that are not HER2-amplified, and they were identified
as oncogenic drivers in gene-amplification-negative breast cancer [@bose2013]. This is a
different disease from HER2-positive breast cancer and it is regularly filed under the same
heading.

Three distinctions matter. The alteration is a point mutation, not an amplification. The tumours
are usually hormone receptor positive and HER2-negative by standard testing, so they belong
clinically to [[BC-560]]. And the mutation is enriched in lobular histology, which makes
sequencing worthwhile in that setting [[BS-4900]].

The drug that works is an irreversible pan-HER inhibitor rather than an antibody. In a basket
study, neratinib produced responses across HER2-mutant and HER3-mutant cancers, with response
depending on the specific allele [@hyman2018]. The breast-specific result comes from the same
programme. Seventy-one patients had hormone receptor positive, HER2-mutant metastatic breast
cancer and had progressed on a CDK4/6 inhibitor. The 57 of them who received neratinib with
fulvestrant and trastuzumab had an objective response rate of 39%, with a 95% confidence interval
of 26% to 52%, and median progression-free survival of 8.3 months. Both figures are computed on
those 57 and not on the 71. No responses occurred with fulvestrant alone or with fulvestrant and
trastuzumab [@jhaveri2023summit]. The last sentence is what establishes that the kinase inhibitor
is doing the work.

Two limits belong with that result. The study was single-arm within a basket, and 57 patients is
a small denominator for a response rate. And longitudinal circulating tumour DNA showed
acquisition of further HER2 alterations and of PIK3CA mutations at progression
[@jhaveri2023summit], which places this branch alongside the others rather than outside them.

## BS-5000 Resistance after conjugate exposure

Progression on a conjugate is not one event. A conjugate has to bind, internalise, traffic,
release payload, retain it and kill with it. Antigen expression answers the first step only, and
no routine assay measures the others. The mechanistic account is in [[BS-3000]].

Two experimental results define the shape of the problem. Cell line models selected for resistance
to trastuzumab emtansine failed by unrelated routes, one through reduced HER2 with MDR1
upregulation and the other through loss of the lysosomal transporter SLC46A3 with PTEN deficiency.
The rescues were not interchangeable [@li2018]. In patients, PTEN loss was associated with de novo
resistance to trastuzumab deruxtecan. The hazard ratio was 3.20, with a 95% confidence interval of
1.47 to 6.97. For sacituzumab govitecan the hazard ratio was 1.18, with an interval crossing one
[@mai2025]. The same payload class, and a genomic lesion that affected only one of the two agents.

The practical position is uncomfortable and should be stated. A patient progressing on
trastuzumab deruxtecan may have lost antigen, may have lost payload handling, or may have lost a
downstream dependency. Only the first is measurable in clinic. Measuring HER2 and finding it
retained does not establish that a different HER2-directed conjugate will work.

What follows in practice is an antibody-independent or payload-independent option. A tyrosine
kinase inhibitor combination changes both the delivery mechanism and the mode of killing
[[BS-4950]]. That is a stronger argument than switching to a second conjugate with the same
payload class.

## BS-5010 Treatment beyond progression and when to stop HER2 blockade

The convention in this disease is that HER2 blockade continues across lines while the backbone
changes. It is a well-founded convention and it is worth knowing what founds it.

The argument is that HER2 amplification is a truncal event in most of these tumours, so the
dependency usually persists even when a particular agent stops working. That is a claim about
the genome and it is generally right. The claim it does not support is that continued blockade
is therefore always beneficial, because dependence is a separate question from presence
[[BS-2990]]. A stable HER2 result at progression establishes that the antigen is still there.

Three situations argue for stopping.

The first is documented loss of the target on recent tissue, in a patient whose disease is now
behaving like HER2-negative disease. That is selection rather than error [[BS-2340]], and it
moves the patient to a different chapter.

The second is cardiac. Cumulative anthracycline exposure and years of antibody therapy produce
declines in left ventricular function that are sometimes not recoverable. Cardiac surveillance
and its management are in [[BC-640]].

The third is that continuing blockade is being used as a substitute for a conversation.
Antibody therapy is easy to continue and hard to stop, because stopping is legible to the
patient as a decision in a way that continuing is not.

```practice
Reassess HER2 on recent tissue before assuming the target is still present, particularly after
conjugate exposure.

Do not treat a retained HER2 result as evidence that a conjugate will work again. It answers one
of six questions [[BS-3000]].

Check left ventricular function on schedule rather than on symptoms, and treat a fall as a
reason to reconsider the whole regimen rather than to pause one drug.

When a patient has progressed through conjugates, kinase inhibitors and antibodies, say so
plainly. Continuing an antibody alongside best supportive care is a reasonable choice made
explicitly. It is a poor substitute for the conversation in [[BC-680]].
```
