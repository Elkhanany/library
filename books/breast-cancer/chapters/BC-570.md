## BS-4920 First-line therapy and maintenance

The first line in this disease was assembled in layers, and the table below is those layers in
order. Three of its trials tested adding an antibody to chemotherapy. Two tested replacing the
chemotherapy with a conjugate. Two tested adding an oral inhibitor to the antibodies. One has no
comparator at all. Reading the result column as a ranking of regimens ignores all four of those
designs.

```evidence setting=metastatic subtype=HER2+ line=1L modality=her2,adc status=reported cols=acronym,phase,n,arms,result,os caption=First-line_trials_in_HER2-positive_metastatic_disease,_oldest_layer_last._The_line-by-line_trial_record,_including_the_trials_that_added_a_kinase_inhibitor_to_the_antibody_pair,_is_argued_in_BC-900.
```

Every randomised addition here worked in the same direction. {{trial:h0648g}} and {{trial:m77001}}
added trastuzumab to chemotherapy and each lengthened both time to progression and survival
[@slamon2001; @marty2005]. {{trial:cleopatra}} then added pertuzumab to trastuzumab and a taxane
and did the same [@swain2020]. That is the whole of the mature survival evidence in this section,
and it is why the taxane triplet held the position for a decade. The 2015 confirmatory analysis
quoted in [[BS-3950]] is an earlier data cut of CLEOPATRA and not a competing estimate
[@swain2015].

The substitution question has been asked twice and answered in opposite directions.
{{trial:marianne}} replaced the taxane with trastuzumab emtansine and reached only
non-inferiority against trastuzumab plus a taxane, including in the arm that added pertuzumab to
the conjugate [@perez2016]. {{trial:destiny-b09}} replaced the taxane with trastuzumab deruxtecan
and beat the full triplet [@tolaney2026destinyb09]. The easy reading is that trial conduct
improved over the intervening decade. It is the wrong one. MARIANNE ran against the weaker of the
two control arms, because its comparator carried no pertuzumab. A conjugate that cannot beat the
weaker comparator, set beside one that beats the stronger, separates the two drugs rather than
the two designs. The chemistry behind that separation is in [[BC-460]].

{{trial:phila}} and {{trial:her2climb-05}} both added an oral HER2 inhibitor, and they are not the
same experiment. PHILA added pyrotinib to trastuzumab and docetaxel in Chinese patients, against a
control arm with no pertuzumab in it [@ma2023; @ma2026]. HER2CLIMB-05 added tucatinib to
trastuzumab and pertuzumab maintenance after induction, against the regimen a clinic actually
gives [@dieras2025]. Part of the PHILA separation is the contribution pertuzumab was never allowed
to make in that trial. The difference between the two results is the control arm before it is the
drug, and the toxicity that decides each is different too. Pyrotinib produced grade 3 or higher
treatment-related events in 90% of patients against 76% on placebo [@ma2023]. Tucatinib produced
diarrhoea in 72.7% of a maintenance population who were otherwise well [@dieras2025].

The decision rests on DESTINY-Breast09, because it is the only trial in the table randomised
against the thing the clinic would otherwise do. Two features belong with its result. Overall
survival was not the primary endpoint and was not mature at the reported interim analysis.
Adjudicated drug-related interstitial lung disease or pneumonitis occurred in 12.1% of patients on
the conjugate combination against 1.0% on the taxane triplet, with two deaths
[@tolaney2026destinyb09].

Four things the table cannot show. The third group in DESTINY-Breast09 received the conjugate
without pertuzumab and is still blinded, so what pertuzumab contributes to the new first line is
unknown. No trial randomises the conjugate strategy against the tucatinib maintenance strategy,
and a patient cannot be given both. {{trial:peruse}} carries a long median overall survival in a
single arm, which describes who enrolled rather than what the regimen did [@miles2021a]. And none
of these trials enrolled patients progressing on or shortly after adjuvant conjugate therapy,
which is now a growing group with no first-line evidence of its own. HER2 eligibility was also
assayed locally in the oldest of these trials and centrally in the newest, so the populations
differ by measurement as well as by era.

```caution
A chemotherapy-free first line is the obvious headline and it is not quite what the trial
tested. The conjugate carries a cytotoxic payload, so what has changed is delivery rather than
the presence of chemotherapy. What is genuinely lost is the finite induction model. The taxane
triplet gives chemotherapy for a defined period and then stops, leaving antibody maintenance.
The conjugate continues until progression. For a patient facing years of treatment, the gain in
median progression-free survival and a continuous 12.1% pneumonitis risk are being traded against
a regimen with a known exit [@swain2020; @tolaney2026destinyb09].
```

This chapter is about how to approach the patient in front of you. Its subjects are the shape of
the first regimen, what each choice costs at the next progression, when the target is worth
remeasuring and when to stop. [[BC-900]] owns the line-by-line trial record and is where each
comparison is argued in full. Central nervous system disease has its own chapter in [[BC-930]],
and hormone receptor-positive HER2-positive disease is developed in [[BC-910]]. The tables here are
generated from the trial registry and filtered on the decision each section argues, so a figure
stays in the prose only where the sentence is about that figure.

## BS-4930 Sequencing HER2-directed agents after first progression

Second-line treatment is the position where the evidence is strongest. It is also the position the
first-line change has damaged most.

{{trial:destiny-b03}} compared trastuzumab deruxtecan with trastuzumab emtansine after
trastuzumab and a taxane. The effect on progression is one of the largest in the disease, and
survival followed it [@cortes2022destinyb03; @hurvitz2023]. The figures are in the table in
[[BS-4940]] and the comparison is argued in [[BC-900]].

That result was measured in a population in which everyone was conjugate-naive. If trastuzumab
deruxtecan becomes the first-line standard on the strength of {{trial:destiny-b09}}, no randomised
trial describes what should follow it.

The practical consequence is that second line now has two answers, and which one applies is set by
what came first. After a taxane triplet, the answer is trastuzumab deruxtecan on direct randomised
evidence. After first-line trastuzumab deruxtecan, the answer is inferred. The candidates are a
tyrosine kinase inhibitor combination [[BS-4950]], trastuzumab emtansine, or a non-cross-resistant
conjugate. None has been tested in that position, and the inference rests on the class argument in
[[BS-5000]] rather than on a trial.

```caution
Trial evidence attaches to a population, not to a line number. {{trial:emilia}} put trastuzumab
emtansine into the second line against a lapatinib doublet that no clinic would now offer, and
{{trial:destiny-b03}} then displaced it in a population that had never seen a conjugate. Reporting
either as establishing a line number was accurate only while the line above it stood still.
Whenever a regimen moves earlier, every downstream result loses the population it was measured in.
```

## BS-4940 Antibody-drug conjugates and the reordering of later lines

Three conjugates now act in this disease and they are not interchangeable. Trastuzumab emtansine
carries a non-cleavable linker and a microtubule payload with no bystander effect. Trastuzumab
deruxtecan carries a cleavable linker and a topoisomerase-I payload that crosses membranes. The
chemistry and its consequences are developed in [[BC-460]].

```evidence setting=metastatic subtype=HER2+ line=2L modality=adc status=reported cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,result,os caption=Randomised_conjugate_trials_at_first_progression._Read_the_population_column_first,_because_the_prior-therapy_requirement_is_what_places_each_result_in_a_sequence_rather_than_the_line_number_on_the_label.
```

Five randomised comparisons sit in that cell and they answer two different questions. Three ask
which conjugate to give, and against what. Two hold trastuzumab emtansine constant in both arms
and ask what to add to it. Effect sizes from the two groups do not belong on one axis.

Where the first three agree is narrow and firm. Wherever a conjugate was randomised against the
accepted treatment of its own day, progression was delayed by a wide margin. That held against a
lapatinib doublet in {{trial:emilia}}, against a capecitabine doublet after emtansine failure in
{{trial:destiny-breast02}}, and against emtansine itself in {{trial:destiny-b03}}
[@verma2012; @andr2023; @cortes2022destinyb03]. The spread between those three results is
therefore partly a spread in how weak each comparator was, and partly a spread in what patients
had already received. That second variable is what the population column records, and it is the
one to read first.

The two add-on trials are the only pair in the table with an identical control arm.
{{trial:her2climb-02}} and {{trial:kate2}} both gave trastuzumab emtansine to everyone, in patients
previously treated with trastuzumab and a taxane. Adding a selective HER2 kinase inhibitor delayed
progression. Adding a checkpoint inhibitor did not, and the study was unmasked early for futility
[@hurvitz2025; @emens2020]. With design, backbone and population matched, that difference is
mechanism rather than trial construction. It is the one place in this chapter where two trials can
fairly be compared with each other.

The second-line decision rests on DESTINY-Breast03. It randomised against the drug that was the
standard in exactly its own population, and the survival result followed the progression result
[@corts2024]. Nothing else in the table carries that combination.

Four limits travel with the rest of it. DESTINY-Breast02 has a direction for overall survival and
no magnitude on the record, so it cannot be set against the survival results either side of it
[@andr2023]. EMILIA's tabulated survival comes from the second interim analysis, while the 2017
final analysis gave medians of 29.9 against 25.9 months with a hazard ratio of 0.75 [@diras2017].
That is one trial reported twice rather than two estimates in conflict. KATE2 is a phase 2 study
with no survival data at all and belongs in the table as a negative result rather than as a
comparator. And both add-on trials were built on a backbone that DESTINY-Breast03 has since
displaced, so their question has not been asked again on a deruxtecan backbone.

The reordering that followed DESTINY-Breast03 was not simply that one drug beat another. It was
that a conjugate whose payload kills neighbouring cells outperformed one whose payload does not,
in a disease where antigen expression is not uniform. That is the heterogeneity argument arriving
as a treatment result, and the evidence for it across the expression range comes from
{{trial:daisy}} [@mosele2023].

Trastuzumab emtansine has not disappeared. It retains a defined role in residual disease after
neoadjuvant therapy, which is an early-disease question and belongs to [[BC-500]]. In the
metastatic setting it is now a later option rather than a second-line standard.

What no trial answers is what a second conjugate is worth after the first has failed. The
available evidence is retrospective. Among 85 patients who received both trastuzumab deruxtecan
and sacituzumab govitecan in either order, progression-free survival on the second conjugate was
shorter than on the first in 75.2%. Fourteen of the 85 were still on treatment at the data cutoff
[@mai2025]. Activity was retained and the expected magnitude was smaller. The reasoning is
developed in [[BS-3030]].

## BS-4950 Tyrosine kinase inhibitor selection

Three oral inhibitors are in use and they differ in selectivity, which determines both their
toxicity and where they are useful.

Tucatinib is highly selective for HER2 and spares EGFR. That is why its dose-limiting toxicity
is not the rash and diarrhoea that constrain the less selective agents. {{trial:her2climb}} added
it to trastuzumab and capecitabine after trastuzumab, pertuzumab and trastuzumab emtansine,
lengthening both progression-free and overall survival [@murthy2020]. The trial record for the
class is in [[BC-900]].

Neratinib is an irreversible pan-HER inhibitor. Its EGFR activity produces diarrhoea severe
enough to require prophylaxis, and it is also the reason it has activity in HER2-mutant disease
[[BS-4990]].

Lapatinib is reversible and dual, and is now largely displaced.

The selection logic is short. Central nervous system disease favours tucatinib, on the evidence
assembled in [[BC-930]]. HER2-mutant disease favours neratinib. Otherwise the choice is driven by
toxicity tolerance and by what the patient can take orally.

One point from resistance biology belongs here. Antibodies and kinase inhibitors are escaped by
different routes, with distinct contributions from oestrogen receptor signalling and from
receptor reactivation [@wangyc2011]. Failure of an antibody is therefore weak evidence about a
kinase inhibitor, and the converse holds. Class detail is in [[BC-450]] and mechanism in
[[BS-2990]].

## BS-4960 Central nervous system active strategies

Brain metastases occur in a large minority of patients with HER2-positive metastatic disease, and
for many years the systemic options were assumed to be inactive there. That assumption was wrong.
Both a selective kinase inhibitor and a bystander-capable conjugate have intracranial activity,
with the trials that establish it collected in [[BC-930]].

Only the decisions that change a systemic plan belong in this chapter. There are two of them.

The first is that a demonstrated intracranial response does not remove the need for local
therapy. A symptomatic or structurally threatening lesion still needs radiotherapy or surgery. What
systemic activity has changed is how often whole-brain radiotherapy can be deferred.

The second is that the two agents most often placed side by side are not supported by the same
kind of evidence. One has a randomised between-arm comparison. The other has a single-arm rate.
That distinction, and what an intracranial response rate can carry, is worked through in
[[BS-7850]]. Leptomeningeal disease and the rest of organ-specific management are in [[BC-930]]
and [[BC-600]].

## BS-4970 Hormone receptor-positive HER2-positive disease and endocrine integration

Roughly half of HER2-positive disease is also hormone receptor positive, and it has been treated
for years as though the second receptor were incidental. It is not.

The biological reason is reciprocal. Oestrogen receptor signalling is upregulated as an adaptive
survival mechanism in HER2-positive tumours under anti-HER2 therapy [@giuliano2015]. Blocking
one pathway increases dependence on the other. That predicts that blocking both should work
better than blocking either, and it predicts that endocrine maintenance should not be optional.

{{trial:patina}} tested the strongest version of that idea by adding palbociclib to HER2-targeted
and endocrine maintenance after induction chemotherapy. Median progression-free survival was 44.3
months with palbociclib against 29.1 months without it [@metzger2026patina]. The trial and its
comparators are argued in [[BC-910]].

What belongs here is the price. Grade 3 adverse events occurred in 79.7% of patients on
palbociclib against 30.6% without it, predominantly neutropenia [@metzger2026patina]. Those events
are being incurred in a maintenance setting, in patients who are well and who have already
finished their chemotherapy. Overall survival has not been reported, so the trade is currently a
progression endpoint against a toxicity rate.

Real-world data support endocrine maintenance itself rather than the addition of palbociclib
[@carausu2022]. Heterogeneity in both receptors, and why a report describing both as positive
has averaged twice, is in [[BS-2370]]. Pathway cross-talk is in [[BC-150]].

## BS-4980 Heterogeneous or changing HER2 expression

HER2-positive is a binary category applied to a continuous and spatially variable quantity. Two
consequences follow for metastatic management.

The first is heterogeneity at diagnosis. A tumour can contain amplified and unamplified
compartments, and the arrangement matters as much as the proportion. In a neoadjuvant study of
trastuzumab emtansine with pertuzumab, no patient with HER2 heterogeneity achieved a pathologic
complete response against 55% of those without it [@filho2021]. The full argument is in
[[BC-280]].

The second is change over time. A biopsy at progression that reports HER2-negative disease is
frequently an accurate measurement of what treatment has selected, not a laboratory error
[[BS-2340]]. Preclinical models with mixed high and low expressing populations reproduce this
during the evolution of resistance [@goyette2026].

Three studies tried to replace the count of previous lines with a measurement of the target, and
they agree on what they found. The antigen is measurable, it is unevenly distributed within a
single patient, and measuring it does not yet allocate treatment. {{trial:zephir}} imaged HER2
with a labelled antibody before a conjugate. It found patients whose lesions took up no tracer,
and it found uneven uptake within individual patients [@gebhart2016]. {{trial:tbcrc-003}} read an
early metabolic response inside a chemotherapy-free dual blockade study, where absence of that
response predicted failure to respond by imaging criteria in both cohorts [@lin2015].
{{trial:daisy}} gave a conjugate to cohorts cut by central immunohistochemistry score and produced
responses in every cohort, including the one scored as non-expressing [@mosele2023].

They diverge in what they measured rather than in what they showed. None of them is an allocation
trial. A predictive value is computed in one cohort all treated the same way, against a
radiological definition of response. It describes how a scan tracks an outcome. It does not
establish that acting on the scan changes one, and nobody has randomised patients to be allocated
by imaging.

{{trial:destiny-breast01}} carries the conclusion that actually changed practice, and it measured
nothing. It gave a deruxtecan conjugate to disease that had already failed a conjugate aimed at
the same antigen, and objective response occurred in 112 of 184 patients [@modi2020]. Failure of
one HER2 conjugate does not predict failure of the next. Because there was no control arm, the
trial cannot say whether that is persistence of the antigen or a difference in payload.

What none of this supports is a threshold. DAISY's cohorts were cut from a central
immunohistochemistry score, so the boundary between them is an assay convention rather than a
biological class. Each cohort was single-arm, so activity in the non-expressing cohort may be
reporting the floor of that assay as much as activity without the target. The reproducibility of
the boundary itself is poor [@robbins2023; @wolff2023]. The generalised version of this argument
is [[BC-590]].

The practical rule is to obtain recent tissue before a decision that depends on expression
level, and to treat the result as a statement about the biopsied lesion at that time. A falling
HER2 result does not mean that HER2-directed treatment is finished. Receptor discordance and how
to interpret it is in [[BC-290]].

```interplay target=BC-350
HER2-positive disease is where the heterogeneity argument of Part VI is most directly
operational. The mean expression level determines eligibility. The variance determines what
survives. A conjugate with a membrane-permeable payload converts part of that variance problem
into a delivery solution, which is why bystander-capable agents outperformed
non-bystander agents in a disease whose antigen is unevenly distributed
[@hurvitz2023; @mosele2023]. Mean and variance are separate axes, and current reporting gives
only the mean.
```

## BS-4990 HER2-mutant disease as a separate therapeutic problem

Activating HER2 mutations occur in tumours that are not HER2-amplified, and they were identified
as oncogenic drivers in gene-amplification-negative breast cancer [@bose2013]. This is a
different disease from HER2-positive breast cancer and it is regularly filed under the same
heading.

Three distinctions matter. The alteration is a point mutation, not an amplification. The tumours
are usually hormone receptor positive and HER2-negative by standard testing, so they belong
clinically to [[BC-560]]. And the mutation is enriched in lobular histology, which makes
sequencing worthwhile in that setting [[BS-4900]].

The drug that works is an irreversible pan-HER inhibitor rather than an antibody. In a basket
study, neratinib produced responses across HER2-mutant and HER3-mutant cancers, with response
depending on the specific allele [@hyman2018]. The breast-specific result comes from the same
programme. Seventy-one patients had hormone receptor positive, HER2-mutant metastatic breast
cancer and had progressed on a CDK4/6 inhibitor. The 57 of them who received neratinib with
fulvestrant and trastuzumab had an objective response rate of 39%, with a 95% confidence interval
of 26% to 52%. Both that figure and the median progression-free survival are computed on those 57
and not on the 71. No responses occurred with fulvestrant alone or with fulvestrant and
trastuzumab [@jhaveri2023summit]. The last sentence is what establishes that the kinase inhibitor
is doing the work.

Two limits belong with that result. The study was single-arm within a basket, and 57 patients is
a small denominator for a response rate. And longitudinal circulating tumour DNA showed
acquisition of further HER2 alterations and of PIK3CA mutations at progression
[@jhaveri2023summit], which places this branch alongside the others rather than outside them. The
wider question of whether blocking a mutant receptor restores endocrine sensitivity is developed
in [[BC-870]].

## BS-5000 Resistance after conjugate exposure

Progression on a conjugate is not one event. A conjugate has to bind, internalise, traffic,
release payload, retain it and kill with it. Antigen expression answers the first step only, and
no routine assay measures the others. The mechanistic account is in [[BS-3000]].

Two experimental results define the shape of the problem. Cell line models selected for resistance
to trastuzumab emtansine failed by unrelated routes, one through reduced HER2 with MDR1
upregulation and the other through loss of the lysosomal transporter SLC46A3 with PTEN deficiency.
The rescues were not interchangeable [@li2018]. In patients, PTEN loss was associated with de novo
resistance to trastuzumab deruxtecan. The hazard ratio was 3.20, with a 95% confidence interval of
1.47 to 6.97. For sacituzumab govitecan the hazard ratio was 1.18, with an interval crossing one
[@mai2025]. The same payload class, and a genomic lesion that affected only one of the two agents.

The practical position is uncomfortable and should be stated. A patient progressing on
trastuzumab deruxtecan may have lost antigen, may have lost payload handling, or may have lost a
downstream dependency. Only the first is measurable in clinic. Measuring HER2 and finding it
retained does not establish that a different HER2-directed conjugate will work.

What follows in practice is an antibody-independent or payload-independent option. A tyrosine
kinase inhibitor combination changes both the delivery mechanism and the mode of killing
[[BS-4950]]. That is a stronger argument than switching to a second conjugate with the same
payload class.

## BS-5010 Treatment beyond progression and when to stop HER2 blockade

The convention in this disease is that HER2 blockade continues across lines while the backbone
changes. It is a well-founded convention and it is worth knowing what founds it.

The argument is that HER2 amplification is a truncal event in most of these tumours, so the
dependency usually persists even when a particular agent stops working. That is a claim about
the genome and it is generally right. The claim it does not support is that continued blockade
is therefore always beneficial, because dependence is a separate question from presence
[[BS-2990]]. A stable HER2 result at progression establishes that the antigen is still there.

Three situations argue for stopping.

The first is documented loss of the target on recent tissue, in a patient whose disease is now
behaving like HER2-negative disease. That is selection rather than error [[BS-2340]], and it
moves the patient to a different chapter.

The second is cardiac. Cumulative anthracycline exposure and years of antibody therapy produce
declines in left ventricular function that are sometimes not recoverable. Cardiac surveillance
and its management are in [[BC-640]].

The third is that continuing blockade is being used as a substitute for a conversation.
Antibody therapy is easy to continue and hard to stop, because stopping is legible to the
patient as a decision in a way that continuing is not.

```practice
Reassess HER2 on recent tissue before assuming the target is still present, particularly after
conjugate exposure.

Do not treat a retained HER2 result as evidence that a conjugate will work again. It answers one
of six questions [[BS-3000]].

Check left ventricular function on schedule rather than on symptoms, and treat a fall as a
reason to reconsider the whole regimen rather than to pause one drug.

When a patient has progressed through conjugates, kinase inhibitors and antibodies, say so
plainly. Continuing an antibody alongside best supportive care is a reasonable choice made
explicitly. It is a poor substitute for the conversation in [[BC-680]].
```
