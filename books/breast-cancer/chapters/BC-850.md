## BS-7190 Scope, and the residual disease decision that organises it

This chapter covers what is given after surgery to a patient with triple-negative disease. What is
given before surgery is in [[BC-860]]. The argument that frames both is in [[BC-510]], which these
two chapters extend with trial-level detail.

One fact organises everything in the sections that follow. Response to neoadjuvant therapy is the
strongest prognostic variable available in this subtype, with its trial-level limits set out in
[[BS-6360]]. It is generated by treatment rather than measured at baseline. A patient taken to
surgery first cannot be stratified by it. The adjuvant options open to that patient are therefore
narrower, which is a reason to settle the neoadjuvant question at the first consultation rather
than after the operation.

The tables divide by the population a trial enrolled rather than by drug class. A trial that
selected patients by residual disease belongs to a different evidence class from one that selected
by stage after primary surgery. Results do not transfer from one of those populations to the
other. Most of the apparent disagreement in this literature is disagreement about which population
a number came from.

The table below is the map of the whole chapter. Each section that follows takes one decision and
shows only the trials that bear on it, so a trial appears more than once when it bears on more
than one decision.

```evidence setting=early subtype=TNBC,BRCA line=adjuvant,post-neoadjuvant modality=chemo,immunotherapy,parp,adc sort=year cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,status caption=Systemic_therapy_given_after_surgery_in_triple-negative_disease._Read_the_population_column_first.
```
Radiotherapy, bone-modifying agents, ovarian protection and surveillance are out of scope.
Molecular residual disease detection is in [[BC-390]].

## BS-7200 Adjuvant chemotherapy regimens and dose density

The backbone itself is not in dispute anywhere in this literature. Sequential anthracycline and
taxane remains the standard against which everything in this chapter is measured, on comparisons
that are subtype-wide rather than specific to this disease [@ebctcg2012].

Dose density is a scheduling change rather than a dose increase. Total cytotoxic exposure is
broadly the same in both arms of these trials, the difference being the interval between cycles.
The label is routinely misread as more chemotherapy, which drives the wrong conversation about
tolerability.

The evidence for it is meta-analytic rather than from any single trial. Across 26 trials and
37,298 women, ten-year recurrence was 28.0% with dose-intensified schedules against 31.4%, with a
rate ratio of 0.86 and a 95% confidence interval of 0.82 to 0.89. Ten-year breast cancer mortality
was 18.9% against 21.3% [@ebctcg2019]. The proportional reduction was similar across subtypes
rather than concentrated in this one. Absolute gain therefore tracks absolute risk, which is why
this schedule matters most in node-positive triple-negative disease.

```evidence setting=early subtype=TNBC line=adjuvant modality=chemo topic=schedule sort=year caption=The_two_randomised_trials_that_anchor_the_dose-density_meta-analysis.
```
What no trial provides is a randomised comparison of adjuvant chemotherapy against no chemotherapy
in stage I triple-negative disease. Treatment below stage II is extrapolation from prognosis, on
the reasoning set out in [[BS-4460]].

```practice
Give a taxane and an anthracycline sequentially, at two-weekly intervals with growth factor
support, unless something specific argues against it.

Do not substitute single-agent capecitabine for that regimen. It was tested as a substitute and
was inferior, most clearly in hormone receptor negative disease {{trial:calgb49907}} [@muss2019].

Reserve the discussion about omitting the anthracycline for patients being treated before surgery,
where it is an open randomised question [[BS-7310]].
```
## BS-7210 Platinum in the adjuvant and post-neoadjuvant setting

Three different questions hide under one word, and the trials answer them separately.

```evidence setting=early subtype=TNBC line=adjuvant,post-neoadjuvant modality=chemo topic=platinum sort=year caption=Platinum_given_after_surgery,_either_added_to_the_backbone_or_replacing_part_of_it.
```
The first is whether carboplatin added to a standard backbone improves outcome. Two randomised
trials say yes on their primary endpoints in unselected triple-negative populations. The hazard in
{{trial:citrine}} is not proportional, which is the reason to read its piecewise estimates. The
hazard ratio was 0.31 in the first year, 0.65 between one and three years, and 1.98 thereafter
[@liu2025citrine]. A curve that separates early and then converges is consistent with deferred
rather than prevented recurrence. Longer follow-up is the only thing that will settle which of
those it is.

The second is whether platinum can replace the anthracycline. {{trial:pattern}} replaced an
anthracycline regimen with a platinum and taxane doublet, changing both drugs at once
[@yu2020pattern]. A comparison in which the experimental arm differs from the control in two
components cannot attribute its result to either one.

The third is whether platinum is the right drug for residual disease. {{trial:ea1131}} tested
platinum against capecitabine in that population and stopped early on the recommendation of its
monitoring committee [@mayer2021ea1131]. The more useful finding in that trial is not the
comparison it was designed to make. The design assumed a four-year invasive disease-free survival
of 67% with capecitabine. Observed three-year estimates in basal residual disease were far below
that in both arms. Residual disease after neoadjuvant chemotherapy is a worse prognostic state
than the trials that study it have assumed.

```caution
All of these trials were run without checkpoint blockade. In stage II to III disease treated with
the {{trial:keynote522}} regimen, carboplatin has already been given to every patient before
surgery [@schmid2020]. The adjuvant platinum question now applies mainly to patients treated with
surgery first, and to patients whose neoadjuvant regimen contained no platinum. Applying these
results to a patient who has already had four cycles of carboplatin is an extrapolation with no
data behind it.
```
## BS-7220 Capecitabine for residual disease

The population in {{trial:create-x}} was HER2-negative, not triple-negative [@masuda2017]. Its
triple-negative patients were a subgroup of that trial rather than the trial itself. The effect in
that subgroup was larger than the effect in the whole population, which is why the subgroup result
changed practice. It remains a subgroup result from a trial that stopped early at a positive
interim analysis.

Two features of that trial constrain how far the result travels. Its control arm received neither
carboplatin nor pembrolizumab at any point. The residual disease it selected was therefore
selected by a weaker regimen than the one used today. The incremental value of capecitabine after
a platinum-containing chemoimmunotherapy backbone has never been measured.

```evidence setting=early subtype=TNBC line=adjuvant,post-neoadjuvant modality=chemo topic=capecitabine sort=year caption=Capecitabine_as_an_addition,_as_a_substitute,_and_as_the_comparator_for_platinum.
```
The two trials that gave capecitabine to unselected early triple-negative disease disagree, and
the disagreement is instructive. {{trial:ciboma}} found a disease-free survival hazard ratio of
0.82 with a confidence interval of 0.63 to 1.06 [@lluch2020]. {{trial:sysucc001}} found 0.64 with
an interval of 0.42 to 0.95 [@wang2021sysucc]. The drug in the two trials is the same. The dose,
the schedule, the duration and the enrolled population are not. One gave eight conventional cycles
and the other gave a year of continuous low-dose treatment.

Their preplanned subgroup signals also point in opposite directions from one another. The
preplanned analysis in {{trial:ciboma}} suggested benefit confined to non-basal disease, with an
overall survival interaction p value of 0.0052 [@lluch2020]. {{trial:create-x}} found its largest
effect in the triple-negative subgroup [@masuda2017]. Both subgroup claims cannot be describing
the same underlying biology.

```practice
Offer capecitabine for residual invasive disease after neoadjuvant chemotherapy in a patient
without a germline BRCA variant. Six to eight cycles, with dose reduction expected.

Warn about hand-foot syndrome before the first cycle rather than after it. It occurred in 73.4% of
treated patients in {{trial:create-x}} [@masuda2017].

Do not use adjuvant capecitabine outside the residual disease indication on the strength of
{{trial:sysucc001}} alone. The trial that used a conventional schedule in the same setting was
negative.
```
## BS-7230 Adjuvant PARP inhibition for germline carriers

{{trial:olympia}} enrolled germline BRCA1 and BRCA2 carriers with high-risk HER2-negative disease,
so its population includes hormone receptor positive patients [@tutt2021]. Entry to that trial ran
through two different routes. Patients treated with surgery first qualified on pathological stage.
Patients treated before surgery qualified on residual disease. For a triple-negative patient the
second route is usually the relevant one, which makes germline status and response status jointly
determine the post-neoadjuvant plan.

```evidence setting=early subtype=BRCA line=adjuvant modality=parp caption=The_only_randomised_adjuvant_PARP_inhibitor_trial_in_early_breast_cancer.
```
Follow-up is now long enough for the survival result to stand on its own [@garber2026]. That is
unusual in this space and it is the reason olaparib rather than capecitabine is offered to a
carrier.

Three things that trial does not establish should be said out loud.

It never compared olaparib with capecitabine, which is the alternative in this population. The
preference rests on a larger population, a longer follow-up and a placebo control, which are
reasons to prefer the evidence rather than evidence of superiority.

It did not test olaparib alongside pembrolizumab in any arm. A patient with residual disease after
{{trial:keynote522}} who carries a germline variant is now routinely offered both, on the strength
of two trials that each excluded the other drug.

It enrolled on germline status alone, with no somatic or functional assay. Somatic BRCA
alterations, PALB2 and the wider homologous recombination gene set were not eligibility criteria.
The distinction between a germline mutation, a somatic one and a genomic scar is developed in
[[BS-5120]] and [[BC-180]].

```caution
Germline testing has to be resulted before the post-neoadjuvant decision, not after it. The window
between surgery and the start of adjuvant treatment is short. A test sent at the time of the
residual disease result will often return too late to change the first cycle, and the pathway in
[[BC-070]] is built around sending it at diagnosis.
```
## BS-7240 Adjuvant immunotherapy and the question stratified by response

Two randomised trials gave checkpoint blockade only after surgery in this disease. Neither
succeeded, though their failures are not the same kind of failure.

```evidence setting=early subtype=TNBC line=adjuvant,post-neoadjuvant modality=immunotherapy sort=year caption=Checkpoint_blockade_started_or_continued_after_surgery._Neither_reported_trial_met_its_primary_endpoint.
```
{{trial:impassion030}} stopped for futility with a hazard ratio above one [@ignatiadis2025]. That
is the cleanest available statement that the timing matters, with the mechanistic reading in
[[BS-4190]].

{{trial:abrave}} is the harder of the two to interpret. It missed both co-primary disease-free
survival endpoints, while reporting a descriptive overall survival hazard ratio of 0.66 against a
disease-free survival hazard ratio of 0.81 [@conte2025abrave]. A trial whose disease-free survival
curves do not separate convincingly should not show a larger separation in overall survival. The
honest reading is that the overall survival analysis was descriptive, the trial was not powered
for it, and the result is hypothesis-generating. It should also be read as a post-neoadjuvant
trial rather than an adjuvant one, because more than four fifths of its patients entered with
residual disease.

The question in the heading is the unresolved one. {{trial:keynote522}} gave pembrolizumab before
and after surgery, to every patient, including those with a pathological complete response
[@schmid2020]. The trial cannot apportion its effect between the two phases. {{trial:geparnuevo}}
gave no checkpoint blockade after surgery at all and still produced a survival benefit
[@loibl2022geparnuevo; @loibl2026geparnuevo]. That is the strongest indirect argument that the
adjuvant phase may be contributing little.

{{trial:optimice}} tests omission after a pathological complete response, which is the half of the
question with the least at stake. The other half of the question has no trial at all. No
randomised study is asking whether pembrolizumab should be continued in residual disease after a
pembrolizumab-containing neoadjuvant regimen, which is where the recurrence risk actually sits.

```caution
A regimen tested as a whole becomes unfalsifiable in parts. The decision to continue pembrolizumab
after surgery was made once, at trial design, and every patient since has inherited it. Nine
adjuvant cycles carry a real risk of permanent endocrinopathy in people who are already cured
[[BS-4510]]. The evidential status of those cycles and their toxicity are not symmetrical.
```
## BS-7250 Conjugates after residual disease

Residual disease is the enrichment that makes a post-surgical trial of an expensive drug feasible.
The event rate is high enough to power an invasive disease-free survival endpoint in a few
thousand patients. That is the design logic established in HER2-positive disease by
{{trial:katherine}} and developed in [[BS-7010]].

```evidence setting=early subtype=TNBC line=post-neoadjuvant modality=adc caption=Trop-2_directed_conjugates_in_residual_triple-negative_disease._Both_trials_are_accruing.
```
Two features of these designs will determine how the results are read.

The control arm in both trials is a physician's choice of treatment. That makes the comparator
depend on local practice. The gap between pembrolizumab alone and pembrolizumab with capecitabine
is not small. A conjugate that beats the weaker of those two options has not beaten current
practice everywhere.

Transfer from the metastatic setting to this one is not automatic. The populations in which these
drugs established themselves had measurable, pretreated, progressing disease [[BC-580]]. Residual
disease is chemotherapy-selected, micrometastatic and invisible to imaging. The metastatic trials
did not require target expression for entry, so nothing is known about whether Trop-2 abundance in
a residual disease specimen predicts anything.

Toxicity carries a different weight in a population treated with curative intent. Neutropenia and
diarrhoea with sacituzumab govitecan and interstitial lung disease with datopotamab deruxtecan are
manageable risks in advanced disease [[BC-460]]. A treatment-related death in a patient who was
going to be cured is a different event.

```interplay target=BC-350
The conjugates are being brought into this setting because they tolerate heterogeneity, which is
the property developed in [[BS-3170]]. A payload that kills neighbouring cells does not require
every cell to carry the antigen. The complication specific to residual disease is that the
population being treated has already been filtered. Cells surviving a platinum, an
anthracycline, a taxane and a checkpoint inhibitor are not a random sample of the untreated tumour.
Their antigen expression is not the antigen expression measured in a pretreatment core. A conjugate
selected on metastatic biology is being asked to work in a compartment defined by what chemotherapy
could not kill.
```
## BS-7260 Unresolved questions and what reads out next

```evidence setting=early subtype=TNBC,BRCA line=adjuvant,post-neoadjuvant status=ongoing,awaited caption=Post-surgical_trials_in_triple-negative_disease_that_have_not_reported.
```
{{trial:optimice}} asks whether the adjuvant pembrolizumab phase can be dropped after a
pathological complete response. A non-inferiority result would remove nine cycles and their
permanent endocrine risk from the majority of treated patients. An inferiority result would
establish that the adjuvant phase carries effect, which no trial has yet shown.

{{trial:ascent05}} and {{trial:tropion-b03}} ask whether a Trop-2 directed conjugate beats the
current post-neoadjuvant standard [@tolaney2024optimicerd; @bardia2024tropionb03]. Either positive
result would displace capecitabine from the post-neoadjuvant standard. Neither would tell you what
to do for a germline carrier, since olaparib is not the comparator.

Four questions have no trial running and should be named as such.

Whether capecitabine adds anything after a platinum-containing chemoimmunotherapy backbone is
unmeasured, because the regimen it was tested against no longer exists.

Whether olaparib is better than capecitabine in a germline carrier has never been asked directly.

Whether pembrolizumab should be continued in residual disease after a pembrolizumab-containing
neoadjuvant regimen is the highest-risk group's open question. It is currently answered by
convention rather than by evidence.

Whether any systemic treatment helps stage I triple-negative disease remains an extrapolation
[[BS-4460]].

Molecular residual disease detection would in principle identify who needs escalation here.
Whether acting on a positive result changes outcome is a separate question, with the trials that
address it collected in [[BC-390]].
