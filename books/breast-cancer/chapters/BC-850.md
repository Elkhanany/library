## BS-7190 Scope, and the residual disease decision that organises it

This chapter covers what is given after surgery to a patient with triple-negative disease. What is
given before surgery is in [[BC-860]]. The argument that frames both is in [[BC-510]], which these
two chapters extend with trial-level detail. Radiotherapy, bone-modifying agents, ovarian
protection and surveillance are out of scope. Molecular residual disease detection is in
[[BC-390]].

One fact organises everything in the sections that follow. Response to neoadjuvant therapy is the
strongest prognostic variable available in this subtype, with its trial-level limits set out in
[[BS-6360]]. It is generated by treatment rather than measured at baseline. A patient taken to
surgery first cannot be stratified by it. The adjuvant options open to that patient are therefore
narrower, which is a reason to settle the neoadjuvant question at the first consultation rather
than after the operation.

The table below is the map of the whole chapter. Its rows are four different kinds of trial, and
the difference between them is the population column rather than the drug.

```evidence setting=early subtype=TNBC,BRCA line=adjuvant,post-neoadjuvant modality=chemo,immunotherapy,parp,adc sort=year cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,status caption=Systemic_therapy_given_after_surgery_in_triple-negative_disease._Read_the_population_column_first.
```
Ten of those rows did not require this subtype to enter. {{trial:calgb-9344}},
{{trial:nsabp-b-28}}, {{trial:ecog-e1199}}, {{trial:us-oncology-9735}}, {{trial:abc-trials}},
{{trial:wsg-planb}}, {{trial:calgb9741}}, {{trial:gim2}}, {{trial:calgb49907}} and
{{trial:mindact}} enrolled early breast cancer selected by nodal status, by other risk features or
by age. None of them required a
triple-negative result to enter. They include the largest trials in the table and have the least
specific populations. Everything they say about triple-negative disease is a subgroup or an
interaction test. [[BS-7200]] argues the chemotherapy comparisons among them. {{trial:mindact}},
which asked whether a genomic assay could spare chemotherapy, is argued in [[BC-490]].

Five rows selected triple-negative disease without reference to response. {{trial:ciboma}},
{{trial:sysucc001}}, {{trial:pattern}}, {{trial:citrine}} and {{trial:impassion030}} enrolled on
pathological stage or on other risk features. Response to neoadjuvant treatment was an entry
criterion in none of them. Five more required residual invasive disease after neoadjuvant
chemotherapy. {{trial:create-x}}, {{trial:ea1131}}, {{trial:ascent05}}, {{trial:tropion-b03}} and
{{trial:sascia-gbg-102}}, the last of them also admitting hormone receptor-positive disease,
enrolled a population that had already declared itself resistant to the chemotherapy it was given.
{{trial:create-x}} did not require this subtype to enter. It enrolled HER2-negative residual disease
of either hormone receptor status. Its triple-negative result is a subgroup.

Those two groups look alike in the table and are not alike in clinic. A stage-selected population
contains a large fraction of patients who would have been cured by what they had already received.
A residual disease population contains very few. The absolute event rate differs accordingly, so a
hazard ratio measured in one does not carry the same absolute consequence in the other. Most of
the apparent disagreement in this literature is disagreement about which population a number came
from.

The remaining four rows are defined by response in one direction or the other. {{trial:olympia}}
and {{trial:abrave}} admitted patients by residual disease or by stage, in strata they were not
powered to separate. In A-BRAVE, however, the residual disease stratum carried its own co-primary
disease-free survival endpoint. {{trial:pearly}} randomised patients treated in either setting.
{{trial:optimice}} enrolls only patients with a pathological complete response. A stratified trial
is not two trials. What it reports is an average over populations whose prognosis differs.

Residual disease marks resistance to the regimen that was actually given. It says nothing about a
drug the tumour never met. Every trial in the sections below makes that assumption, and none of
them tests it.

Two things the map cannot show matter more than anything in it. There is no column for what the
patient received before surgery, so two rows that both read residual invasive disease may describe
disease that survived two drugs or disease that survived four. And no reported post-surgical trial
here enrolled a patient whose neoadjuvant treatment was the regimen now given. Every benchmark in
this chapter was set before carboplatin and pembrolizumab entered that position.

Geography is worth one sentence. {{trial:citrine}}, {{trial:pattern}} and {{trial:sysucc001}} were
run in China and {{trial:pearly}} in Korea. None of the cytotoxic strategies they support has been
reproduced in a trial run outside those countries.

Most sections below open with the question their trials were built to answer, taken from the
[Chapter Stories](stories.html) map, and close with a table filtered on that same question. The
story says what was being looked for and what the design cannot settle. The table says what else
is in the cell. The prose argues the decision.

## BS-7200 Adjuvant chemotherapy regimens and dose density

The backbone is not in dispute anywhere in this literature. None of the trials that fixed it set
out to study this subtype. Sequential anthracycline and taxane remains the standard against
which everything else in this chapter is measured, on comparisons that are subtype-wide
[@ebctcg2012].

Those comparisons agree on the taxane and disagree on what it buys. {{trial:calgb-9344}} and
{{trial:nsabp-b-28}} both added four cycles of paclitaxel after doxorubicin and cyclophosphamide
in node-positive disease, and both lowered recurrence [@henderson2003; @mamounas2005]. Five-year
overall survival in B-28 was 85 percent in both arms. In CALGB 9344 it was 80% with paclitaxel
against 77% without. The two trials tested the same addition and differed in what their survival
analyses could resolve. That is a difference in events and follow-up rather than a disagreement
about the drug. CALGB 9344 also settled a second question in passing. Escalating the doxorubicin
dose changed nothing, which is the first evidence in this chapter that schedule and composition
matter more than intensity.

Anthracycline omission looks like three trials disagreeing and is three different comparisons.
{{trial:us-oncology-9735}} found docetaxel and cyclophosphamide superior to doxorubicin and
cyclophosphamide on disease-free and overall survival at seven years, against a control arm that
contained no taxane [@jones2009]. {{trial:abc-trials}} put six cycles of the same docetaxel and
cyclophosphamide doublet against anthracycline plus taxane and could not declare non-inferiority.
Its hazard ratio for an invasive disease-free survival event was 1.202, against a predefined
inferiority boundary of 1.18 [@blum2017; @geyer2024]. {{trial:wsg-planb}} compared epirubicin and
cyclophosphamide followed by docetaxel with six cycles of docetaxel and cyclophosphamide, and found
them within a 4.4 point margin [@nitz2017; @nitz2019]. Beating an anthracycline doublet is not the
same claim as matching a regimen containing a taxane and an anthracycline. The control arm is where
these trials differ, not the experimental one.

{{trial:ecog-e1199}} is the only one of them that held composition constant and varied schedule
inside a single randomisation. Weekly paclitaxel improved disease-free and overall survival
against paclitaxel every three weeks. Weekly docetaxel improved neither [@sparano2008]. The
schedule effect belongs to the taxane rather than to taxanes, which is why the regimen used in
this subtype is weekly paclitaxel and not weekly anything.

Dose density is a scheduling change rather than a dose increase. Total cytotoxic exposure is
broadly the same in both arms of these trials, the difference being the interval between cycles.
The label is routinely misread as more chemotherapy, which drives the wrong conversation about
tolerability.

```evidence setting=early subtype=TNBC line=adjuvant modality=chemo topic=dose_density sort=year caption=The_two_randomised_trials_that_anchor_the_dose-density_meta-analysis.
```
The two trials in that table agree in direction and neither enrolled this subtype on purpose. Both
took node-positive disease of any receptor status, and both compared a two-weekly schedule with a
three-weekly one inside a factorial design testing something else as well [@citron2003;
@delmastro2015]. Neither is large enough on its own to carry the decision, which is why the case
for dose density is meta-analytic. The patient-level meta-analysis pooled 26 randomised trials and
37,298 women [@ebctcg2019]. Ten-year recurrence and ten-year breast cancer mortality were both
lower with dose-intensified scheduling. The proportional reduction was similar across subtypes
rather than concentrated in this one. Absolute gain therefore tracks absolute risk, which is why
this schedule matters most in node-positive triple-negative disease.

Three things none of these trials provides should be said plainly. None randomised adjuvant
chemotherapy against no chemotherapy in stage I triple-negative disease, so treatment below stage
II is extrapolation from prognosis on the reasoning in [[BS-4460]]. None stratified by receptor
subtype, so every subtype-specific estimate quoted from them is an interaction test rather than a
randomised allocation. And none contained a platinum or a checkpoint inhibitor, so the backbone
they validated is now the part of the regimen that is least often given alone.

```practice
Give a taxane and an anthracycline sequentially, at two-weekly intervals with growth factor
support, unless something specific argues against it.

Do not substitute single-agent capecitabine for that regimen. It was tested as a substitute for
cyclophosphamide, methotrexate and fluorouracil, or for doxorubicin and cyclophosphamide, in
patients aged 65 and over. Neither regimen contains a taxane. Capecitabine was inferior, most
clearly in hormone receptor-negative disease {{trial:calgb49907}} [@muss2019].

Reserve the discussion about omitting the anthracycline for patients being treated before surgery,
where it is an open randomised question [[BS-7310]].
```
## BS-7210 Platinum in the adjuvant and post-neoadjuvant setting

```story SQ-0420
```

Three different questions hide under one word, and the four trials answer them separately.

```evidence setting=early subtype=TNBC line=adjuvant,post-neoadjuvant modality=chemo topic=platinum sort=year caption=Platinum_given_after_surgery,_either_added_to_the_backbone_or_replacing_part_of_it.
```
The first question is whether carboplatin added to a full backbone improves outcome.
{{trial:citrine}} and {{trial:pearly}} both added it without removing anything, and both met their
primary endpoint. Both also cost a large increase in grade 3 or worse toxicity, which is the price
the table has no column for and the clinic pays first.

They are not replicates of each other, and the difference is design rather than drug.
{{trial:citrine}} randomised after surgery and measured disease-free survival [@liu2025citrine].
{{trial:pearly}} randomised patients treated in either setting and measured event-free survival,
which in its neoadjuvant patients counts events occurring before the operation [@kim2026pearly].
Their survival columns also differ in what they were able to establish, and follow-up does not
explain it, because the trial with the shorter median follow-up is the one whose survival
difference reached significance. The reasons to hold that survival result loosely are specific.
{{trial:citrine}} was conducted at a single centre. Its proportional hazards assumption was
formally rejected [@liu2025citrine]. A single hazard ratio therefore does not describe its curves,
which is a statement about the model rather than about the drug. Whether carboplatin prevented
recurrence or deferred it cannot be told apart at this length of follow-up.

The second question is whether platinum can replace the anthracycline. {{trial:pattern}} replaced
an anthracycline regimen with a platinum and taxane doublet, changing both drugs at once
[@yu2020pattern]. A comparison in which the experimental arm differs from the control in two
components cannot attribute its result to either one. Its subgroups with BRCA1/2 and homologous
recombination repair variants are the most quoted part of it and were not randomised allocations.

The third question is whether platinum is the right drug for residual disease. {{trial:ea1131}}
tested platinum against capecitabine in that population as a non-inferiority design, and its
monitoring committee closed it after roughly three fifths of the planned information had accrued
[@mayer2021ea1131]. A non-inferiority trial closed that early can demonstrate neither
non-inferiority nor inferiority. Two features of it are nevertheless load-bearing. The analysis
population was defined by a PAM50 call made on residual tissue, which is a measurement taken after
the selection event rather than a property of the tumour that was enrolled. And the design assumed
a four-year invasive disease-free survival of 67% with capecitabine, while the observed three-year
estimates in basal residual disease fell far below that in both arms. Residual disease after
neoadjuvant chemotherapy is a worse prognostic state than the trials that study it have assumed.

The decision for a patient going to surgery first therefore rests on {{trial:citrine}}, because it
is the only trial here that randomised the addition of carboplatin entirely after surgery. That is a
single-centre trial with non-proportional hazards, which is a thinner foundation than the strength
of its primary result suggests.

Two comparisons nobody has randomised bound all of this. No trial has added carboplatin after
surgery to a patient who already received carboplatin before it. And no trial in this setting has
allocated platinum prospectively by homologous recombination status, so the mechanistic rationale
for the class has never been tested as an eligibility criterion.

```caution
All of these trials were run without checkpoint blockade. In stage II to III disease treated with
the {{trial:keynote522}} regimen, carboplatin has already been given to every patient before
surgery [@schmid2020]. The adjuvant platinum question now applies mainly to patients treated with
surgery first, and to patients whose neoadjuvant regimen contained no platinum. Applying these
results to a patient who has already had four cycles of carboplatin is an extrapolation with no
data behind it.
```
## BS-7220 Capecitabine for residual disease

```story SQ-0410
```

One finding runs through this table and it is narrower than the way the drug is discussed. Where
capecitabine was added to standard treatment rather than substituted for it, the disease-free
survival estimate favoured the drug in every trial. Where it replaced standard chemotherapy, it
was inferior. Addition and substitution are different questions, and only the first of them has a
positive answer.

```evidence setting=early subtype=TNBC line=adjuvant,post-neoadjuvant modality=chemo topic=capecitabine sort=year caption=Capecitabine_as_an_addition,_as_a_substitute,_and_as_the_comparator_for_platinum.
```
{{trial:create-x}} is the trial the decision rests on, because it is the only randomised comparison
against what the clinic would otherwise do, which was nothing further [@masuda2017]. Three
qualifications travel with it. Its population was HER2-negative rather than triple-negative, so
the result that changed practice is a subgroup of the trial rather than the trial. It stopped early
at an interim analysis that had met its endpoint, which is the circumstance in which an effect is
most likely to be overstated. And its control arm received neither carboplatin nor pembrolizumab
at any point, so the residual disease it selected was selected by a weaker regimen than the one
used today. The incremental value of capecitabine after a platinum-containing chemoimmunotherapy
backbone has never been measured.

The two trials that gave capecitabine to unselected early triple-negative disease reached opposite
conclusions. They differ in dose, in schedule and in duration rather than in drug. {{trial:ciboma}}
gave eight conventional cycles [@lluch2020]. {{trial:sysucc001}} gave a year of continuous low-dose
treatment [@wang2021sysucc]. Their confidence intervals for disease-free survival overlap across most of
their range, so what separates the two conclusions is which side of a threshold each interval fell
on. Neither trial is evidence that metronomic dosing works where conventional dosing does not.
That comparison has never been randomised.

{{trial:calgb49907}} is in this table for the opposite reason. It substituted capecitabine for
standard adjuvant chemotherapy in patients aged 65 and over, and standard chemotherapy won
[@muss2019]. Its receptor-negative result is a subgroup of an age-selected, subtype-blind trial. It
nevertheless does the most useful work in the table, because it fixes the direction of the whole
class. Capecitabine is worth giving after an anthracycline and a taxane when residual invasive
disease is found at surgery. It is not worth giving instead of standard chemotherapy. In CALGB 49907
that meant cyclophosphamide, methotrexate and fluorouracil, or doxorubicin and cyclophosphamide,
neither of which contains a taxane.

{{trial:ea1131}} appears here as the comparator rather than as an experimental arm. Its presence
records the point at which capecitabine stopped being the new idea and became the thing to beat,
which is also the role it plays in the conjugate trials in [[BS-7250]].

The preplanned subgroup signals in this table point in opposite directions, and the reason is
measurement rather than biology. {{trial:ciboma}} reported its advantage in non-basal disease, on a
phenotype scored centrally by immunohistochemistry. That interaction reached significance for
overall survival and not for disease-free survival [@lluch2020]. {{trial:create-x}} reported its
largest effect in the triple-negative subgroup, defined by receptor status [@masuda2017]. Those
two partitions do not divide the same patients along the same line. Neither was a randomised
allocation, and an interaction test on a scoring convention is not a claim about repair biology.

```practice
Offer capecitabine for residual invasive disease after neoadjuvant chemotherapy in a patient
without a germline BRCA variant. Six to eight cycles, with dose reduction expected.

Warn about hand-foot syndrome before the first cycle rather than after it. It occurred in 73.4% of
treated patients in {{trial:create-x}} [@masuda2017].

Do not use adjuvant capecitabine outside the residual disease indication on the strength of
{{trial:sysucc001}} alone. The trial that used a conventional schedule in the same setting was
negative.
```
## BS-7230 Adjuvant PARP inhibition for germline carriers

```story SQ-0430
```

{{trial:olympia}} enrolled germline BRCA1 and BRCA2 carriers with high-risk HER2-negative disease,
so its population includes hormone receptor positive patients [@tutt2021]. Entry ran through two
different routes. Patients treated with surgery first qualified on pathological stage. Patients
treated before surgery qualified on residual disease. For a triple-negative patient the second
route is usually the relevant one, which makes germline status and response status jointly
determine the post-neoadjuvant plan.

```evidence setting=early subtype=BRCA line=adjuvant modality=parp caption=The_only_randomised_adjuvant_PARP_inhibitor_trial_in_early_breast_cancer.
```
Follow-up is now long enough for the survival result to stand on its own [@geyer2022; @garber2026].
That is unusual in this space and it is the reason olaparib rather than capecitabine is offered to
a carrier.

Two observations belong in the consent conversation and neither is in the table. The separation was
in distant disease-free survival as well as in invasive disease-free survival, at 87.5% against
80.4% at three years [@tutt2021]. The effect is therefore not carried by second primaries and
non-breast events. And olaparib was added after chemotherapy rather than in place of it, so a
carrier who declines chemotherapy sits outside this evidence entirely.

Three things that trial does not establish should be said out loud.

It never compared olaparib with capecitabine, which is the alternative in this population. The
preference rests on a larger population, a longer follow-up and a placebo control. Those are
reasons to prefer the evidence rather than evidence of superiority.

It did not test olaparib alongside pembrolizumab in any arm. A patient with residual disease after
{{trial:keynote522}} who carries a germline variant is now routinely offered both, on the strength
of two trials that each excluded the other drug.

It enrolled on germline status alone, with no somatic or functional assay. Somatic BRCA
alterations, PALB2 and the wider homologous recombination gene set were not eligibility criteria.
The distinction between a germline mutation, a somatic one and a genomic scar is developed in
[[BS-5120]] and [[BC-180]].

```caution
Germline testing has to be resulted before the post-neoadjuvant decision, not after it. The window
between surgery and the start of adjuvant treatment is short. A test sent at the time of the
residual disease result will often return too late to change the first cycle, and the pathway in
[[BC-070]] is built around sending it at diagnosis.
```
## BS-7240 Adjuvant immunotherapy and the question stratified by response

```story SQ-0440
```

Two of the five trials in this table have reported and neither met its primary endpoint. Two of
the other three are conjugate trials, and they appear here because pembrolizumab now sits inside
their control arms. That is the shape of the problem. The adjuvant checkpoint phase moved from
hypothesis to fixture without any trial testing it on its own.

```evidence setting=early subtype=TNBC line=adjuvant,post-neoadjuvant modality=immunotherapy sort=year caption=Checkpoint_blockade_started_or_continued_after_surgery._Neither_reported_trial_met_its_primary_endpoint.
```
Checkpoint blockade begun after surgery has not lowered recurrence in any trial that tested it.
That is the class finding, and the failures behind it are not the same kind of failure.
{{trial:impassion030}} enrolled patients treated by surgery first, added atezolizumab to adjuvant
chemotherapy, and was halted for futility with its estimate on the wrong side of no difference
[@ignatiadis2025]. {{trial:abrave}} gave a year of avelumab after all treatment was finished, to a
population that was mostly residual disease, and missed both co-primary endpoints
[@conte2025abrave]. Outside this table, {{trial:b59}} started atezolizumab before surgery, with
carboplatin-containing neoadjuvant chemotherapy, and continued it afterwards. It missed its
primary endpoint too. As a neoadjuvant trial, it does not test blockade begun after surgery.

Those are two antibodies on three backbones in three populations. They are not replicates of one
another. A negative result with one PD-L1 antibody on one chemotherapy schedule does not transfer
to another. What does generalise is the timing. Before surgery the antigen and the infiltrating
lymphocytes are still in the breast. Afterwards they are not, and [[BS-4190]] develops that
mechanism.

{{trial:impassion030}} is the load-bearing trial for exactly that reason. It is the only randomised
test of a checkpoint inhibitor added to adjuvant chemotherapy in patients with no neoadjuvant
exposure at all. Nothing in its design confounds the drug with a pre-surgical phase, which is why
its futility result is the cleanest available statement that the timing matters.

{{trial:abrave}} is the harder of the two to read. Its descriptive overall survival hazard ratio is
more favourable than its disease-free survival hazard ratio, and it is widely quoted as though the
trial were positive [@conte2025abrave]. A trial whose disease-free survival curves do not separate
convincingly should not show a larger separation in overall survival. That analysis sat outside
the testing hierarchy and the trial was not powered for it. It is a hypothesis rather than a
result recovered from a negative trial. The trial should also be read as post-neoadjuvant rather
than adjuvant, because the great majority of its patients entered through the residual disease
stratum.

The question in the heading is the one none of them asked. {{trial:keynote522}} gave pembrolizumab
before and after surgery, to every patient, inside a single randomisation [@schmid2020]. It cannot
apportion its effect between the two phases. {{trial:geparnuevo}} gave no checkpoint blockade after
surgery at all. It missed its pathological complete response endpoint. Its later reports carry an
overall survival difference in favour of durvalumab [@loibl2022geparnuevo; @loibl2026geparnuevo].
That is an indirect argument that the adjuvant phase may be contributing little. It is also a
secondary survival endpoint from a randomised phase II trial, which is the grade of evidence this
section has just declined to accept from {{trial:abrave}}.

{{trial:optimice}} tests omission after a pathological complete response, which is the half of the
question with the least at stake. {{trial:ascent05}} and {{trial:tropion-b03}} will not settle the
other half. {{trial:ascent05}} gives pembrolizumab in its experimental arm and in its control arm.
{{trial:tropion-b03}} pairs its conjugate with durvalumab and leaves pembrolizumab inside the
choice from which its control arm is drawn. Both ask what to add to the post-surgical phase rather
than whether a checkpoint inhibitor belongs in it. No randomised study is asking whether
pembrolizumab should be continued in residual disease after a pembrolizumab-containing neoadjuvant
regimen, which is where the recurrence risk actually sits.

```caution
A regimen tested as a whole becomes unfalsifiable in parts. The decision to continue pembrolizumab
after surgery was made once, at trial design, and every patient since has inherited it. The
adjuvant cycles carry a real risk of permanent endocrinopathy in people who are already cured
[[BS-4510]]. No trial in this table carries that toxicity as an endpoint, so the evidential status
of those cycles and their cost are not weighed against each other anywhere.
```
## BS-7250 Conjugates after residual disease

```story SQ-0450
```

Residual disease is the enrichment that makes a post-surgical trial of an expensive drug feasible.
The event rate is high enough to power an invasive disease-free survival endpoint in a trial of
feasible size. That is the design logic established in HER2-positive disease by
{{trial:katherine}} and developed in [[BS-7010]].

```evidence setting=early subtype=TNBC line=post-neoadjuvant modality=adc caption=Trop-2_directed_conjugates_in_residual_triple-negative_disease._All_three_are_accruing.
```
None of these three has reported, so what is on the record is design and rationale. They agree on
most of it. All three randomise residual invasive disease after neoadjuvant chemotherapy. All
three test a conjugate directed at Trop-2 and carrying a topoisomerase-I payload. All three set a
choice of treatment as the comparator rather than a fixed regimen.

Where they differ is the choice they offer the control arm, and that is the difference that will
decide how each result reads. {{trial:ascent05}} offers pembrolizumab with or without capecitabine
[@tolaney2024optimicerd]. {{trial:tropion-b03}} offers capecitabine, pembrolizumab or both
[@bardia2024tropionb03]. {{trial:sascia-gbg-102}} spans hormone receptor positive and
triple-negative cohorts, which have to be read separately, because the two differ in biology and
in what a physician's choice is likely to contain. The gap between pembrolizumab alone and
pembrolizumab with capecitabine is not small. A conjugate that beats the weaker of those options
has not beaten current practice everywhere, and the comparator will drift as practice drifts.

{{trial:tropion-b03}} is the one to watch for a reason unconnected to its drug. It randomises the
conjugate alone as well as the conjugate with durvalumab, so it is the only trial of the three
that can separate the conjugate from the antibody it is given with
[@bardia2024tropionb03]. Without that arm a positive result belongs to a combination rather than
to a class.

{{trial:neostar}} sits outside this table and is the reason the class was brought here at all. A
single-arm neoadjuvant study showing that a conjugate alone produces pathological responses in
untreated disease is a proof of concept, not a comparison. Its patients had received nothing, and
their subsequent systemic treatment was left to the treating physician, so its later outcomes
describe a pathway rather than a drug. A design paper and a single-arm response rate are the two
things in this field most often cited as though they were efficacy results.

Transfer from the metastatic setting is not automatic either. The populations in which these drugs
established themselves had measurable, pretreated, progressing disease [[BC-580]]. Residual disease
is chemotherapy-selected, micrometastatic and invisible to imaging. None of these three trials
requires Trop-2 expression for entry, and the metastatic trials did not require it either, so
nothing is known about whether antigen abundance in a residual disease specimen predicts anything.

Toxicity carries a different weight in a population treated with curative intent. Neutropenia and
diarrhoea with sacituzumab govitecan and interstitial lung disease with datopotamab deruxtecan are
manageable risks in advanced disease [[BC-460]]. A treatment-related death in a patient who was
going to be cured is a different event.

```interplay target=BC-350
The conjugates are being brought into this setting because they tolerate heterogeneity, which is
the property developed in [[BS-3170]]. A payload that kills neighbouring cells does not require
every cell to carry the antigen. The complication specific to residual disease is that the
population being treated has already been filtered. Cells surviving a platinum, an
anthracycline, a taxane and a checkpoint inhibitor are not a random sample of the untreated tumour.
Their antigen expression is not the antigen expression measured in a pretreatment core. A conjugate
selected on metastatic biology is being asked to work in a compartment defined by what chemotherapy
could not kill.
```
## BS-7260 Unresolved questions and what reads out next

```evidence setting=early subtype=TNBC,BRCA line=adjuvant,post-neoadjuvant status=ongoing,awaited caption=Post-surgical_trials_in_triple-negative_disease_that_have_not_reported.
```
{{trial:optimice}} asks whether the adjuvant pembrolizumab phase can be dropped after a
pathological complete response. A result showing no loss from omission would take
the adjuvant phase and its permanent endocrine risk away from the majority of treated patients. An inferiority result would
establish that the adjuvant phase carries effect, which no trial has yet shown.

{{trial:ascent05}} and {{trial:tropion-b03}} ask whether a Trop-2 directed conjugate beats the
current post-neoadjuvant standard [@tolaney2024optimicerd; @bardia2024tropionb03].
{{trial:sascia-gbg-102}} asks the same question across both HER2-negative subtypes, which makes it
the only one of the three whose triple-negative result will arrive alongside a hormone receptor
positive comparison in the same protocol. A positive result in any of them would displace
capecitabine from the post-neoadjuvant standard. None would tell you what to do for a germline
carrier, because olaparib is not the comparator in any arm.

Four questions have no trial running and should be named as such.

Whether capecitabine adds anything after a platinum-containing chemoimmunotherapy backbone is
unmeasured, because the regimen it was tested against no longer exists.

Whether olaparib is better than capecitabine in a germline carrier has never been asked directly.

Whether pembrolizumab should be continued in residual disease after a pembrolizumab-containing
neoadjuvant regimen is the highest-risk group's open question. It is currently answered by
convention rather than by evidence.

Whether any systemic treatment helps stage I triple-negative disease remains an extrapolation
[[BS-4460]].

Molecular residual disease detection would in principle identify who needs escalation here.
Whether acting on a positive result changes outcome is a separate question, with the trials that
address it collected in [[BC-390]].
