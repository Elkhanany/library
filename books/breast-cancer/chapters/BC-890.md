## BS-7500 Scope, and the point at which endocrine therapy is exhausted

This chapter covers what happens after endocrine-based therapy stops working in metastatic hormone
receptor-positive, HER2-negative disease. It covers the three antibody-drug conjugates with
randomised evidence in this population, the sequencing problem they create, and the place single
agent chemotherapy still holds.

Endocrine therapy itself is in [[BC-870]] and CDK4/6 inhibition in [[BC-880]]. Conjugate design,
payload chemistry and resistance mechanism are in [[BC-460]] and are cited here rather than
restated. Chemotherapy agent detail is in [[BC-440]]. The visceral crisis decision is in
[[BS-4890]].

The phrase endocrine exhausted is a clinical judgment and not a test result. It is worth being
explicit about what it usually means in practice. A patient has progressed on an aromatase
inhibitor with a CDK4/6 inhibitor, then on at least one further endocrine-based line chosen by
biomarker, and no remaining endocrine option matches a detectable alteration. Or the disease is
moving fast enough that waiting to find out is not safe.

Both halves of that definition are soft. The first depends on which alterations were tested for and
when. The second depends on a judgment about tempo that no scan reports.

Four of those rows are the same experiment. A conjugate was randomised against the physician's
choice of single-agent chemotherapy in disease out of endocrine options, and each lengthened the
time to progression. That is the only claim the table supports as a class. Everything else in it
varies. None of the variation is between one conjugate and another.

The first difference is where the patient was standing when she entered. Eligibility is written in
units of prior chemotherapy rather than in units of biology. {{trial:tropics02}} required two to
four previous chemotherapy regimens for metastatic disease. {{trial:tropion-b01}} and
{{trial:destiny-b04}} required one or two. {{trial:destiny-b06}} required none, and excluded anyone
who had had chemotherapy for metastatic disease at all. Read down the population column and the
four trials line up as a ladder. That ordering is an artefact of protocol writing rather than a
tested sequence, and stacking the medians would compare populations rather than drugs. A hazard
ratio from the shallowest of them describes a decision the deepest never tested.

The second difference is one of measurement. Two of the four required an assay result for the
antibody's own target, and two required nothing of the kind. Trop-2 expression was never an entry
criterion, so those trials enrolled whoever qualified on treatment history alone. The HER2-low
trials enrolled on a staining category, which is a threshold on a continuous stain rather than a
biological boundary [[BS-2360]]. A patient's eligibility moves with the assay in one pair of trials
and not in the other.

The third difference is the endpoint. Only one of the four named overall survival a primary
endpoint. That is the trial that failed it. Two of the others reported survival separately and one
has not, because follow-up was short when the primary analysis was done. A blank survival cell and
a tested negative survival result are different objects. The column cannot tell them apart.

The three remaining rows answer other questions and should not be read as further conjugate
comparisons. {{trial:saci-io-hr}} randomised the addition of a checkpoint inhibitor to a conjugate
rather than a conjugate against chemotherapy, and belongs to [[BS-7560]]. {{trial:daisy}} and
{{trial:icarus-breast01}} are single-arm. One maps activity across the HER2 staining range, and the
other carries the same payload class to a third antigen.

Two comparisons are missing. Neither can be supplied by longer follow-up. No row sets one
conjugate against another. And no trial here randomised the decision that precedes everything
in the table, which is whether to leave endocrine-based therapy at all. Every patient in every
row had already been judged ready for a cytotoxic by the clinician who enrolled her.

The order within the class is therefore set by eligibility and habit rather than by evidence, and
[[BS-7540]] is where that problem is worked through. Two trials that set a CDK4/6 inhibitor against
capecitabine are sometimes quoted at this point and do not belong to it. Both gave the inhibitor to
patients who had never had one, which is not the patient this chapter is about. They are argued in
[[BS-7550]].

## BS-7510 Sacituzumab govitecan in hormone receptor-positive disease

{{trial:tropics02}} is where this drug's position rests. The reason is the depth of what it
randomised. Every patient had received endocrine therapy, a taxane and a CDK4/6 inhibitor, together
with two to four prior chemotherapy regimens for metastatic disease [@rugo2022tropics02]. The
control arm was the menu of single agents such a patient would otherwise have been given.

The result to hold is that survival moved and not only the time to progression
[@rugo2023tropics02]. Overall survival gains are rare this far into the disease, which is why the
trial changed practice despite absolute medians measured in months. Set a patient's expectations
from those medians rather than from the hazard ratio.

Trop-2 expression did not select the responders. The survival benefit was consistent across
expression subgroups measured by immunohistochemistry [@rugo2023tropics02]. That sits awkwardly
beside the biomarker analysis of the same drug in triple-negative disease, where progression-free
survival tracked a Trop-2 expression gradient [@bardia2021ascentbio]. The two findings are usually
reported as if they conflict.

They may not. The triple-negative analysis graded progression-free survival across three expression
bands in a subset of assessable patients, and it was exploratory [@bardia2021ascentbio]. The
hormone receptor-positive analysis reported overall survival by subgroup [@rugo2023tropics02].
Different endpoint, different population, different analysis plan. The safest reading is that
Trop-2 staining has never been shown to identify a group that should not receive the drug, which is
the only question a clinician needs answered.

The toxicity is predictable and is managed rather than avoided. Neutropenia and diarrhoea dominate,
and both are payload effects rather than target effects [[BS-4100]]. UGT1A1 genotype affects
SN-38 clearance and is worth knowing before the first dose in a patient with a suggestive history.

What the trial cannot support is the use most clinics now want. Its population had exhausted
several cytotoxic lines, so nothing in it establishes the drug ahead of the first of them. That
question is being asked prospectively and is taken up in [[BS-7560]].

## BS-7520 Trastuzumab deruxtecan in HER2-low and HER2-ultralow disease

Two randomised trials moved this drug progressively earlier in the same population, and the second
also moved the eligibility boundary. {{trial:destiny-b04}} enrolled patients with HER2-low disease
after one or two prior chemotherapy lines, and the large majority had hormone receptor-positive
disease [@modi2022]. {{trial:destiny-b06}} enrolled after endocrine-based therapy and before any
chemotherapy for metastatic disease, and it added a HER2-ultralow cohort [@bardia2024destinyb06].

They agree on the thing that matters. Against the physician's choice of chemotherapy, at two
different depths of prior treatment, the conjugate delayed progression. Where they part is on
survival. That is a difference of maturity rather than of effect. The earlier trial has
reported survival with long follow-up [@modi2025]. The later one had too few events at its primary
analysis to say anything, which is what a trial reads out when it is moved forward into a
population that lives longer.

The eligibility boundary is an assay boundary rather than a biological one. That argument is made
in full in [[BS-2360]] and [[BS-5110]]. Its practical form here is uncomfortable. HER2-ultralow
means immunohistochemistry 0 with faint incomplete membrane staining in some cells, which is a
category defined at the least reproducible end of the assay. Pathologist concordance is poorest
exactly there, as [[BS-6710]] sets out. A patient's eligibility can therefore depend on who read
the slide.

{{trial:daisy}} argues that the boundary is arbitrary from the other direction. It gave the same
conjugate across three staining cohorts and confirmed responses occurred well above zero in
immunohistochemistry 0 disease [@mosele2023]. Within that cohort, response did not track the
underlying ERBB2 transcript level either. Activity does not stop where the scoring category does.
The study is single-arm, so it cannot say whether those patients would have done as well on
chemotherapy, and the randomised ultralow evidence remains an exploratory cohort of one trial.

```caution
{{trial:destiny-b06}} is often read as showing that a conjugate should replace endocrine therapy
once resistance appears. It shows something narrower. Its comparator was chemotherapy, and every
patient enrolled had already been judged ready for chemotherapy. The trial therefore establishes
that if the next line is cytotoxic, the conjugate is the better cytotoxic choice. It does not
establish when to leave endocrine-based therapy, which is the decision that actually precedes it
[[BC-870]].
```

Interstitial lung disease is the toxicity that should shape the decision, because it is the one
that can be fatal. Adjudicated interstitial lung disease or pneumonitis occurred in 11.3% of
patients given the conjugate in {{trial:destiny-b06}} against 0.2% of those given chemotherapy
[@bardia2024destinyb06]. In a patient with pre-existing lung disease, a low baseline oxygen
saturation or prior thoracic radiotherapy, that asymmetry is a reason to think carefully about
ordering. Surveillance and management are in [[BS-4100]], and the difference between a grade 1 and
a grade 5 event is mostly how early it was found.

## BS-7530 Datopotamab deruxtecan and the TROP2 question

{{trial:tropion-b01}} carried two primary endpoints, progression-free survival and overall
survival. It met the first and missed the second. The final survival analysis gave a hazard ratio
for death of 1.01, with a confidence interval from 0.83 to 1.22 [@pistilli2026].

That combination is worth separating from the more common pattern of an unpowered secondary
endpoint failing to confirm a progression-free survival result. Here survival was a stated primary
endpoint that the trial was designed and powered to test. The trial answered its own question and
the answer was no.

Three explanations have been offered and none has been excluded. Subsequent conjugate use was
imbalanced, at 12.3% in the datopotamab arm against 24.0% in the chemotherapy arm, which would
favour the control arm after progression [@pistilli2026]. Progression-free survival was assessed by
blinded central review in an open-label trial, where the timing of scans and the decision to stop
treatment are not blinded. Or the drug may delay progression in this setting without extending
life.

The target itself does not resolve the question. Trop-2 is an address rather than a driver, chosen
because it is differentially expressed on epithelium and not because tumours depend on it
[[BS-4030]]. Normal tissue expression is what limits the dose, which is why the dominant toxicities
are mucosal and ocular rather than marrow-based. Two conjugates against the same antigen carrying
the same payload class still differ in dominant toxicity. Linker chemistry and normal tissue
handling separate them rather than the target [[BS-4100]].

The practical consequence is a ranking argument rather than a prohibition. Where more than one
conjugate is available and the disease is hormone receptor-positive, an agent that met a survival
endpoint has a stronger claim than one that missed it. That inference crosses two trials with
different entry criteria and different comparator menus. It should be labelled as such. Nobody
has randomised the two drugs against each other, and no trial has selected patients for either one
by Trop-2 staining.

## BS-7540 Sequencing conjugates and the cross-resistance problem

```story SQ-0660
```

Three conjugates are now used in this population. They engage two antigens and they all carry a
topoisomerase I inhibitor payload. That is the whole problem in one sentence.

A fourth antibody makes the point sharper rather than softer. {{trial:icarus-breast01}} took a
HER3-directed conjugate into patients who had progressed on a CDK4/6 inhibitor and one line of
chemotherapy, and met its response endpoint [@pistilli2025]. Its target is a third antigen. Its
payload is the same deruxtecan class as two of the three agents already in use. Adding antigens
does not add payload classes. Payload class is what the preclinical work in [[BS-4090]] shows
carrying cross-resistance.

That response rate has nothing to be read against. The study is single-arm, so it cannot rank HER3
delivery against another conjugate or against the chemotherapy those patients would otherwise have
had. Its interval is reported at 90% rather than 95%, which is what a single-arm design of this
kind specifies and not a sign of greater precision. Comparing it with the intervals in the
randomised rows above is comparing two different constructions.

Its correlative work is the more interesting part. It also carries a selection problem worth
naming. Baseline associations ran to where the receptor sat in the tissue and to the absence of an
ESR1 mutation, while receptor level itself tracked only the progression endpoint [@pistilli2025].
The on-treatment associations ran to how far the conjugate distributed inside the tumour and to an
interferon response. Those came from biopsies taken only in patients who stayed on the drug long
enough to be rebiopsied. That is selection on the very outcome the biomarkers are being asked to
explain. The authors state that all of it awaits validation.

The mechanistic case for expecting cross-resistance, and the preclinical work separating target
loss from payload resistance and from transporter change, is set out in [[BS-4080]] and
[[BS-4090]]. Three findings from there govern this section and are not repeated. Resistance can
arise at any of six steps. Only the first of those steps is measured by any assay in clinical use.
And payload class, rather than the conjugate as a unit, is what preclinical models show carrying
the cross-resistance.

The clinical evidence for a second conjugate is a retrospective series of 85 patients who received
two in sequence, described in [[BS-4080]] [@mai2025]. It is consistent with partial rather than
complete cross-resistance. It is also 85 patients treated off protocol, which is the best evidence
available for a decision made many times a week. {{trial:trade-dxd}} is the first prospective test
and it has not reported. Its cohorts are defined by which conjugate came first and are
non-comparative by design, so it will estimate how much activity survives a conjugate rather than
rank one order against another.

What no trial in this chapter did is randomise the order of the same drugs. The design that
answers a sequencing question randomises the position and then measures progression on the line
after next, which is how {{trial:sonia}} settled the first-line CDK4/6 argument [@sonke2024]. No
conjugate trial and no chemotherapy trial in this disease has been built that way. Until one is,
moving a drug earlier is demonstrated for the first segment of the sequence and assumed for the
rest.

What follows for practice is a rationale and not a result, and the difference should be stated to
patients. HER2-low or ultralow status determines whether trastuzumab deruxtecan is an option at
all, so it is worth establishing before the conjugate decision rather than during it. Trials
enrolled these agents at different depths of prior treatment, so the trial populations imply an
ordering even though no trial compared orders. And a patient who progressed quickly on the first
conjugate has, on the retrospective data, a poor prospect from the second.

```interplay target=BC-350
This is the clearest clinical instance of the ceiling argued in [[BS-6710]]. The decision is made on
an antigen measurement taken from one block at one time, and that measurement answers only whether
the antibody can bind. It says nothing about internalisation, lysosomal transport, efflux or repair
capacity. A proportion of what is recorded here as conjugate failure is therefore allocation
failure, and no improvement in the payload addresses it. Raising the ceiling requires a different
kind of measurement rather than a better drug.
```

## BS-7550 Single-agent chemotherapy and when it remains the right choice

Conjugates have displaced single-agent chemotherapy from several positions without abolishing it.

It remains the answer in four situations. The first is after conjugate exposure, where the
remaining options are cytotoxic and the expectations are covered in [[BS-4910]]. The second is
where a conjugate is contraindicated, and a history of interstitial lung disease is the common
example, given the pneumonitis rate in {{trial:destiny-b06}} [@bardia2024destinyb06]. The third is
where access to a conjugate is not available, which is a real constraint in most of the world
[[BC-710]]. The fourth is rapid progression requiring a response now, covered in [[BS-4890]].

What belongs here beside those four is the evidence about choosing among the agents themselves.

```evidence setting=metastatic subtype=HR+/HER2- modality=chemo status=reported cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,result,os caption=Randomised_chemotherapy_comparisons_reaching_this_population.
```

That table holds four questions rather than one. Reading it as a ranking of cytotoxics misreads
all four. Two rows ask whether one named single agent beats another. One asks whether a single
agent beats the menu a physician would otherwise have chosen from. Three ask whether endocrine
therapy with a CDK4/6 inhibitor can be given instead of chemotherapy at all. One adds a PARP
inhibitor to a chemotherapy backbone and belongs to the table below as much as to this one.

On the first question the answer is consistently negative. That is the finding to carry.
{{trial:study301}} compared eribulin with capecitabine directly and separated them on neither
co-primary endpoint [@kaufman2015]. {{trial:calgb-40502-ncctg-n063h}} set nab-paclitaxel and
ixabepilone against weekly paclitaxel in the first line, and neither experimental arm beat it
[@rugo2015]. The ixabepilone arm was clearly the worse of the two. Within the menu of single
agents there is no demonstrated best drug. Choice is therefore driven by prior exposure,
residual neuropathy, marrow reserve, renal and hepatic function, route and schedule. Those are
patient variables, not trial variables. Capecitabine, eribulin, vinorelbine, gemcitabine and a
weekly taxane are the working set, and agent detail is in [[BC-440]].

{{trial:embrace}} looks like an exception and is not one. It showed that a single cytotoxic agent
can extend survival after several previous chemotherapy regimens [@cortes2011]. That was not the
expectation at the time. It remains the reason eribulin holds its position. The
difference from {{trial:study301}} is design rather than drug. EMBRACE set eribulin against a
composite of whatever the physician would otherwise have chosen, with overall survival as the sole
primary endpoint, in a deeply pretreated population. Study 301 set it against one named agent,
earlier in the disease, with two co-primary endpoints to satisfy. Beating an average of agents is
not the same test as beating a particular one.

The substitution question divides on design too. {{trial:young-pearl-kcsg-br15-10}} and
{{trial:pearl}} both compared palbociclib with endocrine therapy against capecitabine, and they
disagree on progression-free survival [@park2019a; @martin2020]. The populations are not the same.
One enrolled premenopausal women progressing on tamoxifen and added ovarian suppression, at phase
2 with a one-sided test. The other enrolled postmenopausal women resistant to an aromatase
inhibitor, at phase 3, with co-primary endpoints in a cohort and in an ESR1 wild-type subset. They
agree on the endpoint that does not depend on a scan schedule. Neither showed an overall
survival difference against capecitabine [@ahn2025; @martn2022]. Substituting endocrine therapy
with a CDK4/6 inhibitor for a cytotoxic changes how the time is spent rather than how much of it
there is. That is the honest way to offer it. Both trials also enrolled patients who had never
received a CDK4/6 inhibitor, which no longer describes anyone reaching a second line.

{{trial:right-choice}} asks the same substitution question in aggressive disease, against
combination rather than single-agent chemotherapy [@lu2024]. It is phase 2, its survival data are
not yet reported, and its limits are discussed in [[BS-4890]]. It supports an older principle
rather than replacing it. Combination chemotherapy raises response rate and toxicity together,
while sequential single agents preserve function and allow more lines.

One group of patients should not reach that table at all. In germline carriers the physician's
choice of chemotherapy was the comparator in two registration trials rather than the standard.

```evidence setting=metastatic subtype=BRCA,HR+/HER2- modality=parp weight=any status=reported sort=year cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,result,os caption=PARP_inhibitor_trials_whose_populations_reach_this_chapter._All_enrolled_HER2-negative_disease,_so_hormone_receptor-positive_patients_sit_inside_every_row._Read_the_population_column_first._The_entry_criteria_differ_more_between_these_trials_than_the_drugs_do.
```

The decision rests on {{trial:olympiad}} and {{trial:embraca}}, because they randomised a PARP
inhibitor against the thing the clinic would otherwise have done [@robson2017; @litton2018]. They
agree twice over. Each lengthened progression-free survival against the physician's choice of
chemotherapy, and neither improved overall survival at its final analysis [@robson2019;
@litton2020]. A germline result therefore changes which class the next line comes from. It also
changes how the line is tolerated. Neither trial showed that it changes how long the patient lives.
What those two trials did and did not establish is read in full in [[BS-7740]].

The other four rows differ from those two by design rather than by drug. The population column is
where the difference sits. {{trial:brocade3}} gave a PARP inhibitor with a platinum doublet rather
than in place of chemotherapy. Its question is therefore addition and not substitution, and its
final analysis found no significant overall survival difference [@diras2020; @dieras2024].
{{trial:tbcrc048}} and {{trial:talazoparib-beyond-brca}} moved eligibility off germline BRCA1 and
BRCA2, both single-arm [@tung2020; @gruber2022]. One took germline mutations in other homologous
recombination genes in one cohort and somatic mutations, including somatic BRCA1 and BRCA2, in a
second. The other took germline BRCA wild-type tumours carrying a mutation elsewhere in that
pathway. {{trial:mediola}} added PD-L1 blockade to olaparib in a single arm, so nothing in it
separates the contribution of the second drug from the first [@domchek2020].

Those two beyond-BRCA cohorts nonetheless agree on something the randomised trials never tested.
Widening eligibility beyond germline BRCA1 and BRCA2 produces responses with some genes and none
with others, with germline PALB2 the clearest of them [@tung2020; @gruber2022]. A panel report
naming any homologous recombination gene is not a PARP inhibitor indication. That agreement rests
on two small uncontrolled cohorts. The randomised trial that would settle it does not exist.

Neither table holds the comparison a carrier in this chapter actually faces. No trial randomises a
PARP inhibitor against a conjugate, in either order, and none of the conjugate trials stratified on
germline status. The sequence is chosen on class reasoning and on tolerability.

```practice
Say what the line is for before starting it. Control of a symptom, protection of a threatened
organ, and extension of time are different aims with different evidence behind them.

Choose the agent by what the patient can tolerate rather than by expected efficacy, because the
comparative trials show no efficacy difference within the class.

Test for a germline BRCA1 or BRCA2 mutation before choosing the cytotoxic rather than after, since
the result moves the patient into a different table.

Re-check performance status before each new line rather than at progression. A patient who has had
three conjugates and two cytotoxics is a different physiological proposition from the trial
populations above.

Ask about access explicitly. Conjugate availability is the binding constraint in most of the world,
and single-agent chemotherapy is the realistic comparator there rather than the fallback
[[BC-710]].

Name the alternative of no further systemic therapy as an option, not as a failure. It is the right
answer more often than the number of remaining licensed agents suggests [[BS-4910]].
```

## BS-7560 Unresolved questions and what reads out next

```story SQ-0670
```

The checkpoint question has now been asked properly once and answered negatively.
{{trial:saci-io-hr}} randomised pembrolizumab with a Trop-2 conjugate against the conjugate alone,
and progression-free survival did not improve in the population unselected for PD-L1
[@garridocastro2026]. The PD-L1-positive analysis pointed the favourable way without reaching
significance. It is a key secondary endpoint, restricted to the patients with evaluable tissue who
scored positive on a combined positive score of at least 1. Its own authors present it as a trend
for larger trials to test.

Its population is the part to read carefully. Entry allowed no chemotherapy at all for metastatic
disease, and close to half the patients came in on that basis [@garridocastro2026]. The trial
therefore sits earlier in the disease than the line at which this conjugate is registered in this
subtype. One line label is covering two different clinical situations.

Nothing else in the subtype weighs against that result. {{trial:mediola}} combined a checkpoint
inhibitor with olaparib in a single arm. Its authors say plainly that the design cannot show what
the checkpoint inhibitor added [@domchek2020]. {{trial:nimbus}} produced responses only after
selecting patients on tumour mutational burden. Its threshold subgroup is too small to fix where
the cut-off should fall. Selection matters and nobody has measured where the line is.

{{trial:saci-io-hr}} also returned a useful null on measurement. Neither Trop-2 by
immunohistochemistry, immunofluorescence or plasma epigenome-based assay, nor
tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes, was
associated with outcome, while a higher circulating tumour DNA fraction and PIK3CA mutations
tracked worse progression-free survival [@garridocastro2026]. Three ways of measuring the target
all failed to select anyone, which is the same finding [[BS-7510]] reports from a different trial.

```evidence setting=metastatic subtype=HR+/HER2-,HER2-low modality=adc status=ongoing,awaited cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,endpoint,status caption=Conjugate_trials_in_this_population_that_have_not_reported.
```

{{trial:ascent07}} tests sacituzumab govitecan against chemotherapy in endocrine-resistant disease
with no prior chemotherapy for metastatic disease. That is the slot {{trial:destiny-b06}} now
occupies, so a positive result would create a genuine choice between two conjugates at one decision
point, with no trial comparing them.

{{trial:mk2870-010}} does two things at once. It brings a third Trop-2 conjugate into that same
slot, carrying a topoisomerase I inhibitor payload again, so it extends this chapter's problem
rather than solving it. It also carries a checkpoint-inhibitor arm, which makes it the phase 3 test
of the addition that {{trial:saci-io-hr}} could not exclude at phase 2.

{{trial:trade-dxd}} is the one to watch for sequencing, for the reasons set out in [[BS-7540]].

Three gaps will remain after all three report.

No trial randomises the choice between leaving endocrine-based therapy and continuing it, which is
the decision that precedes everything in this chapter.

No assay in clinical use predicts which patient retains sensitivity after a topoisomerase I
payload, and none of the running trials will produce one.

And no conjugate in this population carries a payload from a different class. Until one does,
sequencing decisions will continue to rest on target expression, which answers one question out of
six [[BS-4090]].
