## BS-4200 Atypical ductal and lobular hyperplasia

Atypical hyperplasia is a diagnosis made at a threshold. Atypical ductal hyperplasia carries the
cytology and architecture of low-grade ductal carcinoma in situ below a limit of size or extent
that pathologists apply by convention. Move the limit and the same slide changes category. The
biology beneath the boundary is developed in [[BC-050]] and the morphology in [[BC-090]].

Two facts govern management and they point in different directions.

The first is that the risk conferred is bilateral. A lesion found in one breast raises risk in
both. That is what a risk marker means and it is not what a precursor means.

The second is that the risk is large enough to act on. In {{trial:p1}}, tamoxifen reduced
invasive breast cancer by 49% across 13,388 women at increased risk. In the subgroup with
atypical hyperplasia the reduction was 86% [@fisher1998]. That was the largest subgroup effect
reported in the trial. After seven years of follow-up the overall effect held, with the cumulative
rate of invasive breast cancer falling from 42.5 to 24.8 per 1,000 women [@fisher2005]. Tamoxifen
also reduced clinically detected benign breast disease and the biopsies it prompts, by 29%, mostly in
women younger than 50 [@tanchiu2003].

Management follows. Excision confirms the diagnosis and excludes an adjacent carcinoma that the
core missed. Risk reduction is then a systemic decision rather than a local one, and the options
are set out in [@hartmann2015] and in [[BC-080]].

```caution
The word atypical reads as a description of the cells. It is closer to a description of where the
reporting pathologist stopped. The same proliferation called atypical ductal hyperplasia on a
core can be called low-grade ductal carcinoma in situ on the excision, because the excision shows
more of it. Nothing about the lesion changed between the two reports.
```

## BS-4210 Lobular carcinoma in situ as risk marker and as precursor

Lobular carcinoma in situ is taught as a pure risk marker. The longitudinal data say it is both a
marker and, in part, a precursor.

In a single-institution cohort of 1,060 women with lobular carcinoma in situ and no concurrent
cancer, 150 women developed 168 breast cancers at a median follow-up of 81 months [@king2015].
The annual incidence was about 2%. The distribution of those cancers is the informative part.
Sixty-three per cent were ipsilateral, 25% contralateral and 12% bilateral. A pure risk marker
predicts an even split between the two breasts. A two-to-one ipsilateral excess does not.

The histology of the subsequent cancers was mixed, with ductal carcinoma in situ in 35%, invasive
ductal carcinoma in 29% and invasive lobular carcinoma in 27% [@king2015]. The lesion therefore
does not commit the later tumour to a lobular phenotype.

Two further findings change practice. Chemoprevention was associated with a 10-year cumulative
breast cancer risk of 7% against 21% without it, with a hazard ratio of 0.27 and a 95% confidence
interval of 0.15 to 0.50. And neither age nor family history was associated with risk in
multivariable analysis [@king2015]. The clinical variables normally used to stratify risk do not
stratify this population.

Pleomorphic and florid variants are managed more like in situ carcinoma by convention. That
convention rests on morphological resemblance to high-grade disease rather than on outcome data.

## BS-4220 Ductal carcinoma in situ, risk stratification and genomic assays

Stratification in ductal carcinoma in situ has to answer a narrow question. It is not whether the
patient will die of breast cancer, because almost none do. It is whether an ipsilateral event will
occur, and whether that event will be invasive.

The clinicopathological variables are grade, lesion size, margin width and age at diagnosis. They
separate risk groups and they separate them coarsely.

The first index combined three of them. The Van Nuys Prognostic Index scored tumour size, margin
width and pathological classification from 3 to 9 [@silverstein1996]. Its authors proposed excision
alone for scores of 3 or 4, and mastectomy for 8 or 9, where eight-year local recurrence exceeded 60%
even with radiation. Retrospective series have since used it to pick out women at low risk after
excision alone [@disaverio2008; @gilleard2008]. Prospective selection gave a cleaner answer.
{{trial:e5194}} treated carefully selected patients by excision without radiation [@hughes2009].
Five-year ipsilateral events were 6.1% in 565 patients with low- or intermediate-grade disease no
larger than 2.5 cm, and 15.3% in 105 with high-grade disease no larger than 1 cm.

Single markers have not filled the gap. HER2 expression was not significantly associated with
recurrence in one series [@stackievicz2010]. Nor has HER2 become a target here. NSABP B-43
randomised 2,014 women with HER2-positive in situ disease treated by lumpectomy to whole-breast
radiation with or without concurrent trastuzumab [@cobleigh2021]. Ipsilateral recurrence fell by a
statistically non-significant 19%, with a hazard ratio of 0.81 and a 95% confidence interval of
0.56 to 1.17. In a population-based cohort, basal-like,
triple-negative and high-grade lesions carried no statistically significant excess risk
[@zhou2010]. A systematic review concluded that the markers that matter in invasive disease have not
been adequately studied in in situ disease [@lari2011]. Combinations did better in one
population-based cohort treated by lumpectomy alone [@kerlikowske2010]. Eight-year risk of invasive
cancer was 19.6% for palpable or p16, COX-2 and Ki67 triple-positive lesions, against 4.1% for
screen-detected triple-negative ones. Receptor testing has one settled use. Testing in situ disease
for ER is recommended, to judge the benefit of endocrine risk reduction, and PgR testing is optional
[@allison2020b].

Two commercial assays address the same gap from different directions. The 21-gene DCIS Score was
measured in 327 patients treated by excision without radiation in {{trial:e5194}}. The continuous
score was associated with an ipsilateral breast event, with a hazard ratio of 2.31 and a 95%
confidence interval of 1.15 to 4.49 after adjustment for tamoxifen use. Ten-year risks of an
ipsilateral event in the prespecified low, intermediate and high groups were 10.6%, 26.7% and
25.9%. The corresponding risks of an invasive ipsilateral event were 3.7%, 12.3% and 19.2%
[@solin2013].

Read the two rows carefully. For any ipsilateral event the intermediate and high groups are
indistinguishable. Only when the endpoint is restricted to invasive disease does the top group
separate from the middle. A three-level assay reporting a two-level result is not a failure of the
biology. It is a property of the endpoint chosen.

The second assay adds treatment prediction rather than risk alone. A biological signature was
developed and cross-validated in 526 patients treated with breast-conserving surgery with or
without radiation. Among patients treated without radiation it identified a low-risk group with a
10-year invasive breast cancer risk of 4%, and an elevated-risk group with a risk of 15%. In the
elevated group the hazard ratio for radiation benefit was 0.3. The signature reclassified 42% of a
clinicopathologically low-risk subset into the elevated group [@bremer2018].

Neither assay has been tested in a randomised trial of assay-directed management. Both were
developed in cohorts assembled for other questions. The distinction between clinical validity and
clinical utility is set out in [[BC-110]], and the evidence bar for adoption in [[BC-380]].

## BS-4230 Surgery and radiation for in situ disease

The local treatment literature in ductal carcinoma in situ is unusually clean, and its central
result is unusually uncomfortable.

Radiation halves invasive local recurrence. Two NSABP trials were analysed at median follow-up of
207 and 163 months. Radiation after lumpectomy reduced invasive ipsilateral recurrence by 52%,
with a hazard ratio of 0.48 and a 95% confidence interval of 0.33 to 0.69. The 15-year cumulative
incidence of invasive ipsilateral recurrence was 19.4% after lumpectomy alone and 8.9% after
lumpectomy with radiation [@wapnir2011]. The UK, Australia and New Zealand trial gave the same
direction, with a hazard ratio of 0.32 for ipsilateral invasive disease [@cuzick2011].

Each trial had reported the same direction earlier. In NSABP B-17, 818 women were randomised, and at
eight years radiation reduced invasive ipsilateral tumours from 13.4% to 3.9% and non-invasive ones
from 13.4% to 8.2% [@fisher1998b]. In EORTC 10853, 1,010 women were randomised, and the 10-year local
recurrence-free rate was 74% after excision alone and 85% with radiation, with similar low risks of
metastasis and death [@julien2000; @bijker2006]. Every subgroup benefited. The UK, Australia and New
Zealand trial first reported a hazard ratio of 0.45 for ipsilateral invasive disease with radiation,
and little evidence for tamoxifen [@houghton2003]. SweDCIS randomised 1,046 women and found a hazard
ratio of 0.33, with seven women treated to prevent one recurrence [@emdin2006]. In a subgroup
analysis its protective effect was low below age 50 and substantial above 60 [@holmberg2008]. A
pooled analysis of the four trials, 3,925 women, gave a hazard ratio of 0.49 for any ipsilateral
event, and nine women needed treatment to prevent one [@goodwin2009]. Deaths from vascular disease,
pulmonary toxicity and second cancers were not significantly higher with radiation. A systematic
review of the wider literature reached the same conclusions for radiation and tamoxifen
[@virnig2010].

This holds even in the lowest-risk disease that has been randomised. {{trial:rtog9804}} enrolled
636 women with mammographically detected disease of low or intermediate nuclear grade, no larger
than 2.5 cm, with margins of 3 mm or more. The 15-year cumulative incidence of ipsilateral breast
recurrence was 7.1% with radiation and 15.1% with observation. For invasive local recurrence the
figures were 5.4% and 9.5% [@mccormick2021]. At seven years, local failure had been 0.9% and 6.7%
[@mccormick2015].

Now the uncomfortable part. Across the same NSABP cohorts the 15-year cumulative incidence of
breast cancer death was 3.1% after lumpectomy alone and 4.7% after lumpectomy with radiation
[@wapnir2011]. A registry analysis of 108,196 women found the same dissociation. Radiation after
lumpectomy reduced 10-year ipsilateral invasive recurrence from 4.9% to 2.5%, with an adjusted
hazard ratio of 0.47. It did not reduce 10-year breast cancer mortality, where the figures were
0.9% and 0.8%, with a hazard ratio of 0.86 and an interval of 0.67 to 1.10 [@narod2015].

Two later analyses of the same registry disagree, and the disagreement is the finding. With
propensity weighting across 32,144 women, 10-year breast cancer mortality was 1.8% with radiation and
2.1% without it, a hazard ratio of 0.73 [@sagara2016]. The difference was confined to higher nuclear
grade, younger age and larger size. Among 140,366 women, 15-year breast cancer mortality was 1.74%
after lumpectomy and radiation and 2.33% after lumpectomy alone [@giannakeas2018]. Its authors
attributed the difference to systemic effects of radiation rather than to local control. All three
are observational, and none can separate the effect of radiation from the reasons it was given.

Invasive recurrence does carry mortality. In the NSABP analysis an invasive ipsilateral recurrence
raised the risk of death, with a hazard ratio of 1.75, while a recurrence that was itself in situ
did not [@wapnir2011]. Preventing the invasive recurrences has nonetheless not translated into
fewer deaths in these datasets.

```caution
The intuitive chain runs from local control to fewer invasive recurrences to fewer deaths. The
first two links are established. The third is not. The most economical explanation is that the
deaths arise from disease that was already disseminated at the time of the in situ diagnosis, in
which case local treatment cannot reach it. That explanation is compatible with the data and it
has not been demonstrated.
```

A boost to the tumour bed was long extrapolated from invasive disease. There, the EORTC trial
showed that a boost improved local control at every age without changing survival, at the cost of
more fibrosis [@bartelink2007; @bartelink2015]. Smaller trials in Lyon and Budapest agreed
[@romestaing1997; @polgar2002]. For in situ disease, a pooled analysis of 4,131 patients from ten
institutions associated the boost with lower ipsilateral recurrence, with a hazard ratio of 0.73
[@moran2017]. BIG 3-07 then randomised 1,608 women with non-low-risk disease [@chua2022]. Five-year
freedom from local recurrence was 92.7% without a boost and 97.1% with it, a hazard ratio of 0.47.
Grade 2 or higher breast pain and induration were more frequent with the boost. Its two-year
quality-of-life report had already found persistent adverse effects of the boost on cosmetic status
and on arm and shoulder function [@king2020].

Partial-breast irradiation trials included in situ disease. The GEC-ESTRO trial randomised 1,184
women with low-risk invasive or in situ carcinoma to multicatheter brachytherapy or whole-breast
irradiation [@strnad2016]. Five-year local recurrence was 1.44% and 0.92%, within the prespecified
margin. The Florence trial found no significant difference in ipsilateral recurrence or survival at
five years, with less toxicity [@livi2015]. The ten-year results and the other partial-breast trials
are in [[BS-3720]].

Margin width matters most when radiation is omitted. In 469 lesions, margins of 10 mm or more gave
an eight-year recurrence probability of 0.04 with no benefit from radiation, while margins under 1 mm
did benefit [@silverstein1999]. In 455 patients treated by excision alone, margins under 10 mm
carried 5.39 times the recurrence risk of wider ones [@macdonald2005]. With radiation, a
meta-analysis of 4,660 patients found a 2 mm margin as good as a wider one [@dunne2009]. In 2,996
women, the association between margin width and recurrence was significant without radiation and
absent with it [@vanzee2015]. The consensus guideline adopted 2 mm as the standard margin with
whole-breast irradiation [@morrow2016b].

Preoperative MRI adds sensitivity without clearly improving surgery. In a prospective series, MRI
diagnosed 92% of pure in situ carcinoma against 56% for mammography, and 98% of high-grade lesions
[@kuhl2007]. It can also misjudge extent, and a decision for mastectomy should not rest on MRI alone
[@allen2010]. Studies of its effect disagree. Some found no reduction in re-excision, conversion to
mastectomy or locoregional recurrence [@davis2012; @pilewskie2014]. One multidisciplinary centre
found fewer operations and more single successful excisions [@lam2020]. In ECOG-ACRIN E4112, MRI
found additional cancer in 6.2%, but its features did not predict pathological or genomic outcome
[@chou2021].

A core biopsy showing in situ disease may be wrong about invasion. Across 52 studies and 7,350
lesions, the pooled rate of invasive cancer at excision was 25.9% [@brennan2011]. A systematic review
for a National Institutes of Health conference put it at about 15% [@virnig2010]. That underestimate
is the argument for sentinel node biopsy when mastectomy is planned, because the axilla cannot be
mapped afterwards. A published debate set the case for it [@cody2003] against the case that it
remained investigational [@edge2003]. ASCO's guideline judged sentinel node biopsy less morbid than
axillary dissection, with its comparative effect on recurrence and survival still unknown
[@lyman2005].

Surgical extent follows the same logic. Mastectomy lowers local recurrence further than
lumpectomy with radiation and has not been shown to lower mortality. The reconstruction questions
belong to [[BC-410]] and the fractionation and omission questions to [[BC-420]].

## BS-4240 Endocrine risk reduction

Endocrine therapy in in situ disease is prevention rather than treatment. It acts on the tissue at
risk in both breasts, not on a tumour that has been removed.

Tamoxifen added to lumpectomy and radiation reduced invasive ipsilateral recurrence by 32%, with a
hazard ratio of 0.68 and a 95% confidence interval of 0.49 to 0.95. The 15-year cumulative
incidence of any contralateral breast cancer was 10.8% with placebo and 7.3% with tamoxifen
[@wapnir2011]. The benefit is confined to ER-positive disease. In a retrospective receptor analysis
of B-24, tamoxifen reduced subsequent breast cancer at 10 years in ER-positive in situ disease, with a
hazard ratio of 0.49, and showed no significant benefit when ER was negative [@allred2012].

The second randomised trial of tamoxifen in this setting reported a different pattern.
Tamoxifen reduced all new breast events, with a hazard ratio of 0.71 and an interval of 0.58 to
0.88. It reduced ipsilateral in situ recurrence and contralateral tumours. It had no effect on
ipsilateral invasive disease, where the hazard ratio was 0.95 with an interval of 0.66 to 1.38
[@cuzick2011]. The two trials differ in design, in the use of radiation and in whether either
treatment could be withheld electively, so the discrepancy is not evidence that one result is
wrong.

Aromatase inhibition has been compared with tamoxifen twice in postmenopausal women, with two
answers. In {{trial:ibis2-dcis}}, 2,980 women were randomised after local excision and 144
recurrences were recorded at a median follow-up of 7.2 years. Anastrozole was non-inferior to
tamoxifen and not superior, with a hazard ratio for all recurrence of 0.89 and an interval of 0.64
to 1.23 [@forbes2016]. In {{trial:b35}}, 3,104 women who had all received lumpectomy and
whole-breast irradiation were randomised, and 212 breast cancer-free interval events occurred at a
median follow-up of 9.0 years. Anastrozole was superior, with a hazard ratio of 0.73 and an
interval of 0.56 to 0.96. A treatment-by-age interaction was significant, and the advantage was
confined to women younger than 60 [@margolese2016].

Dose is a separate axis from agent. In {{trial:tam01}}, 500 women with breast intraepithelial
neoplasia received tamoxifen 5 mg daily for three years or placebo. At a median follow-up of 9.7
years there were 25 breast events against 41. The annual rate was 11.3 against 19.5 per 1,000
person-years, with a hazard ratio of 0.58 and an interval of 0.35 to 0.95. Contralateral events
were 6 against 16. The number needed to treat to prevent one breast event was 22 at five years and
14 at ten years [@lazzeroni2023].

```practice
Decide first whether the goal is ipsilateral control or bilateral risk reduction. Those are
different goals and the evidence separates them.

Offer endocrine therapy on absolute numbers. A hazard ratio of 0.7 applied to a 15-year invasive
recurrence risk of 9% is a different conversation from the same ratio applied to 20%.

In postmenopausal women under 60 the randomised comparison favoured anastrozole over tamoxifen
[@margolese2016]. Over 60 the two trials do not support a preference on efficacy, so choose on the
side-effect profile the patient will actually tolerate.

Low-dose tamoxifen is an option where standard dose was declined or stopped [@lazzeroni2023]. It
is a dose reduction with randomised evidence behind it rather than an informal compromise.
```

## BS-4250 Active surveillance trials and what they will and will not answer

Three randomised trials ask whether some ductal carcinoma in situ can be left alone. They are the
direct experimental form of the question posed in [[BC-050]].

{{trial:comet}} has reported. It enrolled 995 women aged 40 or older with hormone
receptor-positive grade 1 or grade 2 disease and no invasive cancer, and analysed 957. Active
monitoring meant six-monthly imaging and examination. Median follow-up was 36.9 months. The
two-year cumulative rate of ipsilateral invasive cancer was 4.2% with active monitoring and 5.9%
with guideline-concordant care. That difference of minus 1.7 percentage points carried an upper
95% confidence limit of 0.95, against a prespecified non-inferiority bound of 5 percentage points
[@hwang2025]. Forty-six invasive cancers occurred, 19 in the monitoring group and 27 in the
surgery group.

{{trial:loris}} randomises surgery against active monitoring in screen-detected or asymptomatic
low-grade disease, and in intermediate-grade disease with low-grade features [@francis2015loris].
{{trial:lord}} randomises 1,240 women aged 45 or older with pure low-grade disease diagnosed on
vacuum-assisted biopsy of microcalcifications. Its primary endpoint is the 10-year ipsilateral
invasive breast cancer free percentage, with annual mammography in both arms [@elshof2015].

What these trials can answer is whether a defined low-risk group has a similar short-term rate of
invasive diagnosis under monitoring. What they cannot answer is set by three features of their
design.

The first is the endpoint. A diagnosis of invasive cancer is not a death. Surveillance that
converts an operation now into an operation later, for the same eventual outcome, changes when
treatment happens rather than whether it is needed.

The second is the non-inferiority margin. A 5-point bound on a two-year risk is wide relative to
the event rates being compared, and the general problem is developed in [[BC-740]].

The third is the timescale. In a retrospective series of 45 women whose low-grade in situ disease
was treated by biopsy alone, 16 women, which is 36%, later developed invasive carcinoma. Every one
occurred in the same breast and the same quadrant. Eleven appeared within ten years. The rest
appeared at 12, 23, 25, 29 and 42 years, and seven of the 16 women died of metastatic disease
[@sanders2015]. A natural history that runs for four decades cannot be characterised by a two-year
or a ten-year endpoint.

```caution
Non-inferiority at two years is being read as evidence that low-risk disease is safe to watch. The
trial demonstrated that the rate of invasive diagnosis was not higher at two years in a selected
population, under six-monthly imaging, with surgery available on change. Every one of those
conditions is part of the result. The recurring error, conflating no significant difference with
proven equivalence, is the subject of [[BS-6370]].
```

## BS-4260 Recurrence risk, follow-up, and communicating a diagnosis that may not need treating

The numbers that matter in this conversation are absolute and long.

In a registry cohort of 108,196 women, breast cancer specific mortality 20 years after a diagnosis
of ductal carcinoma in situ was 3.3%, with a 95% confidence interval of 3.0 to 3.6. It was 7.8%
for women diagnosed before age 35 against 3.2% for older women. It was 7.0% in Black women against
3.0% in non-Hispanic White women, with a hazard ratio of 2.55 and an interval of 2.17 to 3.01
[@narod2015]. The disparity is larger than most treatment effects in this chapter, and the
structural drivers are developed in [[BC-690]].

One finding in that cohort deserves separating out. Of the women who died of breast cancer, 517
did so without ever having had an in-breast invasive recurrence [@narod2015]. Their disease was
either disseminated before the in situ lesion was found, or arose from tissue that was never
sampled. No local treatment addresses either possibility.

```interplay target=BC-350
In situ disease is where the heterogeneity argument of Part VI is at its most consequential and its
least measurable. A lesion scored as one grade on one block is already subclonally diverse
[[BS-0430]], and the subclone that will invade is not the one the grade was assigned from. That is
why grade, size and margin stratify recurrence coarsely and predict invasion badly, and it is why a
percentage-based assay computed on bulk tissue inherits the same averaging problem described in
[[BS-2160]].
```

```practice
Say what the diagnosis is and what it is not. A carcinoma confined to the duct has not invaded and
has not spread. The word carries a weight the biology does not.

Give the mortality figure before the recurrence figure. Twenty-year breast cancer specific
mortality of about 3% reframes every subsequent number [@narod2015].

Separate the two benefits of treatment explicitly. Radiation and endocrine therapy reduce the
chance of another event in the breast. Neither has been shown in a randomised trial to reduce the
chance of dying, and the registry analyses disagree [@wapnir2011; @narod2015; @giannakeas2018].

State that a randomised trial found active monitoring non-inferior at two years, and state what
two years means [@hwang2025]. Patients who ask about watching deserve the result and its limit in
the same sentence.

Follow-up is annual mammography. Intensified imaging or blood-based surveillance has no
established role here, and the general argument against surveillance intensity is in [[BC-650]].

Name the uncertainty rather than resolving it. The field does not yet know which lesions progress
[[BS-0410]], and saying so is more honest than an implied precision that the assays do not have.
```
