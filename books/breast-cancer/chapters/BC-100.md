## BS-0810 Histology, stage, grade, and clinical phenotype

Stage answers a question about extent. Grade answers a question about appearance. Neither states
which pathway a tumour depends on, and both remain useful for exactly that reason.

The Nottingham system scores three morphological features separately. Tubule formation, nuclear
pleomorphism and a mitotic count performed over a defined field area each receive a score, and the
three scores are summed [@elston1991]. Grade was assessed in 1831 patients in the founding series
and separated survival by a wide margin. The design point is that the criteria were made
semiquantitative in order to be reproducible. That is the same motive behind every threshold in
[[BC-110]].

Mitotic count is the component most sensitive to handling. It depends on the field area used, on
section thickness, and on the delay before fixation [[BS-0960]].

The eighth edition of the American Joint Committee on Cancer staging manual folded grade and
receptor status into a prognostic stage alongside anatomy. In a validation cohort of 3327 patients
at one institution, prognostic stage moved 29.5% of patients up and 28.1% down relative to
anatomic stage [@weiss2018]. The concordance index for disease-specific survival improved from
0.737 to 0.836. A registry cohort of 54727 patients gave the same direction of result.

Note what that shows and what it does not. Adding biology to an anatomic system improves
prognostic discrimination. It does not turn the combined system into a biological taxonomy. Stage
remains a description of extent with prognostic modifiers attached to it.

Morphology also retains predictive content that molecular profiling does not supply, and that
argument belongs to [[BC-090]].

## BS-0820 Hormone receptor and HER2 based clinical categories

The working clinical categories are hormone receptor positive and HER2 negative, HER2 positive,
and triple negative. They are not a taxonomy of breast cancer. They are a map from two assays onto
three treatment pathways, and they were built in that direction.

Each boundary came from a treatment question. Hormone receptor positivity sits at 1% of tumour
nuclei because the guideline preferred over-inclusion to denying endocrine therapy
[@hammond2010; @allison2020]. HER2 positivity sits at immunohistochemistry 3+ or in situ
hybridisation amplification because those were the criteria under which trastuzumab was shown to
work [@wolff2018]. Triple negative is defined by the failure of the other two tests, which makes
it the only category named for an absence.

The categories carry real information despite their origin. Patterns of distant recurrence differ
systematically between them, including the site of first metastasis and the time course
[@kennecke2010]. That is clinical validity. It is not the same claim as the categories marking
natural divisions in the disease.

Two consequences follow for how the categories should be read.

A patient does not move between categories because their biology changed. They move because a
measurement changed, or because a threshold moved. The HER2-low category in [[BS-0930]] is the
cleanest example, since every patient in it was previously called HER2 negative.

A category boundary imported into a trial imports its reproducibility as well. That constraint is
developed in [[BC-740]].

## BS-0830 Intrinsic molecular subtypes and how they were derived

The derivation matters more than the result, because the derivation explains what the subtypes can
and cannot be asked to do.

Perou and colleagues profiled 65 surgical specimens from 42 individuals on complementary DNA
microarrays representing 8102 genes [@perou2000]. Twenty tumours were sampled twice, before and
after a 16-week course of doxorubicin, and two were paired with a node metastasis. The decisive
methodological step used those pairs. Genes were selected for low variation between two samples of
the same tumour and high variation between different tumours. That set was called intrinsic, and
clustering on it produced the subtypes.

So the intrinsic list was built to be stable within a tumour. A gene that moved under treatment
was excluded by construction. The subtypes are therefore a description of between-tumour variation
among features that stay put. That is a narrower object than a classification of breast cancer.

Sorlie and colleagues extended the analysis and split the luminal group into subgroups with
different survival, with basal-like carrying the worst outcome in a uniformly treated locally
advanced cohort [@sorlie2001].

The Cancer Genome Atlas then integrated copy number, methylation, exome sequence, messenger RNA,
microRNA and protein data on primary breast cancers [@tcga2012]. Four main classes emerged when
data from five platforms were combined, and each showed significant molecular heterogeneity
within it. Only three genes were mutated at above 10% incidence across all breast cancers, namely
TP53, PIK3CA and GATA3. Convergence of that kind across independent measurement platforms is the
strongest evidence available that the classes track something real.

```caution
Nearest-centroid assignment gives every sample a subtype. A tumour lying almost equidistant from
two centroids receives one label and no warning. A published subtype is the nearest of a fixed set
of prototypes, not a measurement of how near it was. That distinction is invisible in a report and
it drives much of the discordance described in [[BS-0880]].
```

## BS-0840 HER2-enriched subtype as a predictor of anti-HER2 response

This is the clearest case in the book where intrinsic subtype adds information that receptor
status does not already carry, and the demonstration is worth setting out in full.

A single-group phase II trial gave 18 weeks of lapatinib and trastuzumab, without chemotherapy, to
151 women with previously untreated HER2-positive stage I to IIIA disease [@llombart2017]. Hormone
receptor positive patients also received endocrine therapy. Intrinsic subtype was called by PAM50
on a baseline biopsy. At baseline 101 tumours (67%) were HER2-enriched, 22 (15%) were luminal A,
16 (11%) were luminal B, nine (6%) were basal-like and three (2%) were normal-like.

Pathological complete response in the breast occurred in 46 of 151 patients overall. Among
HER2-enriched tumours it occurred in 41 of 101, which is 41%. Among non-HER2-enriched tumours it
occurred in five of 50, which is 10%. The odds ratio was 6.2, with a 95% confidence interval of
2.3 to 16.8 [@llombart2017].

What makes this informative is that the comparison is internal. Every patient in the trial was
HER2 positive by the routine assay. Subtype separated them anyway, so the information was not
already contained in the receptor result.

A pooled analysis across five trials points the same way and sharpens it. It analysed 305 patients
with early HER2-positive disease, combining the HER2-enriched call with ERBB2 messenger RNA level.
Tumours that were both HER2-enriched and ERBB2-high reached a pathological complete response rate
of 44.5% after lapatinib and trastuzumab, against 11.6% for everyone else [@prat2020]. The adjusted
odds ratio was 6.05.

Three limits belong alongside the result.

The endpoint was pathological complete response to a chemotherapy-free regimen. That is the
setting most sensitive to HER2 dependence, and it is not the setting most patients are treated in.

Subtype was called on research-grade RNA in a trial laboratory. Nothing here establishes that a
routine subtype call would perform the same way.

A 41% response rate still leaves most of the enriched group without a complete response, and 10%
is not zero. The subtype shifts a probability. It does not identify a population that can safely
be treated differently.

## BS-0850 PAM50, BluePrint, and subtype calling in practice

PAM50 is a 50-gene nearest-centroid predictor. It was developed on 189 prototype samples, using
microarray and quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction data. It was then
tested for prognosis in 761 patients who received no systemic therapy, and for prediction of
pathological complete response in 133 [@parker2009].

Applied to archival material it works. In 786 ER-positive tamoxifen-treated cases with a median
follow-up of 11.7 years, the assay ran on formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded blocks and gave more
prognostic information than clinical assays for hormone receptors or Ki-67 [@nielsen2010]. Ten
percent of clinically ER-positive cases were assigned to a non-luminal subtype.

BluePrint is a separate 80-gene profile, developed on 200 specimens and confirmed in four
independent validation cohorts totalling 784 patients [@krijgsman2012]. It sorts tumours into
basal, luminal and HER2 types, and it was also tested against pathological complete response in
133 neoadjuvant patients.

The practical problem is not any single assay. It is that different single-sample predictors
disagree about the same tumour. Three published predictors were applied to one in-house cohort of
53 tumours and three public cohorts totalling 779 [@weigelt2010]. Pairwise agreement across the
whole classification ran from a kappa of 0.238 to 0.740. Only basal-like showed almost perfect
agreement, above 0.812. The proportion of tumours called basal-like was consistent whichever
predictor was used. The proportions called luminal A, luminal B, HER2 and normal-like varied
substantially.

That result should govern how any published subtype is read. The predictors identified subtypes
with broadly similar survival curves, and they did not assign the same patients to the same
subtypes.

```caution
A subtype in a paper is a property of the tumour and the classifier together. Two papers reporting
on luminal B disease may not be reporting on the same patients. Basal-like is the exception. It is
the only intrinsic class that can be compared across publications without first checking which
predictor was used.
```

## BS-0860 Integrative clusters and copy number driven taxonomy

Expression-based subtypes cluster on transcription. Integrative clusters cluster on the joint
structure of copy number and expression, which is a different axis and yields a different
partition.

The METABRIC analysis paired DNA and RNA profiles from 997 discovery and 995 validation primary
tumours, each with long-term follow-up [@curtis2012]. Inherited variants and acquired somatic copy
number aberrations were associated with expression for roughly 40% of genes. Unsupervised analysis
of the paired profiles produced
subgroups that reproduced in the validation set. They included a high-risk ER-positive subgroup
driven by cis-acting amplification at 11q13 and 11q14, and a favourable subgroup with almost no
copy number aberration at all.

The clinically interesting result came later, from the same cohort with longer follow-up. A
statistical model applied to 3240 patients, 1980 of them with molecular data, mapped where and
when relapse occurs across three classification systems [@rueda2019]. Four late-recurring
integrative subtypes were identified. Together they comprise about 26% of tumours that are ER
positive and HER2 negative, and each carries a mean risk of recurrence between 47% and 62% up to
20 years after diagnosis. The same analysis separated a group of triple-negative cancers in which
recurrence after five years is rare from a group that remains at risk.

That is a different kind of claim from a subtype label. It is a statement about timing, and timing
is what an endocrine-treated patient at year eight actually needs to know.

None of this is in routine use, and the reason is clean rather than political. No trial has
randomised a management decision on an integrative cluster. Why that is the deciding question is
set out in [[BS-0970]].

## BS-0870 Transcriptomic, proteomic, and integrated classifications

Every measurement layer produces its own partition of the same tumours. The disagreements between
layers carry information, and they are not noise.

Quantitative mass spectrometry was applied to 105 genomically annotated breast cancers, of which
77 gave high-quality proteomic and phosphoproteomic data [@mertins2016]. Global protein data
confirmed basal and luminal clusters. It also produced a stromal-enriched group that expression
clustering had not separated in the same way. Analysis of the phosphoproteome identified a cluster
driven by G-protein-coupled receptor signalling that was not readily visible at the messenger RNA
level.

Two lessons come out of that, and they should be kept apart.

The first is biological. Protein abundance and phosphorylation sit downstream of transcript
abundance, and the steps between are not uniform across tumours. A pathway can be active without
its transcripts being elevated.

The second is methodological. A stromal-enriched proteomic cluster is partly a statement about
what else was in the specimen. The integrated Cancer Genome Atlas analysis found the same thing
independently, identifying two protein-expression-defined subgroups that the authors attributed to
stromal and microenvironmental elements [@tcga2012]. That is the composition problem developed in
[[BS-0890]].

Convergence across platforms is the strongest available argument that a class is not an artefact
of one measurement. Divergence between platforms is not evidence that one of them is wrong.

## BS-0880 Discordance between clinical categories and molecular class

Clinical categories and molecular classes disagree at a rate that surprises people. The
disagreement is systematic rather than random, which makes it interpretable.

Clinically HER2-positive disease is not uniformly HER2-enriched. In a trial that enrolled only
centrally confirmed HER2-positive patients, 67% were HER2-enriched by PAM50 and 38 of 151, which
is 25%, were luminal A or luminal B [@llombart2017].

Clinically ER-positive disease includes non-luminal calls. Ten percent of ER-positive
tamoxifen-treated cases were assigned a non-luminal subtype [@nielsen2010]. The 1% to 10% ER band
is considerably worse, and the numbers sit in [[BS-0910]].

Subtype is not fixed across time or site either. In 123 paired primary and metastatic samples,
the rate of subtype conversion was 0% for basal-like tumours, 23.1% for HER2-enriched, 30.0% for
luminal B and 55.3% for luminal A [@cejalvo2017]. Luminal A tumours most often converted to
luminal B.

The important move is to stop treating this as error. A clinical category answers whether a drug
class is licensed for this patient. A molecular class answers which transcriptional programme the
bulk of the tumour is running. Those are different questions, and there is no reason for the
answers to coincide.

Discordance does matter when one system is used as a proxy for the other. An immunohistochemical
surrogate for luminal B disease, using Ki-67 and HER2 alongside hormone receptors, was derived
against gene expression profiling in 357 tumours and then applied to 4046 [@cheang2009]. The
surrogate is used because profiling is expensive, and it inherits the Ki-67 reproducibility
documented in [[BS-0950]]. Consensus definitions built on such surrogates have been in general use
for over a decade [@goldhirsch2013].

## BS-0890 Single-cell and spatial data as a challenge to bulk-defined subtypes

Bulk expression is an average over every cell in the specimen, weighted by how many of each there
are. A subtype called from bulk data is therefore partly a statement about composition.

A single-cell and spatially resolved atlas of human breast cancers made that explicit [@wu2021].
The authors built a single-cell method of intrinsic subtype classification and used it to reveal
recurrent heterogeneity among neoplastic cells within individual tumours. Deconvolution of large
bulk cohorts using single-cell signatures then stratified them into nine ecotypes, defined by
cellular composition and associated with different clinical outcomes.

An ecotype is not a competing subtype. It is a different object. A subtype names the
transcriptional programme of the dominant malignant population. An ecotype names what else is
present and in what proportion.

Spatial measurement adds a third axis, which is arrangement. Imaging mass cytometry across large
breast cancer cohorts resolved single-cell phenotypes in place and related them to genomic
features and to outcome [@jackson2020; @ali2020; @danenberg2022]. Two tumours with the same
cellular composition can have different structure, and structure carries prognostic weight of its
own.

Single-cell genomics makes the same point about the malignant compartment alone. Subclonal
structure within one tumour was demonstrated by single-cell sequencing well before the
transcriptional work [@navin2011]. Topographic single-cell sequencing then showed multiple clones
invading together rather than sequentially [@casasent2018].

None of this shows that bulk subtypes are wrong. It shows that one label per tumour cannot carry
composition, arrangement and subclonal structure at the same time. Much of the classifier
disagreement in [[BS-0850]] comes from asking a label to do exactly that.

```interplay target=BC-350
Classification and heterogeneity are the same problem seen from two directions. A taxonomy assigns
one label per tumour, and heterogeneity is the statement that one label is not enough. Every
argument in this chapter about discordance between classifiers, between platforms or between
primary and metastasis is a heterogeneity argument arriving through the door of measurement rather
than through the door of biology.
```

## BS-0900 Classification as a changing framework rather than a fact

A taxonomy is a tool, and the first question about any tool is what it was made for.

Three questions separate a useful classification from a fashionable one. What was it built to
predict. On what measurement. Against what comparator.

The intrinsic subtypes answer the first question differently at different points in their history,
and that is the heart of the critique. They were derived to describe between-tumour variation in a
gene set selected for stability within a tumour [@perou2000]. They were then used for prognosis
[@sorlie2001; @parker2009]. They were then used to predict response [@llombart2017; @prat2020].
Each of those is a separate claim requiring separate evidence. The evidence is strongest for the
first use and thinnest for the third.

The second question is where most published disagreement lives. A class that different classifiers
assign differently cannot be compared across studies [@weigelt2010]. A class derived from bulk
expression cannot separate tumour from stroma without help [@mertins2016; @wu2021].

The third question decides whether a taxonomy enters practice. A classification changes care only
when a trial has randomised a decision on it. That has happened for the multigene prognostic
assays and it has not happened for intrinsic subtype, for integrative clusters, or for any
single-cell derived scheme. The framework behind that statement is in [[BS-0970]].

What survives all three questions is worth naming, because it is not nothing. Basal-like
reproduces across classifiers and across measurement platforms. HER2-enriched carries predictive
information beyond receptor status within HER2-positive disease. Copy-number-based clusters
predict the timing of relapse rather than only its likelihood.

```caution
The instinct on meeting a new classification is to ask whether it is better than the old one. That
question usually has no answer, because the two were built to predict different things. Ask
instead what each was trained on, what it was validated against, and which decision it is being
offered for. A taxonomy that cannot answer the third question is a research result rather than a
clinical tool.
```

Classification in this disease has changed roughly once a decade and it will change again. The
durable part is not the list of classes. It is the discipline of asking what a given class was
built to do.
