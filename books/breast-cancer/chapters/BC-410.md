## BS-3630 Breast-conserving surgery and mastectomy

The randomised evidence that settled this question is old, large and unambiguous. It is worth
knowing precisely, because the modern argument is about which patients rather than about whether.

In {{trial:nsabp-b06}}, 1851 women were randomised to total mastectomy, lumpectomy alone, or
lumpectomy with breast irradiation, and followed for 20 years [@fisher2002b06]. The hazard ratio for
death with lumpectomy alone against mastectomy was 1.05, with a 95% confidence interval of 0.90 to
1.23. With lumpectomy and irradiation it was 0.97, with an interval of 0.83 to 1.14. Ipsilateral
breast recurrence was the outcome that separated the arms, at 14.3% with irradiation and 39.2%
without it.

The Milan trial reached the same conclusion by a different route. Among 701 women randomised to
quadrantectomy with radiotherapy or to radical mastectomy, 20-year all-cause mortality was 41.7%
and 41.2% [@veronesi2002]. Ipsilateral breast recurrence was 8.8% against 2.3%.

The inference is the Fisher hypothesis and it is the intellectual foundation of everything that
follows. The extent of local surgery determines local control. It does not determine systemic
outcome, because distant disease is established or not established before the operation. The history
of how that idea displaced the centrifugal model is in [[BS-0030]] and [[BS-0050]].

Mastectomy remains the right operation for multicentric disease, for an unfavourable ratio of tumour
to breast volume, for inability to receive radiation, and for patients who prefer it. None of those
indications rests on a survival argument, and it is worth being explicit with patients that
mastectomy is not the safer cancer operation.

## BS-3640 Margins, re-excision, and the evidence for less

A margin is a property of the specimen and of how the pathologist sampled its surface. It is not a
property of the tumour. The surface being assessed was created by the operation, and the tissue on
the other side of it is still in the patient.

The consensus standard for invasive disease is no ink on tumour. It was derived from a systematic
review and meta-analysis of 33 studies including 28,162 patients [@moran2014]. Positive margins
roughly doubled the risk of ipsilateral breast tumour recurrence. Wider clear margins did not reduce
that risk further. The absence of additional benefit held in young patients,
in lobular cancers and in tumours with an extensive intraductal component.

Ductal carcinoma in situ has a different standard, and the difference is instructive rather than
arbitrary. A parallel consensus based on 20 studies including 7883 patients set 2 mm as the
threshold, because narrower negative margins carried higher recurrence and wider ones did not carry
lower [@morrow2016]. In situ disease spreads along ducts in a pattern that a two-dimensional margin
assessment samples poorly, which is why the empirical threshold sits further out.

Adopting the invasive standard changed practice measurably. At one centre, re-excision after
breast-conserving surgery for stage I or II invasive disease fell from 21.4% to 15.1% after the
guideline was adopted [@rosenberger2016]. That is a single-institution before-and-after comparison
and should be read as one.

The reason a narrow margin is acceptable is not that residual disease is absent. It is that residual
microscopic disease is the target of the radiation and the systemic therapy that follow. A margin
policy is therefore a statement about the whole treatment package rather than about the operation
alone.

## BS-3650 Axillary staging and the long retreat from dissection

Each step in this retreat removed information rather than treatment, and understanding that is what
makes the sequence coherent.

Sentinel node biopsy replaced dissection for staging. {{trial:nsabp-b32}} randomised 5611 women, and
3989 of them had pathologically negative sentinel nodes [@krag2010]. In that group the hazard ratio
for overall survival comparing sentinel biopsy alone with biopsy plus dissection was 1.20, with a
95% confidence interval of 0.96 to 1.50. Eight-year overall survival was 91.8% and 90.3%.

Dissection was then dropped for limited sentinel node involvement. {{trial:z0011}} randomised 891
women with one or two involved sentinel nodes, all treated by lumpectomy and whole-breast radiation
[@giuliano2017]. Ten-year overall survival was 86.3% without dissection and 83.6% with it, against a
prespecified non-inferiority margin of 1.3 on the hazard ratio.

{{trial:amaros}} asked whether radiation could replace dissection rather than whether treatment
could be dropped. Among 1425 patients with a positive sentinel node, the 10-year cumulative
incidence of axillary recurrence was 0.93% after dissection and 1.82% after axillary radiotherapy
[@donker2014; @bartels2023]. The hazard ratio was 1.71 with a wide interval of 0.67 to 4.39. There
was no difference in overall or disease-free survival. Lymphoedema at five years was 24.5% after
dissection and 11.9% after radiotherapy.

{{trial:senomac}} extended the omission to larger tumours and to macrometastases. Among 2540
patients analysed per protocol, estimated five-year recurrence-free survival was 89.7% with sentinel
biopsy only and 88.7% with completion dissection [@deboniface2024]. The hazard ratio was 0.89 with
an interval of 0.66 to 1.19.

{{trial:sound}} removed axillary surgery altogether in small tumours with a negative axillary
ultrasound. Among 1405 women, five-year distant disease-free survival was 97.7% with sentinel biopsy
and 98.0% with no axillary surgery [@gentilini2023]. Nodal involvement was present in 13.7% of the
patients who had surgery, so the trial did not avoid nodal disease. It declined to measure it.

{{trial:insema}} asked the same question in breast-conserving surgery for clinically node-negative
disease of 5 cm or less, and it is the largest of the three. Its design is the one to notice. It
randomised three to one against surgery, which buys precision where the uncertainty actually sits.

The reason this sequence was possible is that the value of nodal information fell. Adjuvant systemic
therapy is now selected largely by tumour biology and by genomic assays [[BS-4300]], so counting
nodes changes the plan less often than it once did.

```caution
Read what was actually omitted. In {{trial:z0011}} every patient received tangential whole-breast
irradiation, and tangential fields treat part of the axilla. In {{trial:senomac}}, nodal radiation
was given to 89.9% of the sentinel-only group [@deboniface2024].

Neither trial shows that an untreated involved axilla is safe. Both show that surgical clearance is
unnecessary when the axilla is being irradiated. Those are different claims, and the second one does
not transfer to a patient who will not receive nodal radiation.

The non-inferiority margins here also deserve inspection rather than assumption, and [[BS-6370]] is
where the book examines them.
```

## BS-3660 Axillary management after neoadjuvant therapy

Chemotherapy changes the axilla, and that creates a staging problem that does not exist before
treatment. Lymphatic channels are disrupted and residual disease is patchy, so the sentinel node
represents the axilla less reliably.

{{trial:z1071}} measured the size of the problem. Among 525 patients with biopsy-proven node-positive
disease who had two or more sentinel nodes removed after chemotherapy, the false-negative rate was
12.6%, with a 90% Bayesian credible interval of 9.85% to 16.05% [@boughey2013]. Nodal pathological
complete response occurred in 41.0% of evaluable patients, which is why the question matters at all.

{{trial:sentina}} found the same order of magnitude and located the failure more precisely. In the
cohort that converted from clinically node-positive to clinically node-negative, the detection rate
was 80.1% and the false-negative rate was 14.2% [@kuehn2013]. The rate was 24.3% when a single node
was removed and 18.5% when two were removed. Repeating sentinel biopsy after chemotherapy in
patients who had already had one before it performed badly, with a detection rate of 60.8% and a
false-negative rate of 51.6%.

Technique therefore matters more here than anywhere else in axillary surgery. Dual tracer, retrieval
of three or more nodes, and clipping the biopsy-proven node so it can be targeted all lower the
false-negative rate.

The question then moved from surgery to radiation. In {{trial:nsabp-b51}}, 1641 patients with
biopsy-proven nodal disease who reached ypN0 after neoadjuvant chemotherapy were randomised to
regional nodal irradiation or not, and 1556 were included in the primary-event analysis
[@mamounas2025]. Freedom from invasive recurrence or breast cancer death did not differ, with a
hazard ratio of 0.88 and a 95% confidence interval of 0.60 to 1.28. Five-year point estimates were
92.7% and 91.8%.

```caution
A false-negative rate is a property of a procedure and a definition, and it is calculable only in
patients who also had a completion dissection. It quantifies staging accuracy. It does not quantify
the consequence of being missed, because the missed disease in these trials was then removed
surgically and the patients went on to receive radiation and systemic therapy.

The clinically relevant question is what proportion of patients are harmed by the error, and no
false-negative rate answers that.
```

## BS-3670 Oncoplastic surgery

Oncoplastic surgery combines tumour excision with reshaping, either by displacing local tissue into
the defect or by importing tissue from elsewhere. The claim it makes is geometric rather than
biological. It widens the range of tumours that can be removed with an acceptable cosmetic result,
and it does not alter the disease.

For a medical oncologist the consequences are downstream and practical. Tissue rearrangement moves
the tumour bed away from the skin scar, so surgical clips placed at the time of excision become the
only reliable record of where the tumour was. Radiation boost targeting and partial-breast planning
both depend on that record [[BS-3720]].

Re-excision is harder after rearrangement, because the original cavity no longer exists as a
discrete space. That raises the cost of a positive margin and makes intraoperative margin assessment
more valuable in these operations than in a simple lumpectomy.

The evidence base is observational. No randomised trial compares oncoplastic with standard
breast-conserving surgery for disease outcome, and the comparison would be difficult to design
because the populations differ by construction. Claims that oncoplastic technique improves
oncological outcome should be read with that in mind.

## BS-3680 Reconstruction options and aesthetic outcomes

The choice is between implant-based and autologous reconstruction, and between immediate and
delayed timing. Patient-reported outcome data are now good enough to quote.

One prospective multicentre cohort followed 2013 women undergoing immediate reconstruction, of whom
1217 completed questionnaires at two years [@santosa2018]. Autologous reconstruction scored higher
than implant reconstruction on the BREAST-Q scales after adjustment for baseline characteristics.
The adjusted differences were 7.94 points for satisfaction with breasts, 3.27 for psychosocial
well-being and 5.53 for sexual well-being, each on a 0 to 100 scale.

Radiation changes the calculation rather than adding to it uniformly. In the same consortium, at
least one breast complication occurred by two years in 38.9% of irradiated implant patients and
25.6% of irradiated autologous patients [@jagsi2018]. The corresponding figures without radiation
were 21.8% and 28.3%. Among irradiated patients, autologous reconstruction carried lower odds of
complication, at 0.47 with an interval of 0.27 to 0.82. Among unirradiated patients the two
approaches did not differ.

```practice
Decide the radiation plan before the reconstructive plan, not after. The interaction above runs in
one direction only, and a patient who will need post-mastectomy radiation faces a different choice
from one who will not.

Say that reconstruction is usually a sequence of operations rather than one. Revision is expected
rather than exceptional, and patients who were told otherwise experience it as a complication.

Nipple-sparing and skin-sparing techniques change the reconstructive result and not the oncological
one, provided the margins are clear. Treat them as preference-sensitive.

Delayed reconstruction remains available. Framing immediate reconstruction as the only route pushes
a permanent decision into the week of a cancer diagnosis.
```

## BS-3690 Functional morbidity and surgical de-escalation

Every de-escalation in this chapter was made for a reason, and the reason is in this section. If a
trial reports non-inferior cancer outcomes and does not measure function, it has answered half the
question it was asked.

Lymphoedema is the dominant morbidity. In a systematic review of 72 studies the pooled incidence of
unilateral arm lymphoedema after breast cancer was 16.6%, with a 95% confidence interval of 13.6% to
20.2% [@disipio2013]. That figure pools all 72 studies. Restricted to the 30 prospective cohorts the
estimate rose to 21.4%, with an interval of 14.9% to 29.8%. That restricted estimate is what
supports the authors' own headline that more than one in five women who survive breast cancer
develop arm lymphoedema. The two are cuts of one review rather than competing numbers. The framing
used elsewhere in this book is the prospective-cohort cut [[BS-5540]]. The estimate also
depended heavily on how lymphoedema was defined and measured, reaching 28.2% in studies using more
than one diagnostic method. Incidence after axillary dissection was about 19.9% against about 5.6%
after sentinel node biopsy.

Randomised data give the same contrast within a single trial. In ALMANAC, 1031 women were randomised
to sentinel node biopsy or standard axillary treatment, and at 12 months the relative risk of
lymphoedema was 0.37, with absolute rates of 5% and 13% [@mansel2006]. Sensory loss showed the same
relative risk, at 11% against 31%. Arm functioning and quality of life scores were better throughout
the first year.

Substituting radiation for dissection reduces but does not abolish the problem. {{trial:amaros}}
reported lymphoedema at five years in 24.5% after dissection and 11.9% after axillary radiotherapy
[@bartels2023].

```caution
Lymphoedema incidence is not a fixed number. It is a function of the definition, the measurement
method and the duration of follow-up, and those three vary more across studies than the surgery
does [@disipio2013].

Quoting a single incidence figure to a patient without saying how it was measured overstates the
precision of what is known. The comparison between procedures is robust. The absolute number is not.
```

## BS-3700 Can surgery be omitted after exceptional response

This is the last surgical de-escalation and it is not yet established. The logic is the same as
elsewhere in Part VIII. If neoadjuvant therapy has eradicated the tumour, the operation removes
nothing.

The best available evidence is a single-arm phase 2 trial. Fifty patients with triple-negative or
HER2-positive disease had a residual imaging lesion smaller than 2 cm after neoadjuvant therapy
[@kuerer2022]. Each underwent image-guided vacuum-assisted biopsy of the tumour bed with a minimum
of 12 cores. No residual invasive or in situ disease was found in 31 patients, which is 62% with
a 95% confidence interval of 47.2% to 75.4%. Those 31 patients had radiotherapy and no breast
surgery, and at a median follow-up of 26.4 months none had an ipsilateral breast tumour recurrence.

Four limitations should travel with that result. The denominator for the recurrence finding is 31
patients. The follow-up is short relative to the recurrence risk in these subtypes. Selection was
narrow, covering unicentric disease with a small residual lesion in two chemosensitive subtypes.
And the result rests on a specific biopsy protocol rather than on the idea of biopsy.

```caution
A negative biopsy is evidence about the volume that was sampled. Treating it as evidence about the
whole tumour bed is the identical error to reading a single core as representative of a
heterogeneous tumour, and the argument is made in full in [[BC-270]].

The question this approach raises is not whether a pathological complete response predicts good
outcome. It does. The question is whether a biopsy can identify a pathological complete response
reliably enough to act on, and that is a sampling question rather than a biological one.
```

Answering it requires a randomised comparison with an explicit non-inferiority margin, in a
population where the event rate is low. The design problem that creates is developed in
[[BS-6370]], and the conditions under which the neoadjuvant setting can support such a design are
in [[BS-4690]].

```interplay target=BC-270
Surgical de-escalation and the sampling problem are the same argument arriving from opposite
directions. Part VI asks what a biopsy represents when a tumour is spatially heterogeneous. This
chapter asks what an operation is for when the tumour may already be gone. Both questions reduce to
how much of the disease a sampled volume can speak for, and neither has an assay-level answer.
```
