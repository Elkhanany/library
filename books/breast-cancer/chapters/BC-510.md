## BS-4460 Risk assessment and treatment selection

Selection here is driven by stage more than by biology, because no biological variable in routine
use changes the regimen.

Stage II and stage III disease is treated with neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy, which is the
population {{trial:keynote522}} enrolled [@schmid2020]. The evidence in [[BS-4470]] is confined to
that population and does not extend below it.

Below stage II the evidence thins fast. The smallest node-negative tumours were excluded from the
trials that define current practice, so treatment there is extrapolation from prognosis rather
than from a randomised result. Node-negative triple-negative tumours with dense lymphocytic
infiltration have a substantially better prognosis without chemotherapy than those without it
[@park2019; @loi2019]. That observation is prognostic. It has not been used prospectively to
withhold treatment, and the scoring convention that makes it reproducible is in [@salgado2015].

What a surgery-first patient actually receives comes from a different generation of trials. It is
worth seeing what they enrolled.

```evidence setting=early subtype=TNBC line=adjuvant topic=adjuvant-backbone sort=year cols=acronym,n,population,arms,result,os caption=The_randomised_trials_that_built_the_adjuvant_chemotherapy_backbone._Most_enrolled_all_comers_before_subtype_was_routinely_recorded,_which_is_the_population_column's_point.
```

Only two of those rows restricted entry by receptor status. Both did so by HER2 rather than by
triple-negative status. The taxane, the schedule and the anthracycline question were all settled
in populations defined by nodal status and tumour size. They were applied to this subtype
afterwards. Dose density is where the transfer is best supported. [[BS-7200]] holds the two trials
that anchor it.

Two things should happen at diagnosis in every patient regardless of stage.

Germline testing should be arranged before the systemic plan is fixed. A BRCA1 or BRCA2 pathogenic
variant changes the post-neoadjuvant option [[BS-4500]], the surgical conversation and the
family's testing, and the strategy is set out in [[BC-070]].

And the neoadjuvant decision should be taken deliberately rather than by default. If the tumour is
large enough or node-positive enough that residual disease would trigger a different adjuvant
regimen, treating first is what makes that regimen available.

This chapter is the decision architecture and the argument. The trial record it rests on is split
between two neighbours. What is given before surgery is in [[BC-860]]. What is given after it is
in [[BC-850]]. Where the two touch, this chapter points forward rather than restating.

## BS-4470 Neoadjuvant chemotherapy with immunotherapy

```story SQ-0370
```

```evidence setting=early subtype=TNBC line=neoadjuvant,adjuvant modality=immunotherapy sort=year cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,endpoint,result,os,status caption=Checkpoint_blockade_in_early_triple-negative_disease,_including_the_two_trials_that_gave_it_only_after_surgery._Read_the_arms_column_for_whether_treatment_continued_past_the_operation.
```

{{trial:keynote522}} is the trial that made this standard. The reason is the third endpoint rather
than the first. It reported response, then event-free survival, then overall survival, in that
order and years apart [@schmid2020; @schmid2022k522efs; @schmid2024k522os]. The last of those is a
mortality benefit of roughly five percentage points at five years in a curable population. It is
also the single result in this book that does most damage to pathological complete response as a
surrogate endpoint, and [[BS-4490]] takes that up.

{{trial:geparnuevo}} supplies the dissociation in cleaner form. Its response difference was not
significant and its survival difference was large, in the same randomisation and the same patients
[@loibl2019geparnuevo; @loibl2022geparnuevo; @loibl2026geparnuevo]. Durvalumab was not continued
after surgery, which is the feature that makes the trial useful for a question
{{trial:keynote522}} cannot answer.

The class effect is not automatic. {{trial:b59}} added a different checkpoint inhibitor across
both halves of the same pathway and missed its primary endpoint [@loibl2026b59]. And timing
appears to matter more than the agent. {{trial:impassion030}} gave atezolizumab with adjuvant
chemotherapy and no neoadjuvant phase, and was stopped for futility [@ignatiadis2025]. The
argument that an intact primary and its draining nodes are the compartment where priming happens
is developed in [[BS-4190]]. The trial-by-trial reading is in [[BS-7310]].

```caution
Three trials of the same class in the same disease gave a survival benefit, a missed primary
endpoint and a futility stop. The differences include the agent, the chemotherapy backbone,
whether the tumour was in place, and whether treatment continued after surgery. None of those
variables was isolated. Reading any one of these results as a statement about checkpoint blockade
in triple-negative breast cancer, rather than about the regimen actually tested, overstates what
the evidence supports.
```

## BS-4480 Platinum agents, BRCA status, and repair-directed selection

The platinum question in early disease has been overtaken rather than answered.

```evidence setting=early subtype=TNBC line=neoadjuvant modality=chemo topic=platinum sort=year cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,endpoint,result,status caption=Platinum_before_surgery._Two_of_these_rows_randomised_the_drug_and_two_selected_the_patient_by_a_repair_assay.
```

The mechanistic case is clear and it comes from the metastatic setting. {{trial:tnt}} compared
carboplatin with docetaxel in unselected advanced triple-negative disease and found no difference
in response. Among patients with germline BRCA1 or BRCA2 variants, carboplatin produced double the
response rate of docetaxel, at 68% against 33%, with a biomarker-by-treatment interaction p value
of 0.01 [@tutt2018].

The same study is the more useful one for what does not work as a selector. Benefit from platinum
was not seen in patients selected by BRCA1 methylation, by low BRCA1 messenger RNA, or by a high
score on a commercial homologous recombination deficiency assay [@tutt2018]. A germline mutation
predicted platinum sensitivity. The assays intended to capture the same phenotype did not. The two
rows in the table above that selected on such an assay found the same thing.

In early disease the question is now structural rather than empirical. Carboplatin is part of the
{{trial:keynote522}} backbone, given to both arms [@schmid2020]. The trial therefore cannot
estimate the contribution of platinum, and no current trial isolates it in the presence of
checkpoint blockade. Platinum in stage II to III disease is not a separate decision. It is part of
the regimen that was tested.

The biology of homologous recombination deficiency and the difference between a germline mutation,
a functional deficiency and a genomic scar are developed in [[BC-180]], and the assay landscape in
[[BS-3420]]. The practical position for now is that germline testing changes management and
somatic repair assays do not.

## BS-4490 Pathologic response and residual disease adaptation

Pathological complete response in this subtype is the strongest patient-level prognostic marker in
breast oncology, and it is not a valid trial-level surrogate. Both statements come from the same
analysis and both are true.

```story SQ-0400
```

One pooled analysis covered 12 trials and 11,955 patients. Among those with triple-negative
disease, eradication of tumour from breast and nodes carried an event-free survival hazard ratio
of 0.24, with a 95% confidence interval of 0.18 to 0.33. The overall survival hazard ratio was
0.16, with an interval of 0.11 to 0.25 [@cortazar2014]. The trial-level analysis of the same
dataset ran the other way. The increase in pathological complete response frequency between
treatment groups explained almost none of the variation in event-free survival, with a coefficient
of determination of 0.03. For overall survival that coefficient was 0.24 [@cortazar2014].

{{trial:geparnuevo}} is what that failure looks like inside a single trial rather than across a
pooled dataset. The authors' multivariable analysis found a durvalumab effect independent of the
response effect. In the group that did not reach a complete response, three-year invasive
disease-free survival was 76.3% against 69.7% [@loibl2022geparnuevo]. Benefit accrued in the
patients whose tumours were not eradicated, which is precisely the population a response-mediated
mechanism cannot reach.

Residual disease is not one prognosis. It is a spectrum, which is why [[term:rcb]] and the
continuous measures in [[BS-4680]] carry more information than a binary result. Immune context
adds a further axis. Among 39 evaluable patients with residual disease in {{trial:geparnuevo}},
estimated seven-year invasive disease-free survival was 92.3% where stromal tumour-infiltrating
lymphocytes exceeded 10%, with a confidence interval of 56.6 to 98.9. Where they did not, it was
51.4%, with an interval of 29.2 to 69.7 [@loibl2026geparnuevo]. The numbers are small and the
intervals are wide. The direction is large enough to be worth measuring.

```practice
Report response as breast and nodes together. Eradication from the breast alone carries a weaker
association with outcome than eradication from both [@cortazar2014].

Use response to assign the next treatment, not to evaluate the last one. Assigning on observed
response is what [[BS-4500]] rests on, and it is a patient-level use that survives the surrogacy
failure.

When a regimen raises pathological complete response and has no mature survival data, say that the
survival question is open. The trial-level correlation between the two is close to nothing
[@cortazar2014].

Ask for stromal lymphocyte scoring on the residual disease specimen where it is available. It is
prognostic and it costs a slide [@loibl2026geparnuevo; @salgado2015].
```

## BS-4500 Post-neoadjuvant systemic therapy

Residual invasive disease after neoadjuvant treatment is the highest-risk state in early
triple-negative breast cancer, and two randomised options exist.

```evidence setting=early subtype=TNBC,BRCA line=post-neoadjuvant modality=chemo,parp,immunotherapy,adc sort=year cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,endpoint,result,os,status caption=Systemic_therapy_allocated_on_what_surgery_found._Four_of_these_seven_rows_have_not_reported.
```

```evidence setting=early subtype=BRCA line=adjuvant modality=parp cols=acronym,n,population,arms,endpoint,result,os caption=The_germline_option._Its_registry_line_is_adjuvant_because_high_risk_could_be_met_by_stage_as_well_as_by_residual_disease.
```

{{trial:create-x}} established capecitabine for residual disease. Its triple-negative subgroup
carried a larger effect than the hormone receptor-positive one [@masuda2017]. The control arm of
that trial received neither carboplatin nor pembrolizumab. Its residual disease was therefore
selected by a weaker regimen than the one used today, and the incremental value of capecitabine
after a platinum-containing chemoimmunotherapy backbone has not been measured. {{trial:ea1131}}
then asked whether a platinum would do better and closed early without an answer [[BS-7220]].

{{trial:olympia}} is the second option and it is restricted to germline carriers [@tutt2021;
@garber2026]. Its two entry routes were residual disease after neoadjuvant chemotherapy and stage
after primary surgery. The trial was not designed to separate them. What olaparib is worth
specifically in residual triple-negative disease is therefore an inference from a stratified trial
rather than a result of it.

```caution
Olaparib and capecitabine have never been compared. The preference for olaparib in a germline
carrier rests on a larger randomised population, a longer follow-up, a placebo control and a
contemporary chemotherapy background. Those are reasons to prefer the evidence, not evidence of
superiority. A patient told that olaparib is better should be told the comparison does not exist.
```

The third post-neoadjuvant question is whether the adjuvant pembrolizumab cycles do anything.
{{trial:keynote522}} gave up to nine adjuvant cycles to every patient, including those with a
pathological complete response, so the trial cannot separate the neoadjuvant from the adjuvant
contribution [@schmid2020]. {{trial:geparnuevo}} gave no adjuvant checkpoint blockade at all and
still showed a survival benefit [@loibl2022geparnuevo; @loibl2026geparnuevo]. {{trial:optimice}}
is testing omission after a complete response directly.

Molecular residual disease detection would in principle identify who needs post-neoadjuvant
escalation. Whether acting on a positive result improves outcome is unresolved, and the evidence
including the ctDNA-guided intervention trials is in [[BC-390]].

## BS-4510 Immune-related toxicity in a curative-intent population

The calculation that governs immune toxicity here is not the one used in metastatic disease.

About half the patients in the control arm of {{trial:keynote522}} reached a pathological complete
response without pembrolizumab [@schmid2020]. A permanent endocrinopathy in a patient who would
have been cured by chemotherapy alone is a different event from the same endocrinopathy in someone
with incurable disease. The trial cannot identify which patients those were, which is the whole
difficulty.

The rates are substantial and they are attributable. In {{trial:b59}}, immune-related adverse
events occurred in 27.6% of patients receiving atezolizumab and 11.4% receiving placebo. Grade 3
or higher treatment-emergent adverse events occurred in 75.3% against 73.4%, so almost the entire
difference between the arms was immune-mediated rather than cytotoxic. Discontinuation in the
neoadjuvant phase occurred in 25.5% of the atezolizumab group against 18.8% of the placebo group
[@loibl2026b59]. In {{trial:geparnuevo}}, thyroid dysfunction of any grade was the commonest
immune-related event, in 47% of patients [@loibl2019geparnuevo].

Three structural features of these toxicities, developed in [[BS-4180]], matter most in this
setting. Onset is often delayed and is not dose-related. Reversibility differs by organ, and
endocrinopathy frequently does not reverse. And the same numerical grade carries different urgency
in different organs, which is why the management algorithms branch by organ [@schneider2021].

```practice
Hold the immunotherapy and continue the chemotherapy when an immune-related event is suspected.
Those are separable decisions and treating them as one costs the patient the cytotoxic backbone
that most of the curative effect came from.

Check thyroid function before every cycle. It is the commonest event and the cheapest to find
[@loibl2019geparnuevo].

Steroids for a confirmed grade 2 or higher event, by the organ-specific algorithm, rather than by
grade alone [@schneider2021].

Say before the first dose that a minority of patients acquire a permanent endocrine deficiency,
and that around half of patients in the control arm of the defining trial were cured without the
drug [@schmid2020]. That is the honest frame for consent in a curative setting.

Surveillance does not stop at the last dose. Events occur months after treatment ends, so the
handover to primary care has to carry that instruction explicitly [[BC-650]].
```

## BS-4520 De-escalation questions in the immunotherapy era

Three de-escalation questions are live. Each must clear the margin problem set out in [[BC-740]],
and specifically in [[BS-6370]], before it changes practice.

The first is whether the anthracycline can be removed.

```evidence setting=early subtype=TNBC line=neoadjuvant topic=anthracycline-free sort=year cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,endpoint,result,status caption=Regimens_that_remove_the_anthracycline_from_the_preoperative_phase,_or_defer_it_until_after_surgery._Only_the_last_row_randomises_the_comparison_against_an_outcome.
```

{{trial:neopact}} reached a response rate close to the anthracycline-containing standard with no
anthracycline at all [@sharma2024neopact]. It is single-arm, so that rate has no concurrent
control to be set against. {{trial:scarlet}} is the randomised version of the same question. It
carries an event-free survival endpoint, which is why it is the row that will settle this.

The second is whether adjuvant pembrolizumab adds anything after a complete response.

```evidence setting=early subtype=TNBC line=post-neoadjuvant modality=immunotherapy topic=de-escalation cols=acronym,phase,population,arms,endpoint,status caption=The_only_randomised_test_of_omitting_the_adjuvant_checkpoint_phase_after_a_complete_response.
```

The reason that question is answerable at all is that {{trial:geparnuevo}} achieved a survival
benefit with no adjuvant component [@loibl2022geparnuevo].

The third is whether any patient can omit checkpoint blockade entirely. That requires a predictive
biomarker and [[BS-4530]] explains why there is not one.

```caution
De-escalation in this disease is harder to prove than to propose. The cure rate is high, the
events are relatively few, and a non-inferiority trial with a tolerable margin therefore needs
either a very large sample or very long follow-up. {{trial:neopact}} is a single-arm phase 2 study
reporting a response rate [@sharma2024neopact]. That is a reason to run {{trial:scarlet}}, not a
reason to change practice before it reports.
```

## BS-4530 The missing predictive biomarker

Everyone with stage II or III triple-negative breast cancer receives checkpoint blockade because
no test identifies who will benefit. That is a statement about measurement, not about biology.

PD-L1 does not select in this setting. {{trial:keynote522}} enrolled without a PD-L1 criterion and
established benefit in the unselected population [@schmid2020; @schmid2024k522os]. In metastatic
disease the same class works only above a combined positive score of 10, and loosens to nothing
below it [@cortes2022keynote355]. The assays themselves are not interchangeable, because the
combined positive score and the immune cell score count different cells over different
denominators, and that argument is in [[BS-4130]]. The metastatic trials that do select on the
assay are tabulated in [[BS-7320]].

Tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes are prognostic and reproducibly scored, and they are not
established as predictive of checkpoint benefit [@denkert2018; @loi2019; @salgado2015]. Prognostic
and predictive are different properties, and the difference is defined in [[BS-0970]].

Tumour mutational burden carries tumour-agnostic evidence from trials that included no breast
cohort [@marabelle2020], and a high burden is uncommon in this disease [@barrososousa2020]. The
eligible population is small before the question of whether the inference transfers is reached.

Two exploratory signals from the negative trial are worth naming without overreading. In
{{trial:b59}}, event-free survival appeared heterogeneous across subgroups, with benefit in
patients presenting with clinical lymph node involvement. A messenger RNA-based subset analysis
suggested benefit in basal-like immune-activated tumours [@loibl2026b59]. Both are post hoc
analyses of a trial that missed its primary endpoint.

```interplay target=BC-350
The reason no single marker predicts here is the reason Part VI gives for most biomarker failure
in this disease. Immune response is a property of an organisation rather than of a molecule, and
the spatial phenotypes in [[BS-1940]] separate patients that any percentage computed on bulk
tissue cannot. PD-L1 scored on a block reports abundance and discards arrangement, exactly as a
HER2 percentage does in [[BS-2260]]. Immune pressure also selects among a mixed population in the
way drug pressure does, so the compartment that presents antigen is removed first and the residual
disease is enriched for the compartment that does not. That is why a marker measured before
treatment cannot describe the tumour that treatment leaves behind.
```

The honest close is narrow. Checkpoint blockade added to neoadjuvant chemotherapy improves
survival in stage II and III disease [@schmid2024k522os]. The benefit is not uniform, since a
large trial of the same class missed its endpoint [@loibl2026b59]. No assay in clinical use
identifies who is in which group. Until one exists, the field will keep treating everyone, and the
permanent toxicities in [[BS-4510]] will keep falling on patients who were going to be cured
anyway.
