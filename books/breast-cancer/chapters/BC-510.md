## BS-4460 Risk assessment and treatment selection

Selection here is driven by stage more than by biology, because no biological variable in routine
use changes the regimen.

Stage II and stage III disease is treated with neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy, which is the
population {{trial:keynote522}} enrolled [@schmid2020]. The evidence in [[BS-4470]] is confined to
that population and does not extend below it.

Below stage II the evidence thins fast. The smallest node-negative tumours were excluded from the
trials that define current practice, so treatment there is extrapolation from prognosis rather
than from a randomised result. Node-negative triple-negative tumours with dense lymphocytic
infiltration have a substantially better prognosis without chemotherapy than those without it
[@park2019; @loi2019]. That observation is prognostic. It has not been used prospectively to
withhold treatment, and the scoring convention that makes it reproducible is in [@salgado2015].

What a surgery-first patient actually receives comes from a different generation of trials. It is
worth seeing what they enrolled.

```evidence setting=early subtype=TNBC line=adjuvant topic=adjuvant-backbone sort=year cols=acronym,n,population,arms,result,os caption=The_randomised_trials_that_built_the_adjuvant_chemotherapy_backbone._Most_enrolled_all_comers_before_subtype_was_routinely_recorded,_which_is_the_population_column's_point.
```

Only two of those rows restricted entry by receptor status. Both did so by HER2 rather than by
triple-negative status. The taxane, the schedule and the anthracycline question were all settled
in populations defined by nodal status and tumour size. They were applied to this subtype
afterwards.

The rows agree on more than they are usually credited with. Adding a taxane after doxorubicin and
cyclophosphamide lowered recurrence in both trials that tested it. Shortening the interval between
cycles lowered it again in both trials that tested that. Escalating the doxorubicin dose did
nothing, in the same trial that established the taxane. The backbone was therefore built by adding
agents and by compressing intervals. It was never built by giving more of the same drug.

The rows that look like a disagreement are the ones about removing the anthracycline, and the
disagreement is design rather than drug. {{trial:us-oncology-9735}} set docetaxel and
cyclophosphamide against doxorubicin and cyclophosphamide, a control arm with no taxane in it
[@jones2009]. {{trial:abc-trials}} and {{trial:wsg-planb}} set a docetaxel and cyclophosphamide
regimen against a control containing both a taxane and an anthracycline [@blum2017; @nitz2019].
Those are two different questions sharing one experimental arm. The two later trials then disagree
with each other, and their noninferiority margins are not the same object either. ABC tested a
hazard ratio against a fixed boundary. PlanB tested a difference in five-year disease-free survival
counted in percentage points. A regimen can fail one convention and pass the other on data of the
same shape.

ABC is the row the decision rests on. It enrolled HER2-negative disease specifically, it
prespecified a joint analysis across three trials, and it reported early for futility with the
hazard ratio above the boundary it had to clear. Noninferiority was not declared. That is why an
anthracycline is still given where risk is high, and why leaving one out is a judgement rather
than a result [@geyer2024]. Dose density is where the transfer into this subtype is best
supported, and [[BS-7200]] holds the two trials that anchor it.

What the table cannot show is a triple-negative population. Not one of these trials enrolled the
subtype on its own, and the oldest of them ran before subtype was routinely recorded. Nothing here
was randomised in the presence of carboplatin or of a checkpoint inhibitor, so the regimens the
table settled are now the backbone underneath drugs they never met. PlanB's genomic omission rule
applied to hormone receptor-positive tumours only, so the table holds no test of withholding
chemotherapy on an assay in this disease.

Two things should happen at diagnosis in every patient regardless of stage.

Germline testing should be arranged before the systemic plan is fixed. A BRCA1 or BRCA2 pathogenic
variant changes the post-neoadjuvant option [[BS-4500]], the surgical conversation and the
family's testing, and the strategy is set out in [[BC-070]].

And the neoadjuvant decision should be taken deliberately rather than by default. If the tumour is
large enough or node-positive enough that residual disease would trigger a different adjuvant
regimen, treating first is what makes that regimen available.

This chapter is the decision architecture and the argument. The trial record it rests on is split
between two neighbours. What is given before surgery is in [[BC-860]]. What is given after it is
in [[BC-850]]. Where the two touch, this chapter points forward rather than restating.

## BS-4470 Neoadjuvant chemotherapy with immunotherapy

```story SQ-0370
```

```evidence setting=early subtype=TNBC line=neoadjuvant,adjuvant modality=immunotherapy sort=year cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,endpoint,result,os,status caption=Checkpoint_blockade_in_early_triple-negative_disease,_including_the_two_trials_that_gave_it_only_after_surgery._Read_the_arms_column_for_whether_treatment_continued_past_the_operation.
```

Ten rows and four designs. Six randomised a checkpoint inhibitor added to preoperative
chemotherapy. Two gave one only after surgery. One is a single-arm regimen study. One is still
recruiting.

The agreement is narrower than the class language suggests, and it sits on the time-to-event side.
Among the preoperative trials that have followed patients past surgery, every point estimate for
event-free or overall survival favours the antibody. The response endpoint did not track it.
{{trial:keynote522}} and {{trial:impassion031}} raised response over placebo. {{trial:geparnuevo}}
missed its response endpoint and its survival curves separated anyway. That dissociation is the
finding, and [[BS-4490]] is where it is argued.

Where the rows diverge, the variable is rarely the drug. Atezolizumab appears four times in this
table and gives four answers. {{trial:impassion031}} raised response before surgery.
{{trial:neotrip}} did not. {{trial:b59}} moved event-free survival without clearing its
significance boundary. {{trial:impassion030}} was stopped for futility. What differs between them
is the chemotherapy underneath and whether the primary tumour was still in place. IMpassion031 ran
nab-paclitaxel and then an anthracycline with no platinum [@mittendorf2020]. NeoTRIP ran
carboplatin with nab-paclitaxel and deferred the anthracycline until after surgery
[@gianni2022neotrip]. B-59 ran all three agents and continued treatment past the operation
[@loibl2026b59]. ALEXANDRA gave chemotherapy after surgery to patients who had none before it
[@ignatiadis2025]. Reading any of these as a verdict on atezolizumab means ignoring the columns
that differ.

The preoperative trials also differ in what they were powered to detect. {{trial:geparnuevo}}
randomised 174 patients on a response endpoint. B-59 randomised 1550 on event-free survival. A
response difference that misses in the first and a hazard ratio that misses in the second are not
the same kind of negative. {{trial:ispy2-pembro}} is a third kind again. It estimated response
against a shared adaptively randomised control, with Bayesian graduation, in a HER2-negative
population of which a minority had triple-negative disease [@nanda2020]. That is a screening
result, and what it did was send the drug to phase 3.

{{trial:keynote522}} is the trial the decision rests on, and the reason is structural rather than
numerical. It carries a response endpoint and a time-to-event endpoint inside one randomisation,
it reported overall survival, and its control arm is the chemotherapy the clinic would otherwise
give [@schmid2020; @schmid2022k522efs; @schmid2024k522os]. No other row has all three properties.

GeparNuevo is the row that separates the two halves of the pathway. Durvalumab stopped at surgery,
so whatever it did was done before the operation [@loibl2019geparnuevo; @loibl2022geparnuevo;
@loibl2026geparnuevo]. That separation is a secondary endpoint of a phase 2 trial that missed its
primary response endpoint, and it rests on few events. KEYNOTE-522 cannot make the attribution at
all, because every patient in its experimental arm received the antibody on both sides of the
operation.

Both trials that waited until after surgery were negative on their primary endpoints. They are not
replicates of each other. ALEXANDRA gave atezolizumab with adjuvant chemotherapy to patients
treated by surgery first. {{trial:abrave}} gave avelumab alone to a population that was mostly
residual disease after neoadjuvant chemotherapy [@conte2025abrave]. Different drug, different
population, different relationship to the chemotherapy. The case that the preoperative window is
where the drug works is therefore drawn between trials and never inside one. Nobody has randomised
the same antibody before and after surgery.

The rest of the table carries limits of its own. A-BRAVE's overall survival hazard ratio is
descriptive and its own disease-free survival co-primaries were not met, so it is a survival
signal without the endpoint that would support it. ALEXANDRA stopped at a futility analysis with
overall survival not reported, so it cannot exclude a late difference either way. {{trial:neopact}}
is single-arm and appears here only because its regimen contains pembrolizumab, so its response
rate has no concurrent control [@sharma2024neopact]. {{trial:scarlet}} has not reported.

The table also has no column for checkpoint ligand expression, and one would not help. The assays
and their cut-points differ between these trials. IMpassion031's marker-positive co-primary did
not cross its own significance boundary [@mittendorf2020]. [[BS-4530]] is where that is argued, the priming argument
is in [[BS-4190]], and the trial-by-trial reading is in [[BS-7310]].

```caution
Three trials of the same class in the same disease gave a survival benefit, a missed primary
endpoint and a futility stop. The differences include the agent, the chemotherapy backbone,
whether the tumour was in place, and whether treatment continued after surgery. None of those
variables was isolated. Reading any one of these results as a statement about checkpoint blockade
in triple-negative breast cancer, rather than about the regimen actually tested, overstates what
the evidence supports.
```

## BS-4480 Platinum agents, BRCA status, and repair-directed selection

The platinum question was answered late, and on a backbone that is no longer what the clinic
gives.

```evidence setting=early subtype=TNBC line=neoadjuvant modality=chemo topic=platinum sort=year cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,endpoint,result,status caption=Platinum_before_surgery._Two_of_these_rows_randomised_the_drug_and_two_selected_the_patient_by_a_repair_assay.
```

Three of these rows added carboplatin to an anthracycline and taxane course before surgery, on
three different backbones, and all three raised pathological complete response. That is the most
reproducible response finding in this subtype.

They then diverge on outcome, and the divergence is sample size and trial structure rather than
drug. {{trial:calgb40603}} found no long-term gain from carboplatin at a median follow-up of 7.9
years [@shepherd2022]. It randomised 443 patients on a response endpoint, with a second
randomisation to bevacizumab running through the same factorial [@sikov2015]. {{trial:brightness}}
reported a 4.5-year event-free survival hazard ratio of 0.57 for carboplatin with paclitaxel
against paclitaxel alone [@geyer2022brightness]. {{trial:geparsixto}} reported a disease-free
survival hazard ratio of 0.56 for carboplatin in its triple-negative stratum [@loibl2018geparsixto].
Neither of those two was powered for the endpoint it reported. Three trials reading out on an
endpoint none of them was designed to test is not a disagreement about carboplatin.

{{trial:pearly}} is the row the decision now rests on. It randomised carboplatin against no
carboplatin on a doxorubicin, cyclophosphamide and taxane backbone, in 868 patients, with
event-free survival as the primary endpoint [@kim2026pearly]. Two features make it load-bearing
rather than merely positive. The arms differ in the drug and in nothing else. And it enrolled
patients treated before and after surgery, so the gain it reports does not have to run through
pathological response. Overall survival favoured carboplatin without reaching significance.

The other two rows selected patients rather than randomising the drug against a common control.
{{trial:inform}} randomised germline carriers to cisplatin or to doxorubicin with
cyclophosphamide, and cisplatin did not win [@tung2020a]. That is a comparison of platinum against
an alkylator rather than against nothing, and an alkylator is active in a repair-deficient tumour.
{{trial:tbcrc-030}} randomised cisplatin against paclitaxel in wild-type or untested disease and
asked whether a homologous recombination deficiency score, positive at 33 or above, predicted
response [@mayer2020]. It predicted response to neither drug and showed no interaction with
either. Both trials read out at surgery and neither reports a survival endpoint.

The mechanistic case for germline selection comes from the metastatic setting. {{trial:tnt}}
compared carboplatin with docetaxel in unselected advanced triple-negative disease and found no
difference in response. Among patients with germline BRCA1 or BRCA2 variants, carboplatin produced
double the response rate of docetaxel, at 68% against 33%, with a biomarker-by-treatment
interaction p value of 0.01 [@tutt2018]. Benefit was not seen in patients selected by BRCA1
methylation, by low BRCA1 messenger RNA, or by a high score on a commercial homologous
recombination deficiency assay [@tutt2018]. A germline mutation predicted platinum sensitivity there.
The assays built to capture the same phenotype did not. Before surgery only the second half of
that repeats. INFORM's carriers did no better on cisplatin than on an alkylator, and TBCRC 030's
score predicted response to neither drug.

What the table cannot show is the modern backbone. No row randomised carboplatin on top of a
pembrolizumab-containing regimen. Carboplatin sits inside the {{trial:keynote522}} backbone and
was given to both of its arms, so that trial cannot estimate the platinum contribution either
[@schmid2020]. PEARLY's control carried no checkpoint inhibitor. The value of carboplatin in the
regimen actually used today is therefore held by transfer rather than by a trial. BrighTNess
stratified its randomisation on germline BRCA status and the table has no column for that stratum.
BrighTNess also holds the one randomised PARP question asked before surgery here, and adding
veliparib to carboplatin added nothing to response [@loibl2018brightness].

The biology of homologous recombination deficiency and the difference between a germline mutation,
a functional deficiency and a genomic scar are developed in [[BC-180]], and the assay landscape in
[[BS-3420]]. The practical position is that germline testing changes management and somatic repair
assays do not.

## BS-4490 Pathologic response and residual disease adaptation

```story SQ-0400
```

Pathological complete response in this subtype is the strongest patient-level prognostic marker in
breast oncology, and it is not a valid trial-level surrogate. Both statements come from the same
analysis and both are true.

One pooled analysis covered 12 trials and 11,955 patients. Among those with triple-negative
disease, eradication of tumour from breast and nodes carried an event-free survival hazard ratio
of 0.24, with a 95% confidence interval of 0.18 to 0.33. The overall survival hazard ratio was
0.16, with an interval of 0.11 to 0.25 [@cortazar2014]. The trial-level analysis of the same
dataset ran the other way. The increase in pathological complete response frequency between
treatment groups explained almost none of the variation in event-free survival, with a coefficient
of determination of 0.03. For overall survival that coefficient was 0.24 [@cortazar2014].

The gradient itself was established by {{trial:nsabp-b-18}} and {{trial:nsabp-b-27}}, which
randomised when chemotherapy was given and then what was added to the preoperative course. Both
carried survival as the endpoint and recorded response alongside it. Both enrolled all comers,
before subtype was routinely recorded. The sorting this subtype is now managed by was measured in
a population that did not distinguish it.

Two trials in this chapter raised response and did not move outcome. Carboplatin raised
pathological complete response in {{trial:calgb40603}} and produced no long-term gain
[@shepherd2022]. Nab-paclitaxel with carboplatin raised it over nab-paclitaxel with gemcitabine in
{{trial:wsg-adapt-tn}}, and the invasive disease-free survival hazard ratio for the arm that
produced the higher response rate was 1.04 [@gluz2022]. In that same trial, five-year invasive
disease-free survival was 90.6% in patients who cleared the breast and the axilla and 62.8% in
those who did not. One dataset, two directions. The gradient within an arm is steep and the shift
between arms carries almost nothing.

{{trial:ispy2}} is the design that reads response against outcome across molecular subtypes while
holding one chemotherapy control common to all of them. {{trial:keynote522}} moved both endpoints
inside a single randomisation. That is agreement between two measurements rather than evidence
that the first predicts the second.

What counts as a complete response is a convention, and the trials in this chapter do not share
one. GeparSixto scored it as no residual invasive or in-situ carcinoma in breast and nodes.
WSG-ADAPT-TN and INFORM allowed residual in-situ disease. CALGB 40603 reported the breast alone
and the breast with the axilla as two separate rates, which are two separate numbers. A response
rate is therefore not comparable across trials that define it differently, and eradication from
the breast alone carries a weaker association with outcome than eradication from both
[@cortazar2014]. What the pathologist can report also depends on how the specimen was sampled and
on how the post-chemotherapy axilla was handled.

{{trial:geparnuevo}} is what the surrogacy failure looks like inside a single trial rather than
across a pooled dataset. Its response endpoint was not met and its survival curves separated
regardless. The authors' own analysis puts that separation outside the response effect
[@loibl2022geparnuevo]. Whatever moved the curves reached patients whose tumours were not
eradicated, which is precisely the population a response-mediated mechanism cannot reach.

Residual disease is not one prognosis. It is a spectrum, which is why [[term:rcb]] and the
continuous measures in [[BS-4680]] carry more information than a binary result. Immune context
adds a further axis to that spectrum. Stromal lymphocyte density is prognostic in this subtype and
is scored to a published convention [@denkert2018; @salgado2015]. It is measurable on the residual
disease specimen as well as on the diagnostic core, and the long-term GeparNuevo report reads it
there [@loibl2026geparnuevo]. Those subgroups are small, so the direction is the finding rather
than the size.

Residual disease marks resistance to the regimen that was actually given. It says nothing about a
drug the tumour never met. Every post-neoadjuvant trial in [[BS-4500]] makes that assumption, and
none of them tests it.

```practice
Report response as breast and nodes together. Eradication from the breast alone carries a weaker
association with outcome than eradication from both [@cortazar2014].

Use response to assign the next treatment, not to evaluate the last one. Assigning on observed
response is what [[BS-4500]] rests on, and it is a patient-level use that survives the surrogacy
failure.

When a regimen raises pathological complete response and has no mature survival data, say that the
survival question is open. The trial-level correlation between the two is close to nothing
[@cortazar2014].

Ask for stromal lymphocyte scoring on the residual disease specimen where it is available. It is
prognostic and it costs a slide [@loibl2026geparnuevo; @salgado2015].
```

## BS-4500 Post-neoadjuvant systemic therapy

Residual invasive disease after neoadjuvant treatment is the highest-risk state in early
triple-negative breast cancer, and two randomised options exist.

```evidence setting=early subtype=TNBC,BRCA line=post-neoadjuvant modality=chemo,parp,immunotherapy,adc sort=year cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,endpoint,result,os,status caption=Systemic_therapy_allocated_on_what_surgery_found._Four_of_these_seven_rows_have_not_reported.
```

```evidence setting=early subtype=BRCA line=adjuvant modality=parp cols=acronym,n,population,arms,endpoint,result,os caption=The_germline_option._Its_registry_line_is_adjuvant_because_high_risk_could_be_met_by_stage_as_well_as_by_residual_disease.
```

Three of the seven rows in the first table have reported and one of them changed practice. The
other two are negative about different things, which is the distinction the table cannot draw for
the reader.

{{trial:create-x}} is load-bearing because of its control arm. Patients with residual disease were
randomised against no additional chemotherapy, which is what the clinic would otherwise do. Its
triple-negative subgroup carried a larger effect on both endpoints than the trial as a whole
[@masuda2017]. That subgroup is a subgroup of a trial that enrolled both. The control arm received
neither carboplatin nor pembrolizumab, so its residual disease was selected by a weaker regimen
than the one used today. What capecitabine adds after a platinum-containing chemoimmunotherapy
backbone has not been measured anywhere.

{{trial:ea1131}} is negative about drug choice rather than about treating at all. It set a platinum
against capecitabine in patients who were all going to receive something, and it stopped early at
61% of the information it was designed to accumulate [@mayer2021ea1131]. Both arms did worse than
the design had assumed. A noninferiority trial that closes on a control arm performing below its
own planning assumption cannot rule the experimental arm in or out [[BS-7220]].

{{trial:abrave}} appears in this table because most of its population entered on residual disease.
It gave avelumab after surgery with no preoperative phase, and neither co-primary disease-free
survival endpoint was met [@conte2025abrave]. Its overall survival hazard ratio is descriptive.
The section's other timing evidence is in [[BS-4470]], and the same caution applies here. A
survival separation that the trial's own disease-free endpoint does not support is a reason to run
another trial.

{{trial:olympia}} is the second established option and it is restricted to germline carriers
[@tutt2021; @garber2026]. Its two entry routes were residual disease after neoadjuvant
chemotherapy and stage after primary surgery. The trial was not designed to separate them. What
olaparib is worth specifically in residual triple-negative disease is therefore an inference from
a stratified trial rather than a result of it. Its comparator was placebo, which makes it the
cleanest randomisation in this section and also the least like any comparison a clinic now faces.

```caution
Olaparib and capecitabine have never been compared. The preference for olaparib in a germline
carrier rests on a larger randomised population, a longer follow-up, a placebo control and a
contemporary chemotherapy background. Those are reasons to prefer the evidence, not evidence of
superiority. A patient told that olaparib is better should be told the comparison does not exist.
```

The four rows that have not reported are where this section will change, and three of them are
asking one class question with three different control arms. {{trial:ascent05}} randomises
sacituzumab govitecan with pembrolizumab against pembrolizumab with or without capecitabine
[@tolaney2024optimicerd]. {{trial:tropion-b03}} randomises datopotamab deruxtecan with durvalumab,
and datopotamab deruxtecan alone, against investigator's choice of capecitabine, pembrolizumab or
both [@bardia2024tropionb03]. {{trial:sascia-gbg-102}} randomises sacituzumab govitecan against
physician's choice in a population that includes hormone receptor-positive disease. When they
report they will not be comparable with one another, and the control arms are the reason.

The fourth unreported row is the adjuvant checkpoint phase. {{trial:keynote522}} gave up to nine
adjuvant cycles to every patient in its experimental arm, including those with a pathological
complete response. It cannot separate the neoadjuvant from the adjuvant contribution
[@schmid2020]. {{trial:geparnuevo}} gave no adjuvant checkpoint blockade at all. Its
survival curves separated regardless, as a secondary endpoint of a phase 2 trial that missed its
primary [@loibl2022geparnuevo; @loibl2026geparnuevo]. {{trial:optimice}} is randomising
omission after a complete response directly, and [[BS-4520]] is where that design is read.

Neither table carries a column for the neoadjuvant regimen, and that regimen is the variable that
selected the population in every row. Residual disease after anthracycline and taxane alone is not
the same state as residual disease after carboplatin and pembrolizumab. No reported row tested its
experimental arm on top of the adjuvant pembrolizumab most of these patients now also receive.

Molecular residual disease detection would in principle identify who needs post-neoadjuvant
escalation. Whether acting on a positive result improves outcome is unresolved, and the evidence
including the ctDNA-guided intervention trials is in [[BC-390]].

## BS-4510 Immune-related toxicity in a curative-intent population

The calculation that governs immune toxicity here is not the one used in metastatic disease.

About half the patients in the control arm of {{trial:keynote522}} reached a pathological complete
response without pembrolizumab [@schmid2020]. A permanent endocrinopathy in a patient who would
have been cured by chemotherapy alone is a different event from the same endocrinopathy in someone
with incurable disease. The trial cannot identify which patients those were, which is the whole
difficulty.

What the antibody adds does not show up on the cytotoxic scale. In {{trial:b59}}, grade 3 or
higher treatment-emergent adverse events were 75.3% with atezolizumab and 73.4% with placebo
[@loibl2026b59]. A toxicity table built for cytotoxic drugs therefore reports the two arms as
nearly the same. The events that separate them are a different class with a different natural
history. In {{trial:geparnuevo}}, thyroid dysfunction of any grade occurred in 47% of patients
[@loibl2019geparnuevo].

Three structural features of these toxicities, developed in [[BS-4180]], matter most in this
setting. Onset is often delayed and is not dose-related. Reversibility differs by organ, and
endocrinopathy frequently does not reverse. And the same numerical grade carries different urgency
in different organs, which is why the management algorithms branch by organ [@schneider2021].

```practice
Hold the immunotherapy and continue the chemotherapy when an immune-related event is suspected.
Those are separable decisions and treating them as one costs the patient the cytotoxic backbone
that most of the curative effect came from.

Check thyroid function before every cycle. It is the commonest event and the cheapest to find
[@loibl2019geparnuevo].

Steroids for a confirmed grade 2 or higher event, by the organ-specific algorithm, rather than by
grade alone [@schneider2021].

Say before the first dose that a minority of patients acquire a permanent endocrine deficiency.
Say also that about half the control arm of the defining trial cleared the tumour on chemotherapy
alone [@schmid2020]. That is the honest frame for consent in a curative setting.

Surveillance does not stop at the last dose. Events occur months after treatment ends, so the
handover to primary care has to carry that instruction explicitly [[BC-650]].
```

## BS-4520 De-escalation questions in the immunotherapy era

Three de-escalation questions are live. Each must clear the margin problem set out in [[BC-740]],
and specifically in [[BS-6370]], before it changes practice.

The first is whether the anthracycline can be removed.

```evidence setting=early subtype=TNBC line=neoadjuvant topic=anthracycline-free sort=year cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,endpoint,result,status caption=Regimens_that_remove_the_anthracycline_from_the_preoperative_phase,_or_defer_it_until_after_surgery._Only_the_last_row_randomises_the_comparison_against_an_outcome.
```

Five rows, and anthracycline-free does not mean the same thing in any two of them.
{{trial:neopact}} gives a regimen with no anthracycline in it and has no control arm.
{{trial:neostop}} randomises a regimen without one against a regimen with one. {{trial:neotrip}}
defers it until after surgery, so its patients received one. {{trial:wsg-adapt-tn}} gave twelve
weeks of chemotherapy and permitted everything after that to be omitted only where the tumour had
cleared. Reading the response column straight down compares designs rather than regimens.

On the response rate they agree. A carboplatin and taxane course without an anthracycline reaches
a pathological complete response in the range an anthracycline-containing course reaches. NeoSTOP
is the only row that shows this inside one randomisation. Response and the residual cancer burden
distribution were the same in both arms. Grade 3 or 4 neutropenia was 60% against 8%, in favour of
the anthracycline-free arm [@sharma2020]. NeoSTOP randomised 100 patients and read out at surgery.

Nothing here settles the question. NeoPACT is single-arm, so its event-free survival is a cohort
estimate rather than a comparison [@sharma2024neopact]. {{trial:scarlet}} is the only row that
randomises an anthracycline-free regimen against the KEYNOTE-522 regimen with event-free survival
as its endpoint, and it has not reported.

Three things the table cannot show matter more than any row in it. There is no column for cardiac
injury and none for treatment-related leukaemia. Those are the reasons to remove an anthracycline
at all. Follow-up in the reported rows is too short for the second of them. NeoSTOP and WSG-ADAPT-TN
ran without a checkpoint inhibitor, so neither says what happens when pembrolizumab sits on top of
a shortened backbone. And NeoTRIP randomised atezolizumab rather than the anthracycline, so its
two arms do not differ in the variable this table is about.

The second is whether adjuvant pembrolizumab adds anything after a complete response.

```evidence setting=early subtype=TNBC line=post-neoadjuvant modality=immunotherapy topic=de-escalation cols=acronym,phase,population,arms,endpoint,status caption=The_only_randomised_test_of_omitting_the_adjuvant_checkpoint_phase_after_a_complete_response.
```

One row, and it has not reported. {{trial:optimice}} is also the only de-escalation here that uses
pathological response to allocate treatment rather than to evaluate it, which is the patient-level
use that survives the surrogacy failure in [[BS-4490]]. The reason the question is worth asking at
all is {{trial:geparnuevo}}, whose survival curves separated with no adjuvant component
[@loibl2022geparnuevo]. That was a secondary endpoint of a phase 2 trial that missed its primary.

The third is whether any patient can omit checkpoint blockade entirely. That requires a predictive
biomarker and [[BS-4530]] explains why there is not one.

```caution
De-escalation in this disease is harder to prove than to propose. The cure rate is high, the
events are relatively few, and a non-inferiority trial with a tolerable margin therefore needs
either a very large sample or very long follow-up. Every reported row above read out at surgery on
a response endpoint. A response endpoint cannot support a claim of equivalence for an outcome, on
the argument in [[BS-4490]]. That is a reason to run {{trial:scarlet}}, not a reason to change
practice before it reports.
```

## BS-4530 The missing predictive biomarker

Everyone with stage II or III triple-negative breast cancer receives checkpoint blockade because
no test identifies who will benefit. That is a statement about measurement, not about biology.

PD-L1 does not select in this setting. {{trial:keynote522}} enrolled without a PD-L1 criterion and
established benefit in the unselected population [@schmid2020; @schmid2024k522os]. In metastatic
disease the same class works only above a combined positive score of 10, and loosens to nothing
below it [@cortes2022keynote355]. The assays themselves are not interchangeable, because the
combined positive score and the immune cell score count different cells over different
denominators, and that argument is in [[BS-4130]]. The metastatic trials that do select on the
assay are tabulated in [[BS-7320]].

The two early trials that did look at the ligand show the gap between an association and a
randomised prediction. {{trial:impassion031}} carried a marker-positive co-primary endpoint and it
did not cross its significance boundary [@mittendorf2020]. {{trial:neotrip}} found checkpoint
ligand expression the strongest factor for pathological complete response in its multivariable
analysis, in a trial whose randomised comparison of atezolizumab was null [@gianni2022neotrip]. A
marker associated with response in both arms is prognostic. Only an interaction with treatment
makes it predictive, and neither trial produced one.

Tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes are prognostic and reproducibly scored, and they are not
established as predictive of checkpoint benefit [@denkert2018; @loi2019; @salgado2015]. Prognostic
and predictive are different properties, and the difference is defined in [[BS-0970]].

Tumour mutational burden carries tumour-agnostic evidence from trials that included no breast
cohort [@marabelle2020], and a high burden is uncommon in this disease [@barrososousa2020]. The
eligible population is small before the question of whether the inference transfers is reached.

One exploratory signal from the negative trial is worth naming without overreading. In
{{trial:b59}}, the subgroup analyses pointed to benefit in patients presenting with clinical lymph
node involvement [@loibl2026b59]. That is a subgroup of a trial that missed its primary endpoint,
and nodal status is a stage variable rather than an assay. It cannot serve as the predictive
marker this section is looking for.

```interplay target=BC-350
The reason no single marker predicts here is the reason Part VI gives for most biomarker failure
in this disease. Immune response is a property of an organisation rather than of a molecule, and
the spatial phenotypes in [[BS-1940]] separate patients that any percentage computed on bulk
tissue cannot. PD-L1 scored on a block reports abundance and discards arrangement, exactly as a
HER2 percentage does in [[BS-2260]]. Immune pressure also selects among a mixed population in the
way drug pressure does, so the compartment that presents antigen is removed first and the residual
disease is enriched for the compartment that does not. That is why a marker measured before
treatment cannot describe the tumour that treatment leaves behind.
```

The honest close is narrow. Checkpoint blockade added to neoadjuvant chemotherapy improves
survival in stage II and III disease [@schmid2024k522os]. The benefit is not uniform, since a
large trial of the same class missed its endpoint [@loibl2026b59]. No assay in clinical use
identifies who is in which group. Until one exists, the field will keep treating everyone, and the
permanent toxicities in [[BS-4510]] will keep falling on patients who were going to be cured
anyway.
