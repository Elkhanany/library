## BS-7050 Scope, and why this subtype made the neoadjuvant platform work

```story SQ-0120
```

Almost every methodological advantage of the neoadjuvant setting is larger here than anywhere else
in breast cancer.

The tumour is in place, so response is measured rather than inferred. Response arrives in months
rather than years, at rates high enough for a few hundred patients to separate two regimens. And
response carries more prognostic information in this subtype than in any other. The pooled analysis
of 12 trials and 11,955 patients found that association strongest in HER2-positive, hormone
receptor-negative disease treated with trastuzumab. There the hazard ratio for event-free survival
was 0.15, with an interval of 0.09 to 0.27 [@cortazar2014].

That is why the questions asked in this cell are subtractive ones. Most patients here are cured by
chemotherapy given with HER2 antibodies. Most of what the cure costs them comes from the
chemotherapy rather than from the antibodies. Treating first does not itself add cure, because
randomising the order of the same chemotherapy in operable disease changed neither disease-free nor
overall survival [@fisher1998a]. The reasons to treat first are downstaging of the breast and the
axilla, and information.

The information is the part that changed practice. The specimen grades the response and the grade
routes what follows, because residual invasive disease is the entry criterion for post-neoadjuvant
intensification [[BS-7010]]. Subtraction is therefore testable here in a way it is not after
surgery, because a tumour that fails to respond announces itself before the operation. [[BS-7080]]
records what happens when it announces itself too late.

Two consequences follow from all of this and they pull in opposite directions.

The first is productive, because this is where HER2-directed drug development happens on a signal
that is obtainable quickly and cheaply. Most of the agents in [[BC-450]] and [[BC-460]] were first
tested against this endpoint.

The second is a trap, since a measurement that predicts outcome for an individual patient is not
thereby a measurement that predicts the effect of a drug on outcome. [[BS-7070]] is where that
distinction is worked through on this subtype's own trials. Its general statistical form belongs to
[[BC-740]] and [[BS-6360]], and the decision architecture that brings a patient here at all belongs
to [[BC-500]].

This chapter covers what is given before surgery. What is given afterwards is in [[BC-820]] and
[[BS-7010]]. Hormone receptor-positive HER2-positive disease has a lower response rate and its own
chapter in [[BC-840]], while scoring conventions, including [[term:rcb]], are in [[BS-0790]].

Each section below opens with the question its trials were built to answer, from the
[Chapter Stories](stories.html) map, and closes with a table filtered on that same question. Result
cells come from the publication named in the reference column. Cross-trial comparison is argued in
prose, because the populations and the scoring conventions differ.

## BS-7060 Chemotherapy backbones with trastuzumab

```story SQ-0140
```

NOAH established the principle that matters most and it is easy to forget. Adding trastuzumab to
neoadjuvant chemotherapy improved event-free survival, not only response. The trial ran in locally
advanced and inflammatory disease, where the event rate was high enough to show it in 235 patients
[@gianni2010noah].

The taxane is not contested. The anthracycline is.

TRYPHAENA was designed as a cardiac safety study rather than an efficacy comparison. Its useful
output was that an anthracycline-free regimen with dual blockade produced pathological complete
response rates in the same range as the anthracycline-containing regimens [@schneeweiss2013]. Its
three rates sit beside each other rather than against each other, because the arms were never
compared formally. That is a compatibility result and not a demonstration of equivalence.

TRAIN-2 tested the question with a survival endpoint and this is where the reasoning has to be
careful. Three-year event-free survival was nearly identical between the arms. The confidence
interval around the hazard ratio ran from 0.50 to 1.63 [@vandervoort2021]. That interval excludes
neither a substantial benefit from omitting the anthracycline nor a substantial harm. No
non-inferiority margin was set in advance and the trial was not powered against one.

So the case for omitting the anthracycline does not rest on proven equivalence. It rests on a
toxicity difference measured in the same trial. Ejection fraction fell by 10 points or more to
below 50% in 7.7% of patients given an anthracycline and 3.2% of those who were not. Two patients
in the anthracycline group developed acute leukaemia [@vandervoort2021]. That is the argument, and
[[BS-6370]] explains why it is a better argument than a null result would be.

What should stand in the anthracycline's place is a separate question with a thinner record.
HELEN-006 set weekly nab-paclitaxel with dual blockade against docetaxel and carboplatin with dual
blockade, and the nab-paclitaxel regimen raised response [@chen2024a]. Read that result with two
qualifications. The trial changed the taxane formulation and the carboplatin at once, so it cannot
say which change produced the gain. It also enrolled Asian women only, which fixes the comparator
rather than widening it.

Platinum itself was never tested properly in this subtype. It was added to a taxane and
anthracycline backbone in a trial enrolling both triple-negative and HER2-positive disease. It
raised response in the triple-negative population and added nothing in the HER2-positive stratum. A
formal test of interaction separated the two [@vonminckwitz2014]. That stratum was not what the
trial was powered for, so the negative result there is weak evidence rather than none.

```practice
Under dual blockade, treat anthracycline omission as the default question rather than the exception.
The toxicity evidence is randomised and the efficacy evidence shows no signal of loss.

Do not describe the omission as proven non-inferior, but as a trade of an unmeasured and probably
small efficacy risk against a measured cardiac and leukaemogenic one.

Keep the anthracycline in mind for very high-volume nodal disease, where the event rate is high and
the confidence interval in TRAIN-2 is least reassuring.
```

## BS-7070 Dual blockade and what the pathological complete response gains bought

```story SQ-0130
```

Adding a second drug to trastuzumab on a taxane raises pathological complete response, but not
every second drug and not reliably.

Pertuzumab does, in the trial that established the pair and in the trial that repeated it in an
Asian population. Pyrotinib does, against placebo on the same backbone. Lapatinib is inconsistent,
having raised response in one trial, missed significance in a second and missed it again in a
third. Adding a checkpoint inhibitor did not raise response in either of its co-primary
populations. The one trial that replaced trastuzumab with an Fc-engineered antibody is reported not
to have raised it either, but only in conference and registry material, so it sits on neither side
of the question [[BS-7110]].

Before comparing any of those rates, compare what was scored. Some of these trials required only
the breast to be clear. Others required the nodes as well. The backbones differ too, from docetaxel
alone to paclitaxel after an anthracycline to a dose-dense anthracycline followed by paclitaxel.
Rates from different definitions on different backbones are not on one axis, and they are routinely
set side by side as though they were.

Now follow the response gains to their outcomes, because the outcomes are the reason this section
exists.

NeoSphere was followed for five years. Its response difference was close to seventeen percentage
points. The progression-free survival difference that followed was about five points, with a hazard
ratio of 0.69 and an interval from 0.34 to 1.40 [@gianni2016]. The trial had roughly 107 patients
per arm, so it could never have measured an outcome difference of that size. That is precisely the
difficulty, because the trial that generates a response difference is never the trial that can
price it.

NeoALTTO is the cleaner case, because its drug pair was then tested adjuvantly at scale. ALTTO
randomised more than eight thousand patients and returned a disease-free survival hazard ratio of
0.84 against trastuzumab alone, which missed its significance threshold [@piccart2016altto]. The
neoadjuvant response gain was the larger of the two described above, and the outcome gain was the
smaller, so the ordering reversed.

CALGB 40601 is the same failure running the other way, and it is the more uncomfortable case. Its
primary response comparison did not reach significance [@carey2016]. Its survival comparison, from
the same randomisation and at more than seven years, did. Relapse-free and overall survival both
favoured the addition of lapatinib [@fernandezmartinez2020]. A reader who had stopped at the
pathological endpoint would have concluded that the drug did nothing, and would have been wrong in
the direction that costs patients most.

Put the three together and the position is clear. A response gain can overstate an outcome gain, as
in the lapatinib adjuvant programme. It can also understate one, as in the same drug's own
neoadjuvant trial. The endpoint is not biased in a fixed direction, which is what makes correcting
for it impossible.

The general statement of this is trial-level surrogacy and it has been measured. Across 12 trials,
the correlation between a treatment's increase in pathological complete response and its effect on
event-free survival had a coefficient of determination of 0.03. For overall survival the
coefficient was 0.24, and the authors judged neither value sufficient to validate the endpoint
[@cortazar2014]. [[BS-6360]] sets out why that is the quantity a surrogate has to satisfy.

```caution
None of this argues against dual blockade, since pertuzumab produces a real adjuvant benefit in
node-positive disease [[BS-6990]] and the neoadjuvant response gain pointed in the right direction.
What fails is the inference from the size of a response gain to the size of an outcome gain. A
regimen that raises pathological complete response by 20 points has not thereby been shown to
improve survival by any particular amount. In the lapatinib adjuvant trial it improved disease-free
survival by an amount that trial could not distinguish from zero.
```

The clinical consequence is narrow and worth stating, because a neoadjuvant trial reporting a
response improvement licenses a hypothesis about outcome. It licenses a change of regimen only
where response itself changes the next treatment, which in this subtype it does [[BS-7010]].

## BS-7080 Chemotherapy-free and de-escalated neoadjuvant regimens

```story SQ-0150
```

Three different things get called de-escalation here and they carry different risks. Removing the
anthracycline is [[BS-7060]], removing all conventional cytotoxic chemotherapy is this section, and
removing treatment after a response is [[BS-7090]].

The idea is not unreasonable. In the trial that established the antibody pair, the arm given both
antibodies without a taxane still produced complete responses in a minority of patients. If those
tumours could be identified in advance, the cytotoxic drug would be going to them for nothing.

The record since then says three things. Chemotherapy-free blockade produces complete responses in
a minority. That minority is larger with a longer window than a shorter one, larger in oestrogen
receptor-negative than in receptor-positive disease, and larger in tumours called HER2-enriched by
an intrinsic subtype call [@llombart2017]. None of that is a comparison against chemotherapy,
because most of these trials had no chemotherapy arm.

One trial did test omission formally, and it failed. WSG-ADAPT HER2+/HR- randomised twelve weeks of
dual blockade with or without weekly paclitaxel, and non-inferiority was not shown [@nitz2017a].
Read its design before its rate, because the test set early responders in the chemotherapy-free arm
against every patient in the taxane arm. That compares a selected group with an unselected one, in
hormone receptor-negative disease only, and the taxane still won.

Duration is the one variable within chemotherapy-free blockade that has been randomised. Doubling
the window from twelve weeks to twenty-four raised the rate, driven by the oestrogen
receptor-positive subgroup [@rimawi2019]. That trial was built as a pick-the-winner design and was
explicitly not powered to compare the two windows. The gap between them was therefore never tested.
These trials also used lapatinib with trastuzumab rather than the antibody pair a clinic now gives,
which is why their rates do not transfer.

KRISTINE is the trial that shows what removing chemotherapy costs when the substitute is a
conjugate rather than nothing. Its three-year follow-up showed something more specific than a lower
response rate. The hazard ratio for an event-free survival event was 2.61 against the conjugate,
with an interval from 1.36 to 4.98. Fifteen patients on the conjugate had locoregional progression
before surgery, against none on chemotherapy [@hurvitz2019kristine]. Once surgery had happened,
invasive disease-free survival was similar between the arms.

That is a failure mode peculiar to the neoadjuvant window. The tumour is still in the patient, so
an underpowered regimen does not merely fail to shrink it. It permits local progression during the
months the regimen is running. A de-escalation that would be acceptable after surgery is not
automatically acceptable before it.

PHERGain-2 removed chemotherapy entirely from the preoperative phase and reported a high response
rate. Read the eligibility before the rate, since entry required immunohistochemistry 3+,
node-negative disease, and a tumour of 5 to 30 mm on magnetic resonance imaging [@garrigos2026].
That is a population selected for exactly the features that predict response. There is no
comparator arm and the efficacy coprimary endpoint has not reported.

The equivalent question in hormone receptor-positive HER2-positive disease was tested directly by
{{trial:wsg-tpii}}. Replacing the taxane with endocrine therapy more than halved the response rate
[@tpii2026]. That result belongs to [[BC-840]] and [[BS-7160]].

```caution
Single-arm response rates from selected populations are not comparable across trials, and they are
routinely compared. A 60% rate in immunohistochemistry 3+ node-negative disease and a 60% rate in
unselected stage II to III disease describe different achievements, so compare eligibility criteria
before comparing numbers.
```

## BS-7090 Response-adapted strategies and imaging-guided escalation

```story SQ-0160
```

Response-adapted designs differ in when they look, and the timing decides what they can claim.

PHERGain looks early, using metabolic imaging after two cycles to decide whether chemotherapy is
added. That is escalation on a surrogate of a surrogate. Metabolic response at six weeks is not
pathological response at surgery, and the two are separate measurements with separate error.

How separate has been measured. TBCRC026 tested formally whether the metabolic change early in the
first cycle predicts pathological complete response, against a prespecified accuracy threshold. The
area under the curve was 0.72, which did not reject a null of 0.65, so the primary objective was not
met [@connolly2021a]. The signal underneath it was real, since tumours without a substantial
metabolic fall rarely reached a complete response. A real signal that misses a prespecified
accuracy bar is not a licence to route treatment on it.

What PHERGain established should be stated precisely, because it is usually overstated. The
three-year rate in the response-adapted group was measured against a fixed threshold rather than
against a randomised control, and the chemotherapy comparator group against which it is now read
contained 71 patients [@perezgarcia2024]. Grade 3 or higher treatment-related adverse events
occurred in 33% of the response-adapted group against 62% of the chemotherapy group. That is a
promising strategy with a real toxicity advantage. It is not an established equivalence, and
[[BS-6370]] sets out the difference.

Late adaptation is the safer architecture, because the tumour is already out when the decision is
made. It also inherits the strongest evidence in the subtype, since the same logic run in the other
direction is what licenses post-neoadjuvant intensification [[BS-7010]].

Three trials adapt late and each answers a different question. TRAIN-3 stopped chemotherapy when
magnetic resonance imaging showed a complete radiological response, and reported event-free
survival with no comparator arm [@louis2026]. DAPHNe withheld adjuvant chemotherapy after a
pathological complete response and measured whether clinicians and patients would accept that,
which they did [@waks2022]. CompassHER2 pCR is the largest test, with more than two thousand
patients given a taxane with trastuzumab and pertuzumab and chemotherapy omitted after a complete
response [@tung2026].

Two features of the CompassHER2 result deserve attention before the recurrence endpoint arrives.
Response in oestrogen receptor-positive disease was about half the rate seen in receptor-negative
disease, so the route out of chemotherapy opens least often in the group where it is most wanted
[[BS-7140]]. And the design can report only the recurrence rate in the patients from whom
chemotherapy was withheld. It cannot report what would have happened to those same patients had the
chemotherapy been given, because nobody was randomised to receive it.

```practice
Use response-adapted de-escalation inside a trial where one is open. Outside one, say that the
supporting evidence is single-arm and that the omitted treatment has no comparator.

Distinguish the two adaptation points in conversation, since imaging-guided omission during
treatment carries a progression risk that pathology-guided omission after surgery does not
[[BS-3590]].

Do not let a metabolic response report substitute for a pathology report. The decision rules in
[[BS-7010]] act on the specimen.
```

## BS-7100 Conjugates in the neoadjuvant setting

```story SQ-0170
```

Three conjugate trials sit in this cell and they gave different answers. The difference is not the
conjugate but what each conjugate was asked to replace.

KRISTINE asked a conjugate to replace chemotherapy and the response rate fell [[BS-7080]].
DESTINY-Breast11 asked a newer conjugate to replace the anthracycline component while keeping the
taxane and both antibodies, and response rose against the dose-dense anthracycline sequence. The
same trial also gave the conjugate alone, without the taxane and antibodies, and that arm was
clearly inferior to the anthracycline sequence [@harbeck2026db11].

Those three arms make one statement, which is that a conjugate is an addition to the taxane and
antibody backbone rather than a substitute for it. The payload delivers cytotoxic chemistry to
antigen-positive cells and to their immediate neighbours, and the reasons that is not the same as
systemic chemotherapy are in [[BS-4030]] and [[BS-4060]].

Part of the shortfall when a conjugate replaces chemotherapy has a mechanism. One prospective study
biopsied more than one site in each tumour before treatment with a conjugate and dual blockade.
Tumours carrying regions without the target essentially did not respond. Tumours uniform for it
responded well [@filho2021]. Those groups were defined in advance and not randomised. Heterogeneous
tumours were a small minority, so the rate in that group rests on very few patients.

The toxicity comparison in DESTINY-Breast11 favoured the conjugate sequence and this is the
strongest part of the result. Grade 3 or higher adverse events occurred in 37.5% of the conjugate
sequence against 55.8% of the anthracycline sequence, and all-grade left ventricular dysfunction in
1.3% against 6.1%. Adjudicated drug-related interstitial lung disease was similar across the arms,
at 4.4% and 5.1% [@harbeck2026db11].

```caution
Event-free survival in DESTINY-Breast11 was 4.5% mature at the reported analysis, with a hazard
ratio of 0.56 and an interval from 0.26 to 1.17 [@harbeck2026db11]. Everything in [[BS-7070]]
applies to that pairing, in both directions. The response difference is measured, while the outcome
difference remains a hypothesis with a wide interval. It is the outcome that justifies exposing a
curable patient to a conjugate.
```

## BS-7110 Unresolved questions and what reads out next

Both awaited trials test omission rather than addition, and each will settle less than its size
suggests.

CompassHER2 pCR will report recurrence-free survival in patients who achieved a pathological
complete response and then had chemotherapy omitted [@tung2026]. A low rate will make omission
defensible without measuring the loss, because there is no arm that kept the chemotherapy.

PHERGain-2 will report a recurrence-free interval after a fully chemotherapy-free preoperative
phase in a selected population, where the same limitation applies with a smaller sample
[@garrigos2026].

Five further questions have no trial designed to settle them.

Whether the response advantage in DESTINY-Breast11 becomes an event-free survival advantage. That
requires follow-up rather than a new trial, and the current maturity is too low to support a
decision [[BS-7100]].

Whether the Fc-engineered antibody comparison ever reports. It exists as conference and registry
material with no primary publication, so it currently sits on neither side of the antibody
question.

Whether any assay selects patients for chemotherapy omission better than clinical features do.
Genomic response predictors are being reported alongside these trials [@tung2026], and none has
been validated prospectively as a tool for assigning treatment.

Whether omission is safe in the population where it is most tempting. Hormone receptor-positive
HER2-positive tumours respond least often and are the group in which a chemotherapy-free regimen is
most often proposed [[BS-7140]].

Whether molecular residual disease after neoadjuvant therapy adds to pathology in deciding what
comes next. The assay and its lead-time problem are in [[BS-3480]] and [[BS-3490]].

```interplay target=BC-280
This chapter treats response as a property of a tumour and a drug. Part VI argues it is also a
property of a population of cells with unequal antigen. A tumour scored HER2-positive on one core
may contain a compartment that is not, and that compartment is what survives to become residual
disease [[BS-2330]]. Read that way, the response rates in these tables are partly measurements of
how uniform each enrolled population happened to be. That is not speculation here, because the one
trial that biopsied several sites per tumour found response and antigen uniformity tracking each
other [@filho2021]. It also explains why a bystander-capable payload changes the arithmetic in
[[BS-7100]] and a receptor-blocking antibody does not.
```
