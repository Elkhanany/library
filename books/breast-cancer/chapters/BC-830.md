## BS-7050 Scope, and why this subtype made the neoadjuvant platform work

Almost every methodological advantage of the neoadjuvant setting is larger here than anywhere else
in breast cancer.

The tumour is in place, so response is measured rather than inferred. Response arrives in months
rather than years, at rates high enough for a few hundred patients to separate two regimens. And
response carries more prognostic information in this subtype than in any other. The pooled analysis
of 12 trials and 11,955 patients found that association strongest in HER2-positive, hormone
receptor-negative disease treated with trastuzumab. There the hazard ratio for event-free survival
was 0.15, with an interval of 0.09 to 0.27 [@cortazar2014].

Two consequences follow and they pull in opposite directions.

The first is productive, because this is where HER2-directed drug development happens on a signal
that is obtainable quickly and cheaply. Most of the agents in [[BC-450]] and [[BC-460]] were first
tested against this endpoint.

The second is a trap, since a measurement that predicts outcome for an individual patient is not
thereby a measurement that predicts the effect of a drug on outcome. [[BS-7070]] is where that
distinction is worked through, and the statistical form of it is in [[BS-6360]].

This chapter covers what is given before surgery. What is given afterwards, including the
post-neoadjuvant intensification that makes the whole strategy worth pursuing, is in [[BC-820]] and
[[BS-7010]]. Hormone receptor-positive HER2-positive disease has a lower response rate and its own
chapter in [[BC-840]], while scoring conventions, including [[term:rcb]], are in [[BS-0790]].

Read the tables as generated from the trial registry. Result cells come from the publication named
in the reference column. Cross-trial comparison is argued in prose, because the populations differ.

## BS-7060 Chemotherapy backbones with trastuzumab

```evidence setting=early subtype=HER2+ line=neoadjuvant topic=anthracycline sort=year cols=acronym,n,population,arms,endpoint,result,os caption=Neoadjuvant_chemotherapy_backbones_and_the_anthracycline_question
```

NOAH established the principle that matters most and it is easy to forget. Adding trastuzumab to
neoadjuvant chemotherapy improved event-free survival, not only response. The trial ran in locally
advanced and inflammatory disease, where the event rate was high enough to show it in 235 patients
[@gianni2010noah].

The taxane is not contested. The anthracycline is.

TRYPHAENA was designed as a cardiac safety study rather than an efficacy comparison. Its useful
output was that an anthracycline-free regimen with dual blockade produced pathological complete
response rates in the same range as the anthracycline-containing regimens [@schneeweiss2013]. That
is a compatibility result, not a demonstration of equivalence.

TRAIN-2 tested the question with a survival endpoint and this is where the reasoning has to be
careful. Three-year event-free survival was nearly identical between the arms. The confidence
interval around the hazard ratio ran from 0.50 to 1.63 [@vandervoort2021]. That interval excludes
neither a substantial benefit from omitting the anthracycline nor a substantial harm. No
non-inferiority margin was set in advance and the trial was not powered against one.

So the case for omitting the anthracycline does not rest on proven equivalence. It rests on a
toxicity difference measured in the same trial. Ejection fraction fell by 10 points or more to below
50% in 7.7% of patients given an anthracycline and 3.2% of those who were not. Two patients in the
anthracycline group developed acute leukaemia [@vandervoort2021]. That is the argument, and
[[BS-6370]] explains why it is a better argument than a null result would be.

```practice
Under dual blockade, treat anthracycline omission as the default question rather than the exception.
The toxicity evidence is randomised and the efficacy evidence shows no signal of loss.

Do not describe the omission as proven non-inferior, but as a trade of an unmeasured and probably
small efficacy risk against a measured cardiac and leukaemogenic one.

Keep the anthracycline in mind for very high-volume nodal disease, where the event rate is high and
the confidence interval in TRAIN-2 is least reassuring.
```

## BS-7070 Dual blockade and what the pathological complete response gains bought

```evidence setting=early subtype=HER2+ line=neoadjuvant topic=dual sort=year cols=acronym,n,arms,endpoint,result,os caption=Neoadjuvant_dual_HER2_blockade_and_the_response_gains_it_produced
```

Two antibodies against different epitopes of the same receptor raise pathological complete response
reliably. NeoSphere raised it by 16.8 percentage points over trastuzumab with docetaxel. NeoALTTO
raised it by 21.1 points over trastuzumab with paclitaxel, and both differences were large as well
as statistically significant.

Now follow each one to its outcome, because the outcomes are the reason this section exists.

NeoSphere was followed for five years. Progression-free survival was 86% in the dual blockade group
and 81% in the trastuzumab group, with a hazard ratio of 0.69 and an interval from 0.34 to 1.40
[@gianni2016]. A response difference of nearly 17 percentage points was followed by an outcome
difference of about 5 points, in an estimate compatible with no benefit at all. The trial had
roughly 107 patients per arm, so it could never have measured an outcome difference of that size.
That is precisely the difficulty, because the trial that generates a response difference is never
the trial that can price it.

NeoALTTO is the cleaner case, because its drug pair was then tested adjuvantly at scale. ALTTO
randomised more than eight thousand patients and returned a disease-free survival hazard ratio of
0.84 against trastuzumab alone, which missed its significance threshold [@piccart2016altto]. The
neoadjuvant response gain was the larger of the two described above, and the outcome gain was the
smaller, so the ordering reversed.

The general statement of this is trial-level surrogacy and it has been measured. Across 12 trials,
the correlation between a treatment's increase in pathological complete response and its effect on
event-free survival had a coefficient of determination of 0.03. For overall survival the coefficient
was 0.24, and the authors judged neither value sufficient to validate the endpoint [@cortazar2014].
[[BS-6360]] sets out why that is the quantity a surrogate has to satisfy.

```caution
None of this argues against dual blockade, since pertuzumab produces a real adjuvant benefit in
node-positive disease [[BS-6990]] and the neoadjuvant response gain pointed in the right direction.
What fails is the inference from the size of a response gain to the size of an outcome gain. A
regimen that raises pathological complete response by 20 points has not thereby been shown to
improve survival by any particular amount. In the lapatinib case it improved survival by an amount
no trial could distinguish from zero.
```

The clinical consequence is narrow and worth stating, because a neoadjuvant trial reporting a
response improvement licenses a hypothesis about outcome. It licenses a change of regimen only where
response itself changes the next treatment, which in this subtype it does [[BS-7010]].

## BS-7080 Chemotherapy-free and de-escalated neoadjuvant regimens

```evidence setting=early subtype=HER2+ line=neoadjuvant topic=chemotherapy-free sort=year cols=acronym,n,population,arms,endpoint,result,status caption=Neoadjuvant_regimens_that_remove_conventional_cytotoxic_chemotherapy
```

Three different things get called de-escalation here and they carry different risks. Removing the
anthracycline is [[BS-7060]], removing all conventional cytotoxic chemotherapy is this section, and
removing treatment after a response is [[BS-7090]].

KRISTINE is the trial that shows what the middle option costs. Substituting a conjugate for
chemotherapy lowered pathological complete response. The three-year follow-up showed something more
specific and more useful. The hazard ratio for an event-free survival event was 2.61 against the
conjugate, with an interval from 1.36 to 4.98. Fifteen patients on the conjugate had locoregional
progression before surgery, against none on chemotherapy [@hurvitz2019kristine]. Once surgery had
happened, invasive disease-free survival was similar between the arms.

That is a failure mode peculiar to the neoadjuvant window. The tumour is still in the patient, so an
underpowered regimen does not merely fail to shrink it. It permits local progression during the
months the regimen is running. A de-escalation that would be acceptable after surgery is not
automatically acceptable before it.

PHERGain-2 removed chemotherapy entirely from the preoperative phase and reported a high response
rate. Read the eligibility before the rate, since entry required immunohistochemistry 3+,
node-negative disease, and a tumour of 5 to 30 mm on magnetic resonance imaging [@garrigos2026].
That is a population selected for exactly the features that predict response. There is no comparator
arm and the efficacy coprimary endpoint has not reported.

The equivalent question in hormone receptor-positive HER2-positive disease was tested directly by
{{trial:wsg-tpii}}, where replacing the taxane with endocrine therapy more than halved the response
rate, a result that belongs to [[BC-840]] and [[BS-7160]] [@tpii2026].

```caution
Single-arm response rates from selected populations are not comparable across trials, and they are
routinely compared. A 60% rate in immunohistochemistry 3+ node-negative disease and a 60% rate in
unselected stage II to III disease describe different achievements, so compare eligibility criteria
before comparing numbers.
```

## BS-7090 Response-adapted strategies and imaging-guided escalation

```evidence setting=early subtype=HER2+ line=neoadjuvant topic=response-adapted sort=year cols=acronym,n,population,arms,endpoint,result,status caption=Response-adapted_neoadjuvant_strategies_in_HER2-positive_disease
```

Response-adapted designs differ in when they look, and the timing decides what they can claim.

PHERGain looks early, using metabolic imaging after two cycles to decide whether chemotherapy is
added. That is escalation on a surrogate of a surrogate. Metabolic response at six weeks is not
pathological response at surgery, and the two are separate measurements with separate error.

What PHERGain established should be stated precisely, because it is usually overstated. The
three-year rate in the response-adapted group was measured against a fixed threshold rather than
against a randomised control, and the chemotherapy comparator group against which it is now read
contained 71 patients [@perezgarcia2024]. Grade 3 or higher treatment-related adverse events
occurred in 33% of the response-adapted group against 62% of the chemotherapy group. That is a
promising strategy with a real toxicity advantage. It is not an established equivalence, and
[[BS-6370]] sets out the difference.

PHERGain-2 and CompassHER2 pCR look late, at the surgical specimen. Late adaptation is the safer
architecture, because the tumour is already out when the decision is made. It also inherits the
strongest evidence in the subtype, since the same logic run in the other direction is what licenses
post-neoadjuvant intensification [[BS-7010]].

CompassHER2 pCR is the largest test of this, with more than two thousand patients given a taxane
with trastuzumab and pertuzumab and chemotherapy omitted after a pathological complete response
[@tung2026]. Note what its design can and cannot deliver, because it will report only the recurrence
rate in the patients from whom chemotherapy was withheld. It cannot report what would have happened
to those same patients had the chemotherapy been given, because nobody was randomised to receive it.

```practice
Use response-adapted de-escalation inside a trial where one is open. Outside one, say that the
supporting evidence is single-arm and that the omitted treatment has no comparator.

Distinguish the two adaptation points in conversation, since imaging-guided omission during
treatment carries a progression risk that pathology-guided omission after surgery does not
[[BS-3590]].

Do not let a metabolic response report substitute for a pathology report. The decision rules in
[[BS-7010]] act on the specimen.
```

## BS-7100 Conjugates in the neoadjuvant setting

```evidence setting=early subtype=HER2+ line=neoadjuvant modality=adc sort=year cols=acronym,n,arms,endpoint,result,os caption=Antibody-drug_conjugates_tested_before_surgery_in_HER2-positive_disease
```

Two conjugates have been tested preoperatively and they gave opposite answers. The difference is not
the conjugate but what each conjugate was asked to replace.

KRISTINE asked a conjugate to replace chemotherapy and the response rate fell [[BS-7080]].
DESTINY-Breast11 asked a conjugate to replace the anthracycline component while keeping the taxane
and both antibodies, and response rose against the dose-dense anthracycline sequence. The same trial
also gave the conjugate alone, without the taxane and antibodies, and that arm was clearly inferior
to the anthracycline sequence [@harbeck2026db11].

Those three arms make one statement, which is that a conjugate is an addition to the taxane and
antibody backbone rather than a substitute for it. The payload delivers cytotoxic chemistry to
antigen-positive cells and to their immediate neighbours, and the reasons that is not the same as
systemic chemotherapy are in [[BS-4030]] and [[BS-4060]].

The toxicity comparison favoured the conjugate sequence and this is the strongest part of the
result. Grade 3 or higher adverse events occurred in 37.5% of the conjugate sequence against 55.8%
of the anthracycline sequence, and all-grade left ventricular dysfunction in 1.3% against 6.1%.
Adjudicated drug-related interstitial lung disease was similar across the arms, at 4.4% and 5.1%
[@harbeck2026db11].

```caution
Event-free survival in DESTINY-Breast11 was 4.5% mature at the reported analysis, with a hazard
ratio of 0.56 and an interval from 0.26 to 1.17 [@harbeck2026db11]. Everything in [[BS-7070]]
applies to that pairing. The response difference is measured, while the outcome difference remains a
hypothesis with a wide interval. It is the outcome that justifies exposing a curable patient to a
conjugate.
```

## BS-7110 Unresolved questions and what reads out next

```evidence setting=early subtype=HER2+ line=neoadjuvant status=ongoing,awaited cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,endpoint,status caption=Neoadjuvant_HER2_trials_whose_efficacy_endpoints_have_not_reported
```

Both awaited trials test omission rather than addition, and each will settle less than its size
suggests.

CompassHER2 pCR will report recurrence-free survival in patients who achieved a pathological
complete response and then had chemotherapy omitted [@tung2026]. A low rate will make omission
defensible without measuring the loss, because there is no arm that kept the chemotherapy.

PHERGain-2 will report a recurrence-free interval after a fully chemotherapy-free preoperative phase
in a selected population, where the same limitation applies with a smaller sample [@garrigos2026].

Four further questions have no trial designed to settle them.

Whether the response advantage in DESTINY-Breast11 becomes an event-free survival advantage. That
requires follow-up rather than a new trial, and the current maturity is too low to support a
decision [[BS-7100]].

Whether any assay selects patients for chemotherapy omission better than clinical features do.
Genomic response predictors are being reported alongside these trials [@tung2026], and none has
been validated prospectively as a tool for assigning treatment.

Whether omission is safe in the population where it is most tempting. Hormone receptor-positive
HER2-positive tumours respond least often and are the group in which a chemotherapy-free regimen is
most often proposed [[BS-7140]].

Whether molecular residual disease after neoadjuvant therapy adds to pathology in deciding what
comes next. The assay and its lead-time problem are in [[BS-3480]] and [[BS-3490]].

```interplay target=BC-280
This chapter treats response as a property of a tumour and a drug. Part VI argues it is also a
property of a population of cells with unequal antigen. A tumour scored HER2-positive on one core
may contain a compartment that is not, and that compartment is what survives to become residual
disease [[BS-2330]]. Read that way, the response rates in these tables are partly measurements of
how uniform each enrolled population happened to be. It also explains why a bystander-capable
payload changes the arithmetic in [[BS-7100]] and a receptor-blocking antibody does not.
```
