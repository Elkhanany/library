## BS-7050 Scope, and why this subtype made the neoadjuvant platform work

```story SQ-0120
```

Almost every methodological advantage of the neoadjuvant setting is larger here than anywhere else
in breast cancer.

The tumour is in place, so response is measured rather than inferred. Response arrives in months
rather than years, at rates high enough for a few hundred patients to separate two regimens. And
response carries more prognostic information in this subtype than in any other. The pooled analysis
of 12 trials and 11,955 patients found that association strongest in HER2-positive, hormone
receptor-negative disease treated with trastuzumab. There the hazard ratio for event-free survival
was 0.15, with an interval of 0.09 to 0.27 [@cortazar2014].

That is why the questions asked in this cell are subtractive ones. Most patients here are cured by
chemotherapy given with HER2 antibodies. Most of what the cure costs them comes from the
chemotherapy rather than from the antibodies. Treating first does not itself add cure, because
randomising the order of the same chemotherapy in operable disease changed neither disease-free nor
overall survival [@fisher1998a]. That trial predates HER2 testing and used chemotherapy nobody now
gives, so it licenses treating first in general rather than this backbone in particular. The
reasons to treat first are downstaging of the breast and the axilla, and information.

The information is the part that changed practice. The specimen grades the response and the grade
routes what follows, because residual invasive disease is the entry criterion for post-neoadjuvant
intensification [[BS-7010]]. Subtraction is therefore testable here in a way it is not after
surgery, because a tumour that fails to respond announces itself before the operation. [[BS-7080]]
records what happens when it announces itself too late.

Two consequences follow and they pull in opposite directions.

The first is productive. This is where HER2-directed drug development happens on a signal that is
obtainable quickly and cheaply. Most of the agents in [[BC-450]] and [[BC-460]] were first tested
against this endpoint.

The second is a trap. A measurement that predicts outcome for an individual patient is not thereby
a measurement that predicts the effect of a drug on outcome. [[BS-7070]] works that distinction
through on this subtype's own trials. Its general statistical form belongs to [[BC-740]] and
[[BS-6360]], and the decision architecture that brings a patient here at all belongs to [[BC-500]].

This chapter covers what is given before surgery. What is given afterwards is in [[BC-820]] and
[[BS-7010]]. Hormone receptor-positive HER2-positive disease has a lower response rate and its own
chapter in [[BC-840]], while scoring conventions, including [[term:rcb]], are in [[BS-0790]].

Each section below opens with the question its trials were built to answer, from the
[Chapter Stories](stories.html) map, and closes with a table filtered on that same question. Result
cells come from the publication named in the reference column. Cross-trial comparison is argued in
prose, because the populations and the scoring conventions differ.

## BS-7060 Chemotherapy backbones with trastuzumab

```story SQ-0140
```

Five trials sit in this table and only one of them was built to answer the question in the heading.

Start with what is not in dispute. {{trial:noah}} is the only row whose primary endpoint was
survival rather than the specimen. Adding trastuzumab to an anthracycline and taxane backbone
improved event-free survival against the same chemotherapy alone [@gianni2010noah]. It enrolled
locally advanced and inflammatory disease, where the event rate was high enough to show that. Every
other row argues about what to put beside the antibody rather than about whether to give it.

The anthracycline is the contested part, and the rows disagree about it for reasons of design
rather than of drug.

{{trial:train2}} is load-bearing, because it varied the anthracycline and nothing else on a fixed
taxane, carboplatin and dual-blockade backbone, then followed patients for event-free survival
[@vandervoort2021]. Read its interval rather than its point estimate. The hazard ratio for an
event-free survival event ran from 0.50 to 1.63. That excludes neither a substantial benefit from
omitting the anthracycline nor a substantial harm. No non-inferiority margin was set in advance.
The trial was sized on the specimen, and the survival comparison is a follow-up analysis on few
events.

So the case for omission does not rest on demonstrated equivalence. It rests on a harm measured in
the same randomisation. An ejection fraction fall of 10 points or more to below 50% occurred in
7.7% of patients given an anthracycline and in 3.2% of those who were not [@vandervoort2021].
[[BS-6370]] sets out why a measured harm beside an unmeasured efficacy risk is a stronger argument
than a null result would be.

{{trial:tryphaena}} is quoted alongside it and should not be. It was powered as a cardiac safety
study, its response rates were secondary, and its three arms were never compared formally
[@schneeweiss2013]. Three rates sitting beside each other are a compatibility result.

The remaining two rows vary the cytotoxic partner rather than the anthracycline.
{{trial:helen-006}} set weekly nab-paclitaxel with dual blockade against docetaxel and carboplatin
with dual blockade, neither arm carrying an anthracycline, and the nab-paclitaxel arm responded
more often [@chen2024a]. It changed the taxane formulation and the carboplatin at once, so it
cannot say which change produced the gain. It also enrolled Asian women only, which fixes the
comparator rather than widening it. {{trial:geparsixto}} added carboplatin to a taxane and
anthracycline backbone in a trial enrolling triple-negative and HER2-positive disease together.
Platinum raised response in the triple-negative population and added nothing in the HER2-positive
stratum, with a formal interaction test separating the two [@vonminckwitz2014]. That stratum was
not what the trial was powered for, so the null there is weak evidence rather than none.

What the table cannot show is a survival comparison of any of these substitutions. TRAIN-2 is the
only row carrying an event-free survival result against a randomised control. Nothing here
randomises the taxane schedule outside Asia. Nothing stratifies the anthracycline question by nodal
burden, which is where TRAIN-2's interval is least reassuring.

```practice
Under dual blockade, treat anthracycline omission as the default question rather than the exception.
The toxicity evidence is randomised and the efficacy evidence shows no signal of loss.

Do not describe the omission as proven non-inferior. Describe it as a trade of an unmeasured and
probably small efficacy risk against a measured cardiac one.

Keep the anthracycline in mind for very high-volume nodal disease, where the event rate is high and
the confidence interval in TRAIN-2 is least reassuring.
```

## BS-7070 Dual blockade and what the pathological complete response gains bought

```story SQ-0130
```

Adding a second drug to trastuzumab on a taxane raises the proportion of patients reaching a
pathological complete response. That is the class finding and most of these rows carry it. What the
table cannot do is put the rates on one axis.

Compare the endpoint column before the result column. {{trial:neosphere}}, {{trial:neoaltto}},
{{trial:nsabp-b-41}} and {{trial:calgb-40601}} scored the breast alone. {{trial:peony}},
{{trial:phedra}} and {{trial:impassion050}} required the nodes to be clear as well. The backbones
differ by more than the endpoint does, from docetaxel alone, to paclitaxel after doxorubicin and
cyclophosphamide, to a dose-dense anthracycline followed by paclitaxel, to six weeks of antibody
before any chemotherapy at all. Two of the seven were placebo-controlled and blinded. The rest were
open-label.

The lapatinib rows show what that does to a comparison. Added to trastuzumab and a taxane,
lapatinib raised breast response decisively in NeoALTTO, then missed significance in NSABP B-41 and
again in CALGB 40601. The drug and the dose were not the variable. The control arms were. The
trastuzumab control reached 29.5% in NeoALTTO, against 52.5% in NSABP B-41 and 46% in CALGB 40601.
NeoALTTO gave six weeks of antibody alone and no anthracycline before surgery. B-41 gave four
cycles of doxorubicin and cyclophosphamide first. A second HER2-directed drug has most room where
the chemotherapy beside it is doing least, which is a statement about control arms rather than
about lapatinib.

The row the pertuzumab decision rests on is NeoSphere, because it is the randomised comparison
against trastuzumab and docetaxel, which is what the clinic would otherwise give. PEONY repeated
that comparison with a stricter endpoint, independent central review and a placebo control
[@shao2020]. It enrolled Asian women, so it fixes the comparator rather than widening the
population. PHEDRA asked the same question with a kinase inhibitor and answered it the same
way. The gain was bought with diarrhoea, grade 3 or worse in 44.4% of the pyrotinib group against
5.1% of the placebo group [@wu2022].

IMpassion050 added something other than a HER2-directed drug and is the only unambiguous negative.
Atezolizumab changed nothing in either co-primary population, and the PD-L1-positive analysis
pointed the wrong way [@huober2022]. The five grade 5 events in the trial all occurred in the
atezolizumab arm. {{trial:margot}} put an Fc-engineered antibody in place of trastuzumab beside
pertuzumab and paclitaxel. It has no primary publication, so it is absent from the table and
settles nothing.

Now follow the response gains to their outcomes, because that is the reason this section exists.

NeoSphere was followed for five years and its response difference was the largest in the table.
Five-year progression-free survival was 86% in the pertuzumab, trastuzumab and docetaxel group and
81% in the trastuzumab and docetaxel group [@gianni2016]. There were roughly 107 patients per arm,
so the trial could never have measured an outcome difference of that size. The trial that generates
a response difference is never the trial that can price it.

NeoALTTO is the cleaner case, because its drug pair was then tested adjuvantly at scale. ALTTO
randomised more than eight thousand patients and missed its disease-free survival threshold against
trastuzumab alone [@piccart2016altto]. The neoadjuvant response gain was the larger of the two and
the outcome gain the smaller, so the ordering reversed.

CALGB 40601 is the same failure running the other way. Its primary response comparison did not
reach significance [@carey2016]. Its survival comparison, from the same randomisation and at more
than seven years, did, for relapse-free and for overall survival [@fernandezmartinez2020]. A reader
who stopped at the specimen would have concluded the drug did nothing.

Put the three together and the endpoint is not biased in a fixed direction. It can overstate an
outcome gain and it can understate one, which is what makes correcting for it impossible. Across 12
trials, the correlation between a treatment's increase in pathological complete response and its
effect on event-free survival had a coefficient of determination of 0.03. For overall survival it
was 0.24, and the authors judged neither sufficient to validate the endpoint [@cortazar2014].
[[BS-6360]] sets out why that is the quantity a surrogate has to satisfy.

```caution
None of this argues against dual blockade, since pertuzumab produces a real adjuvant benefit in
node-positive disease [[BS-6990]] and the neoadjuvant response gain pointed in the right direction.
What fails is the inference from the size of a response gain to the size of an outcome gain. A
regimen that raises pathological complete response by 20 points has not thereby been shown to
improve survival by any particular amount.
```

The overall survival column would settle these questions and it is almost entirely blank. No trial
here randomised a second HER2-directed drug before surgery with survival as its primary endpoint,
and none compared two second drugs against each other. A response improvement licenses a hypothesis
about outcome. It licenses a change of regimen only where response itself changes the next
treatment, which in this subtype it does [[BS-7010]].

## BS-7080 Chemotherapy-free and de-escalated neoadjuvant regimens

```story SQ-0150
```

Three different things get called de-escalation here and they carry different risks. Removing the
anthracycline is [[BS-7060]]. Removing all conventional cytotoxic chemotherapy is this section.
Removing treatment after a response has already been seen is [[BS-7090]].

The idea is not unreasonable. In the trial that established the antibody pair, the arm given both
antibodies without a taxane still produced complete responses in a minority [@gianni2012]. If those
tumours could be identified in advance, the cytotoxic drug would be going to them for nothing.

Seven rows have tested it and they agree on the shape of the effect. Chemotherapy-free blockade
clears the breast in a minority of patients. That minority is larger with a longer window, larger
in oestrogen receptor-negative disease, and larger in tumours called HER2-enriched by an intrinsic
subtype call [@llombart2017].

They diverge on design rather than on regimen, and the divergence decides what each can claim.
{{trial:tbcrc006}} and {{trial:pamela}} are single-arm. {{trial:tbcrc-023}} randomised the duration
of blockade and not the blockade itself. {{trial:phergain2}} is single-arm and adapts after surgery.
{{trial:phergain}} is chemotherapy-free only for patients whose tumours respond on imaging
[[BS-7090]]. {{trial:kristine}} substituted a conjugate rather than omitting anything.

One row tests the actual question and it failed. {{trial:adapt-her2-hr}} randomised twelve weeks of
dual blockade with or without weekly paclitaxel and tested non-inferiority formally [@nitz2017a].
Read the design before the rate. The test set early responders in the chemotherapy-free arm against
every patient in the taxane arm, which compares a selected group with an unselected one. It
enrolled hormone receptor-negative disease only, the half of the subtype most likely to respond.
The taxane still won. Five-year overall survival was 98% with the taxane and 94% without, on 134
patients, with a hazard ratio whose interval ran from 0.05 to 3.63 [@nitz2022a]. That interval is
the honest summary of what a trial this size can say about survival.

A drug difference runs across the rows and is easy to miss. TBCRC 006, TBCRC 023 and PAMELA gave
lapatinib with trastuzumab. WSG-ADAPT, PHERGain and PHERGain-2 gave pertuzumab with trastuzumab.
Rates from the first group do not transfer to the regimen a clinic now uses.

Duration is the only variable inside chemotherapy-free blockade that anyone randomised. TBCRC 023
doubled the window from twelve to twenty-four weeks and the rate rose, driven by the oestrogen
receptor-positive subgroup [@rimawi2019]. It combined a phase II design in its experimental arm
with a pick-the-winner design and was explicitly not powered to compare the two windows. The gap
between them was never tested.

KRISTINE shows what removing chemotherapy costs when something is put in its place. Its three-year
follow-up gave a hazard ratio for an event-free survival event of 2.61 against the conjugate, with
an interval from 1.36 to 4.98. Locoregional progression before surgery occurred in the conjugate
arm and not in the chemotherapy arm [@hurvitz2019kristine]. Once surgery had happened,
invasive disease-free survival was similar between the arms.

That is a failure mode peculiar to the preoperative window. The tumour is still in the patient, so
an underpowered regimen does not merely fail to shrink it. It permits local progression during the
months the regimen runs. A de-escalation acceptable after surgery is not automatically acceptable
before it.

The same substitution in hormone receptor-positive disease, endocrine therapy in place of the
taxane, was tested by {{trial:wsg-tpii}} and lowered response against the taxane [@tpii2026]. That
result belongs to [[BC-840]] and [[BS-7160]].

```caution
Eligibility does more work in this table than any drug does. TBCRC 006 enrolled stage II to III
disease with a median tumour of 6 cm [@rimawi2013]. PHERGain-2 required immunohistochemistry 3+,
node-negative disease and a tumour of 5 to 30 mm on magnetic resonance imaging [@garrigos2026].
Two single-arm rates from populations that far apart are not measurements of the same thing, and
they are routinely quoted as though they were.
```

What the table cannot show is any comparison of chemotherapy-free blockade against chemotherapy
with survival as the endpoint. Nobody randomised one. Nobody enrolled an assay-selected population
prospectively and then randomised the taxane inside it, so the strongest observation here, that
HER2-enriched tumours respond far more often, has never been used to assign treatment. And the
hormone receptor-positive majority, where the offer is most tempting and the response least likely,
is thinly represented in every row that tested omission [[BS-7140]].

## BS-7090 Response-adapted strategies and imaging-guided escalation

```story SQ-0160
```

Response-adapted designs differ in when they look, and the timing decides what they can claim.

Two of the five rows look while the tumour is still in the patient. {{trial:phergain}} uses
metabolic imaging after two cycles to decide whether chemotherapy is added at all.
{{trial:train-3}} uses magnetic resonance imaging to decide when enough chemotherapy has been
given, sending the patient to surgery once the scan is clear [@louis2026]. The other three look at
the specimen. {{trial:daphne}}, {{trial:compassher2pcr}} and {{trial:phergain2}} all withhold
further chemotherapy from patients whose surgery showed a pathological complete response.

Where they agree is unremarkable and worth saying once. Every adapted group in the table has done
well on the endpoint reported so far, and adherence to withholding chemotherapy was high wherever
it was measured against a feasibility target [@waks2022]. That is what a cohort selected by its own
good response should look like.

Where they diverge is in what the adaptation is for. Early adaptation escalates, adding
chemotherapy to patients who fail to respond. Late adaptation de-escalates, removing chemotherapy
from patients who already have. The second inherits the strongest evidence in the subtype, because
the same logic run in the other direction is what licenses post-neoadjuvant intensification
[[BS-7010]]. The first does not.

Early adaptation is also escalation on a surrogate of a surrogate. Metabolic response at six weeks
is not pathological response at surgery, and the two are separate measurements with separate error.
TBCRC 026 tested that relation formally against a prespecified accuracy threshold. The area under
the curve was 0.72, which did not reject a null of 0.65, so the primary objective was not met
[@connolly2021a]. The signal underneath it was real, since tumours without a substantial metabolic
fall rarely reached a complete response. A real signal that misses a prespecified accuracy bar is
not a licence to route treatment on it. That study required oestrogen receptor-negative disease, so
it says nothing about the majority in whom the decision is hardest.

PHERGain is the only randomised row and the strategy currently rests on it. State what it
established precisely, because it is usually overstated. Its three-year invasive disease-free
survival was measured against a fixed threshold rather than against its randomised comparator, and
that comparator arm held 71 patients by design [@perezgarcia2024]. Grade 3 or higher
treatment-related adverse events occurred in 33% of the response-adapted group against 62% of the
chemotherapy group. That is a real toxicity advantage attached to a strategy nobody has measured
against chemotherapy.

CompassHER2 pCR is much the largest row, and its response rates by receptor status already say
where the strategy will be usable [@tung2026]. The route out of chemotherapy opens least often in
hormone receptor-positive disease, which is the group in which it is most wanted [[BS-7140]].

PHERGain-2 is the only row that measured what de-escalation is supposed to buy. A fall of 10% or
more in global quality of life at one year affected 42.8% of patients on a regimen carrying no
conventional cytotoxic at all [@garrigos2026]. Whatever the omission is worth to a patient, it is
neither nothing nor everything.

What the table cannot show is the counterfactual it exists to produce. Three of the five rows are
single-arm, so a good outcome after a complete response is the prognosis of a responder rather than
the effect of stopping. CompassHER2 pCR and PHERGain-2 have not reported their recurrence
endpoints. And no design here can report what would have happened to the same patients had the
withheld chemotherapy been given, because nobody was randomised to receive it.

```practice
Use response-adapted de-escalation inside a trial where one is open. Outside one, say that the
supporting evidence is single-arm and that the omitted treatment has no comparator.

Distinguish the two adaptation points in conversation, since imaging-guided omission during
treatment carries a progression risk that pathology-guided omission after surgery does not
[[BS-3590]].

Do not let a metabolic response report substitute for a pathology report. The decision rules in
[[BS-7010]] act on the specimen.
```

## BS-7100 Conjugates in the neoadjuvant setting

```story SQ-0170
```

Three rows here, and the filter admitted one of them by a side door. {{trial:train-3}} appears
because patients with residual disease went on to trastuzumab emtansine after surgery [@louis2026].
Its preoperative regimen contains no conjugate at all. It says nothing about giving a conjugate
before surgery and belongs to [[BS-7090]].

That leaves two trials that put a conjugate in front of the operation, and they disagree because
they asked the conjugate to replace different things.

{{trial:kristine}} asked trastuzumab emtansine with pertuzumab to replace docetaxel, carboplatin
and dual blockade outright. Response fell, and the three-year follow-up found the loss was not only
in the specimen [[BS-7080]]. {{trial:destiny-b11}} asked trastuzumab deruxtecan to replace the
anthracycline opening of a sequence that kept the taxane and both antibodies, and response rose
against the dose-dense anthracycline opening [@harbeck2026db11]. The same trial also gave the
conjugate alone, without the taxane and antibodies. That arm closed early and responded least
often.

Those arms make one statement between them. A conjugate is an addition to the taxane and antibody
backbone rather than a substitute for it. The payload delivers cytotoxic chemistry to
antigen-positive cells and to their immediate neighbours, and why that is not the same as systemic
chemotherapy is in [[BS-4030]] and [[BS-4060]].

Part of the shortfall when a conjugate replaces chemotherapy has a mechanism, and it is not in the
table. One prospective study biopsied more than one site in each tumour before giving a conjugate
with dual blockade. Tumours carrying regions without the target essentially did not respond, while
tumours uniform for it responded well [@filho2021]. Those groups were defined in advance and were
not randomised, and heterogeneous tumours were a small minority, so that second rate rests on very
few patients.

Grade 3 or higher adverse events were less frequent with the conjugate sequence than with the
anthracycline sequence in DESTINY-Breast11, and adjudicated drug-related interstitial lung disease
occurred at similar frequency in both [@harbeck2026db11]. That trial enrolled high-risk disease
only, so the trade it describes has not been priced in the smaller tumours where the anthracycline
is already usually omitted.

The substitution was also tested after surgery. {{trial:kaitlin}} gave the conjugate with
pertuzumab against a taxane with dual blockade, both arms following an anthracycline, and it did
not improve freedom from invasive disease. That is a different question in a different setting
[[BC-820]].

```caution
Event-free survival in DESTINY-Breast11 was 4.5% mature at the reported analysis, with a hazard
ratio of 0.56 and an interval from 0.26 to 1.17 [@harbeck2026db11]. Everything in [[BS-7070]]
applies to that pairing, in both directions. The response difference is measured. The outcome
difference remains a hypothesis with a wide interval, and it is the outcome that justifies exposing
a curable patient to a conjugate.
```

## BS-7110 Unresolved questions and what reads out next

Both awaited trials test omission rather than addition, and each will settle less than its size
suggests. Both are single-arm by design, so neither can measure what the omitted treatment was
worth.

CompassHER2 pCR will report recurrence-free survival in patients who reached a pathological
complete response and then had chemotherapy omitted [@tung2026]. A low rate will make omission
defensible without measuring the loss, because no arm kept the chemotherapy. PHERGain-2 will report
a recurrence-free interval after a fully chemotherapy-free preoperative phase in a selected
population, where the same limitation applies to a smaller sample [@garrigos2026].

Five further questions have no trial designed to settle them.

Whether the response advantage in DESTINY-Breast11 becomes an event-free survival advantage. That
needs follow-up rather than a new trial, and the current maturity is too low to support a decision
[[BS-7100]].

Whether the Fc-engineered antibody comparison ever reports. It exists as conference and registry
material with no primary publication, so it sits on neither side of the antibody question.

Whether any assay selects patients for chemotherapy omission better than clinical features do.
Genomic response predictors are being reported alongside these trials [@tung2026], and none has
been validated prospectively as a tool for assigning treatment.

Whether omission is safe in the population where it is most tempting. Hormone receptor-positive
HER2-positive tumours respond least often and are the group in which a chemotherapy-free regimen is
most often proposed [[BS-7140]].

Whether molecular residual disease after neoadjuvant therapy adds to pathology in deciding what
comes next. The assay and its lead-time problem are in [[BS-3480]] and [[BS-3490]].

```interplay target=BC-280
This chapter treats response as a property of a tumour and a drug. Part VI argues it is also a
property of a population of cells with unequal antigen. A tumour scored HER2-positive on one core
may contain a compartment that is not, and that compartment is what survives to become residual
disease [[BS-2330]]. Read that way, the response rates in these tables are partly measurements of
how uniform each enrolled population happened to be. That is not speculation here, because the one
trial that biopsied several sites per tumour found response and antigen uniformity tracking each
other [@filho2021]. It also explains why a bystander-capable payload changes the arithmetic in
[[BS-7100]] and a receptor-blocking antibody does not.
```
