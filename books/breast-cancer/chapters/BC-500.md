## BS-4370 Stage and risk-adapted treatment selection

Selection in this subtype turns on one question asked before any drug is given. Would knowing
whether the tumour was eradicated change what happens next?

If the answer is yes, treat first and operate second. The post-neoadjuvant evidence in [[BS-4400]]
converts residual disease into a different regimen, and that option exists only if response was
observed. If the answer is no, because the tumour is small and node-negative and the plan would be
the same either way, then the neoadjuvant phase buys information nobody will act on.

That produces three bands in practice.

Small node-negative tumours, broadly up to 2 cm with clinically negative nodes, are treated with
surgery first and a de-escalated adjuvant regimen [[BS-4410]].

Tumours of stage II or III, and any clinically node-positive disease, are treated with neoadjuvant
systemic therapy. The backbone is in [[BS-4380]] and the response-adapted consequences in
[[BS-4390]].

Between those bands sits genuine uncertainty, and the commonest error is to resolve it with
imaging alone. Clinical nodal staging is imperfect, so a tumour called node-negative before
surgery may not be. Where that uncertainty is material, treating first preserves the option that
surgery-first destroys. The general form of this reasoning is in [[BC-400]] and the staging
limitations in [[BC-370]].

This chapter is the decision architecture. The randomised trial record it stands on is next door
and is not restated here. Neoadjuvant regimens are in [[BC-830]], with adjuvant and
post-neoadjuvant regimens in [[BC-820]]. Hormone receptor-positive HER2-positive disease takes its
own algorithm in [[BC-840]]. This chapter carries only the part of it that changes a decision. The
tables below are generated from the trial registry by filter, so read the
experimental-versus-control column before reading anything else in one.

## BS-4380 Neoadjuvant backbone and dual blockade

Three levers exist in the preoperative regimen, and each of them has its own table below. They are
the antibodies, the anthracycline, and whether a conjugate can carry the cytotoxic instead of the
patient.

```evidence setting=early subtype=HER2+ line=neoadjuvant topic=dual sort=year cols=acronym,n,arms,endpoint,result caption=Neoadjuvant_dual_HER2_blockade._The_arms_column_is_where_the_four-arm_designs_become_legible.
```

One finding runs through every row of that table. Two agents directed at HER2 eradicate more
tumour than one. It does not matter much whether the second agent is an antibody or a kinase
inhibitor. Nothing in the table contradicts it.

The seven rows are not seven versions of one experiment, and the differences are structural rather
than pharmacological. {{trial:neosphere}} and {{trial:neoaltto}} added a second HER2-directed
agent on top of a taxane. {{trial:tryphaena}} held dual blockade constant and varied the
chemotherapy around it. {{trial:impassion050}} held both constant and added a third mechanism.
{{trial:tbcrc006}}, {{trial:tbcrc-023}} and {{trial:adapt-her2-hr}} took the chemotherapy away
altogether. Four questions share one filter. Only the first of them is about dual blockade as
such.

The decision to give pertuzumab before surgery rests on {{trial:neosphere}}. It is the randomised
comparison against the regimen the clinic would otherwise use. Its four-arm design has not
been repeated [@gianni2012]. Those four arms also settle something a two-arm trial cannot. Dropping
the docetaxel from the triplet cost more response than dropping either antibody did. That ordering
is readable straight off the result column, and it is the most important single constraint on the
chemotherapy-free strategies in [[BS-4440]].

{{trial:neoaltto}} is the row that should be read next to the adjuvant table in [[BS-4420]]. It
raised pathological complete response substantially with lapatinib as the partner, and
{{trial:altto}} then failed to convert the same combination into a disease-free survival benefit
[@baselga2012neoaltto; @piccart2016altto]. One drug, two settings, opposite conclusions. That pair
is the surrogacy problem of [[BS-4390]] in its cleanest form.

{{trial:impassion050}} is the only row testing something other than HER2 blockade. Checkpoint
inhibition added nothing in the intention-to-treat population, and the PD-L1-positive population
moved the wrong way. Five grade 5 events occurred in the trial. All of them were in the
atezolizumab arm [@huober2022]. Immunotherapy is not a lever here, and the reason is not that it
was tested at the wrong dose.

Two things the table cannot show are worth naming. Nobody randomised pertuzumab against lapatinib
as the partner agent, so the class finding above rests on separate trials with separate control
arms. And the endpoint column hides a scoring difference. Some of these trials scored eradication
in the breast alone. Others required the nodes to be clear as well. Those are not the same
quantity and they are not equally associated with outcome [[BS-4390]].

```evidence setting=early subtype=HER2+ line=neoadjuvant topic=anthracycline sort=year cols=acronym,n,arms,endpoint,result,os caption=The_anthracycline_question_under_HER2_blockade._Only_TRAIN-2_carries_a_survival_endpoint.
```

The taxane is not contested. The anthracycline is. Five rows sit under that heading and only one of
them asks the question.

{{trial:noah}} is the historical row rather than a comparison. It established trastuzumab on an
anthracycline and taxane backbone, which is the reason the anthracycline is in the regimen at all
[@gianni2010noah]. {{trial:tryphaena}} appears here because it varied the chemotherapy under
constant dual blockade, but its primary endpoint was cardiac safety and its three response rates
were never compared against one another statistically [@schneeweiss2013]. {{trial:geparsixto}} is
about carboplatin rather than about the anthracycline, and it randomised triple-negative and
HER2-positive patients in the same trial. The HER2-positive stratum gained nothing and the
interaction with subtype was formally significant [@vonminckwitz2014], which is why platinum is
argued in [[BS-4480]] and not here. {{trial:helen-006}} compares two anthracycline-free backbones
against each other, so it presupposes the omission rather than testing it. It also enrolled only
Asian women at six Chinese hospitals, with short follow-up and no survival data [@chen2024a].

That leaves {{trial:train2}}. It is the only randomised test of the anthracycline itself against a
time-to-event endpoint [@vandervoort2021]. Its interval is wide enough to exclude neither a benefit
nor a harm from omission. No non-inferiority margin was set in advance. So the case for omitting
the anthracycline does not rest on proven equivalence. It rests on a toxicity difference measured
inside the same randomisation, which is priced in [[BS-4450]] and argued in full in [[BS-7060]].

What none of these trials enrolled is the patient the decision matters most for. A patient with
established cardiac risk factors was excluded or under-represented everywhere in this table, and
the omission argument is applied to exactly that patient in clinic.

```evidence setting=early subtype=HER2+ line=neoadjuvant modality=adc sort=year cols=acronym,n,arms,endpoint,result,os,status caption=Antibody-drug_conjugates_tested_as_the_preoperative_backbone,_or_as_a_substitute_for_part_of_it.
```

No conjugate given alone has matched a taxane with two antibodies. {{trial:kristine}} substituted a
conjugate for the chemotherapy and lost response [@hurvitz2018kristine]. The conjugate-alone arm of
{{trial:destiny-b11}} lost response against the anthracycline sequence [@harbeck2026db11]. Those two
results agree, and they agree about substitution rather than about either molecule.

{{trial:destiny-b11}} is load-bearing because it did something else as well. It placed the
conjugate in front of the same taxane and antibody triplet instead of in place of it. That
sequence is the first preoperative regimen to beat the standard one rather than match it
[@harbeck2026db11]. The difference between it and {{trial:kristine}} is sequencing against
substitution. It is not a difference between trastuzumab deruxtecan and trastuzumab emtansine, and
reading it that way is the common error.

{{trial:train-3}} asks a third question under the same filter. It used magnetic resonance imaging
to decide when to stop chemotherapy, reserving a conjugate for residual disease [@louis2026]. It
has no comparator arm, so its event-free survival by number of cycles describes one cohort and
cannot show that a shorter course was sufficient. Number of cycles was determined by response
rather than assigned, so the groups it reports differ in biology before they differ in treatment.

The control arm is what this table cannot show. {{trial:destiny-b11}} compared its conjugate
sequence against dose-dense doxorubicin and cyclophosphamide followed by the triplet. The
anthracycline-free regimen that the previous table supports was not in the trial. So the new
sequence has beaten a backbone this chapter argues against giving, and the comparison a clinic
actually faces has never been randomised.

```caution
Two findings in {{trial:destiny-b11}} point in opposite directions and both are easy to lose. The
conjugate given alone was clearly inferior to the anthracycline sequence on pathological complete
response, so the conjugate is not a substitute for the taxane and the antibodies. And event-free
survival is barely mature, at the fraction the overall survival column records
[@harbeck2026db11]. A pathological complete response difference at that maturity is a hypothesis
about survival, not a measurement of it. Why that distinction is load-bearing is set out in
[[BS-4390]] and developed in [[BC-540]].

Its toxicity gradient ran the same way as its efficacy. Grade 3 or higher adverse events occurred
in 22.6% of patients on the conjugate alone, 37.5% on the conjugate sequence and 55.8% on the
anthracycline sequence. Adjudicated drug-related interstitial lung disease was similar across all
three arms, which is the one result nobody predicted. {{trial:kristine}} is the cautionary row in
the same table, which [[BS-7100]] reads in full.
```

## BS-4390 Pathologic response as a decision point

Pathological complete response does two different jobs, and confusing them is the most common
error in reading neoadjuvant trials.

The first job is prognostic, at the level of the individual patient. One pooled analysis covered
12 trials and 11,955 patients. Eradication of tumour from breast and nodes was associated with
better event-free survival, with a hazard ratio of 0.48 and a 95% confidence interval of 0.43 to
0.54. For overall survival the hazard ratio was 0.36, with an interval of 0.31 to 0.42
[@cortazar2014]. The association was strongest in HER2-positive, hormone receptor-negative disease
treated with trastuzumab, which [[BS-7050]] quantifies. Nowhere in breast cancer does response
carry more information about an individual than here.

The second job would be surrogacy, at the level of the trial. In the same analysis, the
correlation between a treatment's increase in pathological complete response and its effect on
outcome was negligible. The coefficient of determination was 0.03 for event-free survival and 0.24
for overall survival, and the authors could not validate pathological complete response as a
surrogate endpoint [@cortazar2014].

Those two results are not in conflict. Patients who respond do better. Drugs that raise response
rates do not reliably extend survival. The first is a statement about biology already present in
the tumour. The second would be a statement about what a drug does, and it does not follow.

This subtype supplies the cleanest demonstration of the gap. Lapatinib added to trastuzumab raised
preoperative eradication substantially in {{trial:neoaltto}} and changed nothing in the adjuvant
trial of the same combination [@baselga2012neoaltto; @piccart2016altto]. The tumour that
disappeared was still the tumour that was going to behave well.

The definition matters for the first job. Eradication from breast and nodes, scored either as ypT0
ypN0 or as ypT0/is ypN0, was better associated with outcome than eradication from the breast
alone. For eradication from the breast alone the event-free survival hazard ratio was 0.60, with
an interval of 0.55 to 0.66 [@cortazar2014]. A response report that does not state the nodal
status is not reporting the quantity that carries the prognosis. Scoring conventions, including
[[term:rcb]], are in [[BC-090]] and the endpoint argument in full is in [[BC-540]].

The legitimate use of response in this chapter is therefore patient-level and observed. Nothing in
[[BS-4400]] depends on predicting who will respond. It depends on knowing who did.

## BS-4400 Post-neoadjuvant therapy for residual disease

Residual invasive disease after neoadjuvant HER2-directed therapy identifies a group whose risk
justifies changing drug class rather than continuing the same one.

```evidence setting=early subtype=HER2+ line=post-neoadjuvant modality=adc sort=year cols=acronym,n,population,arms,endpoint,result,os,status caption=Post-neoadjuvant_conjugate_therapy_for_residual_invasive_disease._The_population_column_is_the_one_that_decides_which_trial_a_patient_belongs_to.
```

Every trial in this table accepts the same premise. Residual disease is treated by changing to a
conjugate rather than by extending the antibody, and no row proposes continuing trastuzumab alone.
That premise was itself tested only once.

{{trial:katherine}} is the trial the decision rests on. It randomised a conjugate against
continuing trastuzumab, which was the alternative the clinic actually had, and it is the only
member of this table with a mature overall survival result [@vonminckwitz2019; @geyer2025]. Every
later trial here inherits its conclusion and asks a narrower question on top of it.

{{trial:destiny-b05}} is the only one of those that has reported. It compared a bystander-capable
conjugate against the established one and improved on it [@loibl2026db05]. Read as a sequence, the
two trials moved this decision from whether to escalate to which conjugate to escalate with.

The eligible populations are not the same, and the divergence is entry criteria rather than drug.
{{trial:destiny-b05}} required residual invasive disease together with node-positive disease at
surgery, or inoperable disease at diagnosis [@loibl2026db05]. {{trial:compassher2-rd}} requires
oestrogen receptor-negative or node-positive disease. A patient with small-volume residual disease
and negative nodes was studied in {{trial:katherine}} and in none of the trials that followed it.
Each successive design has enriched for events. That raises the event rate against which a hazard
ratio is computed. Ranking these trials against one another by effect size therefore compares
populations more than it compares drugs.

{{trial:compassher2-rd}} and {{trial:astefania}} share a second feature that their rows cannot
carry. Both add an agent on top of trastuzumab emtansine, and both were designed before
{{trial:destiny-b05}} reported [@osullivan2021compassher2rd; @hurvitz2022]. Whatever they show, it
will be a comparison against the conjugate that has now lost a randomised trial. They will be
internally valid and externally awkward. [[BS-7010]] follows what each is built to settle.

The comparison nobody has run is between the two conjugates in the population {{trial:katherine}}
enrolled and {{trial:destiny-b05}} excluded. For node-negative residual disease the honest position
is that escalation is established and the choice of agent is extrapolated.

```caution
The price is respiratory and it is not small. Adjudicated drug-related interstitial lung disease
occurred in 9.6% of patients on trastuzumab deruxtecan against 1.6% on trastuzumab emtansine, and
two patients with interstitial lung disease in the deruxtecan group died [@loibl2026db05]. Overall
survival in that trial is immature. So the current position is a large recurrence benefit, an
unmeasured survival benefit and two treatment-related deaths from a toxicity that is detectable
early. The surveillance that makes the difference between grade 1 and grade 5 is set out in
[[BS-4100]] and should be in place before the first dose.
```

## BS-4410 De-escalation in small node-negative tumors

Patients with tumours of 3 cm or less and negative nodes were largely excluded from the pivotal
adjuvant trastuzumab trials [@romond2005], so the regimen used in them was built rather than
inherited. Two questions follow in order, and both are argued at trial level in [[BC-820]]: where
the standard regimen came from [[BS-6980]], and how much of it a small node-negative tumour
actually needs [[BS-7000]].

```evidence setting=early subtype=HER2+ line=adjuvant phase=2 sort=year cols=acronym,n,population,arms,endpoint,result caption=The_two_de-escalated_adjuvant_regimens_for_stage_I_disease._Neither_row_is_a_randomised_comparison_against_the_full_regimen.
```

Both rows report excellent outcomes and neither row is a comparison against a full regimen.
{{trial:apt}} had no comparator at all [@tolaney2015]. A single-arm study in a population with a very
low event rate can show that outcomes are excellent. It cannot show that a shorter regimen is
equivalent to a longer one, because there is no longer one in the dataset. The reason that
distinction matters, and why de-escalation requires a stated margin rather than an impressive rate,
is developed in [[BC-740]] and in [[BS-6370]].

{{trial:atempt}} did randomise, and against the wrong thing for this purpose. Its comparator was
the {{trial:apt}} regimen rather than standard chemotherapy, so it tests substitution within
de-escalation. Its primary endpoint was a protocol-defined composite of clinically relevant
toxicity, which did not separate between the arms [@tolaney2021atempt]. Matching totals are not the
same as two regimens costing a patient the same things. Patients on the conjugate reported less
neuropathy and less alopecia, which is a real difference hidden inside a null composite.

The randomised de-escalation trial does not exist and probably cannot be run. At the event rate
these populations produce, a non-inferiority trial against the full regimen would need a cohort
nobody will fund.

```practice
For a node-negative tumour of 2 cm or less, weekly paclitaxel with trastuzumab is the default and
the evidence for it is a single-arm trial with an excellent rate [@tolaney2015]. Say that out
loud rather than implying a randomised comparison exists.

Do not add pertuzumab in node-negative disease. The randomised trial that tested it found no
benefit in that group [@piccart2021aphinity], and the evidence is in [[BS-4420]].

Trastuzumab emtansine is an alternative where neuropathy or alopecia is the dominant concern, not
where total toxicity is [@tolaney2021atempt].

The smallest tumours, T1a and T1b with negative nodes, sit outside even these datasets. Treatment
there is extrapolation, and the conversation should say so.
```

## BS-4420 Adjuvant escalation and extended HER2-directed therapy

Escalation in the adjuvant setting is governed by one variable, and it is nodal status.

```evidence setting=early subtype=HER2+ line=adjuvant topic=pertuzumab sort=year cols=acronym,n,population,arms,endpoint,result,os caption=Escalation_beyond_chemotherapy_and_a_year_of_trastuzumab._The_result_column_carries_the_nodal_split_that_decides_this_section.
```

Three trials added something to chemotherapy and a year of trastuzumab. None of them moved overall
survival, and only one crossed its primary endpoint. That is the class finding, and it is the
reason escalation here is a small decision argued at length.

The three rows fail in three different ways, which is why they should not be pooled in the reading.
{{trial:altto}} added lapatinib, a second inhibitor class rather than a second antibody. It lost
on a threshold rather than on an effect. After the lapatinib-alone arm was closed for futility the
protocol was amended to require a p value of 0.025 or less for the remaining comparisons, and the
combination returned 0.048 [@piccart2016altto]. That is a multiplicity convention producing a
negative trial, not a biological statement about dual blockade.

{{trial:kaitlin}} substituted a conjugate for the taxane and matched the taxane arm exactly. Its
informative number is not the hazard ratio. Treatment completion was 88.4% in the taxane arm and
65.0% in the conjugate arm [@krop2022kaitlin]. A regimen that a large minority of patients do not
finish cannot demonstrate superiority whatever its biology, and [[BS-6990]] sets out both failures in full.

{{trial:aphinity}} is the trial the decision rests on, because it is the only one that added a
drug the class had already validated and held everything else constant. It found its benefit in
the node-positive stratum and nothing in the node-negative one. Longer follow-up sharpened
rather than softened that split [@vonminckwitz2017; @piccart2021aphinity]. Overall survival has
never reached its significance boundary, including at the third interim analysis [@loibl2024].

The nodal split is a subgroup gradient inside one randomisation rather than a randomised comparison
of two populations. That is the limit on how hard it can be pushed, and it is also the only thing
the table offers for selecting patients. No trial in this table randomised by nodal status, and no
column here reports a biomarker that would do the job instead.

```evidence setting=early subtype=HER2+ line=adjuvant topic=neratinib cols=acronym,n,population,arms,endpoint,result,os caption=The_only_trial_of_extended_oral_HER2_blockade_after_a_completed_year_of_trastuzumab.
```

Extended oral blockade is the other escalation option, and the single row is the whole evidence
base. {{trial:extenet}} bought its invasive disease-free survival difference at the cost of grade 3
diarrhoea in 40% of patients against 2% on placebo [@chan2016]. No antidiarrhoeal strategy was part of the
randomisation, so that is the tolerability of the drug given without one. Randomisation was
stratified by hormone receptor status and the receptor-positive group was reported separately
[@chan2020], which is where the benefit has been claimed to concentrate.

The overall survival column is the one to read here. At final analysis after eight years the two
arms are indistinguishable [@holmes2023]. A recurrence difference at two years that has not become
a survival difference at eight is the shape this section exists to flag. The agents themselves are
covered in [[BC-450]] and the full reading is in [[BS-7020]].

## BS-4430 Hormone receptor-positive HER2-positive disease as a distinct algorithm

A tumour that is both hormone receptor-positive and HER2-positive carries two dependencies, and
the treatment algorithm has to service both on different timescales. The trial record for this
group is in [[BC-840]]. This section carries only what changes a decision here.

The first consequence is that it responds differently to HER2-directed neoadjuvant therapy. In
{{trial:destiny-b11}}, the conjugate sequence gained 9.1 percentage points of pathological
complete response over the anthracycline sequence in hormone receptor-positive disease, with an
interval of 0.2 to 17.9. In hormone receptor-negative disease it gained 16.1 points, with an
interval of 3.0 to 28.8 [@harbeck2026db11]. Both the absolute rates and the increment are lower
when the oestrogen receptor is present.

The second consequence is that the benefit of HER2-directed escalation is not obviously smaller,
which is counterintuitive. In the six-year {{trial:aphinity}} analysis the pertuzumab hazard ratio
was 0.73 in hormone receptor-positive disease, with an interval of 0.59 to 0.92. In hormone
receptor-negative disease it was 0.83, with an interval of 0.63 to 1.10 [@piccart2021aphinity]. An
exploratory subgroup analysis of the same trial found the largest benefit in oestrogen
receptor-positive tumours whose HER2 fluorescence in situ hybridisation ratio sat in the lower
amplified range [@agostinetto2026]. That analysis is hypothesis-generating and [[BS-7170]] reads
it properly.

The third consequence is temporal. HER2-directed therapy finishes at one year. Endocrine therapy
runs for five to ten, and the late recurrence hazard described in [[BS-4350]] applies to this
population too. A patient who completes HER2-directed treatment has not completed treatment.

```interplay target=BC-280
Triple-positive disease is the clinical form of the two-receptor heterogeneity problem in
[[BS-2370]]. The compartment that survives HER2 blockade is not necessarily the compartment that
survives oestrogen deprivation, and a report calling both receptors positive has averaged twice
over a tumour that is uniform in neither. The lower pathological complete response rate in hormone
receptor-positive disease [@harbeck2026db11] is what that looks like from the clinic. It is also
why residual disease in this subtype should not be read as HER2-directed failure alone, and why
the conjugate literature in [[BC-460]] treats antigen level and dependence as separate variables.
```

## BS-4440 Chemotherapy-sparing strategies

Some HER2-positive tumours are eradicated without cytotoxic chemotherapy. Nine trials say so and
none of them says which ones.

```evidence setting=early subtype=HER2+,HR+/HER2+ line=neoadjuvant topic=chemotherapy-free sort=year cols=acronym,n,population,arms,endpoint,result,status caption=Preoperative_regimens_that_remove_conventional_cytotoxic_chemotherapy._Compare_the_eligibility_criteria_before_comparing_the_response_rates.
```

Two findings hold across the whole table. A regimen without cytotoxic chemotherapy eradicates
tumour in a substantial minority of patients, and it costs far less toxicity than the regimen it
replaces. Neither finding is in dispute and neither is sufficient.

The rows divide into three designs, and almost all the apparent disagreement between them is the
design rather than the regimen.

The first group randomised a chemotherapy-free arm against the taxane the clinic would otherwise
give. {{trial:adapt-her2-hr}} did it in hormone receptor-negative disease and {{trial:wsg-tpii}} in
receptor-positive disease, substituting endocrine therapy for the taxane [@nitz2017a; @tpii2026].
{{trial:kristine}} did it with a conjugate in place of the chemotherapy [@hurvitz2018kristine].
All three lost on response, by margins the result column makes plain. These are the load-bearing
rows, and they say the same thing {{trial:neosphere}} said in [[BS-4380]]. The chemotherapy is the
component carrying most of the response.

The second group is single-arm or not powered for comparison. {{trial:tbcrc006}} gave twelve weeks
of chemotherapy-free dual blockade with endocrine therapy in receptor-positive disease
[@rimawi2013]. {{trial:tbcrc-023}} extended the same idea to twenty-four weeks under a
pick-the-winner design that was explicitly not powered for a direct comparison [@rimawi2019].
{{trial:pamela}} was built to test whether the HER2-enriched intrinsic subtype predicts response
rather than to test the regimen at all [@llombart2017]. Reading these rows as rival response rates
is the error the caption warns against. Median tumour size differs several-fold across this table,
and a response rate tracks who was enrolled before it tracks what they were given.

The third group routes patients on a measurement taken after treatment has started.
{{trial:perelisa}} used the relative fall in Ki67 after two weeks of letrozole
[@guarneri2019perelisa]. {{trial:phergain}} used a centrally reviewed positron emission tomography
response after two cycles, switching non-responders to a chemotherapy-containing regimen
[@perezgarcia2024]. {{trial:phergain2}} routes adjuvant treatment by pathological response instead
and has not reported its recurrence-free interval endpoint [@garrigos2026]. A biomarker read on
treatment is not a selection tool. It tells you who is already responding, which is a different
service from telling you in advance who can be spared.

That distinction runs deeper than the table shows, and it is the single most important thing about
this literature. In every one of these designs a patient with residual disease went on to receive
chemotherapy, a conjugate, or both. What was tested was deferral of chemotherapy in patients who
turned out not to need it. Omission was never tested, because nobody left a non-responder
untreated. A reader who takes these rows as evidence that chemotherapy can be dropped has read a
response-adapted strategy as a subtraction.

Two intrinsic-subtype rows point at a selection tool that does not yet exist. HER2-enriched tumours
by PAM50 respond far more often than others in {{trial:pamela}} and in the responder cohort of
{{trial:perelisa}} [@llombart2017; @guarneri2019perelisa]. That is a real biological signal
measured on pre-treatment tissue. It has never been used prospectively to assign treatment, and an
assay that enriches a response rate is not the same object as an assay validated to withhold a
drug.

```caution
{{trial:phergain}} is read as showing that about a third of patients can safely omit chemotherapy.
What it demonstrated is a three-year rate in a phase 2 trial whose primary endpoint for that group
was a single-arm rate against a fixed threshold [@perezgarcia2024]. There is no randomised
comparison against standard chemoimmunotherapy, and the comparator group contained 71 patients.
Grade 3 or higher treatment-related adverse events occurred in 33% of the response-adapted group
against 62% of the chemotherapy group, which is the part of the trial that is not in doubt. That
is a promising strategy, not an established equivalence, and [[BS-6370]] explains what would be
needed to make it one.
```

```practice
Anthracycline omission is the de-escalation with the strongest evidence and the clearest toxicity
argument [@vandervoort2021; @harbeck2026db11]. It should be the default question rather than an
exception.

Chemotherapy omission remains a trial question outside a trial. Where it is used off protocol,
say that the supporting evidence is phase 2 and response-adapted [@perezgarcia2024].

In hormone receptor-positive disease, replacing the taxane with endocrine therapy more than halves
the pathological complete response rate [@tpii2026]. If the plan depends on observing a response,
that matters.

Do not offer a chemotherapy-free strategy to a patient who would decline chemotherapy for residual
disease. Every trial in this table depended on giving it.
```

## BS-4450 Cardiac surveillance and cumulative toxicity

Two cardiac toxicities run through this chapter and they behave differently. Conflating them
produces the wrong surveillance schedule.

Anthracycline injury is dose-dependent and largely irreversible. In a prospective cohort of 2,625
patients receiving anthracycline-containing therapy with serial ejection fraction measurement,
cardiotoxicity occurred in 9%. The median interval from the end of chemotherapy to the event was
3.5 months, and 98% of events occurred within the first year. With heart failure therapy, 11% of
affected patients recovered fully and 71% recovered partially. Two variables predicted the event
independently. Ejection fraction at the end of chemotherapy carried a hazard ratio of 1.37 for
each one-point decrement. Cumulative doxorubicin dose carried a hazard ratio of 1.09 for each 50
mg per square metre [@cardinale2015].

HER2-directed injury is not dose-dependent in the same way and is usually reversible on stopping
the drug. In the randomised adjuvant setting the rates are low. Primary cardiac events remained
below 1% in both arms of {{trial:aphinity}} [@piccart2021aphinity].

The strongest cardiac intervention available is not surveillance. It is avoiding the anthracycline
where the evidence permits, which is where the toxicity half of [[BS-4380]] gets priced. In
{{trial:train2}}, an ejection fraction fall of 10 points or more to below 50% occurred in 7.7% of
patients given an anthracycline and 3.2% of those who were not [@vandervoort2021]. In
{{trial:destiny-b11}}, all-grade left ventricular dysfunction occurred in 6.1% of the
anthracycline arm against 0.7% and 1.3% in the two conjugate-containing arms [@harbeck2026db11].

Baseline risk stratification, monitoring schedules and the management of asymptomatic ejection

```practice
Measure ejection fraction before the first anthracycline dose and again at the end of
chemotherapy. The end-of-chemotherapy value was an independent predictor of subsequent
cardiotoxicity, alongside cumulative anthracycline dose [@cardinale2015].

Concentrate surveillance in the first year after treatment. That is when 98% of anthracycline
events occurred [@cardinale2015].

Start heart failure therapy when a decline is detected rather than when symptoms appear. Full or
partial recovery occurred in 82% of affected patients treated on that basis [@cardinale2015].

A fall on HER2-directed therapy usually warrants holding the drug and reassessing rather than
abandoning it. A fall after anthracycline is treated, not observed [@cardinale2015].

In a patient with cardiac risk factors, the anthracycline decision is the cardiology decision. The
evidence that it can be omitted under dual blockade is randomised [@vandervoort2021].
```
