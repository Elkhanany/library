## BS-4370 Stage and risk-adapted treatment selection

```story SQ-1140
```

Selection in this subtype turns on one question asked before any drug is given. Would knowing
whether the tumour was eradicated change what happens next?

If the answer is yes, treat first and operate second. The post-neoadjuvant evidence in
[[BS-7010]] converts residual disease into a different regimen, and that option exists only if
response was observed. If the answer is no, because the tumour is small and node-negative and
the plan would be the same either way, then the neoadjuvant phase buys information nobody will
act on.

That produces three bands in practice.

Small node-negative tumours, broadly up to 2 cm with clinically negative nodes, are treated with
surgery first and a de-escalated adjuvant regimen [[BS-7000]].

Tumours of stage II or III, and any clinically node-positive disease, are treated with
neoadjuvant systemic therapy. The backbone is in [[BS-7060]] and the response-adapted
consequences in [[BS-7070]].

Between those bands sits genuine uncertainty, and the commonest error is to resolve it with
imaging alone. Clinical nodal staging is imperfect, so a tumour called node-negative before
surgery may not be. Where that uncertainty is material, treating first preserves the option that
surgery-first destroys. The general form of this reasoning is in [[BC-400]] and the staging
limitations in [[BC-370]].

The decision is only worth making if the answer will be acted on. A neoadjuvant plan that routes
residual disease to a conjugate commits the clinic to giving that conjugate, and a patient who
would decline it gains nothing from being treated first. The same applies at the other end. A
complete response opens the de-escalation questions in [[BS-7090]], and those are decisions a
service either offers or does not.

Two things this chapter does not carry. It does not choose the regimen, because that choice is
made against trial evidence rather than against a rule, and it belongs next door. Nor does it
apply to hormone receptor-positive HER2-positive disease without adjustment, because response is
less frequent there and the band boundaries move with it [[BS-7140]].

Neoadjuvant regimens are in [[BC-830]], adjuvant and post-neoadjuvant regimens in [[BC-820]], and
the triple-positive algorithm in [[BC-840]]. What stays here is the question that decides which of
those chapters a patient enters.
