## BS-4370 Stage and risk-adapted treatment selection

Selection in this subtype turns on one question asked before any drug is given. Would knowing
whether the tumour was eradicated change what happens next?

If the answer is yes, treat first and operate second. The post-neoadjuvant evidence in
[[BS-4400]] converts residual disease into a different regimen, and that option exists only if
response was observed. If the answer is no, because the tumour is small and node-negative and the
plan would be the same either way, then the neoadjuvant phase buys information nobody will act on.

That produces three bands in practice.

Small node-negative tumours, broadly up to 2 cm with clinically negative nodes, are treated with
surgery first and a de-escalated adjuvant regimen [[BS-4410]].

Tumours of stage II or III, and any clinically node-positive disease, are treated with
neoadjuvant systemic therapy. The backbone is in [[BS-4380]] and the response-adapted
consequences in [[BS-4390]].

Between those bands sits genuine uncertainty, and the commonest error is to resolve it with
imaging alone. Clinical nodal staging is imperfect, so a tumour called node-negative before
surgery may not be. Where that uncertainty is material, treating first preserves the option that
surgery-first destroys. The general form of this reasoning is in [[BC-400]] and the staging
limitations in [[BC-370]].

## BS-4380 Neoadjuvant backbone and dual blockade

Two antibodies against different epitopes of the same receptor do more than one. In
{{trial:neosphere}}, 417 treatment-naive patients were randomised across four neoadjuvant arms.
Pathological complete response in the breast was 45.8% with pertuzumab, trastuzumab and docetaxel,
with a 95% confidence interval of 36.1 to 55.7. It was 29.0% with trastuzumab and docetaxel, with
an interval of 20.6 to 38.5, and the difference had a p value of 0.0141 [@gianni2012]. Removing
the antibody from the doublet cost more than removing the chemotherapy, because pertuzumab with
docetaxel gave 24.0% and pertuzumab with trastuzumab alone gave 16.8%.

The chemotherapy partner is a separate question and the anthracycline is the contested part. In
{{trial:train2}}, 438 patients with stage II or III disease received dual blockade with or without
an anthracycline component. Three-year event-free survival was 93.6% without an anthracycline and
92.7% with one, giving a hazard ratio of 0.90 and a wide interval of 0.50 to 1.63. A fall in left
ventricular ejection fraction of 10 points or more to below 50% occurred in 7.7% of those who
received an anthracycline. It occurred in 3.2% of those who did not, with a p value of 0.04. Two
patients in the anthracycline group developed acute leukaemia [@vandervoort2021].

A conjugate has now been tested as the backbone. {{trial:destiny-b11}} randomised high-risk
patients to trastuzumab deruxtecan alone, to trastuzumab deruxtecan followed by paclitaxel with
trastuzumab and pertuzumab, or to dose-dense doxorubicin and cyclophosphamide followed by the same
triplet. Pathological complete response was 43.0% in 286 patients on the conjugate alone, 67.3% in
321 patients on the conjugate followed by the triplet, and 56.3% in 320 patients on the
anthracycline sequence. The absolute difference between the conjugate sequence and the
anthracycline sequence was 11.2 percentage points, with an interval of 4.0 to 18.3 and a p value
of 0.003 [@harbeck2026db11].

The toxicity comparison ran the same way. Grade 3 or higher adverse events occurred in 22.6%,
37.5% and 55.8% of patients across the three arms. All-grade left ventricular dysfunction occurred
in 0.7%, 1.3% and 6.1%. Adjudicated drug-related interstitial lung disease was similar across arms
at 4.9%, 4.4% and 5.1% [@harbeck2026db11].

```caution
Two findings in that trial point in opposite directions and both are easy to lose. The conjugate
given alone was clearly inferior to the anthracycline sequence on pathological complete response,
at 43.0% against 56.3%, so the conjugate is not a substitute for the taxane and antibodies. And
event-free survival was 4.5% mature, with a hazard ratio of 0.56 and an interval of 0.26 to 1.17
[@harbeck2026db11]. A pathological complete response difference at that maturity is a hypothesis
about survival, not a measurement of it. Why that distinction is load-bearing is set out in
[[BS-4390]] and developed in [[BC-540]].
```

## BS-4390 Pathologic response as a decision point

Pathological complete response does two different jobs, and confusing them is the most common
error in reading neoadjuvant trials.

The first job is prognostic, at the level of the individual patient. One pooled analysis covered 12
trials and 11,955 patients. Eradication of tumour from breast and nodes was associated with better
event-free survival, with a hazard ratio of 0.48 and a 95% confidence interval of 0.43 to 0.54.
For overall survival the hazard ratio was 0.36, with an interval of 0.31 to 0.42. The association
was strongest in HER2-positive, hormone receptor-negative disease treated with trastuzumab. There
the event-free survival hazard ratio was 0.15, with an interval of 0.09 to 0.27 [@cortazar2014].
Nowhere in breast cancer does response carry more information about an individual than here.

The second job would be surrogacy, at the level of the trial. In the same analysis, the
correlation between a treatment's increase in pathological complete response and its effect on
outcome was negligible. The coefficient of determination was 0.03 for event-free survival and 0.24
for overall survival, and the authors could not validate pathological complete response as a
surrogate endpoint [@cortazar2014].

Those two results are not in conflict. Patients who respond do better. Drugs that raise response
rates do not reliably extend survival. The first is a statement about biology already present in
the tumour. The second would be a statement about what a drug does, and it does not follow.

The definition matters for the first job. Eradication from breast and nodes, scored either as ypT0
ypN0 or as ypT0/is ypN0, was better associated with outcome than eradication from the breast
alone. For eradication from the breast alone the event-free survival hazard ratio was 0.60, with
an interval of 0.55 to 0.66
[@cortazar2014]. A response report that does not state the nodal status is not reporting the
quantity that carries the prognosis. Scoring conventions, including [[term:rcb]], are in
[[BC-090]] and the endpoint argument in full is in [[BC-540]].

The legitimate use of response in this chapter is therefore patient-level and observed. Nothing in
[[BS-4400]] depends on predicting who will respond. It depends on knowing who did.

## BS-4400 Post-neoadjuvant therapy for residual disease

Residual invasive disease after neoadjuvant HER2-directed therapy identifies a group whose risk
justifies changing drug class rather than continuing the same one.

{{trial:katherine}} randomised 1,486 patients with residual invasive disease in breast or axilla
to 14 cycles of trastuzumab emtansine or trastuzumab. At the interim analysis invasive disease or
death had occurred in 91 of 743 patients on the conjugate, which is 12.2%, and 165 of 743 on
trastuzumab, which is 22.2%. Three-year freedom from invasive disease was 88.3% against 77.0%,
with a hazard ratio of 0.50 and a 95% confidence interval of 0.39 to 0.64 [@vonminckwitz2019].

The mature analysis is now available and it converts that into survival. At a median follow-up of
8.4 years the hazard ratio for invasive disease or death was 0.54, with an interval of 0.44 to
0.66. Seven-year invasive disease-free survival was 80.8% with the conjugate and 67.1% with
trastuzumab, a difference of 13.7 percentage points. The hazard ratio for death was 0.66, with an
interval of 0.51 to 0.87 and a p value of 0.003. Seven-year overall survival was 89.1% against
84.4%, a difference of 4.7 percentage points. Grade 3 or higher adverse events occurred in 26.1%
against 15.7% [@geyer2025].

A second conjugate has now been compared with the first. {{trial:destiny-b05}} randomised 1,635
patients to trastuzumab deruxtecan or trastuzumab emtansine. At a median follow-up of about 30
months, invasive-disease events or deaths were reported in 51 patients, which is 6.2%, on
trastuzumab deruxtecan, and 102 patients, which is 12.5%, on trastuzumab emtansine. The hazard
ratio was 0.47, with an interval of 0.34 to 0.66 and a p value below 0.001. Three-year invasive
disease-free survival was 92.4% against 83.7% [@loibl2026db05].

The eligible population is narrower than {{trial:katherine}}'s and this is routinely missed. {{trial:destiny-b05}}
required residual invasive disease together with node-positive disease at surgery, or inoperable
disease at diagnosis [@loibl2026db05]. A patient with small-volume residual disease and negative
nodes was not studied in that trial and was studied in {{trial:katherine}}.

```caution
The price is respiratory and it is not small. Adjudicated drug-related interstitial lung disease
occurred in 9.6% of patients on trastuzumab deruxtecan against 1.6% on trastuzumab emtansine, and
two patients with interstitial lung disease in the deruxtecan group died [@loibl2026db05]. Overall
survival in that trial is immature. So the current position is a large recurrence benefit, an
unmeasured survival benefit and two treatment-related deaths from a toxicity that is detectable
early. The surveillance that makes the difference between grade 1 and grade 5 is set out in
[[BS-4100]] and should be in place before the first dose.
```

## BS-4410 De-escalation in small node-negative tumors

Patients with tumours of 3 cm or less and negative nodes were largely excluded from the pivotal
adjuvant trastuzumab trials [@romond2005], so the regimen used in them was built rather than
inherited.

In {{trial:apt}}, 406 patients with node-negative tumours up to 3 cm received weekly paclitaxel
with trastuzumab for 12 weeks followed by trastuzumab to one year. At a median follow-up of 4.0
years, three-year survival free from invasive disease was 98.7%, with a 95% confidence interval of
97.6 to 99.8. Twelve relapses occurred and two were distant metastatic breast cancer. Grade 3
neuropathy occurred in 3.2% of patients and symptomatic congestive heart failure in 0.5%
[@tolaney2015].

That trial had no comparator. A single-arm study in a population with a very low event rate can
show that outcomes are excellent. It cannot show that a shorter regimen is equivalent to a longer
one, because there is no longer one in the dataset. The reason that distinction matters, and why
de-escalation requires a stated margin rather than an impressive rate, is developed in [[BC-740]]
and in [[BS-6370]].

Substituting a conjugate did not improve the trade. {{trial:atempt}} randomised 497 patients with
stage I disease in a 3:1 ratio to trastuzumab emtansine or to paclitaxel with trastuzumab.
Clinically relevant toxicities occurred in 46% of patients on the conjugate and 47% on the taxane,
with a p value of 0.83. Three-year invasive disease-free survival with the conjugate was 97.8%,
with an interval of 96.3 to 99.3. Patients on the conjugate reported less neuropathy and less
alopecia [@tolaney2021atempt].

```practice
For a node-negative tumour of 2 cm or less, weekly paclitaxel with trastuzumab is the default and
the evidence for it is a single-arm trial with an excellent rate [@tolaney2015]. Say that out
loud rather than implying a randomised comparison exists.

Do not add pertuzumab in node-negative disease. The randomised trial that tested it found no
benefit in that group [@piccart2021aphinity], and the evidence is in [[BS-4420]].

Trastuzumab emtansine is an alternative where neuropathy or alopecia is the dominant concern, not
where total toxicity is [@tolaney2021atempt].

The smallest tumours, T1a and T1b with negative nodes, sit outside even these datasets. Treatment
there is extrapolation, and the conversation should say so.
```

## BS-4420 Adjuvant escalation and extended HER2-directed therapy

Escalation in the adjuvant setting is governed by one variable, and it is nodal status.

{{trial:aphinity}} randomised 4,805 patients with node-positive or high-risk node-negative disease
to pertuzumab or placebo added to adjuvant chemotherapy and one year of trastuzumab. Disease
recurrence occurred in 171 patients, which is 7.1%, on pertuzumab and 210, which is 8.7%, on
placebo. The hazard ratio was 0.81, with a 95% confidence interval of 0.66 to 1.00 and a p value
of 0.045. Three-year invasive disease-free survival was 94.1% against 93.2%. In node-positive
disease it was 92.0% against 90.2%, with a hazard ratio of 0.77. In node-negative disease it was
97.5% against 98.4%, with a hazard ratio of 1.13 and an interval of 0.68 to 1.86
[@vonminckwitz2017].

Longer follow-up sharpened the same split. At a median of 74 months the hazard ratio for invasive
disease-free survival was 0.76, with an interval of 0.64 to 0.91, and six-year rates were 91%
against 88%. In the node-positive cohort the hazard ratio was 0.72, with an interval of 0.59 to
0.87, and six-year rates were 88% against 83%. No benefit was seen in the node-negative cohort.
Overall survival did not reach its significance boundary, with a hazard ratio of 0.85 and a p
value of 0.17 against a required threshold of 0.0012. Six-year overall survival was 95% against
94%, with 125 deaths, which is 5.2%, against 147, which is 6.1%. Primary cardiac events remained
below 1% in both groups [@piccart2021aphinity].

Extended oral blockade is the other escalation option. In {{trial:extenet}}, 2,840 women who had
completed trastuzumab-based therapy received 12 months of neratinib or placebo. At two years there
had been 70 invasive disease-free survival events on neratinib and 109 on placebo. The stratified
hazard ratio was 0.67, with an interval of 0.50 to 0.91 and a p value of 0.0091. Two-year
invasive disease-free survival was 93.9% against 91.6%. Grade 3 diarrhoea occurred in 40% of
patients on neratinib and in 2% on placebo [@chan2016].

The arithmetic of that last trial is worth stating plainly. It bought 2.3 percentage points of
two-year invasive disease-free survival at the cost of grade 3 diarrhoea in two patients in five.
The agents themselves are covered in [[BC-450]].

## BS-4430 Hormone receptor-positive HER2-positive disease as a distinct algorithm

A tumour that is both hormone receptor-positive and HER2-positive carries two dependencies, and
the treatment algorithm has to service both on different timescales.

The first consequence is that it responds differently to HER2-directed neoadjuvant therapy. In
{{trial:destiny-b11}}, pathological complete response with the conjugate sequence against the
anthracycline sequence was 61.4% against 52.3% in hormone receptor-positive disease, a difference
of 9.1 percentage points with an interval of 0.2 to 17.9. In hormone receptor-negative disease it
was 83.1% against 67.1%, a difference of 16.1 points with an interval of 3.0 to 28.8
[@harbeck2026db11]. Both the absolute rates and the increment are lower when the oestrogen
receptor is present.

The second consequence is that the benefit of HER2-directed escalation is not obviously smaller,
which is counterintuitive. In the six-year {{trial:aphinity}} analysis the pertuzumab hazard ratio was 0.73
in hormone receptor-positive disease, with an interval of 0.59 to 0.92. In hormone
receptor-negative disease it was 0.83, with an interval of 0.63 to 1.10 [@piccart2021aphinity]. An exploratory
subgroup analysis of the same trial restricted itself to 4,782 evaluable cases. The largest
benefit fell in oestrogen receptor-positive tumours whose HER2 fluorescence in situ hybridisation
ratio was 2 to below 5, with a hazard ratio of 0.70 and an interval of 0.51 to 0.95
[@agostinetto2026]. That analysis is hypothesis-generating.

The third consequence is temporal. HER2-directed therapy finishes at one year. Endocrine therapy
runs for five to ten, and the late recurrence hazard described in [[BS-4350]] applies to this
population too. A patient who completes HER2-directed treatment has not completed treatment.

```interplay target=BC-280
Triple-positive disease is the clinical form of the two-receptor heterogeneity problem in
[[BS-2370]]. The compartment that survives HER2 blockade is not necessarily the compartment that
survives oestrogen deprivation, and a report calling both receptors positive has averaged twice
over a tumour that is uniform in neither. The lower pathological complete response rate in hormone
receptor-positive disease [@harbeck2026db11] is what that looks like from the clinic. It is also
why residual disease in this subtype should not be read as HER2-directed failure alone, and why
the conjugate literature in [[BC-460]] treats antigen level and dependence as separate variables.
```

## BS-4440 Chemotherapy-sparing strategies

Three lines of evidence say that some HER2-positive tumours are eradicated without cytotoxic
chemotherapy, and none of them yet says which ones.

The first is direct. In {{trial:neosphere}}, pertuzumab and trastuzumab given without any
chemotherapy produced a pathological complete response in the breast in 16.8% of patients, with a
95% confidence interval of 10.3 to 25.3. That arm recorded four serious adverse events in 4% of
patients, against 10% to 17% in the arms containing docetaxel [@gianni2012].

The second adds endocrine therapy to the antibodies in hormone receptor-positive disease. A
randomised phase II trial assigned 207 patients to 12 weeks of trastuzumab and pertuzumab with
either endocrine therapy or weekly paclitaxel. Pathological complete response was 23.7% with
endocrine therapy and 56.4% with paclitaxel [@tpii2026]. Five-year survival was high in both arms,
but the confidence intervals around the invasive disease-free survival estimates are wide enough
that the arms cannot be separated, which is what 207 patients buys.

The third uses imaging to decide who continues without chemotherapy. {{trial:phergain}} randomised
356 patients in a 1:4 ratio. The larger group received trastuzumab and pertuzumab with or without
endocrine therapy, with centrally reviewed positron emission tomography after two cycles. Patients
responding on imaging continued without chemotherapy, and those who did not respond switched to a
chemotherapy-containing regimen. Three-year invasive disease-free survival in that group was 94.8%,
with an interval of 91.4 to 97.1. Grade 3 or higher treatment-related adverse events occurred in
33% of that group against 62% of the chemotherapy group, and serious adverse events in 14% against
28%. Among responders who reached a pathological complete response, 1% had a grade 3 or higher
treatment-related adverse event and none had a serious one [@perezgarcia2024].

```caution
{{trial:phergain}} is read as showing that about a third of patients can safely omit chemotherapy. What it
demonstrated is that a response-adapted strategy produced a three-year rate of 94.8% in a phase 2
trial whose primary endpoint for that group was a single-arm rate against a fixed threshold
[@perezgarcia2024]. There is no randomised comparison against standard chemoimmunotherapy, and the
comparator group contained 71 patients. That is a promising strategy, not an established
equivalence, and [[BS-6370]] explains what would be needed to make it one.
```

```practice
Anthracycline omission is the de-escalation with the strongest evidence and the clearest toxicity
argument [@vandervoort2021; @harbeck2026db11]. It should be the default question rather than an
exception.

Chemotherapy omission remains a trial question outside a trial. Where it is used off protocol,
say that the supporting evidence is phase 2 and response-adapted [@perezgarcia2024].

In hormone receptor-positive disease, replacing the taxane with endocrine therapy more than halves
the pathological complete response rate [@tpii2026]. If the plan depends on observing a response,
that matters.
```

## BS-4450 Cardiac surveillance and cumulative toxicity

Two cardiac toxicities run through this chapter and they behave differently. Conflating them
produces the wrong surveillance schedule.

Anthracycline injury is dose-dependent and largely irreversible. In a prospective cohort of 2,625
patients receiving anthracycline-containing therapy with serial ejection fraction measurement,
cardiotoxicity occurred in 9%. The median interval from the end of chemotherapy to the event was
3.5 months, and 98% of events occurred within the first year. With heart failure therapy, 11% of
affected patients recovered fully and 71% recovered partially. Two variables predicted the event
independently. Ejection fraction at the end of chemotherapy carried a hazard ratio of 1.37 for
each one-point decrement. Cumulative doxorubicin dose carried a hazard ratio of 1.09 for each 50
mg per square metre [@cardinale2015].

HER2-directed injury is not dose-dependent in the same way and is usually reversible on stopping
the drug. In the randomised adjuvant setting the rates are low. Primary cardiac events remained
below 1% in both arms of {{trial:aphinity}} [@piccart2021aphinity].

The strongest cardiac intervention available is not surveillance. It is avoiding the anthracycline
where the evidence permits. In {{trial:train2}}, an ejection fraction fall of 10 points or more to
below 50% occurred in 7.7% of patients given an anthracycline and 3.2% of those who were not
[@vandervoort2021]. In {{trial:destiny-b11}}, all-grade left ventricular dysfunction occurred in
6.1% of the anthracycline arm against 0.7% and 1.3% in the two conjugate-containing arms
[@harbeck2026db11].

Baseline risk stratification, monitoring schedules and the management of asymptomatic ejection
fraction decline are codified in [@lyon2022], and the survivorship-phase management is in
[[BC-640]].

```practice
Measure ejection fraction before the first anthracycline dose and again at the end of
chemotherapy. The end-of-chemotherapy value was an independent predictor of subsequent
cardiotoxicity, alongside cumulative anthracycline dose [@cardinale2015].

Concentrate surveillance in the first year after treatment. That is when 98% of anthracycline
events occurred [@cardinale2015].

Start heart failure therapy when a decline is detected rather than when symptoms appear. Full or
partial recovery occurred in 82% of affected patients treated on that basis [@cardinale2015].

A fall on HER2-directed therapy usually warrants holding the drug and reassessing rather than
abandoning it. A fall after anthracycline is treated, not observed [@cardinale2015].

In a patient with cardiac risk factors, the anthracycline decision is the cardiology decision. The
evidence that it can be omitted under dual blockade is randomised [@vandervoort2021].
```
