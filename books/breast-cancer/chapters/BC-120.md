## BS-0990 Molecular diversity within triple-negative disease

Triple-negative disease is defined by three absences measured by two assays. It is not a positive
biological claim about anything. A tumour enters the category by failing to reach the oestrogen
receptor threshold, the progesterone receptor threshold and the HER2 threshold.

That has a consequence people underuse. The membership of the category depends on where the
thresholds sit. The current oestrogen receptor boundary is 1% of tumour nuclei [@allison2020]. At
a boundary of 10% the category would be larger, and the tumours added would resemble the ones
already in it. Among cancers staining 1% to 9%, 48% are basal-like by PAM50 and only 24% are
positive by ESR1 messenger RNA [@iwamoto2012]. Adjuvant endocrine therapy showed no association
with time to recurrence in that band [@fujii2017]. The measurement argument is in [[BC-110]].

What sits inside the category is genuinely diverse. Whole-genome sequencing of 560 breast cancers
identified 93 protein-coding genes carrying probable driver mutations, twelve base substitution
signatures and six rearrangement signatures [@nikzainal2016]. Three of the rearrangement
signatures appear associated with defective homologous-recombination-based repair, one of them
with deficient BRCA1 function specifically. Those processes are not distributed evenly across
triple-negative tumours, and the differences between them are what the subtyping schemes are
trying to capture.

The recurring axes are basal-like differentiation, homologous recombination deficiency, androgen
receptor signalling, immune infiltration and mesenchymal or metaplastic states. Several schemes
have been built on those axes. None of them is used to select treatment in routine care, and
[[BS-1060]] sets out why.

## BS-1000 Lehmann subtypes and their subsequent refinements

The original analysis clustered gene expression across 21 public data sets and produced six
subtypes, namely basal-like 1, basal-like 2, immunomodulatory, mesenchymal, mesenchymal stem-like
and luminal androgen receptor [@lehmann2011]. Each was matched to preclinical models so that
candidate targeted agents could be tested. The scheme was built to nominate drugs, which is worth
remembering when it is used for prognosis.

Retrospective response data followed. Among 130 evaluable patients given standard neoadjuvant
chemotherapy, pathological complete response reached 52% in basal-like 1, 0% in basal-like 2 and
10% in luminal androgen receptor [@masuda2013]. Subtype was an independent predictor of response
in that series. The per-subtype numbers are small and the confidence intervals are wide.

The refinement is the most instructive part of the story. Histopathological quantification and
laser-capture microdissection showed that the transcripts defining the immunomodulatory subtype
came from infiltrating lymphocytes, and that those defining the mesenchymal stem-like subtype came
from tumour-associated stroma [@lehmann2016]. Both were dropped. Four tumour-intrinsic classes
remained, namely basal-like 1, basal-like 2, mesenchymal and luminal androgen receptor. In over
300 retrospectively subtyped patients, pathological complete response was 41% in basal-like 1, 18%
in basal-like 2 and 29% in luminal androgen receptor.

Two of six original subtypes were properties of what else was in the sample. That is not a flaw in
the original work. It is what bulk expression measurement does, and it is the same composition
problem as in [[BS-0890]].

Other groups reached different partitions of the same disease. Analysis of 198 triple-negative
tumours produced four subtypes, namely luminal androgen receptor, mesenchymal, basal-like
immunosuppressed and basal-like immune-activated, with the immunosuppressed group carrying the
worst survival and the immune-activated group the best [@burstein2015]. A cohort of 465 primary
Chinese triple-negative tumours produced four again, namely luminal androgen receptor,
immunomodulatory, basal-like immune-suppressed and mesenchymal-like [@jiang2019].

## BS-1010 Basal-like and non-basal-like biology

Basal-like is the one intrinsic class that survives a change of classifier. Across three published
single-sample predictors applied to four cohorts, basal-like was the only subtype with almost
perfect agreement, above a kappa of 0.812 [@weigelt2010]. The proportion of tumours called
basal-like was stable whichever predictor was used. Nothing else in the intrinsic scheme behaves
that well.

Basal-like and triple-negative overlap without coinciding, and the two words are measured by
different things. Basal-like is an expression class assigned by a classifier. Triple-negative is a
pair of immunohistochemical results below threshold. Most basal-like tumours are triple negative
and most triple-negative tumours are basal-like, and neither statement is an identity.

Claudin-low illustrates how a class can dissolve under better data. It was originally described as
an additional intrinsic subtype, characterised by low expression of luminal differentiation
markers, enrichment for epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition genes, immune response genes and
stem-cell-like features [@prat2010]. A later reanalysis argued that claudin-low is not analogous
to the intrinsic subtypes at all [@fougner2020]. It behaves instead as a phenotype that can
permeate tumours of several intrinsic subtypes, distinguished by low genomic instability, low
mutational burden, low proliferation and high immune and stromal infiltration.

That reanalysis is worth reading as a template. A class defined by low expression of a marker set
is vulnerable to being a statement about what else is in the sample.

## BS-1020 DNA repair deficiency and genomic scarring

Three things get called the same name here, and separating them removes most of the confusion.

The cause is loss of a repair gene. That may be a germline or somatic BRCA1 or BRCA2 alteration,
or silencing of BRCA1 by promoter hypermethylation, which produces a molecular profile closely
resembling the mutated case [@glodzik2020].

The phenotype is impaired homologous recombination, which is a functional state of the cell at a
given moment [[term:hrd]].

The measurement is a scar score, a mutational signature or a functional assay, and each measures
the phenotype indirectly. The HRD score combines loss of heterozygosity, telomeric allelic
imbalance and large-scale state transitions, and it predicted response to platinum-containing
neoadjuvant chemotherapy in triple-negative disease [@telli2016]. HRDetect uses whole-genome
mutational signatures to predict BRCA1 and BRCA2 deficiency [@davies2017]. Rearrangement
signatures from whole-genome data track the same deficiency through a different feature set
[@nikzainal2016].

The critical property of a scar is temporal. A scar records that repair was deficient while the
genome was being damaged. It does not establish that repair is deficient now. A reversion mutation
restores function and leaves every existing scar in place. So scar-based assays have better
negative than positive predictive value for current sensitivity to a PARP inhibitor.

The clinical anchors remain narrower than the biology. Olaparib and talazoparib have established
benefit in germline BRCA1 or BRCA2 carriers with metastatic disease [@robson2017; @litton2018],
and adjuvant olaparib in the high-risk germline setting [@tutt2021]. {{trial:tbcrc048}} extended
activity to germline PALB2 and somatic BRCA alterations [@tung2020]. No scar score currently
carries a licensed indication in breast cancer. The repair biology is in [[BC-180]] and the assays
in [[BC-380]].

## BS-1030 Androgen receptor driven and luminal androgen receptor disease

Luminal androgen receptor is the most reproducible triple-negative subtype. It appears under
essentially the same name in three independently derived schemes [@lehmann2011; @burstein2015;
@jiang2019]. That reproducibility is a clue about its nature, because it is driven by a
transcriptional programme that is either running or not.

Its genomics differ from the rest of the category. Among 550 triple-negative tumours classified
from METABRIC and the Cancer Genome Atlas, the luminal androgen receptor group carried PIK3CA
mutations in 55%, AKT1 in 13% and CDH1 in 13% [@bareche2018]. In that multivariable analysis it
was the subtype associated with the worst prognosis, and the immunomodulatory subtype with the
best. In the Chinese cohort, the same subtype showed more ERBB2 somatic mutations, infrequent
mutational signature 3 and frequent CDKN2A loss [@jiang2019].

Androgen blockade has been tested twice, and the results are modest. Bicalutamide was given to
androgen receptor positive, ER and PR negative metastatic patients [@gucalp2013]. Of 424 screened,
12% were androgen receptor positive at a threshold of more than 10% nuclear staining. The
six-month clinical benefit rate was 19%, with a 95% confidence interval of 7% to 39%. Enzalutamide
enrolled 118 patients with androgen receptor positive triple-negative disease, using a threshold
of any nuclear staining above 0% [@traina2018]. Clinical benefit rate at 16 weeks was 25% in the
intention-to-treat population. It was 33% in the evaluable subgroup of 78 patients, defined by at
least 10% nuclear androgen receptor staining plus a postbaseline assessment. Median
progression-free survival was 2.9 months and 3.3 months respectively.

The measurement problem is visible on the surface of those two trials. They used different
androgen receptor thresholds, so they did not enrol the same population. No androgen-directed
therapy is approved in this setting.

## BS-1040 Immune-enriched and immune-excluded phenotypes

Tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes are the one immune measurement with an agreed scoring method and a
large evidence base. The International TILs Working Group set out how to score stromal lymphocytes
reproducibly [@salgado2015], and later documented the pitfalls that remain [@kos2020]. Incidence
and magnitude differ systematically by subtype [@stanton2016]. In a pooled analysis of 3771
patients treated with neoadjuvant therapy, higher lymphocytic infiltration was associated with
response and with outcome, and the association differed by subtype [@denkert2018]. A pooled
individual patient analysis in early triple-negative disease confirmed prognostic value
[@loi2019], and a separate series showed it in patients who received no adjuvant chemotherapy at
all [@park2019].

Density and arrangement are different measurements, and this is where the subtyping schemes
diverge from each other. Spatially distinct immune microenvironments stratify triple-negative
tumours into groups that a single percentage cannot separate [@gruosso2019]. Spatial
immunophenotypes predict response to PD-1 blockade and capture distinct routes of T cell evasion
[@hammerl2021]. Multiplexed ion beam imaging showed that triple-negative tumours are either
compartmentalised or mixed, with immune and tumour cells segregated or interleaved, and that the
distinction carries prognostic weight [@keren2018].

Two tumours can report the same lymphocyte percentage and have opposite architecture. An average
density discards the arrangement, in exactly the way that a HER2 percentage discards it in
[[BC-280]].

The clinical test of the immune classifier that exists is instructive. In {{trial:keynote522}},
adding pembrolizumab to neoadjuvant chemotherapy improved pathological complete response in early
triple-negative disease regardless of PD-L1 status [@schmid2020]. The biomarker did not select the
population that benefited. The immunology is developed in [[BC-210]] and the spatial argument in
[[BC-240]].

## BS-1050 Mesenchymal, metaplastic, and rare biological states

Metaplastic carcinoma is a rare special histological type within triple-negative disease, defined
by differentiation towards squamous epithelium or mesenchymal elements. Copy number profiling of
17 cases found only limited differences between the histological subgroups [@piscuoglio2017].
Expression profiling separated them more clearly, with spindle cell tumours differing from
chondroid and squamous tumours in epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition genes. The histological
patterns were judged unlikely to be underpinned by a recurrent expressed fusion gene or a
pathognomonic expressed mutation. PTEN loss or mutations affecting PIK3CA or PIK3R1 were present
in 8 of 17 cases.

So the histological subgroups of metaplastic carcinoma are transcriptionally distinct and are not
defined by a specific genetic lesion. That is a different situation from most special types.

The mesenchymal signal in bulk data has a second source that is not the tumour. Part of it came
from tumour-associated stroma, which is why the mesenchymal stem-like subtype was withdrawn
[@lehmann2016]. A mesenchymal call on a bulk sample may be a statement about the specimen.

Epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition itself is not binary. Multiple intermediate states exist and
they differ in tumour-initiating capacity and metastatic behaviour [@pastushenko2018].
Differentiation-state plasticity in basal-like disease behaves as a resistance mechanism rather
than as a fixed subtype [@risom2018]. A transcriptional state that can change under pressure will
not stay put long enough to be a classification.

Not every triple-negative tumour behaves badly. Several rare histological types, including adenoid
cystic and secretory carcinoma, are usually triple negative by immunohistochemistry and follow an
indolent course [@loibl2021]. The immunophenotype is not the prognosis, and morphology outperforms
it in these cases. The histology is developed in [[BC-090]].

## BS-1060 Why TNBC classifiers disagree with one another

No triple-negative subtyping system is used to select treatment in routine care anywhere. The
reasons divide into four, and they are not equally fixable.

The first is input material. Bulk expression measures a mixture of tumour, immune and stromal
cells. Two of the six original subtypes were shown to come from lymphocytes and stroma rather than
from the tumour [@lehmann2016]. Any scheme built on bulk data inherits that problem.

The second is cohort. The Chinese cohort found PIK3CA mutations and copy number gains at 22q11
more frequent than in the Cancer Genome Atlas [@jiang2019]. A classifier trained on one population
does not automatically transfer to another.

The third is algorithm and class number. The schemes propose six, four and four classes on
overlapping biology. Nearest-centroid assignment forces every sample into a class, and the
instability that causes is measurable even for the intrinsic subtypes, where only basal-like
agreed across predictors [@weigelt2010].

The fourth is purpose. The Lehmann scheme was built to nominate preclinical models and candidate
targets [@lehmann2011]. The Burstein scheme was built around prognosis [@burstein2015]. They are
not competing answers to one question.

Is the disagreement biological or methodological? Mostly methodological, with one real biological
component underneath it. The underlying variation is continuous, and every scheme imposes discrete
classes on a continuum. Luminal androgen receptor is the most reproducible class precisely because
it is the most nearly discrete, being driven by a transcriptional programme that is switched on or
off. Immune and mesenchymal classes are the least reproducible because they sit on gradients, and
because they are partly contributed by cells that are not the tumour.

The deciding argument is the one in [[BS-0970]]. These schemes have shown clinical validity in
retrospective series with small per-subtype numbers [@masuda2013; @lehmann2016]. None has been
tested for clinical utility, which would require randomising a treatment decision on the subtype
call. Until that is done, a subtype is a research finding with a prognosis attached.

```caution
The natural reading of four competing classifiers is that one of them is right. That reading is
almost certainly wrong. They are four discretisations of a continuum, built for different purposes
on different material. Asking which is correct is less useful than asking which axis a particular
patient's tumour actually sits on, and whether anything can be done about it.
```
