## BS-6970 Scope, and the two distinct decisions this chapter serves

This chapter serves two decisions that share a subtype and almost nothing else.

The first belongs to a patient taken straight to surgery. The specimen is out and the pathology is
final. No tumour in this patient was ever exposed to a drug under observation. Everything that
follows is inferred from stage, nodal status, hormone receptor status, age and comorbidity.
Sections [[BS-6980]], [[BS-6990]], [[BS-7000]] and [[BS-7020]] serve that decision.

The second belongs to a patient who received neoadjuvant therapy and has residual invasive disease
at surgery. That tumour has already met HER2-directed therapy and survived it. The selection
variable here is observed rather than predicted, which is why [[BS-7010]] serves that decision and
no other section does.

Merging the two decisions is the commonest error in this subtype, and it is usually made in the
favourable direction. The adjuvant trials randomised patients after definitive surgery, so their
populations contain nobody whose response was known. The post-neoadjuvant trials enrolled only
patients whose response was known and inadequate. A hazard ratio from one population does not
transfer to the other.

Neighbouring chapters carry the rest, beginning with the choice between surgery first and
treatment first in [[BC-500]] and [[BS-4370]]. Neoadjuvant regimens are in [[BC-830]], with
hormone receptor-positive HER2-positive disease taking its own algorithm in [[BC-840]]. Conjugate
design, resistance and toxicity are in [[BC-460]] and are cited here rather than repeated.

Read the tables this way, since each is generated from the trial registry at build time. Each
table answers the question of the section that carries it, so a trial recurs where it bears on
more than one. The result and overall survival cells are taken from the publication named in the
reference column, and a row and its citation therefore always agree. Longer follow-up, subgroup
structure and cross-trial comparison are argued in the prose.

Two of the [Chapter Stories](stories.html) run through this chapter, one for each decision. Each
section below opens with the questions it owns, written as what was being asked, what came back
and what the design could not settle. The stories carry no figures at all, so a story and a table
can never disagree about a number.

## BS-6980 The trastuzumab adjuvant trials and the duration question

```story SQ-0180,SQ-0190
```

```evidence setting=early subtype=HER2+ line=adjuvant topic=pivotal sort=year cols=acronym,n,arms,endpoint,result,os caption=The_trials_that_established_adjuvant_trastuzumab._Each_fixed_its_duration_by_protocol_rather_than_by_comparison.
```

Four trials established the class and none of them tested how much of it to give. They agree on
the finding and differ on nearly everything used to produce it. Recurrence fell in every
comparison, and where follow-up ran longest the curves had not reconverged.

The differences between them are design rather than drug. The antibody ran with the taxane in the
American joint analysis and in BCIRG-006. It began only after all chemotherapy had finished in
HERA. The backbone carried an anthracycline in three of the four, and in one BCIRG-006 arm it did
not. The antibody ran for a year in three and for nine weeks in the fourth. Each of those choices
was written into a protocol rather than randomised, so the table records four conventions
alongside its four comparisons.

One of the four carries the survival claim. NSABP B-31 and N9831 were analysed jointly against a
prespecified number of deaths, on randomisations that stayed intact throughout [@perez2014]. HERA
reached a similar hazard for death, but half of its observation group crossed over to trastuzumab
once the first results were public [@cameron2017]. Crossover on that scale dilutes a survival
comparison by design, and no amount of further follow-up repairs it. HERA is the trial that
answers the duration question. The joint analysis is the trial that answers the survival one.

BCIRG-006 was asked a second question, and its answer is weaker than the way it is usually quoted.
Both trastuzumab regimens beat chemotherapy alone. Neither was set against the other under a
non-inferiority design, and the trial reports no significant difference between the two
[@slamon2011]. That is an absence of evidence rather than a demonstration of equivalence. The
cardiac case for dropping the anthracycline is stronger than the efficacy case, and it is argued
in [[BS-7030]].

The short-course exception is worth naming, because everything downstream came out of it.
{{trial:finher}} gave nine weeks of trastuzumab rather than a year, and it found the same kind of
recurrence benefit [@joensuu2006]. That result came from a 232-patient HER2-positive substudy
nested inside a randomisation of two different chemotherapies. A signal from a nested substudy is
a reason to run a duration trial. It is not a duration result.

Longer is now closed. HERA is the only trial that randomised a second year, and the second year
bought nothing on disease-free survival. It cost cardiac function, and that trade is in
[[BS-7030]].

Shorter is not closed. Four trials tested it and the conclusion in each turns on the margin rather
than on the estimate.

```evidence setting=early subtype=HER2+ line=adjuvant topic=shorter sort=n cols=acronym,n,arms,endpoint,result,os caption=The_four_trials_of_less_than_a_year._Read_the_endpoint_column_before_the_result_column,_because_the_margin_is_where_these_trials_differ_most.
```

[[BS-6370]] names four things to establish before accepting any non-inferiority claim. The margin
and its justification. Whether a per-protocol analysis accompanied the intention-to-treat one. The
observed control event rate against the rate the power calculation assumed. And whether the margin
was set on an absolute or a relative scale. Three of the four decide this section.

Start with the two six-month trials, because they are the pair that makes the point. PHARE
observed a hazard ratio of 1.08 and did not declare non-inferiority. PERSEPHONE observed 1.07 and
did. Two trials of one comparison, in broadly similar populations, reached opposite verdicts on
estimates that differ by one hundredth.

The margin separated them, and so did the scale the margin was set on. PHARE set a hazard margin
of 1.15. PERSEPHONE set three percentage points of four-year disease-free survival. A fixed
allowance in percentage points becomes more permissive the fewer events the control arm has, and
events in this population are few. Converted to the scale PHARE used, the allowance PERSEPHONE
worked to was the wider of the two by a long way.

Put the two allowances side by side and the verdicts stop contradicting each other. PERSEPHONE
established that six months is not worse by more than its own generous allowance. PHARE
established that six months has not been shown to be less than 15% worse on the hazard scale. Both
statements are true of closely similar data. The conclusion that reached practice was written by
whoever set the wider margin.

That is the lesson of [[BS-6370]] arriving inside a decision made weekly. A margin expressed in
percentage points behaves differently from one expressed as a ratio, and the gap between them
widens as the control event rate falls. Neither trial did anything improper, and neither result
should be described as a mistake. Only one of them published an allowance that a patient would
have been asked to accept in advance.

The four shorter trials also differ structurally, and the differences sit upstream of any margin.
PHARE randomised patients who were already receiving adjuvant trastuzumab, between the third and
sixth month, so its population had already tolerated the first part of the course. PERSEPHONE
randomised at the outset, with the antibody given alongside or after chemotherapy. Short-HER used
sequential anthracycline and taxane. SOLD gave docetaxel with trastuzumab up front and then
fluorouracil, epirubicin and cyclophosphamide. SOLD also reported that patients given the lower
docetaxel dose had inferior disease-free survival [@joensuu2018]. Part of what reads as a duration
effect in that trial may be a chemotherapy dose effect, and its design cannot separate the two.

The two nine-week trials are not a matched pair either, and they should not be read as one
finding. Short-HER's interval crossed its own margin and also crossed equality, so it is
inconclusive in both directions. SOLD's interval excluded equality at a lower bound of 1.12, so
its estimate points to harm rather than to absent proof. Failing to demonstrate non-inferiority
and demonstrating inferiority are different findings. Only SOLD produced the second, and that
distinction should reach the patient being offered the short course.

The third of the four checks is the one these trials make hardest to apply. A margin is justified
against an assumed control event rate, so it becomes more or less demanding as the observed rate
departs from that assumption. Recurrence in this subtype has fallen steadily as the rest of
treatment improved, which makes every margin set a decade ago more permissive than it looked when
it was written.

Two things the table cannot show constrain all of this. None of the four randomised duration
inside a defined risk stratum. A failed non-inferiority claim in an unselected population
therefore says nothing about the small node-negative tumour, which is the commonest candidate for
a short course.
And none of them gave dual blockade, so no shortened course has ever been tested under two
antibodies.

Four trials therefore left three states of knowledge rather than one answer. Six months is
acceptable under a generous margin and unproven under a strict one. Nine weeks is unproven in one
trial and probably worse in the other.

```practice
One year remains the default. The evidence for it is a design convention that four trials then
failed to improve on, rather than a prospective comparison of durations.

Six months is defensible where cardiac risk, frailty, distance or cost make twelve unattractive.
Say what the supporting trial allowed. Its margin was an absolute difference in disease-free
survival at a fixed time point, which in a population with few events is a wide allowance
[@earl2019].

Nine weeks is for a patient who has already had a cardiac event on treatment, or whose baseline
risk is very low. One trial of it was inconclusive and the other pointed towards harm [@conte2018;
@joensuu2018].

None of the four duration trials used dual blockade and most predate routine pertuzumab. A duration
result obtained under trastuzumab alone does not license a shortened course under two antibodies.
```

## BS-6990 Dual blockade in the adjuvant setting

```story SQ-0210
```

```evidence setting=early subtype=HER2+ line=adjuvant topic=dual sort=year cols=acronym,n,arms,endpoint,result,os caption=Three_ways_of_adding_a_second_HER2-directed_agent_after_surgery._One_worked_in_one_stratum_and_two_did_not_work_at_all.
```

Three trials asked whether more HER2 blockade after surgery lowers recurrence, and all three were
built on chemotherapy with a year of trastuzumab. Only one moved its endpoint. Reading them as
three verdicts on dual blockade misreads them, because they differ in what they added, in what
they replaced and in the threshold each was judged against.

APHINITY is the one that worked, and its benefit should be described honestly. The gain sits in
the node-positive stratum, and the node-negative stratum shows nothing
[@vonminckwitz2017; @piccart2021aphinity]. That split is the whole clinical content of the trial.

Nodal status is not a subgroup here in the usual weak sense. It was a stratification factor rather
than a post-hoc slice, so the strata are balanced on everything randomisation balances. Balance is
not the issue. A difference in effect between two strata is not itself a randomised comparison, so
it carries less weight than the overall result does.

ALTTO is the instructive failure, and what makes it instructive is arithmetic rather than biology.
Its lapatinib-alone arm closed for futility during the trial, and the protocol was then amended to
require a p value at or below 0.025 for the two remaining comparisons. The combination against
trastuzumab returned 0.048 [@piccart2016altto]. The same estimate reads as a narrow miss or as a
small real effect depending on which threshold is applied to it. The sequential arm is the cleaner
result and it is rarely quoted. Giving lapatinib after trastuzumab rather than with it added
nothing, which separates the question of a second block from the question of which second block.

ALTTO carries a second lesson that belongs to the whole subtype. Its drug pair had raised
pathological complete response substantially before surgery, and the adjuvant trial then failed in
more than eight thousand patients. That sequence is the subject of [[BS-7070]] and its methodology
is in [[BS-6360]]. A response gain measured at one operation predicted nothing about recurrence
years later.

KAITLIN tested substitution rather than addition, which is a different question with a different
failure mode. Pertuzumab ran in both arms, so KAITLIN is not a test of dual blockade at all. It
replaced the taxane and trastuzumab portion of the regimen with a conjugate, and invasive
disease-free survival was unchanged in both coprimary populations. Completion of the planned 18
cycles fell from 88.4% to 65.0%, largely through discontinuation for laboratory abnormality
[@krop2022kaitlin]. That is a comparison of strategies as delivered rather than of drugs as
prescribed. A regimen two patients in five do not finish is a different regimen from the one that
was designed.

The decision rests on APHINITY alone, because it is the only one of the three that randomised an
addition against what the clinic would otherwise give. What the table cannot show is the rest of
that decision. Nobody randomised the duration of pertuzumab, which was fixed at a year by the same
convention that fixed trastuzumab. Nobody powered a trial on node-negative disease alone, so the
absence of benefit there rests on a stratum rather than on a trial. And overall survival has not
separated across three interim analyses [@loibl2024].

```practice
Add pertuzumab for node-positive disease. Quote the absolute difference rather than the hazard
ratio, because the relative effect flatters it [[BS-5690]].

Do not add pertuzumab for node-negative disease. The randomised evidence there shows no benefit at
all [@piccart2021aphinity].

Do not substitute trastuzumab emtansine for the taxane after an anthracycline. It did not improve
outcome and far fewer patients completed it [@krop2022kaitlin].

Do not read ALTTO as a verdict on pertuzumab. It tested a different second agent against a
threshold that moved mid-trial [@piccart2016altto].

Tell a node-positive patient consenting today that the survival endpoint has not separated. What
remains open is set out in [[BS-7040]].
```

## BS-7000 De-escalated adjuvant therapy for small node-negative tumours

```story SQ-0200
```

```evidence setting=early subtype=HER2+ line=adjuvant topic=de-escalated sort=year cols=acronym,n,population,arms,endpoint,result caption=The_two_de-escalated_adjuvant_regimens_for_stage_I_disease._Neither_row_is_a_randomised_comparison_against_a_full_regimen.
```

Patients with small node-negative tumours were largely excluded from the registration trials
[@romond2005]. The regimen used for them was therefore built rather than inherited, and the way it
was built constrains what can be claimed for it. Both rows agree that outcomes in this population
are excellent. Neither row is a comparison against a full regimen, and the reason differs between
them.

APT has no comparator at all, which fixes in advance the ceiling on what it can claim. A
single-group trial in a population with a very low event rate can show only that outcomes are
excellent. It cannot show that a shorter regimen is equivalent to a longer one, because the longer
one is absent from the dataset. No margin was stated and none could have been tested. Ten-year
follow-up has since been reported and it does not change that structure [@tolaney2023a].

ATEMPT was randomised, and its primary endpoint was toxicity rather than efficacy. It was designed
to detect a difference in clinically relevant toxicity, not to exclude a loss of efficacy. The 3:1
allocation left roughly 120 patients in the comparator arm. An efficacy non-inferiority claim from
that arm is unavailable at any margin a clinician would accept [[BS-6370]].

Its null toxicity result also needs reading as a statement about a composite. The totals matched.
That is not the same as the two regimens costing a patient the same things. Neuropathy and
alopecia sit on one side of the trade and hepatic and haematological abnormality on the other
[@tolaney2021atempt].

De-escalation has been tried once from the other end, in the neoadjuvant setting rather than here.
{{trial:adapt-her2-hr}} asked whether patients responding early to dual blockade could be spared
the taxane altogether, and dropping it lost pathological response, including among the early
responders it was meant to protect. That result was read on response rather than on recurrence, in
hormone receptor-negative disease, with very few patients in each response group. It narrows the
question without settling it.

What a real de-escalation trial here would need is worth stating, because it explains why none
exists. A margin narrow enough to matter, against a control event rate of a few per cent, demands
a sample size that no sponsor will fund for a population this favourable. The gap is therefore
structural rather than an oversight.

The honest position is narrow. Twelve weeks of paclitaxel with trastuzumab produces excellent
outcomes in node-negative tumours of 3 cm or less, and that is the whole of what has been shown.
Trastuzumab emtansine produces excellent outcomes in stage I disease with a different toxicity
profile rather than a smaller one. Neither statement is a demonstration of non-inferiority against
a full regimen.

```caution
An impressive event-free rate in a low-risk population is not evidence of equivalence. It is mostly
evidence that the population was low risk. The rate would also have been high on a longer regimen,
and nothing in either trial measures the gap between the two. Treat the phrase "non-inferior" as
unavailable here, because no margin was ever set.
```

```practice
For a node-negative tumour of 2 cm or less, weekly paclitaxel with trastuzumab is the default
[@tolaney2015]. Describe the evidence as a single-arm trial with an excellent rate.

Choose trastuzumab emtansine where neuropathy or alopecia dominates the conversation rather than
where total toxicity does, since clinically relevant toxicity was not reduced
[@tolaney2021atempt].

T1a and T1b node-negative tumours sit outside even these datasets. Treatment there is extrapolation,
and the consultation should say so.
```

## BS-7010 Post-neoadjuvant intensification for residual disease

```story SQ-0230,SQ-0240,SQ-0250
```

```evidence setting=early subtype=HER2+ line=post-neoadjuvant modality=adc status=reported sort=year cols=acronym,n,population,arms,endpoint,result,os caption=The_two_reported_trials_in_this_position._The_population_column_decides_which_of_them_a_patient_belongs_to.
```

Residual invasive disease after adequate neoadjuvant HER2-directed therapy is the strongest
selection rule available in early breast cancer. It identifies a tumour that has met the drug and
survived it, in this patient rather than in a population resembling them. That is a measured
property of this tumour rather than a prediction about tumours like it. The rule was not invented
here. {{trial:create-x}} had already shown that escalating chemotherapy on residual disease lowers
recurrence in HER2-negative disease, which supplies the principle without supplying the subtype.

Both trials in the table agree that a conjugate given on that selection rule lowers invasive
recurrence. They differ in the one thing that decides what each can claim, which is the control
arm. KATHERINE's control was the antibody the tumour had already grown through, so the trial
establishes that a conjugate beats continuing an agent known to have failed in that patient. It
does not establish that a conjugate beats any other escalation that might have been offered.
DESTINY-Breast05 randomised against a comparator already known to work, which makes it the harder
comparison and the more informative one [@loibl2026db05].

Maturity is the second difference and it is follow-up rather than drug. KATHERINE has now run long
enough for an early recurrence benefit to become an overall survival benefit [@geyer2025]. That
conversion is uncommon in this book, and it is why the conjugate rather than the antibody became
the comparator for everything that came after. DESTINY-Breast05 reported recurrence at a median of
about 30 months with overall survival immature. Nothing in the contrast between the two survival
cells is a statement about the newer drug.

The same drug without the selection rule did nothing, which is the cleanest available check on
whether the rule is doing the work. KAITLIN gave trastuzumab emtansine with pertuzumab after
upfront surgery, selected on nodal status and tumour size, and matched the taxane arm rather than
beating it [[BS-6990]]. Observed resistance and predicted risk are not interchangeable selection
variables.

The eligible population of DESTINY-Breast05 is narrower than KATHERINE's, and this is routinely
missed. Entry required residual invasive disease together with node-positive disease at surgery,
or inoperable disease at diagnosis [@loibl2026db05]. A patient with small-volume node-negative
residual disease was studied in KATHERINE and was not studied in DESTINY-Breast05.

That boundary is an eligibility rule rather than a finding, and nobody randomised the threshold
itself. It was chosen once for one trial and then inherited by the trials that followed. Nodal
status after neoadjuvant therapy is also a staging convention before it is a biological variable.
It depends on how the axilla was sampled and on how many nodes were examined, and the conventions
are in [[BS-0790]]. A patient called node-negative after a sentinel procedure has not had the same
test as one after a dissection.

The biological argument for the newer payload rests on a study that no table here carries.
{{trial:her2-heterogeneity}} gave the older conjugate with pertuzumab before surgery and compared
prospectively defined heterogeneous and non-heterogeneous tumours. No tumour called heterogeneous
reached a pathological complete response. Heterogeneity there was read from two biopsies
per tumour against a fixed cut-off for the proportion of amplified cells. The exposure variable is
therefore a sampling scheme and a scoring convention before it is a property of the disease. The groups were
never randomised and the heterogeneous group was very small. It is a reason to expect a
bystander-capable payload to do better and not a demonstration that it does.

One further limitation applies to both trials and is easy to miss. Entry required residual disease
after a taxane and trastuzumab, with an anthracycline optional and pertuzumab not yet routine.
Residual disease in these trials therefore followed a weaker regimen than residual disease follows
in clinic today. The population that survives current neoadjuvant treatment is a harder one.

```caution
The recurrence benefit in DESTINY-Breast05 is large and the price is respiratory. Adjudicated
drug-related interstitial lung disease occurred in 9.6% of patients on trastuzumab deruxtecan
against 1.6% on trastuzumab emtansine, and two patients with it died [@loibl2026db05]. Overall
survival is immature, so the current position is a measured recurrence benefit, an unmeasured
survival benefit, and two treatment-related deaths from a toxicity that is detectable early. The
surveillance that separates grade 1 from grade 5 is in [[BS-4100]] and belongs in place before the
first dose.
```

```practice
Ask one question before choosing the agent. Was the disease node-positive at surgery, or inoperable
at diagnosis? If yes, the patient matches the DESTINY-Breast05 population. If no, the patient
matches KATHERINE, and the randomised evidence for the newer conjugate does not cover them.

Adequate neoadjuvant therapy is part of the selection rule. Residual disease after an inadequate
regimen is a different finding, because the tumour was never properly tested.

Score the specimen so that the rule can be applied. Nodal status after treatment is the variable
both trials selected on, and the conventions are in [[BS-0790]].
```

## BS-7020 Extended adjuvant therapy after trastuzumab

```story SQ-0220
```

```story SQ-0270
```

```evidence setting=early subtype=HER2+ line=adjuvant topic=neratinib cols=acronym,n,population,arms,endpoint,result,os caption=The_only_trial_of_extended_oral_HER2_blockade_after_a_completed_year_of_trastuzumab.
```

One row, and it is the only randomised test of extending HER2-directed treatment with a different
agent after the antibody is finished. The other way of asking the question was put to the antibody
itself, and HERA answered it in [[BS-6980]]. A second year of trastuzumab added nothing over the
first. More time on treatment is therefore not a class effect, and ExteNET is a claim about a
kinase inhibitor rather than about duration.

State the trade plainly, because the two sides of it are measured to very different standards.
ExteNET lowered invasive recurrence against placebo inside its primary window. Grade 3 diarrhoea
occurred in 40% of patients against 2% on placebo, in a trial run without antidiarrhoeal
prophylaxis [@chan2016; @martin2017]. The tolerability on record is therefore a statement about
how the trial was run rather than about the molecule.

The hormone receptor-positive subgroup is where the drug is now used, and the claim needs
handling. Hormone receptor status was a randomisation stratification factor, so the subgroups are
balanced. Balance is not what fails here. A comparison between two subgroups is not itself a
randomised comparison, and a difference in effect across strata carries far less weight than the
overall result. Treat the restriction as a plausible reading of a stratified analysis rather than
as a demonstrated interaction [@chan2020].

The endpoint window constrains the claim further, and in the direction that matters most. The
primary analysis read invasive disease-free survival at an early landmark. The group the drug is
now aimed at is the group whose recurrences arrive late, so the result was declared before that
group had time to relapse. Overall survival did not separate at the final analysis [@holmes2023].

A second feature narrows the population again. Eligibility was amended during recruitment from
stage 1 to 3c down to stage 2 to 3c [@martin2017]. The enrolled population therefore changed
partway through accrual, and a reader applying the result should be clear which population they
mean.

```caution
ExteNET predates routine adjuvant pertuzumab and predates post-neoadjuvant conjugates entirely. A
patient who has completed trastuzumab emtansine or trastuzumab deruxtecan for residual disease was
not represented in it. Extending with neratinib after a conjugate is extrapolation, and the toxicity
is not.
```

## BS-7030 Cardiac safety across the adjuvant trials

The two cardiac toxicities in this chapter behave differently and the mechanisms are set out in
[[BS-4450]]. Surveillance schedules and the management of asymptomatic decline are in [[BC-640]]
and [[BS-5480]]. This section adds only what the adjuvant trials themselves settle.

The duration trials are the cleanest cardiac dataset in breast oncology, because they randomised
exposure time rather than observing it. HERA randomised a second year, and secondary cardiac
endpoints occurred in 7.3% of the two-year group, 4.4% of the one-year group and 0.9% of the
observation group [@cameron2017]. PERSEPHONE randomised half the exposure and early
discontinuation for cardiotoxicity fell from 8% to 3% [@earl2019]. Short-HER randomised nine weeks
and reported a cardiac event risk ratio of 0.33, with an interval of 0.22 to 0.50 [@conte2018].
SOLD reported better maintenance of ejection fraction with nine weeks [@joensuu2018].

Read those four results together. Trastuzumab cardiotoxicity is usually described as idiosyncratic
and reversible, in contrast to the dose-dependent anthracycline injury. The duration trials show
that exposure time behaves as a dose. That is a randomised dose-response relationship, and it is
the reason a shortened course is a genuine cardiac intervention rather than a concession.

It also changes how the margin argument in [[BS-6980]] should be used, and it is the one place in
this chapter where a de-escalation can be defended on positive evidence. A patient with real
cardiac risk is not choosing between equal options under an uncertain margin. They are trading an
unproven loss of disease control against a randomised reduction in cardiac events. The two sides
of that trade are known to different standards, and the consultation should say which is which.

The anthracycline is the other lever and it was tested directly. In BCIRG-006, congestive heart
failure and cardiac dysfunction were significantly more frequent with the anthracycline and
trastuzumab regimen than with docetaxel, carboplatin and trastuzumab. Seven of the eight reported
leukaemias arose in anthracycline-containing groups [@slamon2011].

```practice
Treat planned trastuzumab duration as a modifiable cardiac exposure in a patient with cardiac risk
factors. The evidence that shorter means safer is randomised in four trials.

Treat anthracycline omission as the larger cardiac decision. A non-anthracycline regimen with
trastuzumab was not less effective in BCIRG-006 and was clearly less cardiotoxic [@slamon2011].

Measure ejection fraction before the anthracycline and again at the end of chemotherapy, and
concentrate surveillance in the first year [[BS-4450]].

A fall on HER2-directed therapy warrants holding the drug and reassessing. A fall after an
anthracycline is treated rather than observed.
```

## BS-7040 Unresolved questions and what reads out next

```evidence setting=early subtype=HER2+ line=adjuvant,post-neoadjuvant status=ongoing,awaited cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,endpoint,status caption=Adjuvant_and_post-neoadjuvant_HER2_trials_that_have_not_reported._Both_add_an_agent_to_a_backbone_that_a_newer_conjugate_has_already_beaten.
```

```story SQ-0260,SQ-0280
```

Two trials are running in this position and they are built the same way. Each adds a second agent
to trastuzumab emtansine in residual disease, each randomises against placebo, and each admits a
narrower population than KATHERINE did. CompassHER2 RD requires oestrogen receptor-negative or
node-positive residual disease and adds tucatinib [@osullivan2021compassher2rd]. ASTEFANIA
requires residual disease called high risk and adds atezolizumab [@hurvitz2022]. Neither has
reported anything, so the status column is the only cell in that table with a result in it.

Read the other cells with care. ASTEFANIA's enrolment figure comes from its design paper and is a
plan rather than a count of patients randomised. CompassHER2 RD carries no enrolment figure at
all. Nothing in either row is an outcome, and a reader who sees an endpoint named should read it
as an intention.

What divides the two trials is the strength of the evidence behind each partner rather than
anything in their designs. In {{trial:her2climb}} tucatinib lengthened progression-free and
overall survival in metastatic disease already treated with the antibodies and the conjugate, and
it delayed progression in the patients with brain metastases. In {{trial:impassion050}}
atezolizumab did not raise pathological complete response when added to preoperative chemotherapy
and dual blockade, and it did not do so in the tumours selected for PD-L1 expression either. Borrowed evidence is weaker
still for being borrowed across settings. The tucatinib result comes from disease that had already
progressed on the conjugate, which is not the biology of disease left behind at surgery. The
checkpoint result comes from before surgery and reports a response endpoint.

Both trials share a problem neither created. DESTINY-Breast05 has displaced their comparator in
the node-positive population. A positive result from either will therefore show that an addition
improves on a backbone already beaten by a different drug. No trial compares the two strategies,
and none is planned.

The preoperative regimen is moving underneath all of this, which is the second half of the
problem. Those regimens belong to [[BC-830]] and are not restated here, but their consequence
belongs here, and it is a denominator that shrinks while its composition changes.
{{trial:destiny-b11}} raised pathological complete response against the anthracycline standard,
which removes patients from this position. {{trial:compassher2pcr}} gave a chemotherapy-light
preoperative regimen and left most patients with residual disease, predominantly oestrogen
receptor-positive. {{trial:kristine}} substituted a conjugate for chemotherapy, lowered
pathological complete response and enlarged the residual-disease population, and recurrence on
follow-up was nonetheless more frequent than on chemotherapy. All three moved a surrogate first,
and the recurrence endpoints are immature or unreported. A tumour that survives a conjugate before
surgery has not been studied by anything in this chapter.

Four questions have no trial addressing them at all.

Whether the DESTINY-Breast05 benefit extends to node-negative residual disease. The trial excluded
that group and there is no plan to study it separately.

Whether trastuzumab deruxtecan, or trastuzumab emtansine with tucatinib, is the better choice for
a patient at risk of brain metastasis. No randomised trial exists or is planned to make that
comparison.

Whether a shortened trastuzumab course is acceptable under dual blockade. Every duration trial
predates routine pertuzumab, and the cardiac argument for shortening is strongest in exactly the
patients who now receive two antibodies.

Whether molecular residual disease can select post-neoadjuvant intensification better than
pathology can. The assay question is in [[BS-3480]] and the lead-time problem in [[BS-3490]].

One older question remains open on its own terms. Overall survival in APHINITY has not crossed its
boundary [@piccart2021aphinity; @loibl2024]. The trial continues, and a node-positive patient
consenting to pertuzumab today is consenting on a recurrence benefit rather than a survival one.

```interplay target=BC-350
Both decisions in this chapter are attempts to act on a tumour that is no longer in the body. The
adjuvant decision infers risk from a resected specimen. The post-neoadjuvant decision infers it from
what a specimen looked like after treatment. Part VI argues that a single specimen misrepresents a
heterogeneous tumour, and residual disease is where that argument bites hardest. The surviving
compartment after HER2 blockade need not carry the antigen level the diagnostic biopsy reported
[[BS-2340]]. That is one reason a bystander-capable conjugate outperforms a non-bystander one in
exactly this population, and the mechanism is in [[BS-4060]] rather than here.
