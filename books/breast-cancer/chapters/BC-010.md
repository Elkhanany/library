## BS-0010 Early descriptions and the humoral idea of a systemic disease

Descriptions of breast tumours are older than any theory that explains them. Ancient surviving texts
record hard swellings of the breast. They also record that cutting them out did not help. The
observation was sound. The explanation attached to it was not.

The humoral account held that cancer arose from an excess of black bile. An excess of a body fluid
is systemic by definition. A systemic cause implies that local excision cannot cure, which is why
operating was often withheld rather than attempted.

It is worth noticing what that model got right. It predicted recurrence at a distance after
successful local treatment. Recurrence at a distance is exactly what happens. The model reached a
correct prediction from a premise about bile that is false.

The error corrected was the idea that the palpable lump is the whole disease. The error introduced
was a systemic model so unconditional that local treatment became pointless in every case. Both
halves of that mistake return later in this chapter wearing different clothes.

## BS-0020 The anatomic turn and the discovery of lymphatic spread

Dissection replaced humours with structures. The lymphatic system was described, and breast tumours
were found to involve the axillary nodes. A physical route from breast to axilla was now visible in
the cadaver.

The consequence for surgery followed immediately. If disease travels along a described anatomic
path, then following that path with a knife is a coherent plan. Removal of the breast together with
the axillary contents became the operation of the era.

This is the point at which the argument becomes an argument about route rather than about cause.
The question shifts from what makes a cancer to where a cancer goes. The answer was taken from
anatomy rather than from any comparison of outcomes.

The error corrected was a cause with no anatomy attached to it. The error introduced was the
assumption that the visible route is the only route. Nothing in the dissection evidence excluded a
second pathway through the bloodstream. Nobody was looking for one.

## BS-0030 Halsted, the centrifugal model, and the logic of radical surgery

Halsted reported the results of the radical operation performed at his hospital over roughly five
years [@halsted1894]. The procedure removed the breast, the pectoral muscles and the axillary
contents in continuity.

The model behind the operation matters more than the operation. Breast cancer was held to begin at
one site and to spread outward in order, first through contiguous tissue, then to regional nodes,
then to distant organs. Spread was centrifugal, sequential and permissive. Nodes were a barrier
that the disease had to breach in sequence.

Two surgical conclusions follow with real force, and that force is what kept the model alive for
eighty years. The first is that cure requires removing every station the disease might have
reached. The second is that failure means the resection was not wide enough.

Notice what the second conclusion does. Recurrence becomes evidence for the model rather than
against it. Operations grew accordingly, extending to the internal mammary chain and to the
supraclavicular fossa. Local control improved. Survival did not follow [@hortobagyi2020].

The error corrected was surgery performed without any theory of spread. The error introduced was a
theory that could absorb any result. A model in which every failure is a technical failure makes no
prediction that could ever come out wrong.

## BS-0040 Keynes, McWhirter, and the first organized dissent

Dissent came from two directions, and neither was a randomized trial. Keynes treated breast cancer
with implanted radium instead of radical surgery and reported survival that did not look worse.
McWhirter combined simple mastectomy with radiotherapy and argued that his results matched those of
the radical operation [@mcwhirter1955].

These were case series rather than comparisons. They were open to the objection that different
patients had been selected into them, and that objection was duly made. On the evidence available
it could not be answered.

What the dissenters achieved was narrower than a refutation and more useful. They established that a
smaller operation was not obviously catastrophic. That is the precondition for a trial. A field
convinced that one operation is all that stands between a patient and death will not randomize
anyone away from it.

The error corrected was the assumption that maximal surgery was the only defensible treatment. The
error introduced was an argument built from case series, which cannot settle the question in either
direction. The next section belongs to the people who noticed that.

## BS-0050 Fisher, the alternative hypothesis, and the birth of the randomized trial

Fisher proposed a different model and called it the alternative hypothesis [@fisher1980]. Breast
cancer is systemic early. Tumour cells enter the bloodstream before the disease becomes detectable.
Regional nodes are indicators of dissemination rather than barriers to it. The extent of local
surgery therefore has limited influence on survival.

The model makes predictions that can fail, which is what the centrifugal model never did. If nodes
are indicators, removing more of them will not extend survival. If dissemination is early, a wider
operation will not extend it either.

The trials tested that directly. In a trial of 1079 women with clinically negative axillary nodes
and 586 women with clinically positive nodes, radical mastectomy was compared with less extensive
surgery. At twenty-five years there was no significant difference in overall survival among the
groups [@fisher2002b04]. In a separate trial of 1851 women, total mastectomy, lumpectomy alone and
lumpectomy with breast irradiation gave no significant difference in overall survival at twenty
years [@fisher2002b06]. The cumulative incidence of tumour recurrence in the treated breast was
14.3% with irradiation and 39.2% without it.

An independent trial reached the same place by another route. Among 701 women with tumours no larger
than 2 cm, death from any cause at twenty years occurred in 41.2% after radical mastectomy and in
41.7% after quadrantectomy with radiotherapy [@veronesi2002].

Hold two claims apart here, because the rest of this book depends on the distinction. Local
treatment controls local disease, and it does so measurably. Local treatment does not control
disease that has already left the breast.

The error corrected was the centrifugal model itself. The error introduced was the opposite
absolutism, the reading that local treatment never matters. It does. Irradiation cut in-breast
recurrence by roughly two thirds in a population whose survival it did not change.

## BS-0060 Beatson to tamoxifen, a century of endocrine therapy before the term existed

Beatson removed the ovaries of women with advanced breast cancer in the 1890s and saw tumours
regress in some of them. He had no receptor, no hormone assay and no mechanism. What he had was the
observation that an intervention at a distance from the tumour changed the tumour.

That is the first demonstration in oncology that a cancer can depend on a host signal. It preceded
the isolation of oestrogen by decades and the identification of the oestrogen receptor by more than
half a century. Treatment ran ahead of mechanism for three generations.

The first clinical report of the compound that became tamoxifen describes 46 patients with late or
recurrent breast cancer, of whom 10 responded [@cole1971]. The drug was not given to a
receptor-defined population, because no such population could yet be defined. It was an
antioestrogen offered to unselected patients, and its response rate resembled that of the hormonal
treatments already in use.

Selection arrived later and changed the size of the effect entirely. A patient-level meta-analysis
of 20 trials, comprising 21457 women randomized to about five years of tamoxifen or to none, found
recurrence substantially reduced across the first ten years in oestrogen receptor positive disease
[@ebctcg2011tam]. Breast cancer mortality fell by about a third across fifteen years. In receptor
negative disease the drug had little or no effect.

The error corrected was the belief that tumour behaviour is set entirely inside the tumour. The
error introduced was a century of giving an effective drug to everyone. The assay that identifies
who benefits arrived long after the drug did, and the field was slow to notice the gap.

## BS-0070 From nitrogen mustard to CMF and the adjuvant proof of principle

Cytotoxic chemotherapy entered oncology from chemical warfare research, by way of the observation
that nitrogen mustard depleted lymphoid tissue. The drugs were developed against dividing cells
rather than against breast cancer. Their arrival in this disease was opportunistic.

Giving them after surgery required a separate idea, and the idea is Fisher's. If dissemination
happens early, the patient who appears disease-free after an operation may not be. Adjuvant
treatment means treating disease that nobody can see and that most of those treated do not have.

The first convincing test combined cyclophosphamide, methotrexate and fluorouracil after radical
mastectomy in node-positive disease. Among 386 women randomized, relapse at 27 months had occurred
in 24% of 179 controls and in 5.3% of 207 treated patients [@bonadonna1976]. At twenty years the
adjusted relative risk of death with chemotherapy against surgery alone was 0.76 [@bonadonna1995].

Meta-analysis matters more here than any single trial, because the effect being estimated is modest
and the trials are individually underpowered for mortality. Polychemotherapy reduces both recurrence
and breast cancer mortality, and the size of the proportional reduction depends on the regimen
[@ebctcg2012].

The error corrected was the assumption that an operation with clear margins had removed the disease.
The error introduced was the inference that if some chemotherapy helps, more will help more. That
inference gets its own section, and it follows immediately.

## BS-0080 High-dose chemotherapy and transplant, a field that talked itself into harm

The argument was not stupid, which is the reason this episode is worth studying. Cytotoxic drugs
kill a proportion of cells rather than a fixed number, so raising the dose should raise the kill.
Marrow toxicity sets the ceiling. Autologous stem cell rescue lifts the ceiling. Every step is
locally reasonable.

What was missing was a comparison. Single-arm studies in metastatic disease reported response rates
above those seen at standard doses. Those studies enrolled younger and fitter patients whose disease
had already proved chemosensitive, because only such patients survive a transplant. The control
group was history, and history had been assembled from a different population.

Practice moved before evidence did. Payers came under legal and legislative pressure to fund the
procedure. Patients were treated outside trials. Randomized trials then accrued slowly, in part
because the treatment could be obtained without joining one.

The randomized results, when they came, were negative. Among 553 patients with metastatic disease
given induction chemotherapy, 199 responders were randomized to high-dose treatment with stem cell
support or to conventional-dose maintenance. Three-year survival was 32% and 38% respectively
[@stadtmauer2000]. An individual-patient overview of 15 adjuvant trials covering 6210 patients found
relapse-free survival prolonged, with a hazard ratio of 0.87 against control, and no significant
effect on overall survival [@berry2011].

One influential positive study proved to be fraudulent. An on-site review found that the presented
data did not match the source records, that documented consent was absent, and that a different
control regimen had been used from the one described [@weiss2000].

```caution
The comfortable reading is that a fraud misled the field. The sequence does not support it.
Uncontrolled series, a biased comparison group, patient demand and payer mandates had already
established the practice before the fraudulent data were presented. The fraud accelerated a belief
that was already held. It did not create it.
```

The error corrected was eventually the dose-intensity assumption itself. The error introduced was
structural rather than scientific. Once a treatment is widely available outside a trial, the trial
that would evaluate it becomes hard to complete. Evidence then arrives after the practice has become
expensive to reverse. That problem is entirely current, and it is developed in [[BC-740]].

## BS-0090 HER2, trastuzumab, and the template for biomarker-defined development

HER2 arrived as a prognostic observation before it was a target. Amplification of the gene was
identified in human breast cancer and correlated with relapse and survival [@slamon1987]. The
finding marked out a subgroup with worse outcome, defined by a measurement rather than by anything
visible on a slide.

What followed is the template that the rest of this book keeps meeting. An antibody was raised
against the protein. The trial enrolled only patients whose tumours overexpressed it. Among 469
patients randomized to chemotherapy with or without trastuzumab, median time to progression was 7.4
months against 4.6 months, and median overall survival was 25.1 months against 20.3 months
[@slamon2001]. Moving the same agent into early disease reduced recurrence in patients selected the
same way [@romond2005].

Three features of that sequence recur constantly. The eligible population was defined by an assay.
The assay carried a threshold. The threshold came from trial eligibility rather than from a
demonstrated discontinuity in biology.

The receptor biology belongs to [[BC-150]], and the treatment to [[BC-500]] and [[BC-570]]. What
belongs here is only the shape of the argument.

The error corrected was treating breast cancer as one disease for the purpose of drug development.
The error introduced was the belief that an assay boundary marks a boundary in the disease. That
belief is examined in [[BC-110]] and taken apart in [[BC-280]].

## BS-0100 Microarrays, intrinsic subtypes, and the move to molecular classification

Expression profiling changed the unit of description from a slide to a gene list. Perou and
colleagues clustered breast tumours using genes chosen for stability within a tumour and variation
between tumours [@perou2000]. Sorlie and colleagues extended the analysis and showed that the
resulting groups differed in survival [@sorlie2001].

The claim being made was narrower than the claim that got repeated. The subtypes describe
between-tumour variation among features that do not move much within a tumour. They were not built
to track change under treatment. By construction they cannot do it, because genes that moved under
treatment were excluded when the gene list was assembled.

The derivation, the concordance with immunohistochemical surrogates and the critique are set out in
[[BC-100]]. This section records only what the technology did to the argument.

The error corrected was a taxonomy assembled entirely from appearance. The error introduced was
treating a clustering result as the discovery of natural kinds. A cluster is a summary of one
dataset under one chosen distance. Calling its members a disease entity is a further claim, and it
needs its own evidence.

## BS-0110 Advocacy, landmark trials, and the arrival of deliberate de-escalation

Organized advocacy changed what was funded, who sat on review panels and which endpoints were taken
seriously. Treatment burden and quality of life became legitimate objects of study rather than
afterthoughts. That shift is a precondition for everything described in this section, because a
de-escalation trial asks whether a treatment can be taken away.

De-escalation is harder to run than escalation. It needs a non-inferiority design, a prespecified
margin and an agreed standard to be non-inferior against. Those methodological problems are set out
in [[BC-740]].

Two lines matured in parallel. Surgical de-escalation asked whether axillary dissection could be
omitted, and trials including {{trial:z0011}} and {{trial:sound}} tested that question directly.
That line is developed in [[BC-410]].

Systemic de-escalation used an assay to find patients who could safely omit chemotherapy. In
{{trial:tailorx}}, women with node-negative hormone receptor positive HER2-negative disease and a
recurrence score of 11 to 25 were randomized to endocrine therapy alone or to chemotherapy followed
by endocrine therapy. Endocrine therapy alone was non-inferior for invasive disease-free survival
[@sparano2018]. In {{trial:rxponder}}, the same question in node-positive disease produced an
answer that depended on menopausal status [@kalinsky2021].

The chapter closes where the book opens. The error corrected across this whole history is the
centrifugal model, and every de-escalation trial is a descendant of its refutation. The error
introduced is quieter and still active. It is the assumption that a category defined well enough to
enrol patients is a category that exists in the disease. [[BC-030]] gives that assumption its name.
