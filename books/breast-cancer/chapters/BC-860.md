## BS-7270 Scope, and why pathological complete response carries most weight in this subtype

This chapter covers what is given before surgery in triple-negative disease. What follows surgery
is in the companion chapter [[BC-850]]. The framing argument is in [[BC-510]], with the
neoadjuvant setting as a research instrument in [[BC-540]].

Pathological complete response matters more here than anywhere else in this book, and the reason
is quantitative. In the pooled analysis of 12 trials and 11,955 patients, eradication of tumour
from breast and nodes in triple-negative disease carried an event-free survival hazard ratio of
0.24, with a 95% confidence interval of 0.18 to 0.33. The overall survival hazard ratio was 0.16,
with an interval of 0.11 to 0.25 [@cortazar2014]. No other readily available variable in this
disease separates outcome that sharply.

The same analysis is why the endpoint cannot be used to judge a drug. At trial level, the increase
in response frequency between treatment groups explained almost none of the variation in
event-free survival [@cortazar2014]. The distinction between a patient-level prognostic marker and
a trial-level surrogate is worked out in [[BS-6360]], and it governs every table below.

So read pathological complete response as an instruction about what to do next rather than as a
verdict on what was just given. That is the use that survives the surrogacy failure, and the use
on which [[BC-850]] depends.

The master table for the whole chapter sits in [[BS-7280]]. Later sections narrow it by question,
so a trial recurs when it bears on more than one of them.

The story below is the argument the rest of this chapter is evidence for, and it carries no
figures at all. It states what each group of trials was built to find out, what came back, and
what the design cannot settle, so the tables are read as answers to a question rather than as a
list. It is one node of the [Chapter Stories](stories.html) map.

## BS-7280 Anthracycline and taxane backbones

The chemotherapy backbone in this disease was inherited rather than optimised for it. Every trial
below adds something to a sequential taxane and anthracycline regimen established across all
subtypes. None of the component drugs was selected for triple-negative biology.

```evidence setting=early subtype=TNBC line=neoadjuvant modality=chemo sort=year cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,result,status caption=Neoadjuvant_trials_in_triple-negative_disease._The_arms_column_is_where_the_backbone_differences_live.
```

Two structural facts constrain how the arms in that table should be read.

Giving the same chemotherapy before rather than after surgery does not change survival. In the
individual patient data meta-analysis of ten trials, fifteen-year distant recurrence was 38.2%
with neoadjuvant treatment against 38.0%, with a rate ratio of 1.02. Local recurrence was higher,
at 21.4% against 15.9%, with a rate ratio of 1.37 [@ebctcg2018neo]. Those trials predate modern
surgical planning and response-adapted local therapy. The point that survives is that the timing
buys information and conservative surgery rather than a survival advantage in itself.

Dose density belongs to the same inheritance, and its evidence is in [[BS-7200]]. Several trials
here use a dose-dense anthracycline phase and others do not, which is one more reason cross-trial
response rates are not comparable.

The live backbone question is whether the anthracycline is needed at all when a platinum and a
checkpoint inhibitor are both present. {{trial:neopact}} and {{trial:neotrip}} were built on that
premise from opposite directions [@sharma2024neopact; @gianni2022neotrip]. That question is taken
up in [[BS-7310]], because in practice it is inseparable from the immunotherapy question.

## BS-7290 Platinum and the germline BRCA question

Adding carboplatin to a neoadjuvant backbone raises pathological complete response. Three
randomised trials agree on that and the table carries their numbers.

```evidence setting=early subtype=TNBC line=neoadjuvant modality=chemo topic=platinum sort=year caption=Platinum_added_to_a_neoadjuvant_backbone._PEARLY_enrolled_in_both_the_neoadjuvant_and_adjuvant_settings.
```

The part that surprises people is that the benefit is not confined to germline carriers. In the
homologous recombination analysis of {{trial:geparsixto}}, carboplatin raised response sharply in
deficient tumours and modestly in non-deficient ones. The interaction test was not significant, at
a p value of 0.327, with disease-free survival improving in both groups [@loibl2018geparsixto].
The authors' conclusion on that point was explicit. Deficiency predicted response to the regimen
as a whole, and not benefit from carboplatin specifically.

The apparent contradiction with the metastatic evidence dissolves once the designs are compared.
In advanced disease, carboplatin was tested against docetaxel, where germline carriers responded
better to the platinum than to the taxane [@tutt2018]. That is a substitution design, which can
expose differential sensitivity. The neoadjuvant trials added carboplatin to a full anthracycline
and taxane backbone. Every patient still received the drugs a BRCA-associated tumour is already
sensitive to, so the increment attributable to platinum is measured on top of them. An addition
design cannot reveal the same contrast, whatever the germline result says.

The survival evidence is mixed, and the mixture is itself informative. Long-term follow-up of
{{trial:calgb40603}} found no event-free survival benefit from carboplatin despite the response
gain [@shepherd2022]. Post hoc analysis of {{trial:brightness}} pointed the other way on the same
question [@geyer2022brightness]. {{trial:geparsixto}} and {{trial:pearly}} were both positive on
the survival endpoints they reported. None of these trials was powered for the question it is
being asked.

```caution
Carboplatin is already inside the regimen that most stage II and III patients now receive
[@schmid2020]. No trial has removed it from a chemoimmunotherapy backbone, so its contribution in
that context is unmeasured. The question is not whether to add platinum. It is whether the platinum
that is already there is doing anything, and that trial does not exist.
```

## BS-7300 Checkpoint blockade and what KEYNOTE-522 settled

```evidence setting=early subtype=TNBC line=neoadjuvant modality=immunotherapy topic=pembrolizumab caption=Pembrolizumab-containing_neoadjuvant_regimens_in_triple-negative_disease.
```

{{trial:keynote522}} settled one thing completely and left several others open. Adding
pembrolizumab to neoadjuvant chemotherapy and continuing it after surgery improves overall
survival in stage II and III triple-negative disease [@schmid2024k522os]. An overall survival
benefit in a curable population is rare, which is why the regimen is standard rather than
optional.

Four things it did not settle are worth stating precisely, because each is regularly treated as
though it had been.

It did not establish which phase does the work. Pembrolizumab was given before and after surgery
to every patient [@schmid2020], and [[BS-7240]] takes that up.

It did not establish the contribution of carboplatin, which both arms received.

It did not establish any benefit below stage II. Enrolment required stage II or III disease,
leaving a node-negative 1.2 cm tumour unrepresented.

It did not establish which individual patients benefit. The trial enrolled without a PD-L1
criterion by design, and [[BS-7320]] explains why no marker has since filled the gap.

The effect size was also predicted in advance by a platform trial. {{trial:ispy2-pembro}}
graduated pembrolizumab on a Bayesian estimate of success in a future phase 3 trial [@nanda2020].
That prediction proved right, which is a point in favour of the platform design rather than
evidence about the drug. A graduation is a probability statement about a trial that has not been
run [[BS-6340]].

## BS-7310 The other immunotherapy trials and why they read out differently

Six randomised comparisons have added a checkpoint inhibitor to neoadjuvant chemotherapy in this
disease, without agreeing with one another. One improved survival, one improved response without a
demonstrated survival gain, and one improved survival without improving response. Of the remaining
three, one missed its event-free survival endpoint, one missed on response with its survival
endpoint still unreported, and one graduated on a Bayesian criterion inside a platform.

```evidence setting=early subtype=TNBC line=neoadjuvant modality=immunotherapy sort=year caption=Every_randomised_neoadjuvant_immunotherapy_trial_in_triple-negative_disease,_plus_the_single-arm_and_ongoing_studies.
```

Reading that spread as a statement about checkpoint blockade is the standard error. The trials
differ on at least five axes at once, and no axis was isolated.

The agent differs: pembrolizumab binds PD-1, while atezolizumab and durvalumab bind PD-L1 and
leave the PD-L2 interaction intact.

The chemotherapy backbone differs. {{trial:keynote522}} and {{trial:b59}} used a platinum and an
anthracycline, {{trial:impassion031}} used an anthracycline without a platinum, and
{{trial:neotrip}} used a platinum with the anthracycline deferred until after surgery.

Continuation after surgery differs. {{trial:keynote522}}, {{trial:b59}} and {{trial:impassion031}}
continued the checkpoint inhibitor postoperatively, while {{trial:geparnuevo}} stopped at surgery.

The endpoint each trial was powered for differs. {{trial:impassion031}} and {{trial:geparnuevo}}
were powered for pathological complete response, while {{trial:b59}} and {{trial:neotrip}} were
powered for event-free survival, which is a much harder endpoint to move.

The enrolled population differs. {{trial:neotrip}} took high-risk and locally advanced disease,
and in a pooled reconstruction of five of these trials its four-year recurrence rate was the
highest [@licata2025].

The dissociation between response and survival is the finding that matters. {{trial:geparnuevo}}
missed on response and separated on survival [@loibl2019geparnuevo; @loibl2022geparnuevo].
{{trial:impassion031}} hit its response endpoint, while its survival comparisons remained
descriptive and non-significant [@mittendorf2020; @mittendorf2025impassion031]. That is the
trial-level surrogacy failure of [[BS-6360]] appearing inside one drug class.

The anthracycline-free question sits here rather than in [[BS-7280]] for the same reason.
{{trial:neopact}} reported a response rate in a single arm with no randomised comparator
[@sharma2024neopact]. {{trial:neotrip}} used an anthracycline-free neoadjuvant phase and has not
reported its primary endpoint. {{trial:scarlet}} is the randomised test against the
{{trial:keynote522}} regimen. Until it reports, the omission of an anthracycline remains a
hypothesis.

```caution
Comparing pathological complete response rates across these trials is not informative. The
populations, the backbones and the response definitions were not held constant. A lower rate in one
trial than another is at least as likely to reflect who was enrolled as what was given.
```

## BS-7320 Biomarkers for immunotherapy benefit, and why PD-L1 fails here

PD-L1 selects in metastatic triple-negative disease and does not select in early disease. Both
statements are correct, without the biology having to reverse between the two settings.

```evidence setting=metastatic subtype=TNBC line=1L modality=immunotherapy caption=The_setting_where_PD-L1_does_select._Every_trial_here_restricted_or_stratified_entry_by_an_assay_result.
```

Five differences separate the two settings, each cumulative with the others rather than competing.

The first difference is that PD-L1 positive is not the output of one assay. In advanced disease,
samples scored by three antibodies gave immune cell positivity of 46.4% with SP142, 74.9% with
SP263 and 73.1% with 22C3, and 80.9% were positive by 22C3 combined positive score. Analytical
concordance with SP142 was about 69%, and no inter-assay equivalence was found [@rugo2021].
{{trial:keynote522}} used the combined positive score and {{trial:impassion031}} and
{{trial:neotrip}} used the SP142 immune cell score. Part of what looks like a setting effect is an
assay effect, with the general form of that problem set out in [[BS-4130]].

The second is that the tissue being scored is not the same tissue. Early disease is scored on a
treatment-naive core of an intact primary. Advanced disease is often scored on an archival primary
or on a metastasis. Immune cell positivity was 63.7% in primary tumours and 42.2% in unmatched
metastatic lesions in one series scored with the SP142 assay [@rozenblit2020]. Lymphocyte counts,
PD-L1 and a coordinated set of interferon-regulated and antigen presentation genes are all lower
in metastases [@szekely2018].

The third follows from the second, because the dynamic range of the marker differs. If the early
population is shifted towards the positive end of the scale, the negative group is smaller and
less extreme. A cut-point applied to a compressed distribution separates less, whatever the
underlying biology.

The fourth is the combination partner that the checkpoint inhibitor is added to. In the early
setting the checkpoint inhibitor is added to a curative multi-agent regimen containing a platinum.
Cytotoxic therapy changes the infiltrate and PD-L1 expression itself [@pelekanou2017], and DNA
damage activates innate sensing [@parkes2017]. A strong combination partner compresses the
difference between subgroups defined by a marker.

The fifth is that the statistical claim is weaker than it sounds. {{trial:keynote522}} was not
designed to test a PD-L1 interaction, and its PD-L1 negative subgroup was small [@schmid2020].
Benefit regardless of PD-L1 means that no interaction was demonstrated. It does not mean that no
interaction exists.

Within the early setting the marker has not been inert either. The PD-L1 positive co-primary
population of {{trial:impassion031}} did not cross its significance boundary [@mittendorf2020]. In
{{trial:neotrip}}, PD-L1 was the strongest factor associated with response in multivariable
analysis, with an odds ratio of 2.08, in a trial where the drug itself did not improve response
[@gianni2022neotrip]. A marker associated with response and not with the treatment effect is
prognostic rather than predictive, a distinction defined in [[BS-0970]].

Three other candidates are worth tracking and none is ready.

Tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes are prognostic and reproducibly scored under the convention in
[[BS-1660]] [@salgado2015; @loi2019]. The first randomised signal that they might also be
predictive of checkpoint benefit comes from {{trial:abrave}}, where avelumab improved outcome only
above a baseline stromal score of 30%. In the residual disease stratum the interaction p value was
0.019 [@dieci2026abrave]. That is a biomarker analysis of a trial that missed its primary
endpoint. Residual cancer burden was not predictive in the same analysis.

Tumour-specific class II major histocompatibility expression was associated with response to
atezolizumab and not to chemotherapy alone in {{trial:neotrip}} [@balko2025]. It requires
quantitative imaging mass cytometry that no routine laboratory runs.

Tumour mutational burden carries tumour-agnostic evidence from studies that had no breast cohort
[@marabelle2020]. High burden is also uncommon in this disease [@barrososousa2020].

```interplay target=BC-350
The reason a percentage of PD-L1 staining fails to predict is the reason given in Part VI for most
biomarker failure in this disease. What a checkpoint inhibitor releases is a relationship between
cell types, not a property of one cell. A score computed on a block reports abundance and discards
arrangement, exactly as a HER2 percentage does in [[BS-2260]], and the spatial phenotypes in
[[BS-1940]] separate patients that no abundance measure can. There is a second point specific to
the neoadjuvant setting. Treatment is being given to an intact primary and its draining nodes,
which is where priming happens [[BS-1710]]. A marker measured on a core taken before any of that
describes the starting state of a system whose behaviour depends on what the first two cycles do to
it.
```

## BS-7330 De-escalation after pathological complete response and the immune toxicity cost

After a pathological complete response the residual risk is low and it is front-loaded. In a
pooled reconstruction of five neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy trials, most recurrences occurred
within 24 months of randomisation, in patients with and without a complete response [@licata2025].
A de-escalation decision taken after surgery is therefore a decision about the next two years
rather than the next ten.

```evidence setting=early subtype=TNBC line=post-neoadjuvant modality=immunotherapy topic=de-escalation caption=The_only_randomised_test_of_omitting_the_adjuvant_checkpoint_phase_after_a_complete_response.
```

The cost side of that trade is specific to this setting. More than half the patients in the
control arm of {{trial:keynote522}} reached a complete response without pembrolizumab
[@schmid2020]. A permanent endocrinopathy in one of those patients is a different event from the
same endocrinopathy in incurable disease. The structural features that make these toxicities
unlike chemotherapy toxicity are in [[BS-4180]] and [[BS-4510]].

Two arguments support omission after a complete response, and neither of them is a result.
{{trial:geparnuevo}} produced a survival benefit with no adjuvant checkpoint phase at all
[@loibl2022geparnuevo]. And a complete response identifies a group whose absolute risk leaves
little room for an adjuvant drug to work.

The obstacle to answering it is arithmetic rather than conceptual. Three-year event-free survival
after a complete response on this regimen is high, so a non-inferiority trial needs either a very
large sample or a margin wide enough to permit real harm. The margin problem behind that
arithmetic is worked through in [[BS-6370]].

```practice
Do not stop adjuvant pembrolizumab early on the grounds that the tumour responded. That is the
question {{trial:optimice}} is asking, and it has not answered it.

Stopping it is defensible when a significant immune-related event occurs after a complete response.
The remaining absolute benefit is small and unproven. The toxicity in front of you is neither.

Separate the two drugs when an immune event appears during the neoadjuvant phase. Hold the
checkpoint inhibitor and continue the chemotherapy [[BS-4510]].

Do not de-escalate local therapy on response alone. Nodal management after a complete response is a
separate randomised question {{trial:nsabp-b51}} [@mamounas2025].
```

## BS-7340 Unresolved questions and what reads out next

```evidence setting=early subtype=TNBC status=ongoing,awaited caption=Early_triple-negative_trials_that_have_not_reported_their_primary_endpoint.
```

{{trial:scarlet}} asks whether the anthracycline can be removed from a pembrolizumab and platinum
regimen. A non-inferior result would remove an anthracycline, and with it the cardiac and
leukaemic risks, from a young population treated with curative intent.

{{trial:neotrip}} has yet to report the event-free survival endpoint it was powered for
[@gianni2022neotrip]. Its response result, reported as a secondary endpoint, was negative. A
positive survival result would add a second example of the dissociation that {{trial:geparnuevo}}
produced, and would strengthen the case that response is the wrong readout for this drug class.

{{trial:optimice}} asks whether the adjuvant checkpoint phase can be dropped after a complete
response. That question is discussed in [[BS-7330]] and again in [[BS-7240]].

{{trial:ascent05}} and {{trial:tropion-b03}} concern the other half of the response split and are
covered in [[BC-850]].

Four questions in this chapter have no trial running at all.

Nobody is removing carboplatin from the chemoimmunotherapy backbone, so its contribution there
stays unmeasured.

Nobody is testing checkpoint blockade in stage I disease, where the regimen is increasingly used
by extrapolation.

No biomarker-driven omission trial is running, because [[BS-7320]] explains that there is no
marker to build one on.

And no trial is comparing the neoadjuvant regimens against each other. Every comparison a
clinician makes between {{trial:keynote522}} and its alternatives is a cross-trial comparison,
with all the confounding set out in [[BS-7310]].
