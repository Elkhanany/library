## BS-7270 Scope, and why pathological complete response carries most weight in this subtype

```story SQ-0390
```

This chapter covers what is given before surgery in triple-negative disease. What follows surgery
is in the companion chapter [[BC-850]]. The framing argument is in [[BC-510]], with the
neoadjuvant setting as a research instrument in [[BC-540]].

Pathological complete response matters more here than anywhere else in this book, and the reason
is quantitative. A pooled analysis of 12 trials and 11,955 patients measured what clearance of the
breast and nodes is worth in this subtype. The event-free survival hazard ratio was 0.24, with a
95% confidence interval of 0.18 to 0.33. The overall survival hazard ratio was 0.16, with an
interval of 0.11 to 0.25 [@cortazar2014]. No other readily available variable in this disease
separates outcome that sharply.

The same analysis is why the endpoint cannot be used to judge a drug. At trial level, the increase
in response frequency between treatment groups explained almost none of the variation in
event-free survival [@cortazar2014]. The distinction between a patient-level prognostic marker and
a trial-level surrogate is worked out in [[BS-6360]], and it governs every table below.

So read pathological complete response as an instruction about what to do next rather than as a
verdict on what was just given. That is the use that survives the surrogacy failure, and the use
on which [[BC-850]] depends.

That is also the limit of what a preoperative study can establish alone. The trials in this
chapter that stop at the operation agree on one thing and one thing only. They show that an agent
is active against untreated triple-negative disease, and they show it within months of the first
dose.

They differ in almost everything else, and the difference is design rather than drug.
{{trial:ispy2}} runs candidate agents against a shared chemotherapy control and graduates them on
a Bayesian probability of success in a future phase III trial [@yee2020]. That probability is a
prediction about a trial nobody has run. {{trial:neostar}} gave a conjugate alone for four cycles
in a single arm, and what followed was left to the treating physician [@spring2023]. Its
event-free survival therefore describes a treatment pathway rather than a drug. {{trial:tbcrc-039}}
added a JAK inhibitor to preoperative chemotherapy in triple-negative inflammatory disease, with a
pharmacodynamic conversion as its primary endpoint [@lynce2024]. {{trial:p-rad}} randomises
preoperative radiation dose as an immune primer and reads out on immunological endpoints. Four
different currencies, and none of them is recurrence.

The inflammatory trial earns its space because its pharmacodynamic result inverts. Phosphorylated
STAT3 fell in the biopsies taken after a short run-in of the inhibitor alone. Sustained dosing
alongside the taxane raised the same pathway rather than lowering it [@lynce2024]. The run-in sat
inside the randomisation rather than in front of it, so the two comparisons overlap. The trial also
enrolled very few patients, and its response figure rests on a handful of events.

None of this licenses moving a drug in front of surgery. The randomised conjugate trials in this
disease all sit behind it. {{trial:ascent05}} [@tolaney2024optimicerd], {{trial:tropion-b03}}
[@bardia2024tropionb03] and {{trial:sascia-gbg-102}} randomise a conjugate in patients left with
residual disease after neoadjuvant chemotherapy. A single-arm response rate in an untreated tumour
and a randomised outcome in residual disease are not two readings of one experiment. There is no
randomised basis for a preoperative conjugate, because nobody has built the comparison.

So the division of labour in this chapter is fixed. A preoperative trial retires a candidate
quickly and makes a mechanism visible in paired biopsies. It cannot license adding a drug to a
curative regimen, and it cannot license taking one out.

Each section carries its own table and its own question from the [Chapter Stories](stories.html)
map. A trial recurs where it bears on more than one of them. The stories carry no figures at all,
so a story and a table can never disagree about a number. Read the population column of every
table before the result column, because these trials were built on different populations.

## BS-7280 Anthracycline and taxane backbones

The chemotherapy backbone in this disease was inherited rather than optimised for it. Every trial
in this chapter adds something to a sequential taxane and anthracycline regimen established across
all subtypes. None of the component drugs was selected for triple-negative biology.

Giving the same chemotherapy before rather than after surgery does not change survival. In the
individual patient data meta-analysis of ten trials, fifteen-year distant recurrence was 38.2%
with neoadjuvant treatment against 38.0%, with a rate ratio of 1.02. Local recurrence was higher,
at 21.4% against 15.9%, with a rate ratio of 1.37 [@ebctcg2018neo]. Those trials predate modern
surgical planning and response-adapted local therapy. The point that survives is that the timing
buys information and conservative surgery rather than a survival advantage in itself.

The taxane question was asked twice with the same drugs and answered twice differently.
{{trial:geparsepto-gbg-69}} found that nab-paclitaxel raised pathological complete response over
solvent-based paclitaxel across all subtypes [@untch2016]. {{trial:etna}} tested the same
substitution in HER2-negative disease and found no significant difference [@gianni2018]. Neither
trial isolated the formulation. The first gave nab-paclitaxel at 150 mg/m2 until an amendment
lowered it to 125, against a comparator at 80 mg/m2 [@untch2016]. The second gave 125 mg/m2
throughout against a comparator at 90 mg/m2 on a different cycle length [@gianni2018]. A dose
difference and a schedule difference are inside what is reported as a formulation comparison.

Dose density belongs to the same inheritance, and its evidence is in [[BS-7200]]. Several trials
here use a dose-dense anthracycline phase and others do not, which is one more reason cross-trial
response rates are not comparable.

The live backbone question is whether the anthracycline is needed at all.

```evidence setting=early subtype=TNBC line=neoadjuvant modality=chemo topic=anthracycline-free sort=year cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,endpoint,result,status caption=Regimens_that_remove_the_anthracycline_from_the_preoperative_phase_or_defer_it_until_after_surgery._Only_the_last_row_randomises_that_comparison_against_an_outcome.
```

The rows that have reported converge on the response question. A carboplatin and taxane doublet
clears the breast and nodes about as often as the same drugs followed by an anthracycline. Only
{{trial:neostop}} randomised that comparison directly, and its two arms landed on the same response
rate [@sharma2020]. What separated them was haematological toxicity and cost rather than efficacy.

The rest of the table diverges by what each trial chose to randomise. {{trial:wsg-adapt-tn}} gave
no anthracycline up front in either arm and randomised the partner drug instead [@gluz2018].
Treatment after those twelve weeks depended on how the tumour had responded, so its survival
comparison is confounded by what came next rather than by what was allocated [@gluz2022].
{{trial:neopact}} added pembrolizumab to the short regimen in a single arm [@sharma2024neopact].
{{trial:neotrip}} kept the anthracycline and moved it behind surgery, then randomised the
checkpoint inhibitor on what was left in front [@gianni2022neotrip]. {{trial:scarlet}} is the only
row that randomises the short regimen against the standard one with event-free survival as its
endpoint, and it has not reported.

So the decision rests on a trial that has not read out. Three of the five rows were powered for
what is found at the operation, on the endpoint this chapter has already shown moves without
survival moving with it. The two powered for recurrence have not reported it.

The table also has no column for what the omission is meant to buy. Cardiac failure and
treatment-related leukaemia arrive years after the last dose, in a population young enough that
the relevant follow-up runs into decades. None of these trials was built to count those events,
and the anthracycline-free regimens carry their own costs in neuropathy and marrow suppression.
The argument is taken up in [[BS-7310]], because in practice it cannot be separated from the
immunotherapy question.

## BS-7290 Platinum and the germline BRCA question

```story SQ-0340,SQ-0350,SQ-0360
```

```evidence setting=early subtype=TNBC line=neoadjuvant modality=chemo topic=platinum sort=year cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,endpoint,result caption=Platinum_before_surgery._Four_of_these_rows_randomised_the_drug_into_the_backbone._Two_substituted_it_in_a_population_chosen_by_a_germline_test_or_a_repair_assay.
```

Adding carboplatin to a neoadjuvant backbone raises pathological complete response. The rows above
that randomised it into a backbone and read out at the operation agree on that, across different
anthracyclines and different schedules. Two questions follow. Whether the response gain becomes a
survival gain, and whether any test identifies the patients who need the drug.

The divergence between those trials is in the backbone rather than in the platinum.
{{trial:geparsixto}} added carboplatin to weekly paclitaxel and a liposomal anthracycline given
concurrently, with bevacizumab in every triple-negative patient [@vonminckwitz2014].
{{trial:calgb40603}} added it to weekly paclitaxel followed by dose-dense doxorubicin and
cyclophosphamide, in a factorial that also randomised bevacizumab [@sikov2015].
{{trial:brightness}} added it to weekly paclitaxel followed by the same anthracycline pair, in a
three-arm design whose other question was a PARP inhibitor [@loibl2018brightness]. Each varies the
platinum and something else at once. The added toxicity also cost delivery of the rest of the
regimen, which is a second variable moving with the drug under test.

The survival evidence is mixed, and the mixture is informative rather than contradictory.
Long-term follow-up of {{trial:calgb40603}} found no outcome gain from the carboplatin it had
added [@shepherd2022]. {{trial:brightness}} pointed the other way at four and a half years, on
event-free survival between its carboplatin and paclitaxel arm and paclitaxel alone
[@geyer2022brightness]. That is a randomised carboplatin comparison. It is not the veliparib
question the trial was designed around, and overall survival did not differ between its arms.
{{trial:geparsixto}} reported
a disease-free survival advantage in its triple-negative stratum [@loibl2018geparsixto].
{{trial:wsg-adapt-tn}} set carboplatin against gemcitabine on the same anthracycline-free taxane,
and its response advantage was gone by the time survival was counted [@gluz2022]. None of these
trials was powered for the survival question it is being asked.

{{trial:pearly}} is what the decision rests on. It randomised carboplatin into an anthracycline
and taxane regimen, powered on event-free survival rather than on response, and met that endpoint
[@kim2026pearly]. Two features qualify it. Patients could be treated before or after surgery, so
its population is not the preoperative one this chapter otherwise describes. And its overall
survival favoured carboplatin without reaching significance, which is the endpoint a curative
addition ultimately has to move. {{trial:citrine}} asked the question entirely after surgery and
was positive on disease-free survival, with its proportional hazards assumption violated
[@liu2025citrine]. A single hazard ratio computed under an assumption the data reject does not
describe the whole of the follow-up.

The demonstration that keeps all of this honest is older than any of them. In {{trial:nsabp-b-27}}
a preoperative taxane doubled pathological complete response and changed neither disease-free nor
overall survival [@bear2006]. That is the gap between the two uses of the endpoint, shown once,
with the drug held constant.

The part that surprises people is that the platinum benefit is not confined to germline carriers.
In the homologous recombination analysis of {{trial:geparsixto}}, carboplatin raised response
sharply in deficient tumours and modestly in non-deficient ones. The interaction test was not
significant, and disease-free survival improved in both groups [@loibl2018geparsixto]. Deficiency
predicted response to the regimen as a whole, and not benefit from carboplatin specifically.

The apparent contradiction with the metastatic evidence dissolves once the designs are compared.
In advanced disease, carboplatin was tested against docetaxel, where germline carriers responded
better to the platinum than to the taxane [@tutt2018]. A substitution design can expose
differential sensitivity. The neoadjuvant trials added carboplatin to a full anthracycline and
taxane backbone. Every patient still received the drugs a BRCA-associated tumour is already
sensitive to, so the platinum increment is measured on top of them. An addition design cannot
reveal the same contrast, whatever the germline result says.

The two selection trials in the table were built the other way round and both failed.
{{trial:tbcrc-030}} set single-agent platinum against a single-agent taxane and asked whether a
deficiency score above a fixed cut-point predicted response to the platinum specifically. It did
not [@mayer2020]. That trial was sized to test a predictive claim rather than to compare two
drugs. A large share of its patients crossed to another preoperative regimen before surgery. The
assay could not be scored on all of the rest. {{trial:inform}} gave germline carriers single-agent
platinum against an anthracycline regimen, and the platinum produced no more complete responses
than the chemotherapy those patients would have received anyway [@tung2020a]. Entry there required
a mutation rather than triple-negative disease, so hormone receptor-positive patients sit inside
its answer. No trial has selected patients by the assay and kept an assay-negative arm on the same
treatment. That is the comparison that would show what the selection bought, and nobody has run it.

If the assay cannot allocate the platinum, the next question is whether it can allocate a PARP
inhibitor, and whether that drug can replace the chemotherapy rather than join it. The designs run
from addition to substitution to monotherapy, and they get less comparable as they go.
{{trial:brightness}} added veliparib to a carboplatin and taxane backbone and it added nothing
[@loibl2018brightness]. Veliparib traps the enzyme on DNA weakly, so that null belongs to one
molecule rather than to the class. {{trial:geparola}} put olaparib where the carboplatin would have
gone, in patients selected by a deficiency score or a BRCA mutation. It tested its own arm against
a fixed threshold rather than against its comparator, and it did not clear the threshold
[@fasching2020; @fasching2025a]. {{trial:neotala}} gave talazoparib alone for a full course in
germline carriers and fell short of its prespecified bar, on an evaluable denominator rather than
on everyone enrolled [@litton2023]. {{trial:petremac}} gave olaparib as a short window before
planned chemotherapy in tumours unselected for a repair defect, and many of them responded
[@eikesdal2020]. A window followed by planned chemotherapy says nothing about whether that
chemotherapy could have been left out. {{trial:tbcrc-056}} pairs a PARP inhibitor with a
checkpoint inhibitor and no chemotherapy at all. It carries no PARP-only arm, so it will not
separate the contributions of the two drugs.

```caution
Carboplatin is already inside the regimen that most stage II and III patients now receive
[@schmid2020]. No trial has removed it from a chemoimmunotherapy backbone, so its contribution in
that context is unmeasured. The question is not whether to add platinum. It is whether the platinum
that is already there is doing anything, and that trial does not exist.
```

## BS-7300 Checkpoint blockade and what KEYNOTE-522 settled

```evidence setting=early subtype=TNBC line=neoadjuvant modality=immunotherapy topic=pembrolizumab sort=year cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,endpoint,result,os,status caption=The_pembrolizumab_regimens_in_this_setting._One_defined_practice,_one_predicted_it,_and_two_ask_what_the_backbone_underneath_it_has_to_be.
```

{{trial:keynote522}} settled one thing completely and left several others open. Adding
pembrolizumab to neoadjuvant chemotherapy and continuing it after surgery improves overall
survival in stage II and III triple-negative disease [@schmid2024k522os]. An overall survival
benefit in a curable population is rare, which is why the regimen is standard rather than
optional. It is also the only row in that table that has reported a survival endpoint.

Four things it did not settle are worth stating precisely, because each is regularly treated as
though it had been.

It did not establish which phase does the work. Pembrolizumab was given before and after surgery
to every patient [@schmid2020], and [[BS-7240]] takes that up.

It did not establish the contribution of carboplatin, which both arms received.

It did not establish any benefit below stage II. Enrolment required stage II or III disease,
leaving a node-negative 1.2 cm tumour unrepresented.

It did not establish which individual patients benefit. The trial enrolled without a PD-L1
criterion by design, and [[BS-7320]] explains why no marker has since filled the gap.

The other three rows are variations on the regimen rather than tests of it.
{{trial:ispy2-pembro}} graduated pembrolizumab on a Bayesian estimate of success in a future phase
III trial [@nanda2020]. The prediction proved right, which is a point in favour of the platform
design rather than evidence about the drug. A graduation is a probability statement about a trial
nobody has run [[BS-6340]]. {{trial:neopact}} and {{trial:scarlet}} keep the antibody and change
what sits beneath it. Both put the backbone question of [[BS-7280]] under the practice-defining
drug. Only {{trial:scarlet}} is randomised against an outcome, and it has not reported.

Three of the four rows give carboplatin alongside the taxane, and none of them randomised it. The
contribution of either drug to the other therefore stays unmeasured. The fourth row,
{{trial:ispy2-pembro}}, gave pembrolizumab on a taxane and an anthracycline with no platinum at
all, in HER2-negative disease reported by subtype [@nanda2020]. A response rate from a single arm
or from a platform subgroup is not a statement about the antibody alone.

## BS-7310 The other immunotherapy trials and why they read out differently

```story SQ-0380
```

```evidence setting=early subtype=TNBC line=neoadjuvant,adjuvant modality=immunotherapy sort=year cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,endpoint,result,status caption=Every_randomised_checkpoint_inhibitor_trial_in_early_triple-negative_disease,_including_the_two_that_gave_the_drug_only_after_surgery.
```

One pattern in that table is consistent. Every trial that started the checkpoint inhibitor before
surgery and has reported a time-to-event comparison puts its hazard ratio below one, whether or not
the endpoint was met. The two that gave the drug only after surgery, {{trial:impassion030}}
[@ignatiadis2025] and {{trial:abrave}} [@conte2025abrave], both missed their primary endpoints.
The case for treating while the primary tumour and its draining nodes are still in place is
therefore drawn between trials rather than inside one. Nobody randomised the timing.

Beyond that, reading the spread as a statement about checkpoint blockade is the standard error.
The trials differ on five axes at once, and no axis was isolated in any of them.

The agent differs. Pembrolizumab binds PD-1, while atezolizumab, durvalumab and avelumab bind
PD-L1 and leave the PD-L2 interaction intact.

The chemotherapy backbone differs. {{trial:keynote522}} and {{trial:b59}} used a platinum and an
anthracycline [@loibl2026b59], while {{trial:impassion031}} used an anthracycline without a
platinum. {{trial:neotrip}} used a platinum with the anthracycline deferred until after surgery.
{{trial:neopact}} and {{trial:scarlet}} drop the anthracycline altogether.

The design differs. {{trial:ispy2-pembro}} was not a triple-negative trial at all. It adaptively
randomised HER2-negative disease against a shared control and reported this subtype as a subgroup
[@nanda2020]. {{trial:neopact}} has no control arm [@sharma2024neopact]. {{trial:scarlet}}
randomises the backbone rather than the antibody.

The endpoint each trial was powered for differs. {{trial:impassion031}} and {{trial:geparnuevo}}
were powered for pathological complete response, while {{trial:b59}} and {{trial:neotrip}} were
powered for event-free survival, which is a much harder endpoint to move.

The enrolled population differs. {{trial:neotrip}} took high-risk and locally advanced disease,
and in a pooled reconstruction of five of these trials its four-year recurrence rate was the
highest [@licata2025].

The column this table does not carry is overall survival, and three rows change character when it
is added. {{trial:geparnuevo}} missed on response and separated on survival, and the separation
held at long follow-up [@loibl2019geparnuevo; @loibl2022geparnuevo; @loibl2026geparnuevo].
{{trial:impassion031}} hit its response endpoint while its survival comparisons stayed descriptive
[@mittendorf2020; @mittendorf2025impassion031]. {{trial:abrave}} missed both co-primary
disease-free survival endpoints while its descriptive overall survival estimate favoured avelumab
[@conte2025abrave]. Descriptive means the trial bought no alpha for it. None of these three is a
survival result in the sense {{trial:keynote522}} produced one. The dissociation between response
and survival runs through all of them. That is the trial-level surrogacy failure of [[BS-6360]]
appearing inside a single drug class.

The anthracycline-free question sits in this section rather than in [[BS-7280]], because every
trial that asks it also varies the checkpoint inhibitor. {{trial:neopact}} reported a response
rate in a single arm with no randomised comparator [@sharma2024neopact]. {{trial:neotrip}}
deferred the anthracycline until after surgery, and the checkpoint inhibitor's response advantage
did not appear at all [@gianni2022neotrip]. That is one trial, one endpoint and one backbone. The
published response result is a secondary endpoint, so it cannot be read as showing the
anthracycline was the missing ingredient. {{trial:scarlet}} is the randomised test against the
{{trial:keynote522}} regimen. Until it reports, removing the anthracycline remains a hypothesis
rather than an option.

{{trial:bellini}} takes the same argument to its end and gives no chemotherapy at all. Checkpoint
blockade alone cleared some tumours selected for a high stromal lymphocyte density
[@nederlof2024]. Two things bound what that can be used for. Its window cohorts read out on a
laboratory measure of immune activation rather than on response, and only its selected surgical
cohort carried a response endpoint. A stromal lymphocyte percentage read on one slide against a
fixed cut-point is a scoring convention rather than a property of the tumour [[BS-1660]]. That
trial selected on the convention.

```caution
Comparing pathological complete response rates across these trials is not informative. The
populations, the backbones and the response definitions were not held constant. A lower rate in one
trial than another is at least as likely to reflect who was enrolled as what was given.
```

## BS-7320 Biomarkers for immunotherapy benefit, and why PD-L1 fails here

PD-L1 selects in metastatic triple-negative disease and does not select in early disease. Both
statements are correct, and the biology does not have to reverse between the two settings for that
to be so. The metastatic side of the argument is in [[BS-7730]] and the trials that carry it are
tabulated there rather than here. Six differences separate the settings, each cumulative with the
others rather than competing.

The first is that PD-L1 positive is not the output of one assay. In advanced disease, samples
scored by three antibodies gave immune cell positivity of 46.4% with SP142, 74.9% with SP263 and
73.1% with 22C3, and 80.9% were positive by 22C3 combined positive score. Analytical concordance
with SP142 was about 69%, and no inter-assay equivalence was found [@rugo2021].
{{trial:keynote522}} used the combined positive score, while {{trial:impassion031}} and
{{trial:neotrip}} used the SP142 immune cell score. Those instruments count different cells and
divide by different denominators [[BS-4130]]. Part of what is read as a setting effect is an assay
effect [[BC-470]].

The second is that the tissue being scored is not the same tissue. Early disease is scored on a
treatment-naive core of an intact primary. Advanced disease is often scored on an archival primary
or on a metastasis. Immune cell positivity was 63.7% in primary tumours and 42.2% in unmatched
metastatic lesions in one series scored with the SP142 assay [@rozenblit2020]. Lymphocyte counts,
PD-L1 and a coordinated set of interferon-regulated and antigen presentation genes are all lower
in metastases [@szekely2018].

The third follows from the second, because the dynamic range of the marker differs. The early
population is shifted towards the positive end of the scale, so its negative group is both smaller
and less extreme. A fixed cut-point applied to a compressed distribution separates less, whatever
the underlying biology does.

The fourth is the combination partner the checkpoint inhibitor is added to. In the early setting
it joins a curative multi-agent regimen containing a platinum. Cytotoxic therapy changes the
infiltrate and PD-L1 expression itself [@pelekanou2017], and DNA damage activates innate sensing
[@parkes2017]. A strong combination partner compresses the difference between subgroups defined by
a marker, because it supplies to the negative group some of what the marker was selecting for.

The fifth is that the two settings ask the marker to predict different things. Here it is asked to
predict eradication at one operation, and then recurrence over a few years in patients treated for
cure. In advanced disease it is asked to predict progression and death in disease that will not be
cured. A marker can track one and fail the other without being wrong about either [[BS-6360]].

The sixth is that the statistical claim is weaker than it sounds. {{trial:keynote522}} was not
designed to test a PD-L1 interaction, and its PD-L1 negative subgroup was small [@schmid2020].
Benefit regardless of PD-L1 means that no interaction was demonstrated. It does not mean that no
interaction exists.

Within the early setting the marker has not been inert either. The PD-L1 positive co-primary
population of {{trial:impassion031}} did not cross its significance boundary [@mittendorf2020]. In
{{trial:neotrip}}, PD-L1 was the strongest factor associated with response in multivariable
analysis, with an odds ratio of 2.08, in a trial where the drug itself did not improve response
[@gianni2022neotrip]. A marker associated with response and not with the treatment effect is
prognostic rather than predictive, a distinction defined in [[BS-0970]].

Three other candidates are worth tracking and none is ready.

Tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes are prognostic and reproducibly scored under the convention in
[[BS-1660]] [@salgado2015; @loi2019]. The first randomised signal that they might also be
predictive of checkpoint benefit comes from {{trial:abrave}}, where avelumab improved outcome only
above a baseline stromal score of 30% [@dieci2026abrave]. That is a biomarker analysis of a trial
that missed its primary endpoint, and the score is a percentage read on one slide rather than a
property of the tumour. Residual cancer burden was not predictive in the same analysis.

Tumour-specific class II major histocompatibility expression was associated with response to
atezolizumab and not to chemotherapy alone in {{trial:neotrip}} [@balko2025]. It requires
quantitative imaging mass cytometry that no routine laboratory runs.

Tumour mutational burden carries tumour-agnostic evidence from studies that had no breast cohort
[@marabelle2020]. High burden is also uncommon in this disease [@barrososousa2020].

```caution
Do not carry the metastatic selection rule backwards across surgery. A patient with a combined
positive score below 10 is not a patient for whom neoadjuvant pembrolizumab is unproven, because
the trial that established the regimen did not select on that score at all [@schmid2020]. The
reverse carry is also wrong, and [[BS-7730]] states it from the other side.
```

```interplay target=BC-350
The reason a percentage of PD-L1 staining fails to predict is the reason given in Part VI for most
biomarker failure in this disease. What a checkpoint inhibitor releases is a relationship between
cell types, not a property of one cell. A score computed on a block reports abundance and discards
arrangement, exactly as a HER2 percentage does in [[BS-2260]], and the spatial phenotypes in
[[BS-1940]] separate patients that no abundance measure can. There is a second point specific to
the neoadjuvant setting. Treatment is being given to an intact primary and its draining nodes,
which is where priming happens [[BS-1710]]. A marker measured on a core taken before any of that
describes the starting state of a system whose behaviour depends on what the first two cycles do to
it.
```

## BS-7330 De-escalation after pathological complete response and the immune toxicity cost

After a pathological complete response the residual risk is low and it is front-loaded. In a
pooled reconstruction of five neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy trials, most recurrences occurred
within 24 months of randomisation, in patients with and without a complete response [@licata2025].
A de-escalation decision taken after surgery is therefore a decision about the next two years
rather than the next ten.

```evidence setting=early subtype=TNBC line=post-neoadjuvant modality=immunotherapy topic=de-escalation cols=acronym,phase,population,arms,endpoint,status caption=The_only_randomised_test_of_omitting_the_adjuvant_checkpoint_phase_after_a_complete_response.
```

That single row is the entire randomised record for this question, and it has not reported. It
compares omission against continuation in patients who already cleared, so the verdict it can
return is non-inferiority and nothing else. It will not say what the adjuvant phase is worth in a
patient with residual disease, which is a different population treated under [[BC-850]].

The cost side of the trade is specific to this setting. More than half the patients in the control
arm of {{trial:keynote522}} reached a complete response without pembrolizumab [@schmid2020]. A
permanent endocrinopathy in one of those patients is a different event from the same endocrinopathy
in incurable disease. The structural features that make these toxicities unlike chemotherapy
toxicity are in [[BS-4180]] and [[BS-4510]].

Two arguments support omission and neither of them answers the question. {{trial:geparnuevo}}
separated on overall survival with no adjuvant checkpoint phase at all [@loibl2022geparnuevo]. That
was a secondary endpoint in a trial that missed its primary one. And a complete response identifies
a group whose absolute risk leaves little room for an adjuvant drug to work.

The obstacle is arithmetic rather than conceptual. Three-year event-free survival after a complete
response on this regimen is high, so a non-inferiority trial needs either a very large sample or a
margin wide enough to permit real harm. That is the structure of the trastuzumab duration trials in
[[BS-6980]], where near-identical estimates produced opposite verdicts because the margins
differed. [[BS-6370]] sets out what to demand of the answer when it arrives.

```practice
Do not stop adjuvant pembrolizumab early on the grounds that the tumour responded. That is the
question {{trial:optimice}} is asking, and it has not answered it.

Stopping it is defensible when a significant immune-related event occurs after a complete
response. The remaining absolute benefit is small and unproven. The toxicity in front of you is
neither.

Separate the two drugs when an immune event appears during the neoadjuvant phase. Hold the
checkpoint inhibitor and continue the chemotherapy [[BS-4510]].

Do not de-escalate local therapy on response alone. Nodal management after a complete response is a
separate randomised question {{trial:nsabp-b51}} [@mamounas2025].
```

## BS-7340 Unresolved questions and what reads out next

```evidence setting=early subtype=TNBC status=ongoing,awaited sort=acronym cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,endpoint,status caption=Early_triple-negative_trials_that_have_not_reported_their_primary_endpoint._Four_of_the_six_concern_what_happens_after_surgery_rather_than_before_it.
```

Six trials in this cell have not reported a primary endpoint, and only two of them will change
what is given before the operation.

{{trial:scarlet}} asks whether the anthracycline can be removed from a pembrolizumab and platinum
regimen. A non-inferior result would take an anthracycline, and with it the cardiac and leukaemic
risks, out of a young population treated with curative intent. It is the only randomised outcome
trial of that question anywhere in this chapter.

{{trial:neotrip}} has yet to report the event-free survival endpoint it was powered for
[@gianni2022neotrip]. Its response result, published as a secondary endpoint, was negative. A
positive survival result would add a second example of the dissociation that {{trial:geparnuevo}}
produced, and would strengthen the case that response is the wrong readout for this drug class.

{{trial:optimice}} asks whether the adjuvant checkpoint phase can be dropped after a complete
response. That question is discussed in [[BS-7330]] and again in [[BS-7240]].

The other three sit after surgery, and they are not one experiment repeated. {{trial:ascent05}}
gives pembrolizumab in both arms and asks what a conjugate adds to it, against a control that may
or may not include capecitabine [@tolaney2024optimicerd]. {{trial:tropion-b03}} randomises a
different conjugate with or without an anti-PD-L1 antibody, against a control the investigator
chooses from capecitabine, pembrolizumab or both [@bardia2024tropionb03]. A control arm selected by
the investigator is honest about practice and imprecise about the drug. {{trial:sascia-gbg-102}}
asks the same question across HER2-negative disease rather than triple-negative disease alone, so
hormone receptor-positive patients sit inside its answer. All three belong to [[BC-850]], and they
appear here because a preoperative regimen that raises response decides how many patients reach
them.

Four questions in this chapter have no trial running at all.

Nobody is removing carboplatin from the chemoimmunotherapy backbone, so its contribution there
stays unmeasured.

No trial randomises a checkpoint inhibitor in stage I disease, where the regimen is increasingly
used by extrapolation. {{trial:scarlet}} enrols stage I patients and gives pembrolizumab in both
arms.

No biomarker-driven omission trial is running, because [[BS-7320]] explains that there is no
marker to build one on.

And no trial sets one checkpoint inhibitor against another. Every comparison a clinician makes
between {{trial:keynote522}} and the atezolizumab and durvalumab regimens is a cross-trial
comparison, with all the confounding set out in [[BS-7310]].
