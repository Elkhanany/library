## BS-0200 Breast cancer as a family of diseases rather than one disease

The claim that breast cancer is several diseases is made so often that it has stopped meaning
anything. It is worth restating in a form that can be checked.

Two conditions are different diseases, for practical purposes, when they differ in natural history,
in the site and timing of recurrence, and in what treatment works. Breast cancer meets that test.
Patterns of distant relapse differ systematically between hormone receptor positive, HER2 positive
and triple-negative disease, including which organ is involved first and how long after diagnosis
[@kennecke2010]. Drug sensitivity differs so completely that a trial in one group is uninformative
about another.

Molecular work gave the families a second, partly independent description. Expression clustering
produced intrinsic subtypes [@perou2000; @sorlie2001]. Integration of copy number with expression
produced a different partition again [@curtis2012]. Multiplatform integration recovered a small
number of classes, each still heterogeneous internally [@tcga2012].

Hold two things apart. That the partitions agree in outline is evidence that something real is being
tracked. That they disagree in detail is evidence that none of them is the partition. The derivation
and the disagreement are handled in [[BC-100]], and the diversity inside the triple-negative group
in [[BC-120]].

The working position of this book is deliberately weak. Breast cancer is a family of diseases in the
sense that it needs separate trials, separate drugs and separate expectations. It is not established
that the current family boundaries are where the biology divides.

## BS-0210 Local disease, systemic disease, and metastatic competence

Three claims get compressed into the phrase early breast cancer, and they are not the same claim.
The first is that disease is confined to the breast and regional nodes. The second is that tumour
cells are present elsewhere. The third is that those cells can produce a growing metastasis.

Only the third is metastatic competence, and it is rare. Experimental work tracking individual cells
after extravasation showed that most cells that reach a distant organ do not form a tumour, and that
a substantial proportion persist as solitary dormant cells [@luzzi1998]. Dissemination is common.
Metastasis is not.

Human data point the same way. A pooled analysis of bone marrow aspirates found disseminated tumour
cells in a considerable proportion of patients with operable disease, and their presence carried
prognostic weight without being deterministic [@braun2005]. Dissemination can also precede the
clinical detection of the primary tumour, which undermines any model in which metastasis is a late
event that follows local growth in order [@hosseini2016].

The clinical consequence is the one Fisher drew and is developed in [[BC-010]]. Local treatment
controls local disease. It does not determine whether disease that has already left will grow.

That leaves a real question rather than a slogan. If dissemination is common and metastasis is rare,
then the interesting variable is not whether cells escaped. It is what governs whether an escaped
cell stays quiet or starts dividing. That question is the whole of [[BC-330]].

## BS-0220 Tumor-intrinsic biology and host context as two separate variables

Two tumours with identical molecular profiles in two different people do not behave identically. The
difference lives in the host, and this book treats host context as a separate axis rather than as
noise around the tumour measurement.

Host context includes immune competence, adiposity and its metabolic consequences, skeletal muscle
mass, age, comorbidity and the treatment the patient can actually receive. Fasting insulin
concentration at diagnosis has been associated with outcome in early disease [@goodwin2002].
Sarcopenia has been associated with chemotherapy toxicity and with time to progression in metastatic
disease [@prado2009]. Neither is a property of the cancer.

There is a measurement trap here that catches almost everyone. An assay performed on tumour tissue is
not necessarily reporting tumour-intrinsic biology. A stromal gene expression signature predicts
outcome, and stroma is host [@finak2008]. Tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes carry prognostic
information that differs by subtype, and lymphocytes are host [@denkert2018]. The tissue-resident
memory phenotype associated with better outcome is a property of host T cells sitting inside the
tumour [@savas2018].

So the axis is not tissue against blood. It is tumour cell against everything else, and a single
block of tumour contains both. Keeping the two apart is what makes the microenvironment chapters
coherent. The cellular detail sits in [[BC-210]] and [[BC-220]], and the systemic host factors in
[[BC-250]].

## BS-0230 Spatial and temporal evolution as the organizing axes of this book

Every biopsy is a sample in two dimensions at once. It samples one place in a structure, and it
samples one moment in a process. Almost every measurement controversy in this field is a failure to
state which of the two is being discussed.

The spatial axis is the observation that a tumour is not uniform. Single-cell sequencing showed
discrete subpopulations rather than a smooth gradient within one tumour [@navin2011]. Multiregion
sequencing showed that different regions of one primary carry different subclonal mixtures
[@yates2015]. Invasion itself can be multiclonal, with several genotypes crossing the basement
membrane together [@casasent2018]. The consequences for sampling are worked through in [[BC-270]].

The temporal axis is the observation that the mixture changes, and that treatment is what changes it
most. Paired samples taken before treatment and at progression show alterations that emerged only
under the drug that selected for them [@oleary2018]. That is developed in [[BC-300]].

The two axes interact rather than adding. Spatial diversity at diagnosis supplies the material on
which later selection acts. A tumour with no subclonal diversity has fewer routes available to it
than one with a lot, whatever pressure is applied.

The general framework, including the taxonomy of heterogeneity and the models of evolution, is built
in [[BC-260]]. This section only fixes the two words, so that spatial and temporal are never used
interchangeably in what follows.

## BS-0240 Biomarker, surrogate, and endpoint, three words that are not interchangeable

These three words are used as though they were synonyms, and conflating them has produced more bad
inference in oncology than any other single habit.

An endpoint is what is measured in a trial as the outcome of interest. A biomarker is a
characteristic measured as an indicator of a normal process, a pathogenic process or a response to
an intervention [@bdwg2001]. A surrogate endpoint is a biomarker intended to substitute for a
clinical endpoint, and it carries the additional claim that an effect on the surrogate predicts an
effect on the clinical outcome.

The extra claim is the whole problem, and it is a claim about trials rather than about patients. A
marker can be strongly prognostic in individuals and still fail as a trial-level surrogate.

Pathologic complete response is the worked example, and it is unusually clean. In a pooled analysis
of 12 neoadjuvant trials with 11955 patients, patients achieving pathologic complete response had
better event-free survival, with a hazard ratio of 0.48 against those who did not [@cortazar2014].
At trial level, the association between an increase in the frequency of pathologic complete response
and improvement in event-free survival was negligible, with an R-squared of 0.03.

Read those two results together. Knowing this patient responded completely tells you something real
about this patient. Knowing that a drug raised the response rate in a trial tells you very little
about whether it will extend survival. Both statements come from the same dataset.

The parallel framework for assays, covering analytic validity, clinical validity and clinical
utility, is built once in [[BS-0970]] and used throughout [@teutsch2009]. Endpoint choice and
regulatory consequence are developed in [[BC-740]].

## BS-0250 Definitions that move, HER2-positive to HER2-low to HER2-ultralow

HER2 status began as a binary question asked in order to select patients for an antibody. The assay
and its thresholds were built for that purpose and validated against it.

Two things then happened that the original design did not anticipate. Conjugated drugs became active
at expression levels well below the positivity threshold. Trials were designed with eligibility
boundaries drawn inside the territory previously labelled negative.

{{trial:destiny-b04}} enrolled patients scored immunohistochemistry 1+, or 2+ without amplification,
and excluded immunohistochemistry 0 [@modi2022]. {{trial:destiny-b06}} moved the boundary down
again. Of 866 patients randomized, 713 had HER2-low disease and 153 had HER2-ultralow disease,
defined as immunohistochemistry 0 with membrane staining [@bardia2024destinyb06].

Note what changed and what did not. No new assay was introduced. No biological discontinuity was
demonstrated at either new boundary. A pre-existing continuous measurement was cut in two more
places, and the cuts were placed where a trial needed them.

The guideline position is candid about this. The 2023 update affirmed the existing recommendations
and declined to create new result categories, holding that it was premature to do so [@wolff2023].

The measurement problem is developed in [[BS-0930]] and resolved as a heterogeneity problem in
[[BS-2360]]. Neither argument is repeated here. What this section supplies is the general form of
the case, which the next section states.

## BS-0260 Terminology driven by trial eligibility rather than by biology, a recurring theme

The general pattern is this. A trial needs an operational rule for who may enrol. The rule is written
using an assay that already exists, because writing a new assay would delay the trial. The trial
succeeds. The rule then enters guidelines, pathology reports and ordinary speech as though it named
something in the disease.

HER2-low is the cleanest instance and it is not the only one. The 1% threshold for oestrogen receptor
positivity was chosen so that endocrine therapy would not be withheld from a patient who might
benefit, rather than because behaviour changes at 1% of nuclei stained [@hammond2010]. The ER-low
category of 1% to 10% was then added as a reporting category for a band whose expression profile
resembles receptor negative disease [@allison2020; @iwamoto2012]. Triple-negative disease is named
for the failure of two assays and contains several distinct biologies [[BC-120]].

Three consequences follow, and they are the reason this theme recurs.

A patient can change category without any change in their disease. A moved threshold or a repeated
assay is enough.

A category inherits the reproducibility of the assay that defines it. If observers assign it
inconsistently, every downstream statistic inherits that inconsistency [[BC-110]].

A trial result is a statement about the population the eligibility rule actually selected. Applying
it to patients the rule would have excluded is an extrapolation, and it should be labelled as one
[[BC-740]].

The general lesson about antigen thresholds as eligibility criteria is drawn out in [[BS-5110]].

## BS-0270 Evidence, uncertainty, and what the word cure is doing

Cure is used in two senses that point in different directions, and the ambiguity is not harmless.

The individual sense means that no viable tumour cell remains. It is unobservable. No assay
available today can establish it, and the sensitivity limits of the ones that come closest are set
out in [[BC-390]].

The population sense is statistical. A group is said to be statistically cured when its excess
mortality relative to the background population falls to zero. That is a measurable quantity, and in
hormone receptor positive disease it does not reach zero on any timescale that has been observed.

The numbers make the point without any interpretation. Annual hazard rates of recurrence continue at
a low level for decades after primary therapy [@saphner1996]. Long follow-up of trial cohorts shows
recurrences continuing throughout 24 years of observation [@colleoni2016]. Among women disease-free
after five years of endocrine therapy, the risk of distant recurrence over the following 15 years
remained substantial [@pan2017]. That residual risk scaled with the original nodal status and tumour
diameter.

So for a large group of patients, the word cure asserts something that the follow-up does not
support, and it asserts it at the moment when patients most want to hear it. The alternative is not
despair. It is a hazard curve, which is a truthful object and can be explained.

Two forward pointers close the chapter. The hazard is quantified, and its biology examined, in
[[BC-330]]. Whether durable disease control in metastatic disease should ever be called cure is
taken up in [[BS-6700]].
