## BS-7650 Why this group is under-served by both the hormone receptor-positive and HER2-positive trials

Hormone receptor status divides HER2-positive metastatic disease into two groups of comparable size.
Only one of them has a trial literature of its own, and it is not this one. This group is studied
almost entirely as a subgroup of trials designed for a neighbouring question.

The exclusion runs in both directions. The hormone receptor-positive trials that built modern
endocrine practice took HER2 positivity as an exclusion criterion. The HER2-positive trials enrolled
this group and stratified for it. They were not powered within it. What sits between the two
literatures is the largest single cell in metastatic breast cancer without a dedicated evidence base.

The consequence has a specific shape. Almost every headline figure a reader knows for this group
comes from a subgroup of a trial that was asking something else. The standard for what a subgroup can
support is set in [[BC-740]], and applying it here is uncomfortable. Much of what is practised in
this group rests on analyses that were neither powered nor prespecified as primary.

The biology says this should matter rather than being a bookkeeping complaint. Reciprocal signalling
between the oestrogen receptor and the HER2 axis, described in [[BC-140]] and [[BC-150]], makes each
pathway an escape route from blockade of the other. Blocking HER2 restores oestrogen receptor
signalling. Blocking the receptor pushes growth signalling back through HER2. A tumour carrying both
has a route out that a tumour carrying one does not.

That is the argument for treating both axes. It is also the argument for expecting this group to
behave differently from the hormone receptor-negative half of every HER2 trial it was enrolled in.
The trials were not built to test either claim.

Before any of that, be clear about what the label measures. Triple-positive is an intersection of two
assays read against two conventions rather than a biological class. Oestrogen receptor positivity is
a stain scored against a low threshold, and the tumours sitting just above it do not behave like the
strongly positive ones [[BS-0910]]. HER2 positivity is a separate stain with a separate scoring rule
and its own spatial variation [[BS-2370]]. A patient whose tumour is weakly positive on both assays
has been placed in this category by two conventions acting together. The endocrine arm of the
strategy is the one that convention puts most at risk.

Each section below opens with the question its trials were built to answer, taken from the
[Chapter Stories](stories.html) map, and closes with a table filtered on that same question. The
story says what was being looked for and what the design cannot settle. The table says what else
is in the cell. The prose argues the decision.

## BS-7660 Endocrine maintenance after induction

```story SQ-0750
```

```evidence setting=metastatic subtype=HR+/HER2+ line=1L,2L modality=endocrine sort=year cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,result,os caption=Chemotherapy-free_and_maintenance_endocrine_trials_designed_in_this_population._Every_control_arm_here_is_a_regimen_a_clinic_would_no_longer_offer.
```

The pattern in practice is induction chemotherapy with dual HER2 blockade until best response, then
chemotherapy stops and endocrine therapy joins the continuing antibodies. Adoption is close to
universal. What sits under it is a ladder of superseded control arms rather than a test of the
pattern itself.

Read the control arms in that table before the results. Every trial in it that added a drug to an
existing regimen found the same thing. Each randomised addition delayed progression against the
regimen it was added to. That is a finding about the strategy rather than about any drug. Adding
an antibody to an aromatase inhibitor beat the inhibitor alone. Adding a second HER2-directed drug
to that pair beat the pair. Adding a CDK4/6 inhibitor to antibody and endocrine maintenance beat
the maintenance. No entry compares any two of those regimens with each other.

The table therefore looks like a disagreement about how much HER2 blockade an endocrine backbone
needs. It is not. {{trial:tandem}} and {{trial:egf30008}} were designed when the alternative on
offer was endocrine therapy alone. {{trial:pertain}} and {{trial:alternative}} were designed when
it was one antibody with an aromatase inhibitor. {{trial:patina}} was designed when it was
HER2-directed treatment with endocrine therapy, continued after chemotherapy stopped. Each control
arm is the previous experimental arm. The divergence down the rows is chronological, and reading
it as a comparison between drugs is the commonest error made with this table.

Two entries sit off that ladder. {{trial:alternative}} is the only one that required prior
trastuzumab, so it asks what to do at second line rather than at first [@johnston2020a]. It also
enrolled only patients for whom chemotherapy was not intended, which selects on a clinician's
judgement rather than on anything measured. Its primary report gave a median progression-free
survival of 11 months with the triplet against 5.7 months with trastuzumab and an aromatase
inhibitor, a hazard ratio of 0.62 [@johnston2018]. Lapatinib with an aromatase inhibitor also beat
that control, at 0.71. The updated analysis no longer separated those two arms, at 0.85
[@johnston2020a]. {{trial:sysucc-002}} runs the ladder sideways instead of
up, because it swapped the antibody's partner rather than adding to it, and because it was powered
for noninferiority rather than superiority [@hua2022]. It is argued in [[BS-7680]].

{{trial:egf30008}} is the only entry whose population was not defined by HER2 status at all. That
makes its centrally confirmed HER2-negative stratum an internal negative control, which no
single-subtype trial in the table can offer [[BS-7700]].

Overall survival is the column this table fills worst, and the reasons are design rather than
effect. Most of the endocrine-only arm in {{trial:tandem}} crossed to trastuzumab on progression,
which leaves its survival comparison unable to settle anything [@kaufman2009]. {{trial:pertain}}
reported survival and found its arms no different, in a trial whose progression endpoint had
separated them [@rimawi2018; @arpino2023]. In eLEcTRA, letrozole with trastuzumab was more
effective than letrozole alone as first-line therapy [@huober2012]. {{trial:alternative}} reported a direction without figures.
{{trial:sysucc-002}} and {{trial:patina}} have not reported survival at all. A reader who wants to
know what any of this buys in time alive is not served by the table, and on these designs will not
be.

The decision a clinic actually makes is narrower than anything randomised here. It is whether to
attach endocrine therapy to the antibodies at the point chemotherapy stops. No trial in the table
randomised that. {{trial:patina}} comes closest, because its control arm is the maintenance pattern
itself and is the only modern estimate of what that pattern delivers [@metzger2026patina]. It did
not test the pattern. It assumed it, in both arms.

So endocrine maintenance rests on mechanism. Oestrogen receptor signalling is upregulated as an
adaptive survival mechanism in HER2-positive tumours under anti-HER2 therapy [@giuliano2015]. If an
unblocked receptor is an escape route from HER2 blockade, leaving it unblocked through a phase
measured in years leaves the route open. That is a reasonable inference from [[BC-140]], and it
should be labelled as an inference rather than reported as evidence. Real-world series support the
maintenance itself rather than any particular addition to it [@carausu2022].

The choice of agent inside maintenance has less behind it still. Every trial in this cell that
fixed its endocrine partner fixed it as an aromatase inhibitor. None compared one endocrine class
against another in the presence of HER2 blockade. {{trial:tandem}}, {{trial:egf30008}} and
{{trial:alternative}} were restricted to postmenopausal women by protocol. {{trial:sysucc-002}} is
the only entry that allowed ovarian suppression, and it also left the endocrine agent to the
investigator, so it cannot separate the agent from the choice. Extrapolating to ovarian
suppression with an aromatase inhibitor is reasonable. It is still extrapolation, which is worth
saying to a young patient who asks what the evidence is.

## BS-7670 CDK4/6 inhibition with HER2 blockade

```story SQ-0770,SQ-0780,SQ-0790
```

```evidence setting=metastatic subtype=HR+/HER2+ modality=cdk4-6 sort=year cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,endpoint,result,os caption=CDK4/6_inhibition_added_to_HER2_blockade_in_this_population._The_three_trials_sit_at_different_points_in_the_disease_course_and_are_not_a_dose-ranging_series.
```

Cyclin D1 and CDK4/6 sit downstream of both the oestrogen receptor and the HER2 axis, which is the
convergence described in [[BC-160]]. Blocking a convergence point is a different strategy from
blocking the two inputs above it. It predicts activity where blockade of either input alone has
failed.

All three trials in that table support the prediction. Two of them sit after several HER2-directed
lines have failed, and the third sits in first-line maintenance. They are not a series. The line, the
selection rule and the control arm change together between them, so nothing in the table compares one
CDK4/6 inhibitor with another.

{{trial:monarcher}} isolates the endocrine contribution by design rather than by subgroup
[@tolaney2020]. Its second arm gave the same CDK4/6 inhibitor with trastuzumab and no endocrine
partner. Only the arm carrying the endocrine partner improved on chemotherapy. That internal
comparison is worth more than the headline, because it says which component is doing the work.

It is a screening study and should be read as one. Its progression-free survival result was declared
against a prespecified two-sided alpha of 0.2, a threshold for deciding what to test next rather
than what to do [@tolaney2020]. The distinction is the subject of [[BS-6390]]. Its survival
comparison carried no alpha of its own [@tolaney2024]. Its chemotherapy control predates the move of
a conjugate into second line, so its patients arrived with less behind them than a patient arriving
there today.

{{trial:patricia-solti}} pushes the selection from a receptor stain to a transcriptional class
[@ciruelos2026]. That is a measurement argument rather than a biological one. A receptor stain read
against a scoring threshold and a PAM50 call on the same tumour disagree often enough that neither
stands in for the other. The subtype rule was derived in the study's own earlier single-arm cohorts,
then tested in a randomised cohort built around it [@ciruelos2020]. That is hypothesis generation and
confirmation inside one protocol. Its randomised assignments include seven re-randomisations of
patients already allocated once, so there are fewer participants than the number in the table. No
non-luminal randomised cohort was ever run, so nothing shows the rule excludes anyone correctly.

{{trial:patina}} asked the question at the other end of the course, and is the only phase III of the
three [@metzger2026patina]. It randomised patients whose disease had not progressed after induction,
with no biomarker selection at all. Its population is selected by response rather than by assay, and
its control arm is the maintenance a clinic already gives. That is what makes it the trial the
decision rests on. Median progression-free survival was 44.3 months with palbociclib against 29.1
months without it [@metzger2026patina]. It says what a CDK4/6 inhibitor adds to a patient who is
already doing well. It does not say what it adds to everyone who starts first-line treatment.

The price belongs in the same sentence as that gain, because of where it is paid. Grade 3 adverse
events occurred in 79.7% of patients on palbociclib against 30.6% without it, predominantly
neutropenia [@metzger2026patina]. Those events are incurred in maintenance, in patients who are
well and who have already finished their chemotherapy. Overall survival has not been reported, so
the trade on offer is a progression endpoint against a measured toxicity rate.

Two things the table cannot settle. None of the three carries a survival result that can be leaned
on. That gap matters most for PATINA, which proposes years of an added drug in a phase when
patients feel well and neutropenia is felt. Nobody has randomised the selection question either.
{{trial:patricia}} selected on subtype and PATINA selected on nothing, in different lines, so the two cannot be
set against each other.

## BS-7680 Chemotherapy omission and the evidence that supports it

```story SQ-0760
```

The live de-escalation question here is whether chemotherapy is needed at all in a patient with
low-burden, indolent disease and two targetable pathways. The argument for omission is that such a
patient has a trajectory measured in years. Chemotherapy contributes toxicity to a regimen whose
activity comes from targeted agents.

{{trial:sysucc-002}} is the only randomised test of that idea, and its shape decides what it can
support [@hua2022]. It compared an antibody with endocrine therapy against the same antibody with
chemotherapy, powered for noninferiority on progression-free survival. Its comparator was a single
antibody with chemotherapy rather than the dual blockade with a taxane that {{trial:cleopatra}} had
already made standard. Noninferiority was therefore demonstrated against a regimen its own era had
improved on. Its overall survival is not yet reported, so what is traded for the lower toxicity
remains unmeasured.

The margin discipline in [[BS-6370]] is the reason to be careful with the reading. A trial showing no
significant difference between a chemotherapy-containing and a chemotherapy-free regimen does not
establish equivalence unless it was powered against a prespecified margin. A margin set against a
superseded comparator does not transfer to the comparator it has been superseded by.

{{trial:pertain}} puts a version of the same question informally, because induction chemotherapy in
it was left to investigator discretion [@arpino2023]. Its chemotherapy-free group is a group a
clinician judged did not need chemotherapy. That is not a randomised comparison and it cannot be
converted into one. Retrospective and real-world series carry the same defect in a larger form,
because the patients in whom clinicians omit chemotherapy are the patients with indolent disease.

```practice
Before omitting chemotherapy outside a trial, state what you would accept as the cost. A patient with
a decade of expected survival and low disease burden is a different proposition from one with
visceral crisis, and the second is not a de-escalation candidate whatever the receptor profile.

Say whether you are omitting chemotherapy or deferring it. Deferral keeps an option and is the
easier decision to revisit at the first scan.

Record the reason in the notes. The next clinician needs to know whether the patient declined
chemotherapy, could not have it, or was judged not to need it.
```

## BS-7690 Conjugates in triple-positive disease

```evidence setting=metastatic subtype=HR+/HER2+ modality=adc sort=year cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,result,os caption=Conjugate_trials_that_enrolled_this_group._All_four_were_designed_for_HER2-positive_disease_as_a_whole_and_report_this_group_as_a_stratified_subgroup.
```

Conjugates do not care about the oestrogen receptor. A HER2-directed conjugate delivers payload to a
HER2-expressing cell whether or not that cell also expresses the oestrogen receptor. There is no
mechanistic reason to expect reduced activity in this group. In the trials whose hormone
receptor-positive stratum has been reported, that stratum tracked the trial it sat inside
[@verma2012; @krop2014th3resa; @cortes2022destinyb03].

What differs between the rows is not the receptor. {{trial:emilia}} set a conjugate against
lapatinib with capecitabine. {{trial:th3resa}} set the same conjugate against treatment of
physician's choice after two or more HER2-directed regimens. {{trial:destiny-b03}} set a newer
conjugate against the older one. {{trial:destiny-b09}} set a conjugate with pertuzumab against a
taxane with dual blockade, before anything else. Four control arms and three positions in the
sequence, because two of them enter after trastuzumab and a taxane. A reader comparing
progression-free survival down the column is comparing comparators.

{{trial:th3resa}} is the row whose control arm still resembles a real decision, because physician's
choice is what a heavily pretreated patient is otherwise offered [@krop2014th3resa]. Its survival
comparison did not cross its own stopping boundary at the interim analysis, which is a statement
about the analysis plan rather than about the drug. Its final survival analysis is a separate report
and is not the figure the table carries [@krop2017]. {{trial:emilia}} matters now mainly as the trial
that established the class in this line, against a comparator nobody offers
[@verma2012; @diras2017].

The decision rests on {{trial:destiny-b03}}, because it is the only randomised comparison against the
conjugate a clinic would otherwise reach for [@cortes2022destinyb03; @hurvitz2023]. Nothing in its
hormone receptor-positive stratum argues for handling this group differently from the rest of the
subtype.

What the table cannot show is the endocrine question. No conjugate trial carries an
endocrine-containing arm. None asked whether endocrine therapy should run alongside a conjugate, be
held through it, or resume after it. That is the one thing distinguishing this group from the rest of
HER2-positive disease, and it is the comparison nobody randomised. The conjugate therefore competes
for position against endocrine therapy as well as against other HER2-directed agents, on no evidence
at all.

{{trial:destiny-b09}} sharpens that problem rather than solving it [@tolaney2026destinyb09]. Its
third group is still blinded and its overall survival is not yet reported, so its row is an interim
view of a trial that has not finished speaking. If first-line induction becomes a conjugate with dual
blockade, the maintenance phase {{trial:patina}} improved is not quite the phase a clinic will be
managing. A trial that randomised after chemotherapy-based induction has been overtaken at its starting
point.

The ordering decision that follows has no trial behind it and is made weekly. A patient with slow,
low-burden disease and an intact endocrine axis can often be held on endocrine therapy with HER2
blockade while the conjugate is kept in reserve. A patient with rapid visceral progression cannot,
and the conjugate is the agent with the response rate to match the tempo. That is a judgement about
disease behaviour rather than about receptor status, and [[BC-570]] sets out how to make it.

```interplay target=BC-350
A tumour scored positive for both receptors on one core may contain regions expressing one and not
the other. That is the discordance documented in [[BC-290]] and the spatial HER2 variation in
[[BC-280]]. Treating both axes at once is therefore partly a hedge against a sampling problem rather
than only a hedge against resistance. It also means a failure attributed here to biology may be a
failure of the block that was stained, which is the ceiling argued in [[BS-6710]].
```

## BS-7700 Subgroup analyses from the major trials and what they can support

Most of what is known about this group comes from hormone receptor-positive subgroups of the
HER2-positive registration trials. Those subgroups are informative. They have three limits that
should be stated whenever they are used.

They were not powered. A subgroup holding roughly half a trial holds roughly half its events, with a
correspondingly wider confidence interval. An apparently smaller effect in one subgroup is frequently
compatible with the overall effect of the trial.

They are prone to the interaction fallacy. A treatment effect that reaches significance in one
subgroup and not in the other is not evidence of a differential effect. Establishing that requires a
test of interaction, which is less often reported and less often significant than the subgroup
comparison it would qualify.

{{trial:egf30008}} is the exception worth naming, because it was not a subgroup analysis at all
[@johnston2009]. It enrolled hormone receptor-positive patients regardless of HER2 status and made
the HER2-positive group its prespecified primary analysis. The benefit appeared where HER2 was
amplified and not in the centrally confirmed HER2-negative population. That is an internal control no
single-subtype trial can offer, and it is the strongest evidence in this chapter that the target is
the target. It is also a measurement claim as much as a biological one, because the negative stratum
was defined by central confirmation rather than by the local report.

One consistent observation across trials is prognostic rather than predictive. Hormone
receptor-positive HER2-positive disease follows a more indolent trajectory and a later relapse
pattern than the hormone receptor-negative half of the same subtype. That describes the natural
history. It should not be converted into a claim that treatment works less well, which is a different
proposition needing a different analysis [[BS-6390]].

## BS-7710 Unresolved questions and what reads out next

The questions this group needs answered are questions no current trial is powered for.

Whether endocrine therapy should run concurrently with induction rather than start at maintenance.
Mechanism argues for concurrent, because an unblocked receptor is an open escape route from the
first cycle onwards. No trial has compared the two schedules.

Whether chemotherapy can be omitted in selected patients. This needs a de-escalation trial with a
prespecified margin, in this population, against a modern comparator. It does not exist, and
{{trial:sysucc-002}} is not a substitute for it.

Whether the selection should be a stain or a transcriptional class. {{trial:patricia-solti}} argues
for the class and derived its rule in its own earlier cohorts. {{trial:patina}} improved outcomes
with no selection at all. The two sit in different lines, so neither tests the other's rule.

Whether there is still a maintenance phase to put a CDK4/6 inhibitor into. That depends on what
first-line treatment becomes after {{trial:destiny-b09}}, which is a question about induction rather
than about maintenance.

Whether this group should be enrolled separately rather than stratified. That is a trial design
question and it is the one that would fix the others. A population that is half of a common subtype
and is studied only as a subgroup will keep generating practice that rests on subgroups.
