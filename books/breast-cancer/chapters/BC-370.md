## BS-3270 Evaluation of symptoms and imaging abnormalities

Diagnostic evaluation is a sequence of conditional probabilities, and the order of operations
matters.

Triple assessment pairs clinical examination, imaging and tissue sampling because each has a
different failure mode. A palpable mass with benign imaging is not excluded, because mammography and
ultrasound both miss cancers, particularly in dense tissue [[BS-3210]]. Imaging that is negative in
the face of a suspicious clinical finding lowers the probability of cancer. It does not remove it.

The commonest diagnostic error in this setting is treating a negative test as an answer rather than
as an update. A negative result on a test with 85% sensitivity leaves substantial residual
probability when pretest probability was high to begin with.

Two presentations deserve named exceptions. Inflammatory breast cancer presents as diffuse skin
change without a discrete mass, and imaging is frequently unhelpful, so the diagnosis is clinical
and confirmed by skin and parenchymal biopsy [[BS-0770]]. Invasive lobular carcinoma grows in single
files without forming a mass, so it is systematically underestimated by mammography and ultrasound
[[BS-0740]].

The rule that follows is simple to state and often ignored. When clinical suspicion and imaging
disagree, sample the tissue.

## BS-3280 Tissue acquisition, specimen adequacy, and handling

This section determines the reliability of every result in [[BC-380]] and [[BC-390]], and it is
almost always treated as logistics rather than as measurement.

Core needle biopsy replaced fine needle aspiration for a structural reason. Distinguishing invasive
carcinoma from carcinoma in situ requires seeing whether the basement membrane has been breached,
and that requires intact architecture. A cytological preparation can establish malignancy and cannot
establish invasion.

Preanalytic variables determine what the receptor assays will report. Time from devascularisation to
fixative, the fixative used, and the duration of fixation all alter antigen preservation, and the
ASCO and College of American Pathologists requirements exist because of this [@allison2020;
@wolff2023]. The mechanisms are set out in [[BS-0960]] and are not repeated here. What belongs here
is that these are properties of the specimen journey and not of the tumour, and they are decided by
people who never see the report.

Molecular assays impose their own adequacy requirement, and it is quantifiable. In {{trial:ctrak-tn}}, 208
patients registered, 185 had the tumour successfully sequenced, and 171 of those had trackable
mutations, which is 92.4% [@turner2022ctrak]. Roughly one patient in six who entered the study never
reached the assay, and almost all of that loss occurred at the tissue step.

The practical consequence for a clinician ordering a biopsy is to take enough tissue the first time.
Retrieving a second specimen costs a procedure, a delay, and sometimes the window in which the
result would have changed the decision.

## BS-3290 Radiological and pathological concordance

Concordance is a judgement made by comparing two independent assessments of the same lesion, and it
is the main safety net against a false negative biopsy.

The question is narrow. Does the histology explain the imaging finding. A fibroadenoma reported from
a spiculated mass does not. A benign core from a BI-RADS 5 lesion does not. Fat necrosis reported
from new microcalcification may or may not, depending on the calcification morphology.

Discordance requires action rather than reassurance, and the action is repeat sampling or excision.
The failure mode is to accept a benign result because it is benign.

Two lesion types generate systematic discordance and deserve naming. Ductal carcinoma in situ
extends further than its mammographic calcifications suggest, so a core that samples the
calcification may underestimate the lesion [[BS-0710]]. Invasive lobular carcinoma is underestimated
in extent by every conventional modality [[BS-0740]].

A separate class of discordance is upgrade risk. Atypical ductal hyperplasia and some papillary and
radial lesions on core biopsy are upgraded to malignancy at excision often enough that the core
result cannot stand alone, and [[BS-4200]] covers the management.

## BS-3300 Anatomic TNM and its enduring logic

Tumour size and nodal status have survived a century of proposed replacements, and it is worth being
explicit about why.

They measure two different things. Size measures how much local growth has occurred, which is a
composite of growth rate and elapsed time. Nodal involvement measures something categorically
different, which is demonstrated capacity to leave the primary site and establish elsewhere. Neither
substitutes for the other, which is why a stage system needs both.

Distant metastasis is the third axis and it is nearly binary in its prognostic effect.

The measurement caveat on nodal status is rarely stated. Node status depends on how many nodes were
examined and how thoroughly each was sectioned. A patient staged after a sentinel node procedure and
a patient staged after an axillary dissection have not had the same test. The surgical evidence
behind that change is in [[BC-410]], with {{trial:z0011}} and {{trial:sound}}. Stage migration
follows from a change in sampling intensity without any change in disease.

Anatomic stage remains the backbone for a practical reason as well as a biological one. It requires
no assay beyond a ruler and a microscope, so it is the only staging system that works everywhere
[[BS-6060]].

## BS-3310 Prognostic stage and the entry of biology into staging

The eighth edition of the American Joint Committee on Cancer staging manual added grade, oestrogen
receptor, progesterone receptor and HER2 status to anatomic extent, producing a prognostic stage
alongside the anatomic one.

The effect is much larger than a refinement. In 3,327 patients treated at a single institution,
prognostic stage upstaged 29.5% of patients and downstaged 28.1% relative to anatomic stage
[@weiss2018]. In 54,727 patients from the California Cancer Registry it upstaged 31.0% and
downstaged 20.6%. Roughly six patients in ten changed stage group.

Discrimination improved in both cohorts. In the institutional cohort, the Harrell concordance index
for disease-specific survival was 0.8357 for prognostic stage against 0.737 for anatomic stage. In
the registry cohort it was 0.8426 against 0.8097. Both comparisons had P values below 0.001
[@weiss2018].

The trade is explicit and it is worth naming. Anatomic stage depends on measurements that any
laboratory can make. Prognostic stage depends on immunohistochemistry and in situ hybridisation
results, so it inherits every reproducibility problem those assays carry.

```caution
A prognostic stage is only as reproducible as the receptor calls inside it. The thresholds, the
observer agreement and the preanalytic sensitivity of those assays are set out in [[BC-110]]. A
tumour reclassified from oestrogen receptor-low to oestrogen receptor-negative by a different
laboratory can move prognostic stage group without anything about the tumour changing. That is an
assay property being reported as a disease property, and it is the reason the anatomic stage is still
recorded alongside.
```

## BS-3320 Systemic staging, when it helps and when it harms

Whether to image for distant disease at diagnosis is a question about prevalence, not about
thoroughness.

The arithmetic is unavoidable. In clinical stage I and II disease the prevalence of occult distant
metastasis is low, so even a specific test generates mostly false positive findings. Each false
positive generates further imaging, sometimes a biopsy, a delay in starting treatment, and a period
during which a patient believes she may have metastatic disease.

The harms are therefore not hypothetical, and they are borne by the large majority of patients in
whom the test was always going to be negative.

Prevalence rises with clinical stage, which is where systemic staging earns its place. Locally
advanced and inflammatory disease carry a high enough pretest probability that a positive result is
likely to be true and will change the treatment plan [[BC-520]]. Symptoms or laboratory
abnormalities that point to a specific site raise prevalence for that site and justify directed
imaging at any stage.

The operative rule is to image in order to answer a question whose answer would change management.
Imaging performed because a patient has cancer, rather than because a decision depends on the
result, generates findings without decisions attached to them.

## BS-3330 Functional and molecular imaging, FDG, FES, and HER2 PET

Three positron emission tomography tracers are used in breast cancer and they measure three
different quantities. Treating them as interchangeable is the commonest error.

Fluorodeoxyglucose measures glucose uptake, which is a proxy for metabolic and proliferative
activity. It is not a proxy for receptor status or for any target. Low-grade disease and invasive
lobular carcinoma are frequently poorly avid, so a negative fluorodeoxyglucose study in lobular
disease has low negative predictive value [[BS-0740]].

Fluoroestradiol measures oestrogen receptor binding in vivo, across every lesion, without a needle.
Its value is in predicting benefit from endocrine therapy and in resolving discordant receptor
results between deposits [@fes2025].

Zirconium-89 trastuzumab measures whether an antibody actually reaches and binds its target. In 56
patients with HER2-positive metastatic disease scheduled for T-DM1, pretreatment imaging was
negative in 29%, and a mixed pattern within a single patient was found in 46% [@gebhart2016].
Combining that scan with an early metabolic response assessment separated two groups, with median
time to treatment failure of 2.8 months in 12 patients and 15 months in 25. Integration of molecular
imaging with transcriptomic profiling in this setting is developed further elsewhere [@rediti2025].

The structural point unites all three. A tissue assay reports one lesion at one time. A scan reports
every lesion at one time, which is why molecular imaging detects between-lesion heterogeneity that
no biopsy can [[BS-2930]]. None of these tracers has a randomised trial showing that acting on the
result improves an outcome.

## BS-3340 Molecular breast imaging and its niche

Molecular breast imaging uses a technetium-based tracer taken up by mitochondria in metabolically
active cells, imaged with a dedicated gamma camera.

Its physics give it a specific advantage. Uptake does not depend on tissue density, so masking by
fibroglandular tissue does not apply. That makes it a candidate for the problem in [[BS-3210]].

Its physics also give it a specific cost. The examination delivers a whole-body radiation dose,
since the tracer is systemic, whereas mammography delivers a dose to the breast alone. Dose
reduction has narrowed this gap and has not eliminated it, and the comparison matters most in
exactly the group where repeated screening is proposed.

The honest position is that this is a niche modality. It has a role where magnetic resonance imaging
is contraindicated, unavailable or not tolerated, and it has no randomised evidence of benefit on
any endpoint that matters. The evidence bar in [[BS-3200]] applies unchanged.

## BS-3350 Residual cancer burden and post-treatment restaging

Pathologic complete response is a binary summary of a continuous variable, and the information it
discards is prognostically useful. [[term:rcb]] recovers it.

The validation is large and it covers every subtype. A pooled patient-level analysis of 5,161
patients from 12 institutions and trials found that residual cancer burden score was prognostic
within each phenotypic subtype [@yau2022]. Per one-unit increase in score, the univariable hazard
ratio for event-free survival was 1.55 in hormone receptor-positive HER2-negative disease, with a
95% confidence interval of 1.41 to 1.71. It was 2.16 in hormone receptor-negative HER2-positive
disease, with a confidence interval of 1.79 to 2.61.

The effect survived adjustment. Adjusted for age, grade, clinical T category and nodal status at
baseline, the hazard ratio ranged from 1.52 to 2.09 across subtypes, with all P values below 0.0001
[@yau2022].

The measurement requirements are strict and they are where the score most often fails in practice.
The calculation uses five measurements: the two largest dimensions of the residual tumour bed, the
proportion of that bed that is cellular, the proportion of cellularity that is in situ, the number of
positive nodes, and the diameter of the largest nodal deposit. Producing those figures requires the pathologist to
know the specimen is post-treatment, to have the tumour bed localised, and to sample it by a defined
protocol [[BS-0790]].

Post-treatment imaging does not substitute for this. Radiological response and pathological response
diverge, and the score that predicts outcome is the pathological one. The endpoint argument belongs
to [[BS-4670]] and [[BS-4680]], and the treatment that follows residual disease to
{{trial:katherine}} and {{trial:create-x}}.

## BS-3360 Multidisciplinary diagnostic integration

The multidisciplinary meeting exists to reconcile results that come from different sampling frames,
and that is a harder task than reviewing them in sequence.

Four instruments are usually on the table. Imaging reports every lesion and characterises none.
Histology characterises one lesion in detail. Receptor assays report protein and copy number from
one block. Molecular assays report DNA from that block or from plasma. Each answers a different
question about a different sample.

Most apparent contradictions dissolve once the frames are named. A HER2-positive biopsy and a
negative HER2 positron emission tomography study are not in conflict, because one sampled a core and
the other sampled the body [@gebhart2016]. A plasma result that lacks a mutation found in an
archival block is usually reporting the passage of time rather than an error [[BS-3530]].

The failure mode is a meeting that reads out results rather than reconciling them. The useful output
of the meeting is a single statement of what is known, what is uncertain, and which uncertainty
would change the plan if resolved. The molecular version of the same discipline is in [[BS-3430]],
and the treatment-planning version in [[BC-400]].

## BS-3370 What staging cannot capture

Stage is a description of anatomic extent plus a small number of assay results, and it is worth
being precise about what that excludes.

It does not capture dormancy. Two patients with identical stage can carry different burdens of
disseminated tumour cells, and the state those cells are in determines when and whether they
reactivate [[BC-330]].

It does not capture subclonal composition. A tumour that is subclonally complex has more available
routes of escape than one that is not, and stage is blind to that [[BC-300]].

It does not capture shedding, which is why a stage II patient can have detectable circulating tumour
DNA and a stage III patient can have none [[BS-3460]].

It does not capture the host. Body composition, comorbidity, immune competence and the social
determinants that govern whether treatment is completed all modify outcome, and none appears in a
staging manual [[BC-250]], [[BC-690]].

The clearest demonstration is the late recurrence hazard. Among 62,923 women with oestrogen
receptor-positive disease who were disease-free after 5 years of endocrine therapy, distant
recurrences continued at a steady rate from year 5 to year 20. The magnitude of that risk depended
on original tumour size and nodal status [@pan2017]. Stage predicted the magnitude of that late
risk. It did not explain why the risk persisted, and nothing in the staging system does.

That is not a defect in staging. It is the boundary of what a classification based on anatomy can
do, and the chapters that follow are the attempt to see past it.
