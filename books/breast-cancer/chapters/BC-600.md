## BS-5170 Brain metastases, screening, systemic activity, and local therapy

The central nervous system is the one compartment this book gives a chapter of its own. [[BC-930]]
carries the local therapy, the systemic therapy, the delivery argument underneath both, and the
endpoints these trials report. This section says only why the compartment is separated out, and
what a reader of an organ-specific chapter has to carry away.

Three things make it behave unlike bone, liver or lung.

Most of the randomised evidence came from radiation oncology, in trials of brain metastases from
solid tumours generally. A breast-specific estimate out of those trials is a subgroup at best.

The registration trials of the modern systemic agents excluded untreated or progressing brain
metastases by protocol. The claim that a large molecule cannot reach the compartment was therefore
assumed rather than tested. Nobody enrolled the patients who would have tested it.

The endpoints are different objects. An intracranial response rate, a central nervous system
progression-free rate at a fixed timepoint and a response by neuro-oncology criteria are routinely
lined up in one sentence. They are not comparable, and what separates them is mostly who was
admitted to each study [[BS-7850]].

Two practical points belong here rather than there. Routine imaging of the asymptomatic brain is
not supported by randomised evidence in any subtype, and that should be said rather than implied.
And the lesion in the brain may not carry the target the primary carried, because acquired ERBB2
amplification and intrinsic subtype switching are both described in resected brain metastases
[@priedigkeit2017].

Why the brain is colonised at all belongs to [[BC-320]]. The subtype distribution is in
[@kennecke2010] and the colonisation biology in [@bos2009; @valiente2014].

## BS-5180 Leptomeningeal disease

Leptomeningeal disease is a second compartment inside the first, and it is not the same problem as
parenchymal disease. The diagnosis, the drugs and the evidence behind them are in [[BS-7860]].

Two points belong in an organ-specific chapter. The first is that cytology is highly specific and
insensitive. A negative result in a patient with characteristic symptoms and characteristic
imaging does not exclude the diagnosis, and should not be allowed to.

The second is the compartment point, and it is the one to carry away. Cerebrospinal fluid is
separated from blood, which means that plasma circulating tumour DNA does not report what is in it.
A patient with undetectable plasma can have detectable disease in the cerebrospinal fluid, so the
two assays answer different questions rather than the same question with different sensitivity
[[BC-390]]. Cerebrospinal fluid circulating tumour DNA improves on cytology where it is available,
and the technical account is in [[BS-3520]] [@fitzpatrick2022].

For most patients here the goal is neurological function and symptom control rather than length of
life. Saying so at the outset makes every later decision easier [[BC-680]].

## BS-5190 Bone metastases and skeletal-related events

Bone is the commonest first site of distant relapse in hormone receptor-positive disease
[@kennecke2010]. The colonisation programme behind that is described in [[BS-2750]] and in
[@kang2003].

The endpoint these trials share deserves inspection before any of their results are quoted. A
skeletal-related event is a composite of pathological fracture, radiation to bone, surgery to bone
and spinal cord compression. Two of those four are interventions rather than events. The composite
therefore measures clinical behaviour as well as disease behaviour. That does not invalidate the
endpoint, though it does mean the absolute rates travel badly between health systems with
different referral thresholds.

```evidence setting=metastatic modality=bone sort=year cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,endpoint,result caption=Bone-modifying_agents_in_established_bone_metastasis._One_trial_compares_the_two_drugs_and_two_compare_schedules_of_the_same_drug._The_adjuvant_bone_trials_ask_a_different_question_and_sit_elsewhere.
```

{{trial:denosumab-versus-zoledronic-acid-in-bm}} established that denosumab delays a first skeletal
event more than zoledronic acid does, while changing neither overall survival nor disease
progression [@stopeck2010]. The choice between the two agents is therefore a choice between
toxicity profiles rather than between disease outcomes. Renal adverse events and acute-phase reactions were
more frequent with zoledronic acid. Hypocalcaemia was more frequent with denosumab, which is why
calcium is checked after starting it.

The dosing interval has been tested twice, in two different populations, and the answers agree.
{{trial:calgb-70604}} randomised the interval for the whole two years and enrolled three cancers
together [@himelstein2017]. {{trial:optimize-2}} randomised it in women with breast cancer only,
and only after nine or more monthly doses had already been given [@hortobagyi2017]. Neither found
more skeletal events on the longer interval. Bone turnover was less completely suppressed on it,
which is a pharmacodynamic difference without a measured clinical one.

Read those two as answers to two questions rather than as one answer repeated. Choosing a twelve
weekly interval at the outset and stepping down to it after a year are different decisions. Only
the first is addressed by CALGB 70604 and only the second by OPTIMIZE-2.

The adjuvant literature makes a different claim and is regularly quoted as though it made the same
one. Adjuvant bisphosphonates reduce bone recurrence and breast cancer mortality in postmenopausal
women [@ebctcg2015]. That is prevention of bone metastasis in patients who do not have it. It says
nothing about the treatment of established bone disease [[BC-640]], and the two bodies of evidence
should not be merged.

Bone is also the hardest compartment in which to call a response. Sclerotic change in a healing
lytic lesion reads as a new finding on computed tomography, and tracer uptake on scintigraphy
reflects osteoblastic activity rather than tumour cell number [[BS-4770]].

```caution
None of these trials randomised when to stop. Entry required established bone metastasis and the
randomised comparisons ran for one or two years. The evidence therefore covers the beginning of
treatment and says nothing about a patient five years into a bone-modifying agent.

The interval result has the same boundary. Non-inferiority measured over two years is not a
statement about year six, and the osteonecrosis risk that argues for stopping accumulates with
exposure rather than resetting each year.
```

```practice
Arrange dental assessment before starting a bone-modifying agent, and give calcium and vitamin D
alongside it.

Monitor calcium after starting denosumab. Hypocalcaemia is the toxicity that differs between the
two agents [@stopeck2010].

A twelve weekly schedule is a reasonable default for zoledronic acid. It reduces visits without a
measured cost in skeletal events over two years [@himelstein2017; @hortobagyi2017].

Treat a painful bone deposit with radiation, and refer an impending fracture in a weight-bearing
bone for stabilisation before it breaks rather than after.

Record why the agent is still being given at each annual review. No trial covers the decision to
continue indefinitely, so it should at least be a decision.
```

## BS-5200 Liver and lung disease

Liver disease is what most often converts a chronic illness into an acute one. The variable that
matters is function rather than volume. A patient with extensive parenchymal replacement and normal
synthetic function has a different set of options from one with similar imaging and a rising
bilirubin. Drug clearance changes with the second and not with the first.

Visceral crisis is defined clinically rather than radiologically, as rapidly progressive visceral
involvement with organ dysfunction that requires a rapid response. It is the one situation in
hormone receptor-positive disease where chemotherapy has customarily displaced endocrine and
targeted therapy.

That custom has been tested. The question those trials were built to answer is argued in
[[BS-4890]], and the trials themselves are tabulated in [[BC-880]], where the first-line record
belongs.

{{trial:right-choice}} is the one that reaches this section, and its result needs stating
carefully. Clinically aggressive disease as enrolled there is not the same population as visceral
crisis with organ failure [@lu2024]. What the result establishes is that the reflex to
chemotherapy is wrong more often than it was assumed to be. It does not establish that it is never
right.

The practical form of that is a question about time rather than about drug class. Endocrine
therapy with a cell cycle inhibitor takes longer to produce a response than combination
chemotherapy does. Where organ function is failing over days, that difference is the whole
decision. Where it is declining over months, it is not.

Lung parenchymal metastases are usually asymptomatic until they are extensive, with a tempo slower
than liver disease of comparable burden. The decision is therefore rarely forced by the lung
itself.

Lymphangitic carcinomatosis is a separate problem with a separate tempo, and it is the one to
recognise early. Breathlessness out of proportion to the parenchymal findings, with septal
thickening on imaging, is attributed first to infection and second to treatment-related
pneumonitis. In a patient receiving a deruxtecan-based conjugate that second possibility is urgent,
because drug-related interstitial lung disease in this class carries real mortality [@powell2022].
The distinction changes what happens in the next 24 hours and cannot be made on a chest
radiograph.

Why particular organs are colonised is developed in [[BC-320]], with the lung programme described
in [@minn2005] and the vesicle-based account of preferential seeding in [@hoshino2015].

## BS-5210 Malignant effusions and chest wall disease

A malignant pleural effusion has three management options, and the choice between them turns on
prognosis and on preference rather than on efficacy in relieving the symptom.

```evidence setting=metastatic modality=supportive sort=year cols=acronym,n,population,arms,endpoint,result caption=The_two_randomised_comparisons_of_an_indwelling_pleural_catheter_against_talc_pleurodesis._They_chose_different_primary_endpoints,_which_is_why_they_read_as_disagreeing_and_do_not.
```

{{trial:time2}} measured breathlessness and found no difference over the first six weeks
[@davies2012]. {{trial:ample}} measured days in hospital and found one [@thomas2017]. Neither
strategy relieves the symptom better than the other. The catheter buys hospital days back and
reduces repeat procedures. It costs a higher rate of adverse events and the burden of managing a
drain at home.

That makes this a preference-sensitive decision rather than an efficacy one, and it should be
offered as such. A patient with months to live and a strong wish for time out of hospital chooses
differently from one expecting a longer course. Neither trial enrolled breast cancer alone, so
neither carries a breast-specific estimate.

Pericardial effusion is dangerous for physiological reasons rather than volumetric ones. Tamponade
depends on the rate of accumulation and on pericardial compliance. A small rapidly accumulating
effusion is more dangerous than a large slow one. Echocardiography answers the question that a
computed tomography report describing a moderate effusion does not.

Malignant ascites is managed by paracentesis, and by an indwelling catheter where drainage is
repeated, on the same logic as the pleural space.

Chest wall disease produces a symptom burden disproportionate to its extent and disproportionate to
the attention it receives. Ulceration, exudate, odour, bleeding and pain occur together and each
needs specific management. Radiation and systemic therapy address the tumour. The wound needs
dressing selection, odour control and bleeding management, and that is a specialist skill.
Referring it to tissue viability or wound care services is not an admission that oncology has
failed. Treating it as one leaves a manageable problem unmanaged.

## BS-5220 Obstructive complications and oncologic emergencies

Metastatic spinal cord compression is the emergency in this disease where hours change the outcome.
The variable predicting function afterwards is function beforehand.

```evidence setting=metastatic topic=cord-compression sort=year cols=acronym,n,population,arms,endpoint,result,os caption=The_two_randomised_trials_of_what_to_do_about_a_compressed_cord._Both_enrolled_metastatic_cancer_of_any_primary_site,_and_breast_cancer_was_a_minority_of_each.
```

{{trial:patchell-cord-compression-trial}} is why a surgical opinion is sought before radiotherapy
alone is accepted [@patchell2005]. Two limits belong with it, and both are about how far the result
travels. The trial enrolled metastatic cancer generally rather than breast cancer. It also stopped
early at a prespecified interim analysis, which tends to inflate the estimated effect.

{{trial:scorad}} asked a narrower question, and the way it failed is the instructive part. A single
fraction did not meet its non-inferiority margin for walking at eight weeks, while meeting that
margin at weeks one, four and twelve [@hoskin2019]. The explanation is attrition rather than
biology. Of the 686 patients randomised, 255 died before the week eight assessment, so the primary
endpoint was measured in half the trial. An endpoint set at a timepoint most of the population does not reach is
an endpoint about the survivors.

Superior vena cava obstruction in breast cancer is usually subacute rather than immediately
life-threatening. Endovascular stenting relieves it faster than radiation does, and the two are
commonly combined. The immediate priorities are airway and cerebral oedema rather than the caval
anatomy itself.

Hypercalcaemia of malignancy is treated with rehydration and a bone-modifying agent. Its
appearance is also information, because it frequently marks a change in disease behaviour, so it
should prompt reassessment rather than only correction.

Biliary and ureteric obstruction present the same structural question. The decision to stent is a
decision about what follows the stent. A stent placed without a subsequent systemic plan buys a
laboratory value rather than time.

```practice
New back pain with any neurological sign in a patient with known metastatic disease is imaged the
same day, with magnetic resonance of the whole spine rather than the symptomatic level alone.

Start corticosteroids while arranging the imaging, not after it.

Obtain a surgical opinion for cord compression before settling on radiotherapy alone, particularly
where the patient is still walking or the spine is unstable [@patchell2005].

Choose the radiotherapy schedule from the expected survival. A single fraction is reasonable where
that is short, and the eight-week evidence for it is weakest exactly there [@hoskin2019].

Record ambulatory status at presentation. It is the strongest predictor of ambulatory status
afterwards and it is the number most often missing from the notes.
```

## BS-5230 Local intervention in the metastatic setting

Local treatment in metastatic disease is given for three different reasons, and they should never
be argued as one.

Symptom control. A painful bone deposit, a bleeding chest wall, an obstructing lesion. The
justification is the symptom, and no survival claim is required or implied.

Structural protection. An impending fracture, a threatened cord, a compromised airway. The
justification is the event being prevented [[BS-5220]].

Disease control intent. The oligometastatic and oligoprogressive arguments, where local treatment
is given in the hope of changing the course of the disease.

Only the third rationale needs a randomised test, and only the third has had one.

```evidence setting=metastatic modality=surgery,radiation line=any sort=year cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,endpoint,result,os caption=The_randomised_tests_of_local_treatment_given_with_disease-control_intent._One_treated_the_primary_tumour_and_one_treated_the_metastases._Only_the_first_was_conducted_in_breast_cancer.
```

{{trial:ea2108}} is the clearer of the two, and it contradicts the stronger intuition. Women
presenting with metastatic disease and an intact primary received systemic therapy for four to
eight months. Those without progression were then randomised to locoregional treatment of the
primary or to continuing systemic therapy alone [@khan2022]. Removing the primary improved local
control while improving neither survival nor quality of life. Local control and survival are
separate outcomes, and improving the first delivered neither of the others.

{{trial:sabr-comet}} points the other way and is much the weaker design. It was a randomised phase
2 screening study with its significance threshold set at 0.20 [@palma2020]. It enrolled four common
primary tumours, and eighteen of its 99 patients had breast cancer. That is a hypothesis worth
testing rather than a result to act on, and the breast-specific evidence inside it is thinner than
the headline suggests.

Oligoprogression is the one situation where a disease-control rationale rests on a coherent
biological argument. A single progressing deposit while everything else responds suggests a
resistant subclone in one place, which is the divergence picture in [[BS-2570]]. Treating that site
and continuing the systemic regimen follows from the inference. It has still not been shown to
extend survival [[BS-4780]].

```interplay target=BC-350
Organ-specific management is where heterogeneity stops being a property of a tumour and becomes a
property of a patient. Different deposits sit in different microenvironments. They are exposed to
different drug concentrations and have taken different evolutionary routes [[BS-2570]]. A bone
deposit is held in a signalling programme the liver does not supply [[BS-2750]]. A brain metastasis
can carry an acquired ERBB2 amplification that the primary did not [@priedigkeit2017], and the
cerebrospinal fluid compartment is invisible to the assay used to monitor everything else
[@fitzpatrick2022]. Mixed response is therefore an expected finding rather than an anomaly. A
treatment decision made on the behaviour of the largest lesion is a decision about that lesion.
This chapter is the clinical form of the argument that a patient with metastatic breast cancer has
several related diseases in several places.
```
