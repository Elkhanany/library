## BS-6740 Scope, and how to read the evidence tables in this chapter

This chapter is the evidence compendium for adjuvant endocrine therapy. It collects the randomised
trials that settled which agent, for how long, and with or without ovarian suppression. It ends on
adherence, because that is the largest single modifiable determinant of how much of the benefit is
actually delivered.

Three neighbouring chapters carry what is deliberately absent from this one. The pharmacology of
each agent class is in [[BC-430]]. The decision architecture for early hormone receptor-positive
disease, including the genomic assays and the chemotherapy question, is in [[BC-490]]. Adjuvant
CDK4/6 inhibition is a separate decision and has its own chapter in [[BC-800]].

The tables here are generated from the trial registry by filter, which has one consequence worth
stating before you use them. The filter is mechanical, so a trial appears whenever its registry
fields match, whatever it actually randomised. The table below is filtered on setting, subtype,
line and modality. It therefore includes {{trial:calgb9343}}, which randomised radiotherapy with
tamoxifen in both arms, and {{trial:positive}}, which randomised nothing and observed a planned
interruption. Read the experimental-versus-control column before reading anything else in a
generated table.

```evidence setting=early subtype=HR+/HER2-,HR+/HER2+ line=adjuvant modality=endocrine cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,status caption=Every_randomised_adjuvant_endocrine_trial_in_the_registry_for_this_subtype._Outcomes_appear_in_the_per-question_tables_that_follow.
```

Each section below has its own table and its own question. The division of labour is that tables
enumerate and prose argues. A number that sits in a table is not repeated in the text unless the
argument is about that number.

The story below is the argument the rest of the chapter is evidence for, and it carries no figures
at all. It states what each group of trials was built to find out, what came back, and what the
design cannot settle, so that the tables that follow are read as answers to a question rather than
as a list. The same story is one node of the [Chapter Stories](stories.html) map, where the whole
landscape is laid out as questions.

## BS-6750 Tamoxifen and the trials that settled duration

```evidence setting=early line=adjuvant modality=endocrine topic=tamoxifen-duration
```

The duration question is not about how long the drug works. It is about how long its effect
persists after it stops.

{{trial:atlas}} and {{trial:attom}} both measured that directly, and the shape of the ATLAS
result matters more than its size.
Among women with ER-positive disease the recurrence rate ratio for continuing was 0.90 during
years 5 to 9. It fell to 0.75 in later years. Breast cancer mortality behaved the same way, at
0.97 during years 5 to 9 and 0.71 afterwards [@davies2013atlas]. The benefit of the second five
years is mostly collected in the third five years.

That shape is the whole case for extending anything. It is also the mirror image of the late
recurrence hazard curve set out in [[BS-2880]]. A treatment whose effect arrives late is worth
giving only to a woman whose risk also arrives late. Hormone receptor-positive disease is the
subtype where that is reliably true [@pan2017].

Two features of the population constrain how the figures transfer. ATLAS randomised women who had
already completed five years and were free of disease, so the estimate is conditional on surviving
that far. ER status was unknown in 4,800 of the randomised women, which reflects assay practice
between 1996 and 2005, and the effect in that group was intermediate [@davies2013atlas].

The price of the second five years is mostly endometrial and thrombotic. Cumulative endometrial
cancer risk during years 5 to 14 was 3.1% with continuation and 1.6% with stopping, with mortality
of 0.4% against 0.2%. The rate ratio for pulmonary embolus was 1.87 and for ischaemic heart
disease 0.76 [@davies2013atlas]. Those are hospitalisation or death rates rather than incidence,
which is a reporting convention and not a biological claim.

The patient-level overview of the first five years remains the anchor for the whole edifice, and
it is what established that a carry-over effect exists at all [@ebctcg2011tam]. Tamoxifen is still
the correct first choice in a premenopausal woman who will not receive ovarian suppression, and in
anyone for whom bone loss is the dominant competing risk.

## BS-6760 Aromatase inhibitors against tamoxifen, and the menopausal status that decides

```evidence setting=early line=adjuvant modality=endocrine topic=ai-vs-tamoxifen sort=year
```

Menopausal status is not a patient characteristic in this decision. It is a statement about where
oestrogen is being made, and [[BS-4280]] develops the consequences.

The six trials in the table answer three different questions and are habitually read as one.
{{trial:atac}} and {{trial:big1-98}} compared five years of an aromatase inhibitor against five
years of tamoxifen from the start. {{trial:ies}} and {{trial:abcsg-8-arno-95}} compared a switch at
two to three years against continuing tamoxifen. {{trial:team}} and {{trial:fata-gim3}} compared
starting with an aromatase inhibitor against starting with tamoxifen and switching later.

TEAM constrains practice more than the other three and is cited least. Its null result means that
starting with an aromatase inhibitor is not superior to starting with tamoxifen and switching
later. The case for starting with an aromatase inhibitor therefore rests on tolerability and on
the gynaecological and thromboembolic profile. It does not rest on efficacy, because TEAM found no
difference to rest it on.

BIG 1-98 shows how much of the survival claim is model-dependent. After the 2005 analysis,
patients still on tamoxifen were unblinded and offered letrozole, and 619 crossed over. The
intention-to-treat overall survival hazard ratio is in the table. Weighting for that selective
crossover moves it to 0.79, with an interval of 0.69 to 0.90 [@regan2011]. Both are correct
estimates of different quantities, and only the first describes the policy that was actually
studied.

The measurement point is the one that causes harm in clinic. Menopausal status after chemotherapy
is inferred from amenorrhoea and from gonadotrophin and oestradiol assays. Amenorrhoea is not
menopause. An aromatase inhibitor given to a woman with recoverable ovarian function lowers
oestrogen, raises gonadotrophins and can restart the ovary. The patient-level meta-analysis that
prices that trade in genuinely postmenopausal women is [@ebctcg2015ai]. Its recurrence, mortality
and fracture figures sit in [[BS-4280]] and are not repeated here.

## BS-6770 Ovarian function suppression and who benefits

```evidence setting=early line=adjuvant modality=endocrine topic=ovarian-suppression sort=year
```

Two questions live under one heading and they have different answers.

The first is whether adding suppression to tamoxifen helps. {{trial:soft}} was designed to answer
it and its first report did not. At a median follow-up of 67 months the hazard ratio was 0.83,
with an interval of 0.66 to 1.04 and a p value of 0.10 [@francis2015soft]. The eight-year report
reversed that reading on disease-free and overall survival alike [@francis2018]. Both sets of
figures are in the table, three years of follow-up apart. No biology changed between the first
report and the second. The events needed time to accrue, which is the structural problem with
adjuvant endpoints set out in [[BS-6330]].

The second question is whether an aromatase inhibitor is the better partner once suppression is
used. The patient-level meta-analysis of 7,030 women answers yes on recurrence and no on mortality
[@ebctcg2022ofs]. Its figures and the practice guidance that follows are in [[BS-4290]].

{{trial:abcsg12}} is the trial that does not fit. It deserves more attention than the pooled
estimate usually gives it. With goserelin in both arms, anastrozole was not better than tamoxifen.
Three features distinguish it from the pooled analysis: three years of treatment rather than five,
a lower-risk population, and complete suppression in every patient. Any of the three could explain
the discordance, and the trial cannot separate them. The pooled analysis contains this trial and
averages its result into the total.

{{trial:astrra}} answers a narrower question that comes up more often in clinic. It enrolled women
who remained premenopausal or resumed ovarian function after chemotherapy, assessed every six
months by follicle-stimulating hormone and bleeding history. Two years of goserelin was enough to
improve disease-free survival. Its overall survival difference rests on a very small number of
deaths and should not be quoted to a patient as a survival benefit.

```caution
Every trial in this table enrolled premenopausal women, and in every one the absolute benefit
concentrated in those who were young, node-positive, high grade or chemotherapy-treated. Being
premenopausal is the entry criterion, not the indication.

A woman with a small grade 1 node-negative tumour belongs to the enrolled population and not to the
group that gained. Saying the absolute number out loud before prescribing is the discipline
[[BS-4290]] asks for.
```

## BS-6780 Extended adjuvant therapy and the shape of the late benefit

```evidence setting=early line=adjuvant modality=endocrine topic=extended-endocrine sort=year
```

The case for extension is a statement about hazard. Distant recurrence in this subtype accrues at
a roughly constant rate from year 5 to year 20 [@pan2017]. The size of that residual risk by
tumour size and nodal status is tabulated in [[BS-2880]] and [[BS-4350]]. Extension is therefore a
dormancy intervention rather than consolidation of a falling risk, and [[BC-330]] is where that
argument is made properly.

The eight trials in the table look inconsistent until they are sorted by what came before the
extension. {{trial:ma17}} extended after five years of tamoxifen and was stopped early at its
first interim analysis. {{trial:gim4}} also extended after tamoxifen, and also found something:
twelve-year disease-free survival of 67% against 62% for the shorter arm, with a hazard ratio of
0.78 and an interval of 0.65 to 0.93 [@delmastro2021]. Every trial that extended treatment in
patients who had already received an aromatase inhibitor has been at most marginal.
{{trial:nsabp-b42}} missed the significance threshold that its own design had prespecified.
{{trial:abcsg16}}, {{trial:data}} and {{trial:ideal}} found nothing that reached significance.

{{trial:sole}} is in the table and answers none of this. Both of its arms were extended, and what
it varied was the schedule, giving letrozole intermittently against continuously. It found no
difference, with a hazard ratio of 1.08 and an interval of 0.93 to 1.26 [@colleoni2017]. How
to give an extension is a separate question from whether to give one.

The reading that fits is about the marginal year rather than the total. The first exposure to
oestrogen deprivation buys the most. A further year of aromatase inhibition after five years of
aromatase inhibition buys little, because the mechanism has already been applied to whatever is
sensitive to it.

```caution
Four of these trials tested one duration against another rather than against nothing.
{{trial:abcsg16}}, {{trial:ideal}}, {{trial:data}} and {{trial:gim4}} all randomised a longer
extension against a shorter one. A null result between seven and ten years is not a null result
for extension itself.

{{trial:ma17}}, {{trial:ma17r}} and {{trial:nsabp-b42}} are the placebo-controlled three, and they
are the only ones that bear on whether to extend at all. {{trial:sole}} bears on neither question,
because both of its arms were extended. Collapsing the table into one message is the commonest
error in this literature. The general form of that error, and how to spot it in a
non-inferiority report, is in [[BS-6370]].
```

The patient-level meta-analysis across 22,031 postmenopausal women now prices both sides
[@ebctcg2025]. Its recurrence, mortality and fracture figures belong to [[BS-4340]]. Its most
useful property is that the proportional reduction was similar across tumour characteristics.
Absolute benefit therefore scales with baseline risk, which is the arithmetic in [[BS-5690]].

One component of the extension benefit is not recurrence at all. Contralateral and second primary
breast cancers fall with continued aromatase inhibition, and {{trial:ideal}} isolates that effect
because its two arms differed only in duration [@blok2018]. Preventing a new primary is a real
benefit. It is not the same achievement as preventing relapse, and [[BS-3840]] sets out why the
distinction matters when quoting a disease-free survival difference.

## BS-6790 Oral selective estrogen receptor degraders in the adjuvant setting

```evidence setting=early line=adjuvant modality=endocrine topic=adjuvant-serd
```

No adjuvant oral selective estrogen receptor degrader has reported. Everything in this section is
a hypothesis with a trial design attached to it.

The rationale was transposed from advanced disease, where the target is an ESR1 ligand-binding
domain mutation conferring ligand-independent receptor activity. The biology is in [[BC-140]] and
the agents are in [[BS-3830]]. The transposition has a problem that is rarely stated. ESR1
mutations are selected by aromatase inhibitor exposure and are uncommon at diagnosis [[BS-2490]].
In the adjuvant setting there is no measurable tumour, and usually no mutation to target.

The adjuvant hypothesis therefore cannot be that a degrader treats a mutant receptor. It has to be
that more complete receptor blockade stops resistance from forming. That is a different claim and
it has never been tested in any setting.

The two ongoing trials test two versions of it. {{trial:cambria1}} switches patients to
camizestrant after two to five years of standard endocrine therapy, which is the late-selection
version. {{trial:cambria2}} replaces standard endocrine therapy from the start for seven years,
which is the complete-blockade version. Both use invasive breast cancer-free survival as the
primary endpoint, and CAMBRIA-2 permits abemaciclib in both arms for the first two years
[@hamilton2025].

Two design features deserve attention now rather than at readout. Invasive breast cancer-free
survival counts second primaries, so part of any positive result will be prevention rather than
recurrence control, exactly as in the extension trials above. And CAMBRIA-1 admits patients who
received a CDK4/6 inhibitor, so its comparator is current practice rather than endocrine
monotherapy. A positive result there is a result about a strategy added to what we already do.

```caution
The prior from advanced disease is weaker than the enthusiasm suggests. In ESR1 wild-type
populations the oral degraders have produced modest effects against standard endocrine therapy. The
progression-free survival figures that support that statement are in [[BS-3830]].

Reading a positive metastatic result as support for adjuvant use is the error that four adjuvant
CDK4/6 trials made visible. Two were positive and two were negative within one class, and
[[BC-800]] works through why.
```

## BS-6800 Adherence, toxicity, and the gap between prescribed and taken

Every hazard ratio in this chapter was generated in a population in which a large minority stopped
the drug. [[BS-4360]] makes that point about what an effect estimate means. This section makes the
arithmetic point that follows from it.

Compare the size of the two levers a clinician actually controls. Choosing an aromatase inhibitor
over tamoxifen in a postmenopausal woman changes recurrence by a few percentage points over a
decade. Not taking the tablet removes the entire effect of the drug. In a cohort of 8,769 women
with stage I to III disease, 49% took adjuvant endocrine therapy for the full duration on the
intended schedule [@hershman2010]. Clinic time is spent almost entirely on the smaller lever.

The randomised evidence says most of that loss is not pharmacological. In the placebo-controlled
extension trials, non-adherence ran at 39.0% on further aromatase inhibition and 37.6% on placebo
[@ebctcg2025]. Almost as many women stopped an inert tablet as stopped an active one. Symptom
management therefore has a ceiling, and the rest of the problem is structural.

Measurement matters here more than usual, because the three common methods disagree by design.
Self-report overestimates, and most of all when the prescriber is the one asking the question.
Pharmacy refill measures collection from a dispensary rather than ingestion. Serum tamoxifen or
endoxifen, and serum oestradiol during aromatase inhibition, measure exposure directly and are
almost never used. A trial reporting adherence by refill and a clinic reporting it by self-report
are not describing the same quantity, and neither is wrong.

Dose is the one pharmacological lever with randomised evidence behind it. {{trial:tam01}} tested 5
mg of tamoxifen daily against placebo and reported durable benefit at ten years [@lazzeroni2023].
That trial enrolled breast intraepithelial neoplasia rather than invasive disease. Using low-dose
tamoxifen to rescue an intolerant patient with invasive cancer is an extrapolation, and it should
be described to her as one.

```practice
Ask about adherence at every visit, and ask in a form that permits a true answer. Roughly half of
patients do not complete the prescribed course [@hershman2010].

Set the expectation at the first visit rather than at the first symptom. Say that most women get
vasomotor or musculoskeletal symptoms, that these usually settle, and that stopping is the outcome
you are trying to prevent.

Treat the symptom before shortening the course. Switching between aromatase inhibitors, adding a
vasomotor agent, or moving the dose to bedtime all keep patients on treatment that stopping does
not.

Quantify the loss out loud when a patient asks to stop early. A woman stopping at year two has
taken about two fifths of the intended course, and she is entitled to know roughly what that costs
in the absolute framing of [[BS-5690]].

Assess bone density before and during aromatase inhibition or ovarian suppression, because
fractures rise with both [@ebctcg2015ai; @ebctcg2022ofs]. The management belongs in the same visit
and is in [[BS-5570]].

Record which adherence measure you used. A refill ratio and a patient's answer are different
numbers. Record which of the two you used, because they will disagree.
```

## BS-6810 Unresolved questions and what reads out next

```evidence setting=early modality=endocrine,cdk4-6 status=ongoing
```

Five questions are open, and only two of them have a trial that will answer them.

Whether an oral degrader beats an aromatase inhibitor in the adjuvant setting is being tested in
two positions at once. {{trial:cambria1}} and {{trial:cambria2}} could disagree. A split result
would be more informative than two concordant ones. A positive switch trial with a negative
upfront trial would say the benefit lies in late blockade, after resistance has begun to form.

Whether extension can be targeted rather than offered broadly has no trial at all. Nobody has
randomised extended endocrine therapy on a molecular signal, and circulating tumour DNA is the
obvious candidate. The assay performance and the interception problem are in [[BC-390]] and
[[BS-2890]].

Duration in premenopausal women is close to unstudied. The aromatase inhibitor extension trials
enrolled postmenopausal women. {{trial:atlas}} is the only large duration trial that included
premenopausal women. It tested tamoxifen, which is not the agent most of these women now receive.

The duration of ovarian suppression has never been compared head to head in a randomised trial.
{{trial:astrra}} used two years and {{trial:soft}} used five. The question is not academic,
because suppression is the least tolerated component of the regimen in young women [[BC-610]].

Whether cell cycle inhibition changes the endocrine question is being approached from an
unexpected direction. {{trial:adaptcycle}} asks whether ribociclib with an aromatase inhibitor can
replace chemotherapy in intermediate-risk disease [@hamann2025]. A positive result would move part
of the chemotherapy decision in [[BS-4300]] into the endocrine column, which is a larger change
than any oral degrader result would produce.

```interplay target=BC-350
Adjuvant endocrine therapy applies selective pressure for five to ten years to a population that
cannot be sampled. That is the heterogeneity argument of Part VI running in slow motion. What
emerges at year eight has been shaped by the agent chosen at year zero, and the ESR1 mutations that
define endocrine resistance in metastatic disease [[BS-2500]] are selected by exactly the aromatase
inhibitor exposure this chapter recommends. The oral degrader trials in [[BS-6790]] are the first
attempt to act on that selection before it has an observable output. They will be reported as
duration trials. They are better understood as experiments on which escape route the adjuvant years
build.
```
