## BS-7790 Scope, and why the central nervous system is treated as a compartment

This chapter covers parenchymal brain metastases and leptomeningeal disease, together with the
local therapy, the systemic therapy, the delivery argument that sits under both, and the endpoints
these trials report.

The biology of organ tropism is in [[BC-320]], the brain microenvironment in [[BC-240]] and
radiation technique in [[BC-420]]. The HER2-directed agents themselves are in [[BC-900]], where
their pharmacology is set out rather than repeated here.

The compartment is a population defined by anatomy rather than by tumour biology, with two
consequences that shape everything below.

The first is that most of the randomised evidence was generated by radiation oncology in brain
metastases from solid tumours generally. A breast-specific estimate from those trials is a
subgroup at best.

The second is that the registration trials of the modern systemic agents excluded untreated or
progressing brain metastases by protocol. The claim that a large molecule cannot reach the
compartment was therefore assumed rather than tested. Nobody enrolled the patients who would have
tested it.

The breast-specific randomised record is about half a dozen trials wide. Three of them held brain
radiotherapy constant and varied what was given alongside it: {{trial:a-plus}},
{{trial:wbrt-with-or-without-temozolomide}} and {{trial:nrg-oncology-krog-rtog-1119}}. Of the
rest, {{trial:attain}} varied the systemic drug, {{trial:deposein}} varied the route into the
compartment, and {{trial:proton-csi-versus-photon-ifrt}} varied the irradiated field. Not one
randomised a modern brain-active systemic agent against local therapy. Everything else in this
chapter is single-arm. Outside HER2-positive disease the record is thinner still.
{{trial:u-bomb}} and {{trial:abc-study}} are the first prospective cohorts of any size.

One warning belongs at the front rather than at the back. Intracranial response rates from these
studies are not comparable with one another, because the ranking tracks who was let in at least as
closely as it tracks what they were given. [[BS-7850]] is where that is argued, and every section
between here and there depends on it.

## BS-7800 Local therapy and the cognitive cost of controlling the compartment

The randomised base for this decision is larger than for anything else in the chapter, though it
is almost entirely borrowed. These trials enrolled brain metastases from solid tumours and were
powered on that mixed population.

The story below is the argument this section is evidence for, and it carries no figures at all. It
states what each group of trials was built to find out, what came back, and what the design cannot
settle. It is one node of the [Chapter Stories](stories.html) map.

```story SQ-0920,SQ-0930
```

```evidence setting=cns topic=local-therapy status=reported sort=year cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,endpoint,result,os caption=Randomised_trials_of_how_much_of_the_brain_to_irradiate,_and_of_whether_the_cognitive_cost_of_irradiating_all_of_it_can_be_engineered_down._All_of_them_enrolled_brain_metastases_from_solid_tumours_rather_than_from_breast_cancer_alone.
```

Three of those trials randomised whether the whole brain was irradiated at all. They agree on two
things. Whole-brain radiotherapy lowers relapse at treated sites and at new ones. It lengthens
neither survival nor the time to loss of performance status. The decision therefore turns entirely
on what the control costs.

Where they differ is in what each set out to measure, and the field quotes them as though they had
all measured cognition. {{trial:eortc-22952-26001}} made time to performance-status deterioration
its primary endpoint and scored no cognitive battery. {{trial:ncctg-n0574}} made cognition primary
at three months. {{trial:ncctg-n107c-cec-3}} paired cognitive-deterioration-free survival with
overall survival as co-primary endpoints. {{trial:mahajan-postoperative-srs-trial}} asked only
about local control in the resection cavity. Four questions, four designs, one conclusion
attributed to all of them.

Cognition here is a scoring convention rather than a clinical event. The trials that made it
primary did not use one convention. {{trial:ncctg-n0574}} called deterioration a decline beyond a
set margin on any single test in a battery. {{trial:nrg-cc001}} used a reliable change index. Both
scored only the patients still well enough to be tested, which attrition selects unevenly across
arms as the disease progresses. None of that overturns the direction of the finding. It does mean
the magnitude belongs to the instrument as much as to the brain.

The decision rests on the two trials that randomised whole-brain treatment against what a clinic
would otherwise do, which is focal treatment and an MRI. {{trial:ncctg-n107c-cec-3}} carries the
same message from the other side of the operation. {{trial:mahajan-postoperative-srs-trial}} is
the guard rail. Leaving the resection cavity untreated is a different question with a different
answer, because it costs local control. The case here is against irradiating the whole brain, not
against irradiating anything.

Two rows point the other way, and neither is what it is usually taken for. {{trial:rtog-9508}}
added a radiosurgical boost to whole-brain treatment and missed its survival endpoint in the
population as a whole. The survival advantage quoted from it lives in a single-lesion subgroup on
univariate analysis. {{trial:jlgk0901}} is not a randomised comparison at all. It is a prospective
cohort given radiosurgery alone and compared against itself across lesion-count strata. It is the
evidence usually cited for treating five to ten metastases focally. A cohort compared with
itself cannot say what whole-brain treatment would have done to the same patients.

The cost can be lowered without giving up intracranial control. NRG CC001 conformally avoided the
hippocampi and lowered cognitive failure against a conventional plan delivering the same dose to
the metastases. Both arms received memantine. {{trial:rtog-0614}}, the trial that put memantine
there, never crossed its own threshold on its primary measure of delayed recall. Only a minority
of those randomised were analysable at that timepoint, and the report states its own power as far
below what the design called for. Its failure was attrition rather than a demonstration of no
effect. The avoidance question was therefore asked on a backbone the field had adopted rather than
proved, which is worth knowing before quoting either result.

What the table cannot show is the patient this chapter is now about. Median follow-up in these
trials runs to months, in populations where most deaths were extracranial. A woman with
HER2-positive disease and years of systemic control ahead of her sits outside the range they
sampled. She is the one who lives long enough to pay the cognitive bill in full. No trial here
stratified by subtype. None was run in an era with brain-active systemic therapy to defer to.

```practice
For a limited number of metastases amenable to focal treatment, treat focally and follow with MRI.
Do not add whole-brain radiotherapy by default.

Where whole-brain radiotherapy is unavoidable, ask for hippocampal avoidance unless a metastasis
sits in the region to be spared.

Say the trade out loud. Whole-brain treatment buys intracranial control, costs memory, and does
not buy time.

Set the expected survival before choosing, because survival after a breast cancer brain metastasis
varies several-fold with performance status, subtype, lesion number and extracranial disease
[@sperduto2020]. A patient with months and a patient with years want opposite sides of the same
trade.

Do not abandon an effective systemic regimen for an isolated intracranial progression that focal
treatment can handle [[BS-4780]].
```

## BS-7810 Adding a systemic drug to brain radiotherapy

The rationale is one of the better ones in this chapter. Radiation and the drug act on the same
lesion at the same time. The barrier around that lesion is already disrupted by the tumour, and by
the radiation itself.

```story SQ-0940
```

The HER2-positive case was tested on its own, in the trial usually quoted as settling the
question.

```story SQ-0890
```

```evidence setting=cns topic=drug-with-radiotherapy status=reported sort=year cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,endpoint,result caption=Randomised_and_single-arm_trials_that_held_brain_radiotherapy_constant_and_added_a_systemic_drug.
```

Three of those four studies randomised something. None raised the early radiographic response
above radiotherapy alone. The conclusion usually drawn is that concurrency does not work. That
conclusion is larger than the evidence, and the reason sits in the endpoint column rather than in
the drugs.

{{trial:nrg-oncology-krog-rtog-1119}} and {{trial:wbrt-with-or-without-temozolomide}} gave the drug
concurrently and scored a response on MRI at an early fixed timepoint. The two timepoints are not
the same one, which is enough on its own to stop their response rates being set side by side.
{{trial:a-plus}} did neither thing. It gave three cycles before whole-brain radiotherapy rather
than with it. It also measured time to brain-specific progression rather than an early response. It
is the only one of the three that separated from its control. Whether that reflects the schedule,
the endpoint or the cytotoxic backbone is not recoverable from these three trials.

A-PLUS is also the one to read most carefully. It declared its brain-specific result against a
prespecified alpha far looser than convention. Its interval for brain progression or death
included no difference. Its early brain response was no better than the control arm's, which is
consistent with the endpoint being the variable that moved. That is a signal worth following
rather than a positive trial.

{{trial:srs-plus-nivolumab}} is in the table because it held radiosurgery constant and added a
drug. It is not a comparison. Twelve patients received a checkpoint antibody with radiosurgery in
a single arm sized for safety. The only report on its record is a long-term follow-up rather than
a primary analysis. Its local control has nothing to be read against.

```caution
Concurrency failed once, in HER2-positive disease, with lapatinib. Lapatinib is the weakest
brain-penetrant member of its class, while the same class given without radiotherapy did change
intracranial outcomes [[BS-7830]]. The trial is evidence against one drug on one schedule.

The endpoint compounds the problem. An intracranial complete response at an early fixed timepoint
is a radiographic surrogate chosen because it reads out quickly. It is also the measurement most
exposed to post-radiotherapy imaging change, where treatment effect, necrosis and residual tumour
resemble one another. A negative early complete response rate is not a statement about how long
the compartment stays controlled [[BC-740]].

Entry required an unirradiated measurable lesion, so none of this speaks to adding a drug to
re-irradiation, which is where the question is asked most often in clinic.
```

The concept has not yet been tested with a drug that plausibly reaches the lesion. {{trial:tutor}}
pairs a brain-penetrant inhibitor with radiosurgery and is a dose-finding protocol with no
outcomes on the record. A maximum tolerated dose cannot settle efficacy whatever it eventually
reports.

One column is missing from every row. Each of these studies added a systemic drug to brain
irradiation and then measured the tumour. None measured cognition.

## BS-7820 Whether exclusion by the barrier is the explanation it is taken to be

```story SQ-1130
```

The barrier argument is a statement about intact vasculature, which a metastasis large enough to
measure on imaging does not have. Whether a molecule crosses healthy brain is therefore the wrong
question. Whether it reaches tumour is the right one.

```story SQ-0950
```

The same prediction was tested a second time, from the direction of exposure rather than of
design.

```story SQ-0900
```

```evidence setting=cns topic=reaching-the-compartment weight=any sort=year cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,endpoint,result caption=Studies_whose_rationale_was_exposure._Raise_the_dose,_build_a_carrier_that_crosses,_choose_a_molecule_that_crosses,_or_measure_the_drug_in_the_resected_lesion.
```

Seven studies took four routes to the same prediction and returned one answer. A higher antibody
dose, a carrier built to cross, a molecule chosen for crossing, a concentration read out of the
lesion itself. All four converge on intracranial activity far below what the exposure argument
predicts. That convergence is the finding of this table. It is not a finding about any of these
drugs individually.

Delivery and activity came apart most visibly in the two studies that measured drug rather than
patients. {{trial:sacituzumab-govitecan-phase-0-window-stu}} recovered payload from resected brain
metastases at the concentration it set out to demonstrate.
{{trial:jpbo-abemaciclib-cns-study}} measured unbound drug in resected metastases well above what
its two target kinases require, then returned almost no confirmed intracranial response in either
treatment cohort. Those cohorts ran under separate two-stage designs, so that study offers two
small single-arm results rather than one.

The divergence here is in what each study was allowed to claim, not in how well the drugs worked.
{{trial:ang1005}}, {{trial:lccc1025}} and
{{trial:atezolizumab-plus-pertuzumab-and-high-do}} each set a response rule in advance and each
missed it. {{trial:patricia}} set none. That matters, because PATRICIA is the study in this group
most often quoted as encouraging, and its two results point in opposite directions. Its confirmed
intracranial response rate reached only a small minority of those treated, while its median
overall survival is among the longest in that table. A single arm with no comparator cannot say
which of those belongs to the drug and which to the cohort it admitted. ANG1005 carries a related
problem. Its investigator-read and independently read response rates differ, and its preset rule
was assessed on the independent read. Those two rates have to be read separately rather than
averaged.

{{trial:attain}} is the row the argument rests on, because it is the only randomised comparison
here. A formulation designed for prolonged exposure was tested against the chemotherapy a
clinician would otherwise have chosen, with overall survival as the primary endpoint, and survival
did not move. Two qualifications travel with it. Entry required stable, previously treated brain
metastases, so the trial says nothing about activity in progressing intracranial disease. And its
central nervous system progression-free survival ran in the drug's favour without reaching
conventional significance, which is the kind of secondary result that gets quoted as though the
trial had been positive.

Three candidates for what else is limiting are worth holding, none of which has been separated
from the others in a trial. The metastasis is not the primary, because acquired amplifications and
subtype switching are both described in resected brain lesions [@priedigkeit2017]. The brain
microenvironment supplies survival signals the same tumour does not receive elsewhere [[BC-240]].
And efflux at the residual barrier around microscopic disease is not addressed by a concentration
measured in a lesion large enough to resect.

That last point is also the main limit on this table. A drug concentration in resected tissue is a
pharmacological surrogate with no clinical outcome attached. The tissue came only from patients
selected for being operable, which means large and accessible lesions. The window study also
enrolled a glioblastoma cohort alongside the breast one, and its drug is aimed at a different
target from everything else under this question. No design here connects concentration to response
in the same patient.

```interplay target=BC-350
The compartment is usually filed as a pharmacokinetic problem and it behaves like a heterogeneity
problem. A brain metastasis is a clonal selection event that happened somewhere the drug
historically was not. What grows there has been selected by a different pressure from what grows
in the liver [[BC-320]]. Exposure is the variable this literature measures because it is the
variable it can measure. The measurements that would separate a delivery failure from a biological
one, paired concentration and response in the same lesion, exist for almost nobody. Until they do,
every intracranial failure is allocated to the barrier by default, which is the same reasoning
error that allocated conjugate failure to antigen loss.
```

## BS-7830 Brain-penetrant tyrosine kinase inhibition in HER2-positive disease

This is the position where the compartment stopped being radiation's problem alone.

```story SQ-0860
```

```evidence setting=metastatic topic=tucatinib sort=year cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,endpoint,result,os caption=The_randomised_tucatinib_trials._None_of_them_required_brain_disease_to_enter,_and_only_the_first_admitted_untreated_or_progressing_brain_metastases.
```

All three trials give the same answer to a question none of them was built to ask. Adding
tucatinib lengthens progression-free survival on whichever HER2 backbone it is added to, with
chemotherapy in later lines, with a conjugate in the second, and with two antibodies as first-line
maintenance. They differ in what they admitted through the door, and that difference is the whole
of their value in this chapter.

Three features of {{trial:her2climb}} matter more than its effect size.

Entry was opened to patients whose brain metastases were untreated or progressing after local
therapy. That is a design change rather than a drug property. It is the reason the trial can say
anything at all about active intracranial disease.

Progression-free survival in the patients with brain metastases was a prespecified secondary
endpoint [@murthy2020]. That result is not a subgroup found afterwards, which is the usual status of
an intracranial result [[BC-740]]. The intracranial response and central nervous system
progression-free survival analyses were exploratory [@lin2020].

The control arm carried capecitabine, which has intracranial activity of its own. The contrast is
against an active backbone rather than against local therapy or against nothing. That makes the
separation harder to achieve and easier to believe.

The limits are equally specific. Entry required prior trastuzumab, pertuzumab and trastuzumab
emtansine, so the same question is unanswered earlier in the disease. {{trial:her2climb-02}} and
{{trial:her2climb-05}} did not require brain metastases to enter, and the maintenance trial
admitted only asymptomatic ones. Neither carries a central nervous system population that stands
on its own. Interim overall survival on the conjugate backbone was immature and did not favour the
tucatinib arm, which is a reason to hold that trial's place in the sequence loosely. Agent-level
detail and the rest of the HER2 sequence are in [[BC-900]] and [[BS-7610]].

The other inhibitors in this class were tested the other way round, in cohorts entered by the
state of the brain disease. Their results are in the table under [[BS-7850]], where they should be
read rather than ranked against the randomised trials above.

## BS-7840 Conjugates in the compartment, and why size was the wrong objection

Molecular weight governs transit across an intact barrier. It does not govern access to a lesion
whose vasculature is already leaking. That distinction is the whole of the objection and the whole
of the answer to it.

A conjugate also has a second route the argument never considered. It has only to deliver a
payload, and a membrane-permeable payload acts on cells the antibody never bound [[BC-460]].

```story SQ-0870
```

```evidence setting=cns modality=adc status=reported sort=year cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,endpoint,result caption=Conjugate_studies_entered_by_the_state_of_the_brain_disease_rather_than_by_line_of_treatment._The_endpoints_in_this_column_are_not_the_same_object_and_should_not_be_ranked.
```

Both HER2-directed conjugates in that table produced intracranial responses. They occur in active
disease as well as in disease already treated and stable, in the small dedicated cohorts and in
the brain metastasis subgroup of a large safety study. The size objection is answered. Nothing
else in the table is.

The endpoint column holds four different objects. {{trial:destiny-b12}} reports a progression-free
rate at a fixed timepoint. {{trial:tuxedo-1}} and {{trial:debbrah}} report intracranial response
by neuro-oncology criteria against a two-stage design. {{trial:kamilla}} reports best overall
response by solid tumour criteria in the measurable subset of a safety study.
{{trial:sacituzumab-govitecan-phase-0-window-stu}} reports payload recovered per gram of resected
tissue and no response endpoint at all. Ranking that column is not a weak version of comparing
drugs. It is a category error.

The apparent gap between the deruxtecan and the emtansine cohorts is the one a reader will want to
treat as a drug effect. Some of it may be. The rest is design. KAMILLA's brain metastasis result
is a post hoc exploratory analysis of a subgroup of a single-arm safety study rather than a
prespecified endpoint. {{trial:tbcrc-022}} gave neratinib with the emtansine conjugate in three
parallel cohorts, one of which had already progressed on that conjugate. Neither study was built
to be set against a dedicated brain metastasis protocol, and neither enrolled against one.

The decision rests on DESTINY-Breast12, on size and structure rather than on randomisation. Its
brain metastasis cohort was enrolled prospectively alongside a cohort without brain disease, on
the same drug and the same schedule, which is the nearest thing in this literature to an internal
control. The central nervous system progression-free rate in the brain cohort ran close to the
overall progression-free rate in those same patients. That observation, more than any single
response rate, is what makes the compartment look less exceptional than it did. The trial
performed no formal hypothesis testing, so its rates are descriptive rather than tested, and the
two cohorts were never randomised against each other.

{{trial:debbrah-cohort-5}} appears in this table because it is the same drug given in
leptomeningeal disease. Its comparator is a historical median, so it is argued under [[BS-7860]]
rather than here.

Two limits travel with all of it. None of these studies has a control arm, so each is measured
against what its own cohort was expected to do. And nothing here speaks to a conjugate given
before any HER2-directed therapy, which is the position {{trial:destiny-breast07-module-7}} is
aimed at.

The practical consequence is a real change in what can be offered. A patient with HER2-positive
disease and asymptomatic brain metastases can reasonably be given a conjugate before radiotherapy,
with MRI surveillance rather than immediate local treatment. That is a judgement built on
single-arm activity, because no trial has randomised the order. What deferring radiotherapy costs,
if anything, is unmeasured.

## BS-7850 Reading an intracranial response rate, and what the cohort contributes to it

```story SQ-1120
```

Three different objects are reported as intracranial activity, and they are routinely lined up in
one sentence. A progression-free rate at a fixed timepoint is not a response rate. A response by
neuro-oncology brain metastasis criteria is not a response by solid tumour criteria. The fixed
timepoints are not the same timepoint either.

```story SQ-0880
```

```evidence setting=cns topic=intracranial-response sort=year cols=acronym,phase,n,population,endpoint,result caption=Single-arm_studies_reporting_an_intracranial_response_rate._Read_the_population_column_before_the_result_column._The_entry_criteria_differ_more_between_these_studies_than_the_drugs_do.
```

That column is more mixed still. {{trial:landscape}} scored a volumetric reduction of at least
half, a rule it wrote for itself before either modern criterion was in general use.
{{trial:pembrolizumab-in-bm-of-diverse-histologi}} counted stable disease as intracranial benefit.
It also enrolled brain metastases from several primary tumours, of which breast was one. Neither
number is a response rate in the sense the others are.

The criteria matter less than the cohort, and there is now one place to see that directly.
{{trial:u-bomb}} scored the same 47 patients under both sets of criteria and reported two rates
within a couple of percentage points of each other. The spread between the studies in that table
is many times larger. Whatever is driving it, the scoring convention is not the main part.

What does move the number is who was admitted. A radiotherapy-naive lesion has not been selected
for surviving radiation. It is also easier to measure, because after radiotherapy treatment
effect, necrosis and residual tumour resemble one another on MRI. Prior radiotherapy is therefore
two variables at once, a selection variable and a measurement variable. No design in this
literature separates them.

The within-trial comparisons point the same way and do not agree perfectly. In {{trial:permeate}},
where one regimen was given to both strata, the radiotherapy-naive cohort responded far more often
than the cohort progressing after radiotherapy. {{trial:debbrah}} splits the same way across its
cohorts. {{trial:tbcrc-022}} does not reproduce the pattern. Its previously untreated cohort
held six patients. The generalisation should be held loosely, which is an argument for reading the
population column rather than for substituting a new ranking.

The two ends of the table make the point without any comparison of drugs being possible.
{{trial:patricia}} required progression after radiotherapy and sits at the bottom. LANDSCAPE
required that no brain radiotherapy had been given at all and sits near the top, on the
combination now considered the weakest of its class. {{trial:abc-study}} sits at the very top, in
triple-negative disease, which is the subtype with the least intracranial evidence behind it. Read
as a ranking of drugs, the column says a lapatinib combination outperforms the deruxtecan cohorts,
and that a chemotherapy and antibody regimen in triple-negative disease outperforms everything.
Nobody believes either claim, which is the tell.

What the table cannot show is a comparison of any kind. Not one of these studies randomised
anything, least of all the order of radiotherapy and systemic treatment, which is the variable its
rows differ by most. There is also no column for who read the scans. Most of these rates are
investigator-assessed. The one study in this chapter that published an investigator read and an
independent read of the same scans is tabulated under [[BS-7820]]. Its two rates were not the
same.

```caution
Reading the result column of that table as a ranking of drugs is the commonest error in this
literature. It is made in review articles, in slide decks and at tumour boards.

The studies that look best are in large part the studies that admitted radiotherapy-naive,
asymptomatic, measurable disease. The studies that look worst are in large part the studies that
required progression after radiotherapy. Neither the drugs nor the patients have been randomised
against each other at any point.

When a colleague quotes an intracranial response rate, the useful question is not which drug it
belonged to. It is what the patient had to have to get into the study.
```

## BS-7860 Leptomeningeal disease and whether delivery has to be intrathecal

Leptomeningeal disease is a different problem from parenchymal disease, in three ways that matter
before any drug is chosen.

There is no bulky lesion with disrupted vasculature to exploit, so the mechanism that lets a
conjugate work in the parenchyma is largely absent. Disease is read from cerebrospinal fluid
cytology and from neurological examination rather than from target lesions. Cytology is highly
specific and insensitive, so a negative result in a patient with characteristic symptoms and
characteristic imaging does not exclude the diagnosis. Repeated large-volume sampling raises the
yield. Cerebrospinal fluid circulating tumour DNA improves on cytology where it is available
[[BS-3520]]. Survival is short enough that the choice of endpoint constrains the design before the
drug is chosen.

```story SQ-0960,SQ-0970
```

The HER2-positive case has been pursued by both routes, never by both in the same trial.

```story SQ-0910
```

```evidence setting=cns topic=leptomeningeal status=reported weight=any sort=year cols=acronym,phase,n,population,arms,endpoint,result,os caption=Studies_entered_on_leptomeningeal_disease,_by_the_systemic_and_by_the_compartment-directed_route._The_endpoints_are_different_objects_and_most_of_the_comparators_are_historical.
```

Ten rows, two routes, and almost no comparators. Two things in that table would have been
surprising a decade ago. Systemic therapy can change the course of the disease. Drug can also be
put into the compartment safely, at concentrations in the range sought. Neither has been
established against a fair comparator.

The endpoints divide the table into four kinds of study, and the division does not follow the
drug. {{trial:tbcrc-049}} and {{trial:debbrah-cohort-5}} set overall survival against a historical
median named in the protocol. {{trial:pembrolizumab-lmd-cohort}} set survival at a fixed early
timepoint against a two-stage null, in a cohort that was mostly but not only breast cancer.
{{trial:deposein}}, {{trial:proton-csi-versus-photon-ifrt}} and
{{trial:intrathecal-trastuzumab-oberkampf-phase-}} measured progression in the compartment being
treated. {{trial:intrathecal-trastuzumab-bonneau-phase-i}} and
{{trial:intrathecal-trastuzumab-kumthekar-phase-}} measured a tolerated dose. A maximum tolerated
dose, a cerebrospinal fluid response and a neurological progression-free survival cannot be pooled
into anything.

A prespecified historical median is the weakest comparator on offer, and it fails in a specific
way. The medians available were assembled before the brain-penetrant HER2 drugs these patients had
already received existed. A cohort enrolled now beats such a number partly by being enrolled now.
Both HER2-positive systemic single arms are also very small, and one of them extremely so.
{{trial:ang1005}} carries a milder form of the same problem. Its leptomeningeal group is a subset
of a parenchymal study, never selected for HER2, and set against historical survival rather than
against an arm.

The two intrathecal trastuzumab dose-finding programmes are the clearest measurement lesson in the
chapter. They arrived at different recommended doses on different schedules. That is not a
disagreement about the drug. One defined its maximum tolerated dose by reaching a target
concentration in cerebrospinal fluid, and the other by observed dose-limiting toxicity. Two
definitions of the same phrase produce two answers. One of the two also admitted HER2-positive
tumours of any origin rather than breast cancer alone. The third intrathecal study gave the weekly
dose and reported a neurological endpoint, which is the most clinically meaningful thing measured
by this route and the hardest to set beside anything else here.

The only randomised test of the compartment-directed principle is DEPOSEIN, and its construction
decides how far it carries. It added an intrathecal drug that is not HER2-directed, in a
population not selected by subtype. It measured progression in the compartment it was treating,
while allowing systemic therapy to vary from patient to patient. An endpoint scoped to the treated
compartment, with the background treatment uncontrolled, favours the intervention by construction.
Overall survival did not separate. The trial supports the principle of treating the compartment
rather than the use of intrathecal trastuzumab, which is a different drug on a different
rationale.

The radiotherapy comparison has the same shape. Craniospinal irradiation lengthened central
nervous system progression-free survival against an involved field that by definition did not
cover disease outside it, so part of that separation is field size rather than particle. The trial
randomised breast and lung cancer together and stopped early at a planned interim analysis, which
tends to leave an effect estimate at its most flattering.
{{trial:radiotherapy-followed-by-intrathecal-tra}} combines the two routes in a phase 1 sized for
safety. Its record carries no result, so it settles nothing about either route.

What no row offers is the comparison the clinic needs. Nobody has randomised compartment-directed
against systemic treatment, or added the intrathecal route to a modern systemic backbone. The case
definition also varies across these studies, some requiring cytology and some accepting imaging
and clinical signs, which is a case definition rather than a biological boundary. And the outcome
that matters most to a patient here, neurological function over time, appears as a primary
endpoint in one row of ten.

```practice
Do not let a negative cytology close the question when the symptoms and the MRI say otherwise.
Repeat the tap, take a large volume, and image the whole neuraxis before the puncture rather than
after it.

In HER2-positive disease, start with systemic therapy that has leptomeningeal evidence behind it,
however weak the comparator. That is where the survival signal is.

Reserve the intrathecal route for disease progressing in the compartment while systemic treatment
holds elsewhere. Nothing tests it as an addition to modern systemic therapy, so it is a rationale
rather than a result. Describe it to the patient in those terms.

Name the goal at the outset. For most patients here it is neurological function and symptom
control rather than length of life. Saying so at the start makes every later decision easier
[[BC-680]].
```

## BS-7870 Screening, surveillance, and a decision the trials have not tested

Routine brain MRI in asymptomatic patients is not supported by randomised evidence in any subtype.
That should be stated to the patient rather than left for her to assume otherwise.

The argument for screening is that smaller lesions are more amenable to focal treatment and less
likely to require whole-brain treatment. The argument against is lead time without benefit,
together with the cascade that follows finding lesions which would never have become symptomatic.
Neither has been tested against survival in this disease.

Two developments have shifted the balance without settling it. Focal treatment is better tolerated
and reaches more lesions than it did when the screening question was last asked. And systemic
agents with intracranial activity now exist, so finding a lesion earlier can change the systemic
choice rather than only adding a local one. Both are arguments. Neither is evidence.

Prognosis is what should set surveillance intensity, and it is not uniform. Survival after a
breast cancer brain metastasis varies several-fold across prognostic groups defined by subtype,
performance status, age, lesion number and extracranial disease [@sperduto2020]. The same scan
interval cannot be right at both ends of that range.

The decision the trials have not tested is the one made most often. A patient on effective
systemic therapy develops isolated intracranial progression while everything else is controlled.
No trial randomises whether to change the systemic regimen or to treat the brain and continue. The
general oligoprogression argument is in [[BS-4780]] and applies here with one addition. The brain
may be a sanctuary rather than a site of resistance, so a regimen still working everywhere else
may be working in the only sense available to it.

```practice
Image the brain for symptoms. Where you screen an asymptomatic patient, say that you are doing it
on a rationale rather than on evidence. Say what you would do with an abnormal result.

Set the interval from the prognostic group rather than from the calendar [@sperduto2020].

Biopsy a solitary brain lesion when the diagnosis is uncertain. Request receptors whenever tissue
is being removed anyway. Acquired ERBB2 amplification and intrinsic subtype switching are both
described in brain metastases [@priedigkeit2017], so the target in the brain may not be the target
in the primary.

Treat isolated intracranial progression as a question about the compartment before treating it as
systemic failure [[BS-4780]].
```

## BS-7880 Unresolved questions and what reads out next

```evidence setting=cns status=ongoing,awaited cols=acronym,phase,population,arms,endpoint,status caption=Trials_entered_on_central_nervous_system_disease_that_have_not_reported.
```

{{trial:destiny-breast07-module-7}} would put a conjugate into active brain metastases in the
first line, which is the one position in HER2-positive disease where nothing has been tested.
{{trial:swog-s2007}} takes the conjugate question into HER2-negative disease, where the
prospective record currently amounts to two single-arm studies. {{trial:radiate-lm-nrg-bn014}}
repeats the craniospinal comparison at phase 3, against the same involved field, so it will
confirm or overturn a result without removing the field-size objection to it.

{{trial:tutor}} is the one to be careful about. It is dose-finding, so it will report a tolerated
dose rather than an answer about concurrency. A positive safety result will be quoted as though it
were an efficacy result, and it will not be one.

Four gaps will survive all of them.

No trial randomises the order of local and systemic therapy in asymptomatic brain metastases. That
is now the commonest decision in this chapter, made harder rather than easier by the conjugate
results.

No design separates prior radiotherapy as a selection variable from prior radiotherapy as a
measurement problem. Until one does, the strata tabulated under [[BS-7850]] cannot be read as
biology.

Nothing connects tissue concentration to intracranial response in the same patient. The window
studies measure delivery and stop there, while the trials that measure response take no tissue.

And no trial randomises compartment-directed against systemic treatment in leptomeningeal disease.
The endpoint that could do it has not been agreed, which is the more fundamental problem of the
two.
