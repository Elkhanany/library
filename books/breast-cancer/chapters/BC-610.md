## BS-5240 Breast cancer in adolescents and young adults

The adolescent and young adult boundary is administrative. Registries draw it at 15 to 39 years,
and nothing in the biology changes at either edge. The category is useful because the consequences
of treatment run for decades rather than because the tumours are a class.

Young-onset disease is enriched for higher stage at presentation, higher grade, and for
triple-negative and HER2-positive phenotypes. The incidence trend and its interpretation are set
out in [[BS-0160]]. What is less often quoted is where the survival deficit actually sits.

One multicentre cohort examined 17,575 women with stage I to III disease, of whom 1,916 were aged
40 years or younger. Median follow-up was 6.4 years. After adjustment, breast cancer mortality was
higher in the younger group, with a hazard ratio of 1.4 and a 95% confidence interval of 1.2 to
1.7. Stratified by subtype, the excess was significant in luminal A disease. The hazard ratio
there was 2.1, with an interval of 1.4 to 3.2. It was also significant in luminal B disease, with
a hazard ratio of 1.4 and an interval of 1.1 to 1.9. It was borderline in triple-negative disease
and absent in HER2-positive disease [@partridge2016].

```caution
The intuitive account is that young women do badly because they get aggressive subtypes. This
dataset says the opposite. Once subtype is held constant, the age effect concentrates in the
luminal tumours, which are the ones with the best average prognosis. Age is doing something
inside hormone receptor positive disease that subtype assignment does not capture.
```

Young women describe their experience in terms the tumour registry does not record. Definitions of
young vary, and the concerns cluster around fertility, partners, children and work
[@dunn2000; @gorman2012]. Quality of life in survivors is generally good, with more emotional
disruption among the youngest, much of it tied to an abrupt treatment-induced menopause
[@ganz2003]. A systematic review found distinct psychosocial and menopause-related concerns, weight
gain and physical inactivity [@howardanderson2012]. Interventions that treat the woman without her
partner may not be the best design, and couple-based approaches have early support [@baucom2005].
The reproductive consequences are reviewed for gynaecologists in [@hickey2009].

Three tasks belong at diagnosis and are time-critical. Germline testing changes surgical and
systemic decisions, and the indications are in [[BC-070]]. Fertility referral must precede the
first dose, which is the subject of [[BS-5250]]. And the conversation about pregnancy after
treatment starts now, because it constrains the endocrine plan described in [[BC-490]].

## BS-5250 Fertility preservation before treatment

Two different problems hide under one heading. One is banking gametes or embryos, which preserves
the material. The other is protecting the ovary during chemotherapy, which preserves function.
They are not substitutes for each other, and a patient offered only the second has been offered
half of what exists [@oktay2018].

Cryopreservation of mature oocytes or embryos after controlled ovarian stimulation is the
established method, and guidelines treat embryo and oocyte cryopreservation as standard practice
[@loren2013]. Stimulation with concurrent letrozole keeps oestradiol low, and pregnancy rates with
the resulting embryos were comparable to those expected in women without cancer [@oktay2015]. The
options and their evidence are reviewed in [@oktem2009; @dunn2009; @redig2011; @moffat2014]. Ovarian tissue cryopreservation is now an option rather than an experimental
procedure. Random-start protocols allow stimulation to begin at any point in the cycle, which
removes most of the scheduling delay that used to make referral feel incompatible with starting
treatment. Referral before surgery rather than after it shortened the interval to chemotherapy and
allowed more stimulation cycles [@lee2010].

Ovarian protection with a gonadotropin-releasing hormone agonist has been tested directly. In
{{trial:poems}}, premenopausal women with operable hormone receptor negative disease received
standard chemotherapy with or without goserelin. Among 135 patients with complete primary endpoint
data, ovarian failure at 2 years occurred in 8% of the goserelin group and 22% of the
chemotherapy-alone group. The odds ratio was 0.30, with a 95% confidence interval of 0.09 to 0.97
[@moore2015]. An individual patient level meta-analysis of five trials and 873 patients found
premature ovarian insufficiency in 14.1% with the agonist and 30.9% without it. The adjusted odds
ratio was 0.38, with an interval of 0.26 to 0.57. Post-treatment pregnancy occurred in 10.3% and
5.5% respectively [@lambertini2018]. The earlier literature was less settled, and a systematic review
found insufficient evidence either way [@cruz2010]. The debate that followed is visible in the
correspondence between investigators [@blumenfeld2015; @delmastro2015b; @lambertini2015].
Amenorrhoea itself is a poor proxy for lasting ovarian failure. About half of women with six months
of chemotherapy-induced amenorrhoea resumed bleeding within three years [@sukumvanich2010].

```caution
Most of this evidence uses amenorrhoea or a postmenopausal follicle-stimulating hormone level as
the endpoint. Those are measurements of menstrual and hormonal status. They are not measurements
of the capacity to conceive. Anti-Mullerian hormone and antral follicle count measure reserve,
which is a third quantity again. A patient told that her ovarian function was protected has been
told something narrower than she will hear.
```

```practice
Refer before the first dose, not after the first cycle. The window is the interval between
diagnosis and treatment, and it closes.

Offer cryopreservation and ovarian suppression as separate decisions. A patient may reasonably
take one, both, or neither.

Ask what the patient wants, not what her age predicts. Uptake is poor and the commonest reason is
that the conversation did not happen. At four cancer centres, infertility risk was documented in 26%
of records and referral in 13% [@quinn2015]. Patients report that they raised the subject
themselves, and that they were dissatisfied with how it was discussed
[@yee2012; @yeomanson2013; @peate2011].
```

## BS-5260 Pregnancy-associated and postpartum breast cancer

Cancer complicates a small fraction of deliveries. Among 3,168,911 deliveries in California,
breast cancer was the most frequently associated malignancy, at 0.13 per 1,000, and maternal and
neonatal morbidity were increased [@smith2001]. Reviews of breast cancer during pregnancy set out the clinical
problem [@gwyn2001]. The tumours share histological and prognostic features with those of other
young women [@middleton2003].

Pregnancy-associated breast cancer is a phrase that collapses two different situations. One is
diagnosis during gestation. The other is diagnosis in the years after delivery. Only the first is
a treatment problem in the obstetric sense. The second is a biological problem, and it is the more
consequential of the two.

Parity confers long-term protection and short-term risk. A pooled analysis of 15 prospective
studies found that risk in parous women remained above that of nulliparous women for more than 20
years after childbirth. Crossover to reduced risk was observed for oestrogen receptor positive
disease and not for receptor negative disease. The pattern was more pronounced with a family
history, with older age at first birth, and with more births. Breastfeeding did not modify it
[@nichols2019].

The mechanism is postpartum involution, developed in [[BS-0310]] and [[BS-2050]]. Involution is a
programmed tissue remodelling event with the features of wound healing, including collagen
deposition and COX-2 dependent inflammation, and it promotes progression of ductal carcinoma in
situ in model systems [@lyons2011]. The tumour is not more aggressive by grade. The tissue it
grows in is transiently permissive.

The clinical correlate is a stage-independent excess of metastasis. In a cohort study, diagnosis
within 10 years post partum was associated with increased risk of metastasis. The excess was
greatest in stage I and stage II disease, was present in both receptor positive and receptor
negative cancers, and persisted in receptor positive cases for up to 15 years after diagnosis.
Postpartum cases showed more lymphovascular invasion and more nodal involvement, and no increase
in Ki67 index [@goddard2019].

The measurement caveat matters here more than usual. The window chosen to define postpartum
disease determines the answer. Five years and ten years are different definitions, and studies
using them are not directly comparable. Neither is a biological boundary.

```interplay target=BC-250
Postpartum disease is the cleanest available demonstration that host tissue state, rather than
tumour genotype, can carry prognostic weight. The tumours are not separated from others by the
receptor and grade measurements of [[BC-110]]. The stroma they arose in is different. This is the
microenvironment argument of [[BC-230]] arriving through an obstetric history.
```

## BS-5270 Treatment during pregnancy and lactation

The governing principle is that the pregnancy is usually continued and the cancer usually treated.
Termination has not been shown to improve oncologic outcome, and the decision belongs to the
patient rather than to the algorithm.

Diagnosis is delayed by the pregnant breast. Ultrasound is the first imaging test, mammography
with abdominal shielding is acceptable, and core biopsy is unaffected by gestation. In one series,
cancers diagnosed during pregnancy were mammographically evident despite dense parenchyma, and
ultrasound showed every mass and tracked the response to chemotherapy [@yang2006]. Staging avoids
computed tomography and isotope bone scan where alternatives exist.

Surgery is safe in any trimester, and breast conservation is possible when radiation can wait
[@kuerer2002; @annane2005]. Sentinel node mapping with technetium radiocolloid is acceptable, and
blue dye is avoided. Dosimetry puts the fetal dose far below the threshold for harm
[@gentilini2004; @keleher2004; @pandittaskar2006]. Small clinical series found mapping feasible
during pregnancy [@khera2008; @mondi2007], and a review of contraindications did not list pregnancy
as absolute [@filippakis2007]. Radiotherapy is deferred until after delivery in almost all
cases.

Chemotherapy is contraindicated in the first trimester because organogenesis is occurring.
Anthracycline and taxane regimens are given in the second and third trimesters. The evidence is
cumulative rather than randomised. Reviews of cytotoxic exposure in pregnancy found the first
trimester to be the period of risk [@doll1989; @ebert1997]. Among 160 pregnancies exposed to
anthracyclines, fetal outcome was normal in 73%, complications were commoner in the first
trimester, and doxorubicin doses above 70 mg/m2 per cycle raised the risk [@germann2004].
A prospective protocol of FAC in the second and third trimesters, in 57 women, found the children
healthy at follow-up [@hahn2006], and a smaller series agreed [@garciamanero2009]. Taxanes and
vinorelbine appeared feasible after the first trimester in a review of 24 pregnancies [@mir2008],
supported by case reports [@gonzalezangulo2004; @gainford2006]. Delivery is
planned with an interval after the last cycle sufficient for maternal and fetal marrow recovery.
Chemotherapy is withheld beyond about 35 weeks so that spontaneous labour does not occur at the
neutrophil nadir.

Three drug classes are contraindicated throughout. Endocrine therapy is teratogenic. HER2-directed
antibodies cause oligohydramnios through their effect on fetal renal epithelium. That warning rests
on case reports, several describing reversible anhydramnios and one a poor fetal outcome
[@watson2005; @bader2007; @sekar2007; @witzel2008]. Others describe uneventful pregnancies
[@fanale2005; @waterston2006; @shrim2007; @pant2008], including one after first-trimester exposure
to lapatinib [@kelly2006]. Checkpoint
inhibitors have no safety data in pregnancy and a mechanism that predicts harm.

The outcome data are more reassuring than the mechanism suggests, and the reason is instructive. A
multicentre case-control study compared 129 children whose mothers had cancer during pregnancy
with matched controls. Of the exposed children, 96 had received chemotherapy in utero. There was
no significant between-group difference in cognitive development on the Bayley scales, and cardiac
evaluation at 36 months was normal. Gestational age at birth correlated with cognitive outcome in
both groups. Prematurity predicted worse cognition, and that effect was independent of cancer
treatment [@amant2015].

```caution
The clinical instinct is to deliver early and then treat freely. This dataset says that iatrogenic
prematurity, not cytotoxic exposure, is the measurable harm to the child. Elective early delivery
to start treatment is a decision that trades a documented risk for a hypothetical one.
```

Lactation is not compatible with cytotoxic therapy, endocrine therapy, or HER2-directed
antibodies. Breastfeeding from a previously irradiated breast is often unsuccessful for mechanical
reasons, and the contralateral breast is usually adequate. After breast-conserving therapy,
lactation from the treated breast was possible but diminished in most women [@moran2005].

## BS-5280 Pregnancy after breast cancer and interrupting endocrine therapy

The clinical conflict is arithmetic. Adjuvant endocrine therapy runs for 5 to 10 years, and the
patients who most want a pregnancy are the ones for whom that interval consumes the remaining
fertile years.

{{trial:positive}} addressed the question directly. Women aged 42 years or younger with stage I to
III hormone receptor positive disease, who had received 18 to 30 months of adjuvant endocrine
therapy, interrupted treatment to attempt pregnancy. Among 516 women the median age was 37 years
and 93.4% had stage I or stage II disease. Of 497 followed for pregnancy status, 368 had at least
one pregnancy and 317 had at least one live birth. At 1638 patient-years of follow-up there had
been 44 breast cancer events against a prespecified safety threshold of 46. The 3-year incidence
of breast cancer events was 8.9% in the interruption group and 9.2% in an external control cohort
[@partridge2023].

The follow-up duration must be stated every time this result is quoted. Median follow-up was 41
months. Hormone receptor positive disease recurs on a hazard curve that stays meaningfully above
zero for two decades, which is the argument in [[BS-2880]]. A 41-month result addresses the fear
that interruption causes an early relapse. It cannot address whether interruption shifts risk into
years 10 to 20, because the trial has not been running that long.

Three further limits belong with the number. The design was a single group with an external
control rather than a randomised comparison. The population was selected, both by stage and by
having already completed 18 to 30 months of therapy without recurrence. And the protocol resumed
endocrine therapy after the pregnancy attempt, so the result describes an interruption rather than
an abandonment.

Observational data on pregnancy after breast cancer are consistent and must be read carefully. An
early matched cohort of 107 women with a later pregnancy found no adverse effect on long-term
prognosis, with a small non-significant excess among those who conceived within 12 months
[@kranick2010]. A
systematic review and meta-analysis found that survivors were less likely to conceive than the
general population, with a relative risk of 0.40 and a 95% confidence interval of 0.32 to 0.49.
Caesarean section, low birth weight, preterm birth, and small for gestational age were all more
frequent, particularly after chemotherapy. Congenital abnormality was not increased. Compared with
survivors who did not conceive, those who did had better disease-free survival, with a hazard
ratio of 0.66, and better overall survival, with a hazard ratio of 0.56 [@lambertini2021]. A later
analysis restricted to hormone receptor positive disease found no disease-free survival
difference, with a hazard ratio of 0.96 and an interval of 0.75 to 1.24. Overall survival in that
analysis favoured the pregnancy group, with a hazard ratio of 0.46 [@arecco2023].

```caution
The apparent survival advantage of pregnancy is almost certainly selection. Women conceive when
they are well, are still well at the point of conception, and have already survived the interval
in which early recurrence occurs. Presenting that hazard ratio to a patient as a benefit of
pregnancy misrepresents it. The defensible statement is that pregnancy has not been shown to be
harmful, and that is a different sentence.
```

```practice
Raise this before the first prescription, not at the two-year review. The plan for interruption
shapes which endocrine regimen is chosen and whether ovarian suppression is used.

Quote the follow-up. Say that the reassurance covers roughly three years and that the trial
continues.

Resume endocrine therapy afterwards. That was the protocol, and the result does not license
stopping.

Fertility does not return because treatment stopped. Reassess reserve rather than assume it.
```

## BS-5290 Parenting, relationships, and long-horizon survivorship

A woman treated at 34 has a survivorship horizon measured in decades, and every late effect in
[[BC-650]] applies to her for longer than it applies to anyone else.

Premature menopause is the dominant late effect and it arrives abruptly. Chemotherapy-induced
ovarian failure and ovarian suppression produce in months what normally takes years. The
consequences are bone loss, vasomotor symptoms, genitourinary atrophy, sexual dysfunction, and a
cardiovascular risk profile shifted forward by decades. The management belongs to [[BS-5560]] and
[[BS-5570]], with one modification. Systemic hormone replacement, which is the standard answer in
the general population, is not available here.

Adherence is the quiet failure. In a cohort of 8,769 women, 32% had discontinued adjuvant
endocrine therapy by 4.5 years, and only 72% of those who continued were fully adherent. Women
under 40 years had the highest risk of discontinuation, with a hazard ratio of 1.51 and a 95%
confidence interval of 1.23 to 1.85 [@hershman2010]. The population with the most to gain from
prolonged therapy is the population least likely to complete it. Poor adherence to long-term
medication is a problem across medicine, not a peculiarity of oncology [@osterberg2005].

Second primary risk is also age-weighted. In a population cohort of women with early invasive
breast cancer, contralateral breast cancer by 20 years was 3.1 percentage points more common than
expected. The absolute excess was greater in younger women than in older ones [@mcgale2025]. That
shapes the surveillance argument in [[BS-5590]].

The non-medical load is not a soft topic. Young patients carry dependent children, active careers,
mortgages, and partners who become caregivers. Work disruption and out-of-pocket cost fall on the
years of lowest accumulated wealth, which is the mechanism in [[BC-700]]. Fear of recurrence and
body image after mastectomy at 30 are covered in [[BS-5620]] and [[BS-5630]].

```practice
Ask about children by name and age. It changes what the patient is optimising for and it is not
recorded anywhere in the chart.

Treat adherence as a clinical outcome with its own follow-up, not as patient behaviour.

Document who is at home. The partner of a 35-year-old patient is doing childcare, income, and
caregiving at once.
```
