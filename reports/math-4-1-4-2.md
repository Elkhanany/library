## Verdict

Both chapters are sound: every derivation follows step by stated operation, no step is skipped,
and the two loud claims the brief singled out — that $g_{1}B_{12}=g_{2}B_{21}$ really follows from
the $T\to\infty$ limit, and that the sharpened Frobenius bound
$\norm{[\hat x,\hat p]-\ii\hbar\hat I}_{F}\ge\hbar\sqrt n$ with equality exactly for commuting pairs
is correct — both check out exactly, the second including its equality condition and its numerical
attainment at $n=2,3,4,5$. There are **no BLOCKERs**; there are **four MAJOR** defects (one wrong
printed ratio in 4.2 §10.2 that does not follow from its own displayed inputs, one probability
quoted against the wrong comparison in two places, one item on 4.2 §1.2's central "assumed versus
proved" ledger that its own §7.1 contradicts, and one clinical analogy in 4.1 that maps the wrong
Einstein coefficient onto $k_{\text{off}}$) and **fourteen MINOR** ones, mostly last-printed-digit
and internal-consistency slips. The ⚑ budgets are met exactly — seven in 4.1, ten in 4.2, on
exactly the permitted items — every 4.2 postulate is boxed before use, the Born rule is named as
the first thing in the book posited rather than cornered, and every row of the renaming table cites

## §2 Gaps in the chain

Only one place asks the reader to supply a move. Everything else I traced runs step by stated
operation.

**G1 — `src/ch4-1.html` §4.1: the inference from universality to "one constant" is not yet available
where it is made.**

§4.1 argues: "First, by §1.2 the radiation's spectrum is universal, so a relation between the atom's
level spacing and the frequency it couples to cannot depend on which atom is used — it is a property
of the field." Kirchhoff's theorem is about $u(\nu,T)$; it says nothing about atomic level spacings
until the atoms have been coupled to the field, which happens in §§4.4–4.6. The deduction becomes
available exactly at `e-uB`, and is worth one sentence there. **The missing line, and where it
goes** — immediately after `e-uB` in §4.6, before the "Recap":

> Notice that this also closes §4.1's identification. $A_{21}/B$ is a property of the atom and
> carries no temperature, so the whole $T$-dependence of $u$ at fixed $\nu$ sits in the exponent.
> Two atoms coupling to the same $\nu$ with different level spacings would give the same universal
> $u$ two different temperature dependences, which §1.2 forbids. So $E_{2}-E_{1}$ is a function of
> $\nu$ alone, the same function for every atom — and the only thing §4.1 assumed beyond that is
> that the function is a straight line through the origin.

That is what makes the identification a *narrow* assumption rather than a broad one, and the
chapter's own three tests (§5.5, §5.6, §6) are then tests of the linearity alone.

Otherwise: the identification **is** honestly announced — §4.1 sets it in bold as "This is an
identification, not a derivation", states precisely what is assumed ("that one constant suffices —
that the ratio $(E_{2}-E_{1})/\nu$ is the same number at every frequency rather than a function of
frequency"), gives the dimensional argument for why *some* constant is unavoidable (correct:
$[\nu^{a}c^{b}]=\mathsf{L}^{b}\mathsf{T}^{-a-b}$ carries no mass for any exponents), and names three
independent checks. The writing agent's flag was warranted and the handling is right; G1 is the one
line it is short of.

Two smaller items, both already covered but worth recording as places a reader could stall:

- **§4.5's closure argument** ("the only term available that is not already present") is sound only
  under the ground rule that every rate is linear in the populations and in $u$. The rule is stated
  in §4.4 and restated in §4.5, so nothing is smuggled; but the rule is a modelling choice, not a
  consequence, and the chapter never says so in as many words.
- **`src/ch4-2.html` §9.1** builds $V\otimes W$ and derives $\dim=mn$ before P7's box. The object is
  a definition and the *assertion* (that nature uses it) is boxed before §9.2 spends it, so the
  ordering rule is honoured; the reader who reads the section title as the postulate may not see
  that.
