*Re-derived from scratch in sympy/mpmath by an agent that did not write either chapter. Nothing below
was checked against recall; every symbolic claim was recomputed from the book's own definitions
(signature $(+,-,-,-)$, $x^{0}=ct$, $[\nabla_\mu,\nabla_\nu]V^\rho = R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}V^\sigma$,

## Verdict

**The physics is right.** Every symbolic result in both chapters — the nine Christoffel symbols, the
three Ricci components, the $(AB)'/(rB)$ collapse, Birkhoff's $R_{01}=\dot B/(rB)$, the circular-orbit
quadratic, $L_{\min}=2\sqrt3\,GM/c$, the ISCO, $\omega^{2}$, $\Delta\varphi=6\pi GM/c^{2}p$, the null
potential, $r_{\rm ph}$, $b_{\rm crit}$, the exactly-zero first-order null residual, $\alpha=4GM/c^{2}b$,
the two-halves split, $K=48G^{2}M^{2}/c^{4}r^{6}$ and the Eddington–Finkelstein metric, determinant and
null slopes — reproduced exactly, and the signature never slips: $g_{00}=+(1-r_s/r)$ follows from the
book's own $g_{00}\simeq1+2\Phi/c^{2}$ with $\Phi=-GM/r$, and every downstream sign is consistent with it.
**Three printed numbers are wrong** — two unit conversions in §6.5's tidal table (off by $10^{3}$) and one
proper-time value in Worked example 1 (off by $10$) — none of which feeds a derivation, and every headline
figure ($r_s$, ISCO, $42.98''$, $1.7512''$, $0.8756''$, $+45.7/-7.2/+38.5\ \mu$s, $K(r_s)$) is right to the
last printed digit. **Two structural defects need fixing before ship**: four unescaped `<` characters in
`ch3-7.html` silently delete text from the rendered page — one of them removes the sign analysis that
identifies which root of the circular-orbit quadratic is the maximum, another removes part (c) of Problem 3
— and §8.3's stated error bound on the linearisation ($w/u_c$, which is $e=0.21$ for Mercury) is far too
weak to license §8.5's comparison against $42.98\pm0.04$.

### Gap 1 — `src/ch3-7.html` §2.2 (and §4.1): the areal radius needs $S$ to be usable as a coordinate

$r\equiv\sqrt S$ is introduced as spending the relabelling freedom. It is only a legitimate coordinate

needed on each time slice. Insert after "This is the **areal radius**":

> One condition is being assumed and it should be said: $S$ must be strictly monotonic in $\rho$, so that
> $r=\sqrt S$ is invertible and can serve as a coordinate. Where it is not — a "throat" at which the
> spheres stop growing outward — a different label has to be used, and the solution below does not
> describe that region.

### Gap 2 — `src/ch3-7.html` §4.1: the removal of $g_{01}$ is asserted in one clause

The text says: "complete the square and supply an integrating factor, which exists because only one other
variable is present, and a function $T(x^{0},r)$ appears for which the whole of it is $\tilde A c^{2}\dd

> Complete the square: $A(\dd x^{0})^{2}+2g_{01}\dd x^{0}\dd r = A\big(\dd x^{0}+ (g_{01}/A)\dd r\big)^{2} -
> (g_{01}^{2}/A)\dd r^{2}$, the second piece joining the $\dd r^{2}$ term. The one-form
> $\omega=\dd x^{0}+(g_{01}/A)\dd r$ involves two variables only, and in two variables every non-vanishing

### Gap 3 — `src/ch3-7.html` §8.3: the error control on the linearisation (see MAJOR 6)

Replace the sentence beginning "The term in $w^{2}$ is dropped" with:

> The term in $w^{2}$ is dropped. Be careful about why that is safe, because the obvious reason is the
> wrong one: $w/u_{c}$ is the fractional variation of $1/r$ around the orbit, and for Mercury that is the

### Gap 6 — `src/ch3-8.html` §7.3: why increasing $\tilde t$ is the future *inside* $r_{s}$

The table's whole force is that inside $r_{s}$ both null slopes $\dd r/\dd\tilde t$ are negative *toward the
future*. Outside, $\tilde t$ is manifestly a time function; inside, $\partial_{v}$ is spacelike
($g_{vv}=A<0$) and the orientation has to be argued. One line, after "Both slopes are now explicit
functions of $r$":

> One thing has to be fixed before the signs mean anything: which way is the future. It is settled once
> and for all outside the horizon, where $v=ct+r_{*}$ increases with $t$, and a time orientation is a
> continuous choice on a connected spacetime, so it carries inward unchanged. An infalling particle has
> $\dd v/\dd\lambda>0$ and $\dd r/\dd\lambda<0$, hence $\dd\tilde t/\dd\lambda=\dd v/\dd\lambda-\dd
> r/\dd\lambda>0$: $\tilde t$ increases toward the future everywhere, inside $r_{s}$ as well as outside.
